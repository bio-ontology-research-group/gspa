\subsection{Stage 0 calibration, reliability weights, and correlation groups}

Stage 0 is implemented in \fn{CalibrationTable.groovy}, \fn{EvidenceCombiner.groovy}, and \fn{EvidenceType.groovy} (package \texttt{gspa.integration}). A claim record that carries a calibrated probability is used as given; otherwise its raw score $s(c)$ is mapped to $\tilde{q}(c) = \text{clip}(\sigma_{\text{log}}(a_e s(c) + b_e), 10^{-4}, 1 - 10^{-4})$ with source-specific coefficients $(a_e, b_e)$: $(6.0, -3.0)$ for DIAMOND and MMseqs2 (raw score = percent identity / 100), $(4.0, -1.5)$ for HMMER and Pfam, $(5.0, -2.0)$ for InterProScan and FoldSeek, $(3.5, -1.3)$ for eggNOG-mapper, $(3.0, -1.0)$ for SignalP and DeepTMHMM, $(3.0, -1.2)$ for operon claims, $(4.0, -1.8)$ for gapseq, $(8.0, -4.0)$ for AMRFinder, $(5.0, -2.0)$ for dbCAN, $(4.0, -1.5)$ for antiSMASH, and $(4.0, -2.0)$ for every other source, which includes the deep-learning and protein-language-model predictors.

Each claim is weighted by the reliability $r_e$ of its evidence type (method \texttt{defaultReliability} of \texttt{EvidenceCombiner}): $0.70$ for sequence similarity and orthology, $0.75$ for sequence domains, structure-based deep learning, and BGC and virulence-factor predictors, $0.80$ for structural similarity and CAZyme predictors, $0.85$ for AMR predictors, $0.65$ for sequence deep learning and protein-language-model embeddings, $0.55$ for metabolic context and genomic language models, $0.50$ for localization and sequence motifs, $0.45$ for genomic context, and $0.60$ for the remaining types (dark-matter, reaction-local-context, cross-genome, and ranker claims, and region-level predictors). A \texttt{--theta} file can override these weights; the per-benchmark run scripts list the files they pass.

For each protein--function pair, claims are grouped by the correlation group of their evidence type (method \texttt{correlationGroup} of \texttt{EvidenceType}). There are ten shared groups (homology, protein-sequence machine learning, structure, context, genomic language models, inferred context, motif, localization, region features, and viral) and one group for each of the four domain-specific predictor types (AMR, CAZyme, BGC, and virulence factor). The homology group contains sequence similarity, sequence domain, and orthology claims, so DIAMOND, Pfam, and InterProScan claims for the same pair count once, through the strongest of them. Within each group only the claim with the largest $r_e \tilde{q}(c)$ contributes (the second-opinion bonus is $0$ by default), its probability is clipped to $[0.001, 0.999]$, the group probabilities are combined by noisy-OR, and the result is converted to log-odds clipped to $[-12, 12]$.

\subsection{Targeted rescue and specificity terms of the Stage 1 priors}
\label{sec:supp_rescue}

The iterative refiner records the number of supporting claims for every protein--function pair, and when these counts are present (which is the case in every \texttt{gspa integrate} run) the essentiality ($B_1$), coherence ($B_2$), gap-filling ($B_5$), and genomic-context ($B_6$) priors apply a targeted form. Each of these priors collects borderline candidates for every unmet invariant (an uncovered essential category, a missing step of a process or pathway, an open metabolic gap, or a missing pathway term in an operon), ranks them, and raises only the top-ranked candidate. A candidate is a pair whose Stage 0 likelihood probability $P^{(0)}$ lies in $[0.16, 0.50]$ for $B_1$ and in $[0.18, 0.50]$ for $B_2$, $B_5$, and $B_6$. Candidates are ranked by a rescue tier and then by $P^{(0)}$ (method \texttt{candidateRescueTier} of \texttt{IntegrationState}): tier 4 if $P^{(0)} \ge 0.45$, tier 3 if $P^{(0)} \ge 0.40$, tier 2 if the protein's highest GO likelihood is below $0.50$, tier 1 if the pair has at least two supporting claims, and tier 0 otherwise. $B_2$, $B_5$, and $B_6$ raise the top candidate only when its tier is at least 1; $B_1$ has no tier requirement. With $L^{(0)}$ the Stage 0 log-odds of the selected pair, the boosts are
\begin{align*}
  B_1 &: \max\bigl(\min(2.0\,\alpha_{\text{ess}}, 1.30\,\alpha_{\text{ess}}),\ 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr) = \max\bigl(1.30\,\alpha_{\text{ess}},\ 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr),\\
  B_2 &: \max\bigl(1.80\,\alpha_{\text{coh}}\,w,\ 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr),\\
  B_5 &: \max\bigl(1.15\,\mu,\ 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr),\ \mu = \alpha_{\text{gap}}\ (0.7\,\alpha_{\text{gap}}\text{ for gapseq-guessed gaps}),\\
  B_6 &: \max\bigl(1.65\,\alpha_{\text{ctx}},\ 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr),
\end{align*}
where $w$ is the process or pathway weight defined below (source lines: \texttt{EssentialityPrior.groovy} 106--124, \texttt{CoherencePrior.groovy} 186--222, \texttt{GapFillingPrior.groovy} 114--125, and \texttt{GenomicContextPrior.groovy} 206--217). The second argument guarantees that the boost alone lifts the pair to log-odds $0.14$ (probability $0.535$) before damping. In this targeted form the uniform base terms written in the Methods for $B_1$, the process and pathway part of $B_2$, $B_5$, and the pathway-consensus part of $B_6$ are not applied to the other candidates; the obligate-complex weights of $B_2$ and the co-operonic complex partner weight of $B_6$ are applied as defined in the Methods. When claim counts are absent (programmatic use of the priors without the refiner), the priors fall back to the base terms.

The same four priors also attenuate uncorroborated secondary terms (method \texttt{computeProteinSpecificityPrior} of \texttt{IntegrationState}). On a protein whose top GO term in an aspect has $P^{(0)} \ge 0.55$ and at least two supporting claims, every other term of that aspect with a single supporting claim and $P^{(0)} \in [0.40, 0.46)$ that lies outside the prior's own domain terms receives the adjustment $\max(-\kappa, -0.52 - L^{(0)})$, which moves it towards log-odds $-0.52$ (probability $0.373$). The magnitudes are $\kappa = 0.30\,\alpha_{\text{ess}}$ ($B_1$), $0.42\,\alpha_{\text{coh}}$ ($B_2$), $0.35\,\alpha_{\text{gap}}$ ($B_5$), and $0.38\,\alpha_{\text{ctx}}$ ($B_6$, restricted to proteins in operons). A pair is adjusted at most once per iteration, by the first prior that reaches it.

\subsection{Process and pathway weights of the coherence prior}

The coherence prior computes two weights for missing terms (\fn{CoherencePrior.groovy}:91--123). For GO \fn{has_part} process dependencies triggered by the baseline annotation, the weight is $w_{\text{proc}} = \max(0.25, n_{\text{sat}}/n_{\text{trig}})$, where $n_{\text{trig}}$ and $n_{\text{sat}}$ are the numbers of triggered and satisfied dependencies, so the weight grows as the genome's process coverage grows. For every pathway or module of the pathway database (KEGG pathways and modules) with at least one required GO term annotated, the weight of its missing required terms is $w_{\text{path}} = \max(0.35, 0.35 + 0.65\,c_W)$ with step coverage $c_W$; missing terms of pathways with $c_W < 0.15$ do not enter the targeted-rescue set. Each missing term takes the largest weight over the processes and pathways that require it, and $w$ in the $B_2$ boost above is this weight.

Stage 2A runs only when \texttt{--enforce-stage2} is given, which is the case for the ablation configurations A8 and A9, the perturbation configurations A8 and A9, the reference panels, the 1,000-genome and multi-kingdom runs, the leave-subunit-out Stage 2 configuration, the eukaryote configurations E2 and E4 (remove) and E5 (relax), and the Stage 3 benchmark runs. The reaction-local genomic context suggester (\texttt{--rlc-suggester}) is used in the leave-reaction-out runs (\fn{benchmark/leave_reaction_out/run_ablation_rlgc*.py}) and in the MAG runs, and the dark-matter suggester in the Stage 3 benchmark runs whenever a gap table is present. On every split, the per-protein baselines are retrained on that split's training set. \section{Secondary metabolism, metagenomic dark matter, and Stage 2B stoichiometric refinement}
\label{sec:supp_fba_bacdive}

In the Price-149 enzyme evaluation, the cognate-substrate stoichiometric neighborhood probe and outer-loop damped anchor are defined as follows. For each candidate pair $(p, u)$, let $n_1(p, u)$ and $n_2(p, u)$ count chromosomal neighbors within $\pm 10$ genes whose reactions share non-currency metabolites with $u$ at 1 and 2 hops, $f_{\text{op}}(p)$ denote the co-directional operon fraction, and $s_{\text{nb}}(p, u) = \min(2.0, \sum_{|g' - g| \le 10} (1 + 0.25|g' - g|)^{-1} P_{\text{cand}}(g', u))$ measure distance-weighted neighbor EC support. The local stoichiometric probe shift $\Delta L_{\text{probe}}(p, u) = 0.85 \min(1.5, 0.55 n_1 + 0.20 n_2) + 0.45 f_{\text{op}}(p)\mathbb{I}[n_1 + n_2 > 0] + 0.35\mathbb{I}[u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{bal}}] - 0.50\mathbb{I}[u \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{ec}}]$ and Stage 1 neighbor coherence shift $\Delta L_{\text{stage1}}(p, u) = 0.90 s_{\text{nb}}(p, u) + 0.50 f_{\text{op}}(p)\mathbb{I}[s_{\text{nb}}(p, u) > 0.20] + 0.30\mathbb{I}[u \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{kegg}}]$ are applied over $T=3$ damped outer iterations ($\lambda_t \in \{0.70, 0.42, 0.252\}$):
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{single}}^{(t)}(p, u) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\!\Bigl(0.75 \, \text{logit}\bigl(P^{(t-1)}(p, u)\bigr) + 0.25 \, \text{logit}\bigl(P^{(0)}(p, u)\bigr) + \lambda_t \bigl(\Delta L_{\text{stage1}}(p, u) + 0.65\,\Delta L_{\text{probe}}(p, u)\bigr)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:supp_outer_anchor}
\end{equation}
and the multi-predictor posterior blends $0.55 P_{\text{single}}^{(t)}(p, u) + 0.45 P_{\text{NoisyOR}}(p, u)$. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_bgc} reports the biosynthetic gene cluster benchmark, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_mags} the metagenome-assembled genome panel, and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_invariant_audit} the 108-genome stoichiometric invariant audit, the comparison with six curated BiGG models, and the Price-149 arms split by metabolic stratum.

The metagenome-assembled genome (MAG) panel (\fn{paper-reproducibility/pipelines/P14_mag_pathway_gaps/}) uses single-sample SemiBin MAGs from Empty Quarter metagenomes. Of $3{,}835$ MAGs labelled high quality, all have CheckM2 completeness $\ge 90\%$ and contamination $\le 5\%$ and a GTDB-Tk r226 phylum, and $3{,}794$ in $35$ phyla also have a completed gapseq run and a Bakta annotation. From the $17$ phyla with at least $10$ eligible MAGs we drew a phylum-stratified sample of $100$ MAGs ($88$ bacterial and $12$ archaeal; four per phylum, the remaining $32$ in proportion to phylum size; seed $20261019$). Gap reactions are the SEED reactions of gapseq 1.4.0's gap-filled model that are absent from its draft model, mapped onto Bakta loci together with gapseq's own GPR genes and sub-threshold tblastn hits. Stage 0 claims come from DIAMOND 2.1.16 (\texttt{blastp}, E-value $\le 10^{-5}$, five targets) against $83{,}592$ Swiss-Prot entries with experimental GO annotations and from HMMER 3.3.2 (\texttt{hmmsearch --cut\_ga}) against Pfam-A with \texttt{pfam2go}; InterProScan was not run. The gap-filling prior produced $1{,}254$ of the fills at $q \ge 0.30$ and RLGC $18$ ($4$ at $q \ge 0.50$).

\section{Experimental phenotype prediction in BacDive and statistical testing}
\label{sec:supp_bacdive}

The BacDive benchmark uses the two comparison tables published with gapseq: $2{,}332$ ProTraits carbon-source assays from $791$ strains and $50$ substrates, and $17{,}496$ BacDive enzyme assays from $3{,}164$ strains after keeping assays with a definitive activity and calls from all three reconstructions. In the five-fold genus-blocked cross-validation, each genus (the first word of the organism name) is assigned to fold $\text{MD5}(\text{genus}) \bmod 5$, so that no genus contributes to both training and test data. Within each training split, the conditional probabilities of a positive phenotype given the gapseq call ($p_{g,\text{cal}}$) and the ModelSEED call ($p_{m,\text{cal}}$) and the assay prevalence $a_{\text{prev}}(y)$ are estimated with additive smoothing, and they are combined with the Track 1 posterior and the coupling score $\psi(s, y)$ into a fold-local pooled probability
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{logit} p_{\text{pool}}(s, y) = \beta_g \operatorname{logit} p_{g,\text{cal}} + \beta_m \operatorname{logit} p_{m,\text{cal}} + \beta_a \operatorname{logit} a_{\text{prev}}(y) + \beta_1 \operatorname{logit} P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y) + \beta_\psi\,\psi(s, y),
\end{equation}
with $(\beta_g, \beta_m, \beta_a, \beta_1, \beta_\psi) = (0.45, 0.25, 0.15, 0.15, 0.50)$ for carbon sources and $(0.48, 0.24, 0.14, 0.14, 0.45)$ for enzymes. The blended reconstruction call used for $\psi$ is $0.50g + 0.32m + 0.18c$ for carbon sources and $0.52g + 0.30m + 0.18c$ for enzymes. The GSPA hybrid trains a gradient-boosted classifier on the three calls, these fold-local quantities, the strain's call profile, and a one-hot assay indicator, and reports $0.88$ times its output plus $0.12\,p_{\text{pool}}$. The supervised baselines are re-implementations in scikit-learn, after the designs of Traitar ($L_1$-regularized linear SVM with probability calibration), BacDive-AI (random forest), and DiASPora (gradient-boosted trees), trained on the three binary calls and a one-hot assay indicator. In Track 2, $30\%$ of each strain's assays (selected by an MD5 hash of strain, assay, and row index) are revealed and the remaining $70\%$ are evaluated. All Track 2 quantities, including $p_{\text{pool}}$, are estimated on the revealed assays only. For carbon sources, $\operatorname{logit} P_{\text{Track2}} = 0.72 \operatorname{logit} p_{\text{pool}} + 0.28 \operatorname{logit} \hat{a}(y) + 0.85\,\psi_{\text{rev}}(s, y)$, where $\hat{a}(y)$ is the smoothed positive rate of assay $y$ among revealed assays and $\psi_{\text{rev}}$ applies the coupling matrix $\Psi$ to the strain's revealed outcomes coded as $\pm 1$. For enzymes, $\operatorname{logit} P_{\text{Track2}} = 0.70 \operatorname{logit} p_{\text{pool}} + 0.30 \operatorname{logit} \hat{g}(s, y)$, where $\hat{g}$ is the smoothed revealed positive rate of the enzyme in the strain's genus, or of the enzyme overall when the genus has no revealed assay of it. The decision threshold is tuned on the revealed assays, and the Track 2 hybrid reports $0.65$ times a gradient-boosted classifier trained on the revealed assays plus $0.35\,P_{\text{Track2}}$. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_bacdive_folds} reports per-fold $F_1$ for both assay panels; the code is \fn{benchmark/unified/pillar3_bacdive/run_bacdive_tracks1_2.py}.

Paired comparisons of binary outcomes use the exact two-sided McNemar test, computed as a binomial test of the discordant counts $b$ and $c$ against $\text{Bin}(b + c, 0.5)$; this applies to the BacDive, Price-149, 95-genome, leave-subunit-out, and Stage 3 comparisons. Confidence intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals: over proteomes for the multi-kingdom $F_{\max}$ differences ($10{,}000$ resamples, seed $20261019$) and over proteins for Price-149 Top-1 accuracy ($2{,}000$ resamples, seed $42$).

\section{Stage 3: tool schemas, benchmark construction, model settings, and seed stability}
\label{sec:supp_agent}

Stage 3 gives the language model five deterministic, read-only tools, defined in \fn{benchmark/agent/run_gspa_agent.py}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \fn{inspect_operon_neighborhood(protein_id)} returns the operon membership of a candidate protein and the annotated functions of its neighbouring genes.
  \item \fn{inspect_pathway_or_complex_gap(anomaly_id)} returns the context of the missing reaction or subunit and the candidate proteins of the anomaly.
  \item \fn{query_protein_evidence(protein_id)} returns the sequence, domain, structure, language-model, and genomic-context claims of a candidate protein.
  \item \fn{check_symbolic_constraints(go_term, organism_taxon)} checks a GO term against the taxonomic (SAT) and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints for the organism and lists the nearest valid ancestors when the term is forbidden.
  \item \fn{propose_resolution(anomaly_id, action, protein_id, function_id, confidence, rationale)} submits the decision, one of \textsc{promote}, \textsc{relax}, \textsc{demote}, or \textsc{abstain}, with a confidence in $[0.50, 0.95]$ for \textsc{promote} and \textsc{relax} or in $[0.05, 0.25]$ for \textsc{demote}. The symbolic guardrail verifies the proposed term before the decision is accepted.
\end{enumerate}

The benchmark (version 2) is built from the anomaly records that the integrator itself writes (\fn{paper-reproducibility/pipelines/P10_stage3_agent/}). Seven genomes were integrated with all priors, Stage 2A relaxation, operons, and gapseq gaps: \textit{E.~coli} K-12, \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{P.~aeruginosa}, and \textit{M.~tuberculosis} with DIAMOND, Pfam, and InterProScan claims, and \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, \textit{C.~elegans}, and \textit{A.~thaliana} with DIAMOND and Pfam claims. For pathway gaps, every annotated pathway step covered by a single protein was held out in turn by deleting that protein's claims for the step's term and its descendants; the step was added to the gap table, the integrator was re-run with the anomaly cap lifted, and the extractor's own pathway-gap record was taken. Of $597$ hold-outs, $50$ produced a record in which the held-out protein re-entered the candidate list through the dark-matter suggester. These were split by SHA-256 order into a true-positive half and a decoy half before any decoy was run; in a decoy the held-out protein is removed from the genome and the operon table. Decoys were matched one-to-one to true positives on candidate count and maximum candidate posterior ($\pm 0.05$). For ontology conflicts, $40$ proteins per genome received two claims for a GO term that the guardrail forbids for the organism, at raw scores on a grid from $0.40$ to $0.85$, in Stage 1 runs without Stage 2A (with Stage 2A, all $280$ injected violations were relaxed or removed before extraction). Genuine conflicts are the extractor's borderline-conflict records for injected terms (posterior in $[0.35, 0.50)$); traps are borderline-conflict records from the same runs whose term is supported by the truth annotations and valid for the organism, matched on candidate count, supporting-claim count, and posterior ($\pm 0.05$). The benchmark holds $20$ true positives and $20$ decoys from four bacteria and $20$ genuine conflicts and $20$ traps. Incomplete-complex and borderline-essential anomalies could not be constructed in this way: of $7$ complex and $32$ essential hold-outs, at most one produced a record containing the held-out protein, in line with their rarity in natural data ($6$ and $83$ of $31{,}839$ uncapped anomalies over the 88 multi-kingdom proteomes). GO labels shown to the model are the \texttt{name} fields of GO release 2026-03-25, and $8$ records whose target term is absent from the guardrail's GO release (2025-06-01) were excluded.

Every protein identifier the model can see (candidates, operon neighbours, anchors) is replaced by an opaque token \texttt{P\#\#\#\#}, operons by \texttt{O\#\#\#\#}, and anomaly identifiers by \texttt{A\#\#\#}, assigned in SHA-256 order, and candidate order is shuffled by SHA-256 within each anomaly; no gene symbol or accession appears in the model input. The mapping is in \fn{results/pillar4_v2_id_mapping.tsv}, and the leak checks in \fn{results/pillar4_v2_leak_checks.tsv} show identical model-visible fields for positives and negatives in both categories and anomaly-level AUCs of $0.47$ to $0.53$ for true positives against decoys on the maximum posterior, the maximum suggestion score, and the fraction of candidates in the pathway operon. In the conflict category, every genuine item carries the integrator's consistency prior ($-0.68$) and no trap does, so this category tests whether a method uses that symbolic signal.

The model is \fn{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}, served by vLLM on two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with a context of $131{,}072$ tokens, reasoning mode off, and at most $450$ output tokens per turn. The configurations are zero-shot (one turn, only \fn{propose_resolution}, no guardrail), fast (two turns, guardrail), guarded (six turns, guardrail; seed $42$ at temperature $0.0$ and seeds $43$ and $44$ at temperature $0.25$), and unguarded (six turns, no guardrail, seed $42$). Full transcripts, including every request and response with timestamps and the SHA-256 of the agent code in the header, are in \texttt{results/pillar4\_v2\_transcripts\_<configuration>\_<seed>.jsonl}, and those of the LRO-50 runs in \texttt{results/pillar4\_v2\_transcripts\_lro50\_<configuration>\_<seed>.jsonl}. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_stage3_v2} reports the three seeds of the guarded agent and the per-category decisions of every configuration, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_agent_mcnemar} the paired tests, and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_trigger_rates} the trigger rates. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_cafa6} reports the isolated-protein CAFA evaluation (\fn{results/suite1_cafa6_prospective_summary.tsv}).

\section{Pan-phylum AllTheBacteria analysis}
\label{sec:supp_allthebacteria}

The AllTheBacteria analysis (\fn{paper-reproducibility/pipelines/P11_allthebacteria/}) runs in four stages on KAUST Ibex. Stage A calls ORFs with pyrodigal, records co-directional neighbour chains, removes exact duplicates, and clusters each batch with MMseqs2 \texttt{easy-linclust} ($90\%$ identity, $80\%$ coverage); every ORF inherits the scores of its cluster representative. Stage B aligns every representative with DIAMOND (\texttt{blastp --sensitive -k 25}, E-value $\le 10^{-3}$) against the experimentally annotated Swiss-Prot training set of the CAFA (Kaggle) release, which gives the maximum identity of each representative and the DIAMOND $k$-NN GO transfer, and scores the representative with the GSPA-Seq channels. Stage C writes per-genome claims and KEGG-coverage gap claims (pathways with $40$--$100\%$ of their GO terms covered at $\ge 0.45$; these are not gapseq gaps) and runs the integrator. Stage D runs the same integrator call on every genome of each completed batch, $96$ genomes per JVM with a shared ontology load; a test on $20$ genomes reproduced all $40$ Stage C outputs and all $40$ regenerated inputs byte for byte.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_atb_survey} reports the alignment survey over $72{,}533$ genomes, $11{,}632$ species, and $37$ phyla. Across $292$ million ORFs, $41.0\%$ had less than $30\%$ identity to any training protein ($85.8\%$ of these had no alignment) and a further $27.0\%$ had $30$--$50\%$ identity. The dark fraction ranged from $36.5\%$ in Actinomycetota and $40.4\%$ in Pseudomonadota to $60.8$--$66.6\%$ in Spirochaetota, Cyanobacteriota, Bacteroidota, and Planctomycetota. These counts depend only on the Stage B alignments.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_atb_integrator} reports the integrator results. The integrator call is \texttt{gspa-unified.jar} v2.1.0 \texttt{integrate} with all six priors, three iterations, and Stage 2A relaxation (\texttt{--enforce-stage2 --enforce-consistency-mode relax --lite}), compared with Stage 0 noisy-OR on the same claims. The Stage D snapshot holds all $7{,}009$ genomes of the $15$ wave-1 batches finished on 2026-10-19 ($29$ phyla, $3{,}965$ species); run (a) holds the $4{,}592$ genomes that Stage C had integrated, the first $20$ genomes by sample identifier in each of $230$ batches. In the snapshot, $15{,}603$ proteins ($0.06\%$ of ORFs) gained a first annotation above $0.50$, mostly through the essentiality prior and in the twilight regime; no zero-alignment protein was annotated, and the gap-filling prior fired on no row. Of the $875{,}786$ suppressed pairs, $284{,}481$ were taxon-forbidden and $521{,}301$ were genome-wide co-annotation conflicts.

Several properties of the batch pipeline bound these numbers. No ESM-2 3B forward pass was run for any AllTheBacteria representative: representatives that occur in the training or benchmark sets use cached embeddings, and all others receive a bitscore-weighted interpolation of the embeddings of their DIAMOND hits, so zero-alignment ORFs have no language-model channel. No \texttt{hmmsearch} was run; the InterPro and Pfam channel copies the domain lists of Swiss-Prot hits with at least $35\%$ identity and is also empty for zero-alignment ORFs, which therefore rely on the DeepGOCNN channel alone. Stage C writes each claim with a single merged source and one supporting claim, so correlation-grouped pooling has one group per pair, and the rescue paths that require two supporting claims or a candidate in the gap-claim window rarely apply. Taxon-forbidden suppression requires the genome's taxid in the integrator's bundled NCBI hierarchy, which holds $1{,}302$ taxa; only $43$ of the $6{,}460$ distinct taxids of the collection are in it, and the other genomes are constrained only through genome-wide co-annotation conflicts. The \texttt{priors\_fired} field holds each prior's boost at the final refinement state, so attributions to priors that fired only in earlier, damped iterations are missed.

Stage D over all completed batches ($43$ wave-1 batches with $20{,}683$ genomes and $204$ wave-2 batches with $449{,}653$ genomes, about $33{,}700$ CPU hours at $258$ CPU seconds per genome) is still running. When it finishes, the per-batch partials are combined and copied into \texttt{results/allthebacteria/} with \texttt{bash run.sh combine \$R/partials\_stageD \_stageD; bash run.sh fetch \_stageD} in the P11 folder, which writes \texttt{atb\_java\_summary\_stageD.json} and the \texttt{*\_java\_stageD.tsv} tables. The first AllTheBacteria tables (\texttt{insight*.tsv} without \texttt{\_java}) were produced by a Python heuristic in Stage C and are not used here except for their alignment columns.
