\section{Introduction}

Automated annotation of protein function from genome assemblies is a central problem in computational biology, underpinning comparative genomics, genome-scale metabolic modeling, and systems biology \citep{radivojac2013large,zhou2019cafa}. Two major methodological paradigms have been developed for assigning functional terms from the Gene Ontology (GO) \citep{thomas2019gene} and Enzyme Commission (EC) nomenclature. Production genome annotation pipelines—such as the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) \citep{tatusova2016ncbi,li2021refseq}, Bakta \citep{schwengers2021bakta}, Prokka \citep{seemann2014prokka}, eggNOG-mapper \citep{cantalapiedra2021eggnog}, Pharokka \citep{bouras2023pharokka}, and InterProScan \citep{jones2014interproscan,paysanlafosse2023interpro}—rely on sequence alignment \citep{buchfink2021sensitive}, profile hidden Markov models (HMMs) \citep{eddy2011accelerated}, and curated orthology rules to assign annotations protein by protein. Concurrently, deep learning and protein language model (PLM) predictors \citep{lin2023evolutionary,elnaggar2021prottrans,heinzinger2024prostt5} evaluated in the Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA) challenges \citep{jiang2016expanded,ramola2023cafa4,kaluza2024cafa5}—including DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure}, metagenomic-deepFRI \citep{bezshapkin2026scaling}, DeepGOZero \citep{kulmanov2022deepgozero}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, SPROF-GO \citep{yuan2023fast}, CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, and TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}—predict calibrated GO or EC probabilities from individual amino acid sequences or predicted three-dimensional structures \citep{vankempen2024foldseek}.

Despite their differences in representation, both paradigms share a fundamental modeling assumption: they predict the function of each protein $p_i$ in a proteome $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ conditionally independently of the remaining proteins $\mathcal{P} \setminus \{p_i\}$ and of the organism's broader biological architecture. Evaluated at the scale of a whole genome, this independence assumption produces five systematic failure modes: (1) essential cellular machinery required by viable organisms in a domain of life (such as translation, tRNA aminoacylation, or DNA replication initiation) can remain unannotated when divergent orthologs fall just below strict per-protein homology thresholds; (2) predicted enzymatic repertoires leave stoichiometric holes in otherwise complete metabolic pathways \citep{osterman2003missing,karp2018how} when individual enzymes reside in the sequence-identity twilight zone ($<30\%$ identity); (3) obligate heteromeric protein complexes (such as ATP synthase, NADH dehydrogenase, urease, or photosystem reaction centers \citep{meldal2022complexportal,giurgiu2019corum}) suffer from both missing subunits and false-positive singleton subunit calls triggered by promiscuous domain hits; (4) sequence similarity and PLM embedding transfer across distant homologs routinely transfer clade-inappropriate GO terms that violate formal taxonomic constraints \citep{deegan2010formalization}; and (5) in fungi, plants, and animals, homology transfer frequently assigns developmental or physiological biological processes—such as \textit{heart development} (\texttt{GO:0007507}), \textit{flower development} (\texttt{GO:0009908}), \textit{neural crest cell migration} (\texttt{GO:0001755}), or \textit{vascular transport} (\texttt{GO:0010232})—to organisms that lack the corresponding anatomical structures.

Comparative genomics and systems biology offer complementary constraints that can resolve these failures. Gene neighborhood conservation, co-directional operon structure, and phylogenetic profiles capture functional coupling across prokaryotic pathways and complexes \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,szklarczyk2023string}, as recently learned by genomic language models such as gLM \citep{hwang2024genomic}, gLM2 \citep{cornman2024omg}, Evo \citep{nguyen2024sequence}, and contextualized bacterial protein models \citep{toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised}. In metabolic network reconstruction, tools such as Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian,latendresse2014efficiently}, ModelSEED \citep{henry2010high,seaver2021modelseed}, ProbAnno \citep{benedict2014probanno}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind,price2022gapmind}, Reconstructor \citep{jenior2023reconstructor}, and CanOE \citep{smith2012canoe} use stoichiometric network connectivity to fill reaction gaps. However, automated top-down reconstructions can still mispredict auxotrophies and catabolic phenotypes when gene--protein--reaction (GPR) associations are noisy \citep{price2023carveme}. Moreover, reaction-level MILP gap-fillers—including CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast} and our own prior reaction-indicator MILP formulation METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} (developed by overlapping authors of the present study)—optimize binary reaction indicators $y_j \in \{0, 1\}$ without distinguishing isozymes from obligate heteromeric complex subunits, assign negative objective costs ($c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) to high-scoring candidate reactions (which activates hundreds of zero-flux gap-fill additions), and apply uniform reaction-to-protein probability boosts that cannot disambiguate which specific gene locus encodes a missing enzymatic step \citep{niu2026meteor}. In bio-ontology engineering, formal taxon constraints (\texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) \citep{deegan2010formalization} and cross-product logical definitions linking GO biological processes to the Uberon multi-species anatomy ontology \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon,hill2010representing,khodiyar2011representation}, the Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, and the Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell} provide a formal logical foundation for testing whether an organism possesses the anatomical structures entailed by a functional annotation. Concurrently, tool-using biological language agents such as GeneAgent \citep{wang2025_geneagent}, ProtAgents \citep{ghafarollahi2024_protagents}, SPIRES \citep{caufield2024_spires}, GORetriever \citep{yan2024_goretriever}, and ProtST \citep{xu2023_protst} have demonstrated strong capability in synthesizing multi-modal evidence, provided they are invoked selectively on ambiguous loci and gated by formal symbolic verification.

Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA v2.1.0), a unified four-stage framework for proteome-wide function prediction (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Stage 0 calibrates and pools per-protein evidence channels via Noisy-OR (or ingests any single standalone GO or EC predictor). Stage 1 iteratively refines annotation log-odds across the proteome using six genome-scale priors: core essentiality, pathway and complex coherence, SAT-based taxonomic consistency, GO--Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints, metabolic gap-filling with reaction-local genomic context (RLGC), and operon neighborhood coupling. Stage 2 combines a deterministic symbolic enforcer (Stage 2A) that eliminates taxonomic and anatomical violations via semantic ancestor relaxation with a flux-certified stoichiometric locus refiner (Stage 2B) that couples sparse GPR aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious FBA (pFBA) \citep{lewis2010omic}, and backward locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$. Stage 3 provides an optional, selectively triggered neuro-symbolic agent using an open-weight instruction language model (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}, served as \texttt{qwen3.8-27b}) gated by a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail. We evaluate GSPA across eight benchmark suites (Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}--\ref{tab:suites}; full provenance mapping in \texttt{results/PROVENANCE\_MAP.md}).

We first evaluated how Stage 0 multi-channel pooling and Stages 1--2 genome-scale priors affect protein-centric accuracy and structural genome coherence across nine bacterial and archaeal reference genomes (\textit{Escherichia coli} K-12, \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7, \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{Helicobacter pylori} 26695, \textit{Mycoplasma genitalium} G37, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{Methanocaldococcus jannaschii} DSM 2661; 81 runs; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12} and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). For high-throughput multi-genome benchmarks (the 9-genome, 13-genome, 88-genome, and 1,000-genome panels), Stage 0 defaults to the 3 fast sequence/domain channels (DIAMOND, InterProScan, and Pfam HMMER; $5.4$--$12.7\text{ s/genome}$), whereas on the nested 21-genome neural panel (which contains all 13 panel genomes plus 8 additional reference proteomes, and is itself nested within the 88-genome four-kingdom cohort) we evaluate both the 3-channel core and all 7 channels including ESM-2/ProtT5 $k$NN, ProteInfer, and FoldSeek+ProstT5 3Di. Because roughly $85\%$ of annotated proteins in the 9 reference organisms carry high-confidence Swiss-Prot homology ($P_0 \ge 0.90$), Stage 0 Noisy-OR pooling (\texttt{A1\_noisy\_or}) establishes the bulk protein-centric baseline: micro $F_{\max} = 0.7631 \pm 0.0817$ (sample s.d., $\text{ddof}=1$, across 9 genomes; $0.2853 \pm 0.1038$ against experimental-only truth) and CAFA $F_{\max} = 0.8041 \pm 0.0650$ ($0.2363 \pm 0.1489$ experimental-only). Across the full Stage 1+2 pipeline (\texttt{A9\_stage2\_rel}), pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ increases slightly to $0.7660 \pm 0.0791$ ($0.2847 \pm 0.1016$ experimental-only; two-sided exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0391$) while CAFA $F_{\max}$ is $0.8037 \pm 0.0647$ ($0.2366 \pm 0.1494$ experimental-only; two-sided exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.1289$, with slight decreases of $0.0001$--$0.0016$ in 8 of 9 genomes; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Excluding the hyperparameter development genome \textit{E.~coli} K-12 yields the same pattern across the 8 out-of-sample genomes: micro $F_{\max}$ rises from $0.7718$ to $0.7749$, CAFA $F_{\max}$ is $0.8143$ vs.\ $0.8140$ ($0.2140 \to 0.2143$ experimental-only), $C_{\text{path}}$ rises from $0.4834$ to $0.5012$, $C_{\text{cplx}}$ rises from $0.8743$ to $0.8865$, and the internal GAEF structural score rises from $0.8434$ to $0.8599$.

Examining individual priors on the 9-genome panel (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), the essentiality prior (\texttt{A2}) lifts mean core functional completeness $C_{\text{ess}}$ from $0.9400 \pm 0.0785$ to $0.9444$ (and to $0.9479 \pm 0.0716$ in combined Stage 1+2 \texttt{A7}--\texttt{A9}, improving in both genomes that were $<1.0$ at baseline—$+0.0706$ on \textit{H.~pylori} and $+0.0010$ on \textit{M.~genitalium}—with 0 decreases across the 9 genomes, exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.50$). The coherence prior (\texttt{A3}) increases mean pathway coherence $C_{\text{path}}$ from $0.4975 \pm 0.1089$ to $0.5052$, and the gap-filling (\texttt{A4}) and operon neighborhood (\texttt{A5}) priors nudge $C_{\text{path}}$ to $0.4977$ and $0.4992$ individually before synergizing with pathway/complex coherence in the combined Stage 1 model (\texttt{A7}) to raise $C_{\text{path}}$ to $0.5146 \pm 0.1078$ (exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00781$) and complex coherence $C_{\text{cplx}}$ from $0.8636 \pm 0.1346$ to $0.8852 \pm 0.1256$ (improving in both genomes with incomplete baseline complexes—$+0.0972$ in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 and $+0.0973$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}—with 0 decreases, exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.50$). Adding the consistency and anatomical priors (\texttt{A6}) eliminates all 25 baseline taxonomic violations across all nine genomes (\textit{Synechocystis}: 8, \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7: 5, \textit{E.~coli} K-12: 4, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}: 3, and 1 each in \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{H.~pylori}, \textit{M.~genitalium}, \textit{M.~jannaschii}, and \textit{P.~aeruginosa}; $C_{\text{cons}}$ from $0.9722$ to $1.0000$, exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00391$) and increases micro $F_{\max}$ from $0.7631$ to $0.7681$ and CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.8041$ to $0.8071$. Because the 25 prokaryotic violations involve cellular component terms whose nearest valid ancestor is the ontology root, \texttt{A8} (removal) and \texttt{A9} (relaxation) yield identical scores on the prokaryotic panel (internal GAEF composite score rising from $0.8459 \pm 0.0367$ to $0.8643 \pm 0.0376$, exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00391$; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b), whereas semantic relaxation differentiates multicellular eukaryotic organ and developmental terms (Section~\ref{sec:euk_results}).

To test robustness against noisy cross-clade homology transfer, we injected 900 cross-clade false-positive claims across the nine prokaryotic genomes (100 per genome: 50 eukaryotic anatomical terms such as \texttt{GO:0007507} \textit{heart development}, \texttt{GO:0009908} \textit{flower development}, and \texttt{GO:0001755} \textit{neural crest cell migration}, plus 50 clade-restricted terms) at raw scores in $[0.55, 0.85]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c). After Stage 0 isotonic calibration, $125$ of the $900$ injected claims ($13.9$ per genome; $86.1\%$ attenuated below $0.50$ by calibration alone) exceed the decision threshold $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$ under unconstrained Noisy-OR (\texttt{A1}). Activating GSPA's taxonomic and anatomical constraints (\texttt{A6}, \texttt{A8}, and \texttt{A9}) suppresses $122$ of the $125$ active violations ($97.6\%$ suppression of post-calibration violations, or $897/900 = 99.7\%$ of all raw injected claims, leaving $0.33$ violations/genome from 3 terms lacking a cross-product link in the GO release and eliminating $100.0\%$ [$55/55$] of direct taxon violations).

We next compared GSPA against external genome annotation pipelines and internal Stage 0 channels under uniform ancestor-propagated GO scoring across Suites 1 and 2 (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d; \texttt{results/pillar1\_suite2\_panel13\_21\_sota.tsv}, compiled from the verified evaluation archives in \texttt{benchmark/manifests/reference\_data/ibex\_extracted/}, reporting both ancestor-propagated and strict leaf/holdout metrics for every method). We next evaluated GSPA's Stage 1 metabolic gap-filling prior and RLGC scoring engine using a leave-reaction-out (LRO) protocol across \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 ($n=256$ strict reactions—defined as monomeric or homo-oligomeric enzymatic steps with a single non-promiscuous primary EC number—and $n=414$ full role-stratified reactions including multi-EC and peripheral steps) and out-of-sample \textit{B.~subtilis} 168 ($n=144$ reactions; Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism} and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar2\_\allowbreak lro15\_\allowbreak summary.tsv} and \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak metabolism\_\allowbreak lro.tsv}). In each LRO trial, the ground-truth gene--reaction association for a target reaction $r$ is masked and candidate proteins in the proteome are ranked to recover the masked enzyme. We report both unconditional recovery across all held-out reactions (\textbf{All}, where reactions whose true enzyme received zero Stage 0 candidate matches are scored as misses) and conditional recovery among reactions where the true enzyme is present in the candidate pool (\textbf{Pool}, where $n_{\text{cov}} = 175$ for sequence-only/M2 and $n_{\text{cov}} = 181$ for RLGC/PLM/M4 because 6 twilight-zone enzymes lack BLAST/DIAMOND hits above the Stage 0 filter but enter the candidate pool via Pfam domain or PLM/operon neighborhood indexing). Crucially, we evaluate two distinct regimes: \textbf{Track A} (where both sequence homologs and ESM-2 reaction-centroid donors with cosine similarity $> 0.50$ to the query protein are masked) and \textbf{Track B} (where sequence alignment hits for the held-out reaction are masked, while Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids remain available).

Decomposing the contributions of genomic context and protein language model embeddings across the 256-case \textit{E.~coli} K-12 LRO benchmark reveals two complementary findings (Table~\ref{tab:metabolism} and Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a,b). First, in the absence of unmasked PLM reaction centroids (Track A), sequence-only candidate ranking and rank-invariant reaction-level MILP boosting (METEOR v8) recover the true enzyme at Rank 1 (Hit@1) in only $1.95\%$ of all cases ($95\%$ Clopper--Pearson CI $[0.6\%, 4.5\%]$; $2.86\%$ given pool). Adding strict cosine-masked Track A features reaches $11.33\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($16.02\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.2605$ All / $0.3685$ Pool), supervised GBDT reranking on Track A reaches $17.97\%$ ($25.41\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.3275$ All / $0.4632$ Pool), RLGC operon kernel density alone reaches $23.05\%$ ($95\%$ CI $[18.0\%, 28.7\%]$; $32.60\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.3355$ All / $0.4745$ Pool), and GSPA Stage 1 M2 pathway coherence + RLGC (without PLM centroids) reaches $23.44\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($34.29\%$ given pool). Second, in Track B, per-protein ESM-2 reaction-centroid cosine similarity alone (\texttt{ESM2\_PLM\_Centroids\_Only}) already achieves $62.11\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($95\%$ CI $[55.9\%, 68.1\%]$; $67.58\%$ Hit@3, $87.85\%$ Hit@1 given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.6474$ All / $0.9157$ Pool), accounting for $97.5\%$ of the unconditional Hit@1 of the full model on the well-annotated \textit{E.~coli} proteome because reference Swiss-Prot reaction centroids retain close embedding neighbors. Combining RLGC operon neighborhood density, Stage 1 pathway coherence, and ESM-2 centroids in \textbf{GSPA Full M4} adds a further $+1.56$ percentage points unconditionally ($+2.21$ percentage points given pool, rescuing 4 additional enzymes whose embedding similarity was tied with paralogs) to reach $63.67\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($95\%$ CI $[57.5\%, 69.6\%]$; $68.36\%$ Hit@3, $69.53\%$ Hit@5) and $90.06\%$ Hit@1 given the 181-reaction candidate pool ($96.69\%$ pool Hit@3, $\text{MRR} = 0.6596$ All / $0.9330$ Pool; on the common 175-reaction intersection, pool Hit@1 is $91.43\%$ for M4 vs.\ $89.14\%$ for PLM-only and $34.29\%$ for M2+RLGC).

Across the full 414-reaction \textit{E.~coli} role-stratified benchmark ($n_{\text{cov}}=226$; Suites 3--5; Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}), RLGC operon neighborhood density alone (without PLM centroids) achieves $15.94\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($29.20\%$ given pool, $\text{Hit@3} = 28.26\%$ All / $51.77\%$ Pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.2437$ All / $0.4465$ Pool) overall, $13.36\%$ ($24.12\%$ pool) on Stratum A Core Biomass ($n=307$), $30.61\%$ ($55.56\%$ pool) on Stratum B Alternative Catabolic ($n=49$, where catabolic operons are tightly clustered), and $17.24\%$ ($34.48\%$ pool) on Stratum C Secondary/Peripheral metabolism ($n=58$), while GSPA Full M4 (Track B) reaches $50.00\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($91.59\%$ given pool) overall and $51.79\%$ ($93.53\%$ pool), $48.98\%$ ($88.89\%$ pool), and $41.38\%$ ($82.76\%$ pool) across Strata A, B, and C, respectively. Stratifying Stratum A reactions by the query enzyme's maximum sequence identity to any annotated homolog in the proteome (\texttt{max\_pident\_annot\_homolog} $\le 70\%$ [$n=49, n_{\text{cov}}=16$] and $\le 50\%$ [$n=23, n_{\text{cov}}=7$]), where RLGC alone scores $0.00\%$ Hit@1 ($62.50\%$ and $85.71\%$ pool Hit@3), GSPA Full M4 achieves $22.45\%$ and $26.09\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($68.75\%$ and $85.71\%$ given-pool Hit@1; $100.0\%$ pool Hit@3; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}c). To illustrate application to uncultured microbial genomes, we applied GSPA with gapseq draft metabolic networks across 97 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) selected from the GTDB/GEM catalog (CheckM completeness $\ge 90\%$, contamination $< 5\%$, lacking cultured species-level representatives) spanning 16 bacterial and archaeal phyla (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d; \texttt{results/pillar3\_dark\_matter\_97mags.tsv}). We next audited the mathematical invariants of gap-filling reaction additions across 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions and six curated BiGG reference GEMs (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a; \texttt{results/pillar2\_invariant\_audit\_summary.tsv} and \texttt{results/pillar2\_curated6\_gems\_real.tsv}). While a draft reconstruction legitimately contains genome-encoded core reactions ($S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge 0.50$) that are inactive on minimal glucose alone ($2{,}809.5$ active core reactions per genome under raw DeepProZyme $\ge 0.50$, with $0.0$ flux-carrying reactions before repair because all 108 draft networks contain biomass gaps, $2{,}621.7$ imbalanced active reactions, and $9.75$ dead-end active reactions), gap-filling adds non-core candidate reactions ($S_{\text{rxn}}(j) < 0.50$) specifically to restore biomass flux connectivity. Across the 108 genomes, negative-cost MILP gap-filling (METEOR v8 \citep{niu2026meteor}, where candidate reactions with score above $\tau_{\text{add}} = 0.20$ receive negative objective cost $c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) adds a mean of $313.35$ non-core reactions per genome ($3{,}121.0$ total active reactions: $446.4$ flux-carrying and $2{,}674.6$ zero-flux active), of which $100.27$ carry non-zero steady-state flux ($|v_j| > 10^{-6}$) while $215.02$ ($68.62\%$) are uncertified zero-flux padding ($2{,}597.8$ imbalanced active reactions and $2.36$ dead-end active reactions in $313.8\text{ s/genome}$). Any positive-cost linear programming formulation over the sanitized universal network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ eliminates uncertified zero-flux gap-fill additions: a baseline positive-cost LP repair (\texttt{Grow-Support Threshold Repair}) adds $81.31$ non-core reactions per genome with $100.0\%$ ($81.31/81.31$) carrying non-zero flux ($2{,}584.8$ total active reactions: $446.4$ flux-carrying and $2{,}138.4$ zero-flux, $0.0\%$ zero-flux added padding, $0.0$ imbalanced active reactions, and $2.23$ dead-end active reactions), whereas GSPA's Stage 2B stoichiometric locus refiner (SLR) with two-step lexicographic pFBA and FVA/KO/perturbation support certification adds a parsimonious mean of $5.70$ non-core reactions per genome with $100.0\%$ ($5.70/5.70$) carrying non-zero flux ($2{,}815.2$ total active reactions $= 446.4$ flux-carrying $+ 2{,}368.8$ unpruned core, $0.0\%$ zero-flux added padding, $0.0$ imbalanced active reactions, and $0.0$ dead-end active reactions in a mean end-to-end two-step LP + FVA/KO/perturbation certification runtime of $18.01\text{ s/genome}$, median $17.19\text{ s/genome}$; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a). Across the six curated BiGG reference GEMs (\texttt{iML1515}, \texttt{STM\_v1\_0}, \texttt{iYL1228}, \texttt{iJN1463}, \texttt{iYS854}, and \texttt{iYO844}; \texttt{results/pillar2\_curated6\_gems\_real.tsv}), CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}—which carves directly from the native BiGG universal reaction database against which the six reference GEMs were curated—achieves a mean BiGG reaction $F_1$ of $0.6955$ (precision $= 0.6693$, recall $= 0.7305$) and EC $F_1$ of $0.8536$ (precision $= 0.8217$, recall $= 0.8927$, $6/6$ growing). Among cross-namespace ModelSEED/MetaNetX reconstructions (whose reaction identifiers only partially map 1-to-1 onto BiGG reaction IDs), standalone Stage 2B positive-cost pFBA repair achieves $100\%$ growth feasibility ($6/6$ growing), BiGG reaction precision $= 0.1795$--$0.2098$ (reaction $F_1 = 0.2187$ on DeepProZyme, $0.2208$ on CLEAN, and $0.2180$ on EnzBERT), and EC precision $= 0.4547$ on DeepProZyme, $0.4750$ on CLEAN, and $0.4750$ on EnzBERT (EC $F_1 = 0.5700$, $0.5737$, and $0.5669$), improving reaction precision and EC precision over METEOR v8 (BiGG reaction precision $= 0.1655$, reaction $F_1 = 0.2091$, EC precision $= 0.4383$, recall $= 0.8842$, EC $F_1 = 0.5851$) and growth feasibility over Reconstructor \citep{jenior2023reconstructor} (EC precision $= 0.4387$, recall $= 0.8787$, EC $F_1 = 0.5844$, $4/6$ growing).

To evaluate external biological accuracy on experimental strain characterization data, we compared GSPA against automated metabolic reconstruction pipelines (CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, and negative-cost MILP gap-filling METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor}) and supervised genome-to-phenotype classifiers—Traitar L1-SVM \citep{weimann2016traitar}, the Random Forest phenotype baseline of Koblitz et al.\ \citep{koblitz2025diaspora} (\texttt{BacDive RF Baseline}), and DiASPora Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}—across experimental carbon-source utilization and diagnostic enzyme assays in the DSMZ BacDive database \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac} (Suites 7 and 8; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}d and Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}; per-fold CV metrics and runtimes in \texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_per\_fold\_cv.tsv}; architectural details in Section~\ref{sec:methods_bacdive}).

First, across $50{,}075$ strain--enzyme assay pairs spanning 10 diagnostic ECs in $6{,}313$ strains (\texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_track1\_summary.tsv} and \texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_enzyme\_per\_ec.tsv}), negative-cost minimal-glucose MILP gap-filling across three parameterizations (\texttt{METEOR v6} with default addition threshold $\tau_{\text{add}}=0.20, n=50{,}075$; \texttt{METEOR a025} with stricter $\tau_{\text{add}}=0.25, n=50{,}075$; and \texttt{METEOR v7} with relaxed core threshold including 30 additional borderline strain--EC pairs, $n=50{,}105$) trades precision for recall: precision drops from $0.6442$ (DeepProZyme baseline, recall $= 0.8105$, $F_1 = 0.7178$, balanced accuracy $= 0.6792$) to $0.6194$--$0.6285$ (recall $= 0.8285$--$0.8640$, $F_1 = 0.7148$--$0.7215$, balanced accuracy dropping to $0.6631$--$0.6670$). Similarly, on the 108-genome non-glucose catabolic panel ($n=1{,}622$ assays, evaluated with \texttt{METEOR v8} using updated MetaNetX reaction mappings), minimal-glucose MILP gap-filling reduces precision from $0.6254$ to $0.6051$, balanced accuracy from $0.5386$ to $0.5101$, and ROC-AUC from $0.5716$ to $0.5484$ ($F_1 = 0.6711$ vs.\ $0.6722$). On obligate heteromeric urease (\texttt{EC 3.5.1.5}, requiring UreA, UreB, and UreC/D/F/G subunits across $n=5{,}026$ assayed strains in the 6,313-strain panel and $n=871$ in the 3,017 type-strain panel), negative-cost MILP reduces precision from $0.4520$ to $0.4320$ and $F_1$ from $0.5748$ to $0.5610$ ($\text{BAcc} = \text{binary ROC-AUC} = 0.7294 \to 0.7184$) by boosting isolated singleton subunits, whereas complex-aware pathway reconstruction (\texttt{gapseq} / \texttt{GSPA Track 1} on the type-strain panel) achieves $F_1 = 0.7626$.

Second, in Track 1 ($2{,}332$ experimental carbon-source growth assays across $529$ strains and $17{,}496$ diagnostic enzyme assays across $3{,}017$ type strains), we separate 100\% blind unsupervised prediction (zero phenotype training labels) from supervised 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}). When precomputed reaction/pathway call matrices from \texttt{gapseq}, \texttt{ModelSEED}, and \texttt{CarveMe} are available, \texttt{GSPA Track 1} integrates them with Stage 1+2 enzyme and complex coherence posteriors in $6.1\text{ s/genome}$ ($651.1\text{ s/genome}$ end-to-end when running all three base reconstructions from scratch). Under 100\% blind evaluation, \texttt{GSPA Track 1} preserves \texttt{gapseq}'s binary decision boundary at $\theta = 0.50$—matching \texttt{gapseq} on carbon-source binary calls ($\text{Precision} = 0.8071$, $\text{Recall} = 0.7836$, $F_1 = 0.7952$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8082$, vs.\ $F_1 = 0.6474$ for ModelSEED and $0.5132$ for CarveMe) and on the 17,496-assay blind enzyme panel ($\text{Precision} = 0.8393$, $\text{Recall} = 0.7204$, $F_1 = 0.7753$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.7461$, vs.\ $0.5943$ for ModelSEED and $0.4319$ for CarveMe)—while providing calibrated continuous confidence scores that lift threshold-swept $\text{ROC-AUC}$ from $0.8082$ (binary) to $0.8567$ ($+0.0485$) on carbon growth and from $0.7461$ to $0.7520$ on blind enzymes. Under strict 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (\texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_per\_fold\_cv.tsv} and \texttt{pillar3\_bacdive\_track1\_summary.tsv}), combining GSPA's continuous posteriors with the supervised classifiers (\texttt{GSPA + DiASPora Hybrid}) improves pooled out-of-genus carbon-source $F_1$ from $0.8448$ (BacDive RF; 5-fold mean $0.8329 \pm 0.0659$), $0.8483$ (DiASPora alone; 5-fold mean $0.8408 \pm 0.0390$), and $0.8571$ (Traitar; 5-fold mean $0.8467 \pm 0.0438$) to $0.8710$ (5-fold mean $0.8616 \pm 0.0495$, $\text{Precision} = 0.8766$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8783$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9567$) and improves diagnostic enzyme $F_1$ from $0.8458$ (BacDive RF; 5-fold mean $0.8456 \pm 0.0076$), $0.8550$ (DiASPora; 5-fold mean $0.8550 \pm 0.0075$), and $0.8593$ (Traitar; 5-fold mean $0.8595 \pm 0.0076$) to $0.8713$ (5-fold mean $0.8712 \pm 0.0083$, $\text{Precision} = 0.8652$, $\text{Recall} = 0.8776$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8257$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9097$).

Third, in Track 2 (where $30\%$ of each strain's assays are revealed to calibrate strain metabolic activity and co-occurring pathway posteriors, and evaluation is performed strictly on the held-out $70\%$ assays), \texttt{GSPA Track 2 Bayesian Anchor} improves unsupervised held-out carbon-source $F_1$ ($n=1{,}641$) from $0.7922$ (gapseq and unassisted GSPA Track 1 on the same 70\% split) to $0.8871$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8362$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9447$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8889$, continuous $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9534$) and held-out enzyme $F_1$ ($n=12{,}316$) from $0.7737$ (gapseq) to $0.8545$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8067$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9084$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.7749$, continuous $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.8738$). Combining Track 2 posteriors with DiASPora (\texttt{GSPA Track 2 + DiASPora Hybrid}) reaches $F_1 = 0.9225$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.9048$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9409$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.9258$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9788$) on held-out carbon sources and $F_1 = 0.8727$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8638$, $\text{Recall} = 0.8819$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8264$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9080$) on held-out diagnostic enzymes, outperforming DiASPora ($0.8854$ / $0.8507$), BacDive RF ($0.8625$ / $0.8492$), and Traitar ($0.8386$ / $0.8569$; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).

We next evaluated completing obligate heteromeric protein complexes when individual subunits have twilight-zone homology while suppressing false-positive singleton subunit predictions (Suite 5; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}; \texttt{results/pillar3\_complex\_lso.tsv}). Across curated heteromeric complexes from Complex Portal and GO (including proton-transporting ATP synthase \texttt{GO:0045259}, respiratory chain complex I \texttt{GO:0045271}, cytochrome $bo_3$/$c$ oxidase, DNA-directed RNA polymerase, 30S/50S ribosomal subunits, and the SecYEG translocon), we performed 175 leave-subunit-out (LSO) trials across six prokaryotic proteomes (\textit{E.~coli} K-12 [$n=30$], \textit{B.~subtilis} 168 [$n=30$], \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv [$n=30$], \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1 [$n=30$], \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7 [$n=30$], and \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803 [$n=25$]). In each trial, a true complex subunit's primary evidence was attenuated into the twilight zone ($q_e \in [0.22, 0.38]$) alongside five matched decoy proteins carrying comparable raw domain scores in unrelated genomic loci (6 candidates per trial; random-chance Hit@1 $= 1/6 = 0.1667$; Precision@0.5, Recall@0.5, and $F_1\text{@0.5}$ are evaluated per trial and macro-averaged across all trials).

Under baseline Noisy-OR pooling (\texttt{M1}), all 175 attenuated subunits remain below the $\theta = 0.50$ threshold ($\text{Recall@0.5} = 0.0000$, $F_{1}\text{@0.5} = 0.0000$, genome Complex Coherence $= 0.3901$), and Hit@1 among the 6 candidates is $0.5600$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.483, 0.635]$). Enabling the Stage 1 complex coherence prior alone (\texttt{M2}) detects that the partner subunits of the complex are present in the genome and boosts all domain-matching candidates equally, leaving Hit@1 at $0.5600$ while lifting Recall@0.5 to $0.5029$, Precision@0.5 to $0.1729$, and per-trial $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ to $0.2459$. Coupling complex coherence with the Stage 1 operon neighborhood prior (\texttt{M3}) disambiguates the true co-operonic subunit from non-operonic decoys: across all 175 trials, Hit@1 rises from $0.5600$ to $0.9371$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.890, 0.968]$), Recall@0.5 reaches $0.9257$, Precision@0.5 rises more than three-fold to $0.5951$, and $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ reaches $0.6680$ ($0.9310$ Hit@1, $0.9103$ Recall@0.5, $0.5886$ Precision@0.5, and $0.6595$ $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ across the 145 non-\textit{E.~coli} K-12 trials; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).

Comparing per-genome recovery across the six species highlights the complementary roles of operon context and Stage 3 agentic reasoning (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}b). In \textit{E.~coli} K-12, \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}, \textit{P.~aeruginosa}, and \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7, most heteromeric complex subunits reside in contiguous operons, allowing \texttt{M3} to achieve $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ between $0.6862$ (\textit{P.~aeruginosa}) and $0.7279$ (\textit{E.~coli} O157:H7; $0.7089$ in \textit{E.~coli} K-12, $0.7090$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}, and $0.7244$ in \textit{M.~tuberculosis}). In the cyanobacterium \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, subunits of photosystem I/II, phycobilisomes, and NADH dehydrogenase are frequently split across dispersed chromosomal loci: baseline \texttt{M1} achieves $0.5200$ Hit@1 (Complex Coherence $= 0.3587$), \texttt{M2} reaches $0.4800$ Recall@0.5 and $0.2507$ $F_1\text{@0.5}$ (Complex Coherence $= 0.6125$), and operon context (\texttt{M3}) reaches $0.6400$ Hit@1 and $0.4080$ $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$. We next evaluated anatomical and developmental constraint enforcement across six eukaryotic species spanning unicellular fungi (\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}), non-chordate invertebrates (\textit{Caenorhabditis elegans} and \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}), non-vascular bryophyte mosses (\textit{Physcomitrium patens}), vascular flowering plants (\textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}), and vertebrates (\textit{Mus musculus}), evaluating both (i) the unperturbed endogenous homology-transfer proteome ($6{,}155$ curated proteins across the 6 species; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak eukaryote\_\allowbreak endogenous\_\allowbreak vs\_\allowbreak injected.tsv}) and (ii) a cross-clade perturbation stress-test adding $1{,}546$ cross-clade developmental claims across $7{,}655$ proteins ($300$ in 5 species and $46$ in \textit{P.~patens}), scored strictly against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground-truth annotations (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c,d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak eukaryotic\_\allowbreak anatomy.tsv} and \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak relax\_\allowbreak vs\_\allowbreak remove.tsv}). On the unperturbed endogenous proteomes alone, unconstrained Noisy-OR (\texttt{E1}) already produces $1{,}009$ formal cross-clade anatomical and taxonomic violations transferred from mammalian or angiosperm homologs ($3$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, $289$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, and $717$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}; $0$ in \textit{P.~patens}, \textit{A.~thaliana}, and \textit{M.~musculus}; mean $168.2$ violations/species); GSPA eliminates $100\%$ ($1{,}009/1{,}009$) of these endogenous violations and increases endogenous 6-species mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.7096$ (\texttt{E1}) to $0.7117$ at Iter 1 ($+0.0021$ mean gain; $0.7109$ at Iter 3 with Stage 2A relaxation: $+0.0086$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae} [$0.6644 \to 0.6730$], $+0.0032$ in \textit{C.~elegans} [$0.5797 \to 0.5829$], $+0.0010$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster} [$0.5863 \to 0.5873$], $+0.0010$ in \textit{P.~patens} [$0.8841 \to 0.8851$], and $+0.0011$ in \textit{A.~thaliana} [$0.6594 \to 0.6605$]). Across the combined stress-test panel ($1{,}452$ total baseline violations at $\theta = 0.50$: $66$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, $389$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, $894$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}, $3$ in \textit{P.~patens}, $59$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}, and $41$ in \textit{M.~musculus}), applying direct GO taxon constraints alone (\texttt{E2}, using the $26{,}911$ rules in \texttt{go-taxon-constraints.tsv}) eliminates $810/1{,}452$ ($55.79\%$) of violations while leaving $642/1{,}452$ ($44.21\%$) unresolved ($190$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, $426$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}, and $26$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}), because those errors involve vertebrate, arthropod, or angiosperm organ terms whose taxonomic restrictions are defined in Uberon, PO, or CL rather than directly in GO. Applying the Stage 1 soft anatomical prior alone (\texttt{E3}, $-\alpha_4 = -4.0$ log-odds shift) eliminates $100\%$ of violations in three species and $98.3\%$ ($58/59$) in \textit{A.~thaliana}, but leaves $201$ high-confidence violations in \textit{C.~elegans} ($48.3\%$ eliminated) and $559$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster} ($37.5\%$ eliminated; $80.97\%$ unweighted species mean elimination across the 6 species, or $47.59\%$ [$691/1{,}452$] pooled), where raw homology scores exceed $q_e \ge 0.99$, demonstrating why Stage 2A deterministic enforcement (\texttt{E4} and \texttt{E5}, $0$ surviving violations) is necessary after soft Bayesian updates.

Comparing unconstrained Noisy-OR (\texttt{E1}), Stage 2A naive removal (\texttt{E4}), and Stage 2A semantic ancestor relaxation (\texttt{E5}) on transfer-evaluated micro $F_{\max}$ against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground truth clarifies the exact regime in which ancestor relaxation outperforms naive deletion (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}; \texttt{results/pillar3\_relax\_vs\_remove.tsv}). Naive deletion (\texttt{E4}) strips ancestral GO branches whenever an overly specific cross-clade developmental term is transferred, causing recall to drop by $0.56$ to $7.70$ percentage points across the six species. Under semantic ancestor relaxation (\texttt{E5}), forbidden terms are relaxed to their nearest anatomically valid GO ancestor, retaining $96.0\%$--$98.8\%$ of unconstrained \texttt{E1} mean annotation Information Content ($12.07$ vs.\ $12.48$ bits in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, $12.09$ vs.\ $12.52$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, $12.35$ vs.\ $12.86$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}, $9.79$ vs.\ $10.10$ in \textit{P.~patens}, $11.18$ vs.\ $11.76$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}, and $12.85$ vs.\ $13.01$ in \textit{M.~musculus}; Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants (\textit{C.~elegans}, \textit{D.~melanogaster}, \textit{A.~thaliana}, and \textit{M.~musculus}), where homologous developmental regulators genuinely participate in ancestral tissue and organ morphogenesis, \texttt{E5} outperforms \texttt{E4} in both micro $F_{\max}$ ($\Delta F_{\max}(\text{E5}-\text{E4}) = +0.0006$ in \textit{M.~musculus}, $+0.0050$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}, $+0.0061$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, and $+0.0157$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}; 6-species mean $F_{\max}$ rising from $0.9038$ [\texttt{E1}] and $0.9568$ [\texttt{E4}] to $0.9605$ [\texttt{E5}]) and Recall ($+0.67$ to $+3.05$ percentage points). Conversely, in unicellular yeast (\textit{S.~cerevisiae}) and sparsely annotated non-vascular moss (\textit{P.~patens}), where injected organ-development claims land on metabolic or housekeeping proteins whose unmodified Swiss-Prot annotations lack multicellular developmental ancestors, \texttt{E5} still recovers higher Recall ($+1.00$ and $+0.99$ percentage points) and higher Mean IC ($12.07$ vs.\ $12.02$ and $9.79$ vs.\ $9.64$ bits) than \texttt{E4}, whereas complete removal (\texttt{E4}) achieves slightly higher precision and $F_{\max}$ ($0.9604$ vs.\ $0.9592$ in yeast; $0.9828$ vs.\ $0.9792$ in moss). To evaluate anomaly resolution under strictly controlled, de-identified conditions without gene-symbol or formatting leakage, we constructed a 120-anomaly benchmark ($30$ pathway gaps, $30$ incomplete complexes, $30$ borderline essentiality gaps, and $30$ borderline conflicts across 7 genomes: \textit{E.~coli} K-12 [$n=29$], \textit{P.~aeruginosa} [$n=18$], \textit{B.~subtilis} [$n=17$], \textit{M.~tuberculosis} [$n=16$], \textit{S.~cerevisiae} [$n=16$], \textit{C.~elegans} [$n=13$], and \textit{A.~thaliana} [$n=11$]; \texttt{results/pillar4\_agent\_benchmark\_120.jsonl}). \section{Discussion}

Protein function prediction has traditionally been formulated as a per-protein classification problem evaluated on isolated sequences or structures \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,kulmanov2024deepgose}. Our evaluation across eight benchmark suites demonstrates that while per-protein multi-channel calibration (Stage 0) establishes bulk protein-centric accuracy on well-annotated reference proteomes, treating the whole genome as the unit of inference (Stages 1--3) resolves structural failure modes that per-protein predictors and negative-cost MILP gap-fillers cannot address.

Four main conclusions emerge from our study. First, our audit of 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions clarifies why negative-cost MILP gap-filling (including our prior METEOR formulation \citep{niu2026meteor}) degrades gene-level enzyme precision on diagnostic and catabolic panels: assigning negative addition costs ($c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) to candidate reactions above an addition threshold causes $68.62\%$ ($215.02 / 313.35$) of added reactions to carry zero steady-state flux, while uniform reaction-to-gene boosting inflates false-positive calls across paralogs. While any positive-cost LP formulation over the sanitized universal network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ eliminates uncertified zero-flux additions ($81.31/81.31$ flux-certified additions under threshold LP repair and $5.70/5.70$ under two-step lexicographic pFBA with FVA/KO/perturbation certification in $18.01\text{ s/genome}$), combining role-aware DNF mechanism aggregation (Top-$M_{\text{iso}}$ isozyme OR and bottleneck-gated geometric-mean subunit AND), two-step lexicographic pFBA repair, cognate substrate flux probing, and competitive locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ eliminates the $2.23$--$2.36$ dead-end active reactions per genome left by unregularized LP/MILP repair, improves \texttt{Price-149} enzyme Top-1 hits under both single-predictor refinement (CLEAN $60 \to 63$, MAPred $50 \to 56$, DeepProZyme $49 \to 51$ with cognate SLR, GraphEC $18 \to 20$) and multi-predictor refinement ($+0.0528$ to $+0.1987$ $F_{\max}$), and improves \texttt{BacDive} phenotype predictions across blind, Track 2 phenotype-anchored, and genus-blocked evaluation tracks.

Second, our ablation of the leave-reaction-out (LRO) benchmark separates the contribution of protein language model embeddings from that of local genomic synteny. On the well-studied \textit{E.~coli} K-12 proteome (Track B), per-protein ESM-2 reaction-centroid similarity alone recovers $62.11\%$ of masked reactions at Rank 1 ($87.85\%$ given the candidate pool), with Stage 1 pathway coherence and RLGC operon neighborhood density adding $+1.56$ percentage points unconditionally ($+2.21$ percentage points given pool, to $63.67\%$ / $90.06\%$). Conversely, when close PLM donor centroids are masked (Track A, cosine $> 0.50$) or when multiple candidate genes in a proteome share similar domain and embedding scores ($n=50$ unique-protein ambiguous LRO gaps and $n=175$ heteromeric complex LSO trials), sequence similarity alone recovers only $1.95\%$ (LRO), $34.00\%$ (ambiguous LRO), and $56.00\%$ (LSO), whereas RLGC operon neighborhood coupling, Stage 1 coherence, and Stage 3 neuro-symbolic disambiguation elevate Hit@1 to $23.44\%$ (Track A LRO), $96.67\%$ ($3$-seed mean on ambiguous LRO), and $94.29\%$ (LSO across 6 candidates per trial).

Third, our multi-kingdom eukaryotic evaluation shows that enforcing ontology consistency in multicellular organisms requires bridging the Gene Ontology with external anatomy ontologies (Uberon, PO, and CL). On unperturbed 7-channel predictions across six model eukaryotes, GSPA eliminates all $1{,}009$ endogenous homology-transferred violations while improving mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ by $+0.0021$ ($+0.0086$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}). Under cross-clade perturbation stress-testing evaluated against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground truth ($1{,}452$ total baseline violations across fungi, nematodes, insects, mosses, flowering plants, and mammals), direct GO taxon constraints leave $44.21\%$ ($642/1{,}452$) of errors unresolved and soft Stage 1 log-odds penalties still leave $201$--$559$ high-score violations in invertebrate proteomes, whereas Stage 2A semantic relaxation over our 2,715 GO--Uberon/PO/CL cross-product axioms eliminates $100\%$ of violations, retains $96.0\%$--$98.8\%$ of unconstrained mean IC, and outperforms naive deletion across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants ($\Delta F_{\max}(\text{E5}-\text{E4}) = +0.0006$ to $+0.0157$; Recall $+0.67$ to $+3.05$ percentage points), while recovering higher Recall and Mean IC than deletion in unicellular yeast and non-vascular moss.

Fourth, comparing the zero-shot Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agent (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}) against both pre-tool 3-scalar and oracle-tool 8-feature supervised 5-fold CV classifiers on the de-identified 120-anomaly and 50-LRO benchmarks clarifies when an LLM agent is warranted over a lightweight tabular classifier. When in-distribution labeled anomaly data and pre-extracted tool features are available and only binary promotion or candidate ranking is needed, an 8-feature supervised classifier paired with \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} achieves $F_1 = 0.9423$--$0.9720$ on the 120 anomalies and $94.0\%$--$100.0\%$ Hit@1 on LRO-50 in $<0.01\text{ s}$. However, when labeled anomaly training data are unavailable for a new clade or when multi-class semantic ancestor relaxation is required on cross-clade conflicts, zero-shot multi-turn tool use with \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} achieves $F_1 = 0.9275 \pm 0.0084$ and accuracy $= 0.9389 \pm 0.0104$ on the 120 anomalies and $96.67\% \pm 1.89\%$ Hit@1 on LRO-50 across 3 seeds, outperforming pre-tool 3-scalar CV classifiers on anomaly resolution ($F_1 = 0.7500$--$0.8762$), proposing $9.7/15$ valid ancestral GO relaxations, and guaranteeing a $0.0\%$ accepted ontology violation rate.

\subsection{Overview of the four-stage architecture and notation}

\subsection{Stage 0: Multi-channel per-protein evidence calibration and Noisy-OR pooling}

GSPA can refine either a single standalone predictor (such as CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, or TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}) or integrate claims from up to seven per-protein evidence channels $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$: (1) sequence alignment via DIAMOND \citep{buchfink2021sensitive} against Swiss-Prot GOA entries; (2) profile HMM domain architecture via HMMER 3 \citep{eddy2011accelerated} against Pfam-A/Pfam2GO; (3) domain signatures via InterProScan v5 \citep{jones2014interproscan}; (4) ESM-2 (650M) embedding $k$NN transfer \citep{lin2023evolutionary}; (5) ProtT5-XL-U50 embedding $k$NN transfer \citep{elnaggar2021prottrans}; (6) sequence-based neural classifiers (ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, DeepGOWeb \citep{kulmanov2021deepgoweb}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}); and (7) structural similarity transfer via FoldSeek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and ProstT5 3Di \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}.

For each channel $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$, raw scores are mapped to calibrated probabilities $\tilde{q}_e(p, t) \in (0, 1)$ in \texttt{CalibrationTable.groovy} via piecewise-linear interpolation over pre-fitted isotonic calibration knots derived from a temporally pre-cutoff Swiss-Prot validation split (20\% of pre-$t_0$ Swiss-Prot proteins, strictly disjoint from all Suite 1--8 test proteins; falling back to a parameterized logistic curve $\sigma(k(s - s_0))$ when custom knot tables are omitted) and multiplied by the GO evidence-code reliability weight $w_{\text{ev}}(c)$: experimental codes (\texttt{EXP, IDA, IPI, IMP, IGI, IEP, HTP, HDA, HMP, HGI, HEP}) receive $w_{\text{ev}} = 1.00$, curated computational/author statements (\texttt{ISS, ISO, ISA, ISM, IGC, RCA, TAS, NAS, IC}) receive $w_{\text{ev}} = 0.80$ ($0.65$ for \texttt{RCA}/\texttt{NAS} in \texttt{EvidenceType.groovy}), phylogenetic codes (\texttt{IBA, IBD, IKR, IRD}) receive $w_{\text{ev}} = 0.70$--$0.75$, and electronic annotations (\texttt{IEA}) receive $w_{\text{ev}} = 0.50$--$0.55$, yielding $q_e(p, t) = w_{\text{ev}}(c) \tilde{q}_e(p, t)$. To prevent correlated channels from over-counting redundant evidence, \texttt{EvidenceCombiner.groovy} partitions claims into correlation groups $\mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}$ (defaulting to sequence-homology vs.\ domain-signature vs.\ structure/PLM groups, or donor-accession groups in the Python multi-channel runner where $n_{\text{don}}(p, t)$ channels sharing the same Swiss-Prot donor accession $d_e(p, t)$ receive discount exponent $\rho_e(p, t) = 1 / \sqrt{n_{\text{don}}(p, t)}$; in \texttt{EvidenceCombiner.groovy}, redundant claims within the same correlation group $g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}$ are collapsed to their group maximum $q_g(p, t) = \max_{e \in g} q_e(p, t)$ prior to across-group Noisy-OR pooling). Pooling across channels and upward along the GO DAG $\mathcal{T}$ gives:
\begin{equation}
  P_0(p, f) = 1 - \prod_{t \sqsubseteq f} \prod_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}(p, t)} \bigl(1 - q_e(p, t)\bigr)^{\rho_e(p, t)}.
  \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
Clamping $P_0(p, f)$ to $[\varepsilon, 1 - \varepsilon]$ ($\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ in Python; $10^{-6}$ in \texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy}) yields the anchored Stage 0 log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f) = \log \frac{P_0(p, f)}{1 - P_0(p, f)}$.

\subsection{Stage 1: Proteome-wide iterative Bayesian log-odds refinement}

In Stage 1 (\texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy}), GSPA updates the log-odds matrix $\mathbf{L}^{(k)}$ across iterations $k = 1, \dots, K_{\max}$ by adding six genome-scale prior shifts to the anchored Stage 0 log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f)$ as a function of the previous iterate's probabilities $P^{(k-1)}(p, f) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(k-1)}(p, f)\bigr)$, where $\sigma_{\text{log}}(x) = (1 + e^{-x})^{-1}$, with damping factor $\gamma_{\text{iter}} = 0.50$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f) &= L^{(0)}(p, f) \\
    &\quad + \sum_{m=1}^{6} \alpha_m \, B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr), \\
    P^{(k)}(p, f) &= (1 - \gamma_{\text{iter}}) P^{(k-1)}(p, f) + \gamma_{\text{iter}} \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(\tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f)\bigr).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
After each iteration, updated probabilities are propagated upward on $\mathcal{T}$ via $P^{(k)}(p, a) \leftarrow \max_{t \sqsubseteq a} P^{(k)}(p, t)$ (and whenever a parent term $a$ is suppressed below $0.50$ by a taxonomic or anatomical penalty, its descendants $t \sqsubseteq a$ are capped at $\min(P^{(k)}(p, t), P^{(k)}(p, a))$). In \texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy}, the inner loop runs for up to $K_{\max} = 6$ iterations with convergence tolerance $5 \times 10^{-3}$ on the mean probability change across active pairs and automatic divergence rollback if the mean change increases across consecutive iterations (in the standalone Python LRO/EC runners, up to $K_{\max} = 20$ undamped iterations with $\max_{p,f} |P^{(k)}(p, f) - P^{(k-1)}(p, f)| < 10^{-3}$). The six functional forms $B_1, \dots, B_6$ are implemented as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Essentiality prior ($m=1$, $\alpha_1 = 1.5$, \texttt{EssentialityPrior.groovy}):} Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the domain-specific essential function profile and $P_{\max}^{(k-1)}(f) = \max_{p \in \mathcal{P}} P^{(k-1)}(p, f)$. For $f \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ with $P_{\max}^{(k-1)}(f) < \theta_{\text{ess}} = 0.50$, let $\text{rank}_f(p) \in \{1, 2, \dots\}$ be the rank of protein $p$ among candidates with $P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \ge 0.05$ ordered by descending $P^{(k-1)}(p, f)$ (ties broken deterministically by descending Stage 0 score $P_0(p, f)$ and locus identifier); then for the top $M_{\text{ess}} = 3$ candidates,
  \begin{align*}
    B_1(p, f) &= \mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \wedge P_{\max}^{(k-1)}(f) < \theta_{\text{ess}}] \\
    &\; \times \mathbb{I}[P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \ge 0.05 \wedge \text{rank}_f(p) \le M_{\text{ess}}] \\
    &\; \times 0.5^{\text{rank}_f(p) - 1} \bigl(1 - P_{\max}^{(k-1)}(f)\bigr),
  \end{align*}
  where the geometric rank decay $0.5^{\text{rank}_f(p)-1}$ in \texttt{EssentialityPrior.groovy} (or $1/\text{rank}_f(p)$ in the analytical Python ablation runner) concentrates the rescue boost on the top candidate.
  \item \textbf{Pathway and complex coherence prior ($m=2$, $\alpha_2 = 1.5$, $\beta_{\text{single}} = 1.2$, \texttt{CoherencePrior.groovy}):} For a pathway or complex $C \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ containing $f \in C$, let $c_{-f} = \text{cov}^{(k-1)}(C \setminus \{f\}) = \frac{1}{|C|-1}\sum_{f' \in C \setminus \{f\}} \mathbb{I}\bigl[\max_{p'} P^{(k-1)}(p', f') \ge 0.50\bigr]$. For $|C| \ge 3$, \texttt{CoherencePrior.groovy} boosts candidates when $c_{-f} \ge \theta_{\text{coh}} = 0.70$ with normalized ramp $(c_{-f} - \theta_{\text{coh}})/(1 - \theta_{\text{coh}})$ (plus $+0.25\,c_{-f}$ for 2-step modules when $c_{-f} \ge 0.50$) and penalizes isolated subunits in heteromeric complexes ($f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$, $|C| \ge 3$) when $c_{-f} < \theta_{\text{het}} = 0.35$:
  \begin{align*}
    B_2(p, f) &= \mathbb{I}\bigl[P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \in [0.05, 0.50)\bigr] \\
    &\quad \times \max_{C \ni f} \Bigl(\mathbb{I}[c_{-f} \ge \theta_{\text{coh}}] \frac{c_{-f} - \theta_{\text{coh}}}{1 - \theta_{\text{coh}}}\Bigr) \\
    &\quad - \frac{\beta_{\text{single}}}{\alpha_2} \mathbb{I}\bigl[P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \ge 0.05 \wedge f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}\bigr] \\
    &\qquad \times \mathbb{I}[c_{-f} < \theta_{\text{het}}] \Bigl(1 - \frac{c_{-f}}{\theta_{\text{het}}}\Bigr).
  \end{align*}
  \item \textbf{Taxonomic consistency prior ($m=3$, $\alpha_3 = 3.0$, \texttt{ConsistencyPrior.groovy}):} Let $\mathcal{V}_{\text{SAT}}^{(k-1)}(p) \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ be the set of terms participating in minimal unsatisfiable subsets (MUS) extracted by SAT4J over the propositional encoding of $\mathcal{T}$, GO disjointness axioms, and direct GO taxon constraints for $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ on $\{f : P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \ge 0.20\}$; then $B_3(p, f) = -w_{\text{MUS}}(f)\,\mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{SAT}}^{(k-1)}(p)\bigr]$ with $w_{\text{MUS}}(f) = 1.5$ for direct taxonomic violations and $1.0$ for general disjointness conflicts.
  \item \textbf{Anatomical prior ($m=4$, $\alpha_4 = 4.0$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}} = 1.10$, \texttt{AnatomicalPrior.groovy}):} Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ be the set of GO terms anatomically forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}), and let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{act}}^{(k-1)}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the set of developmental GO terms linked to an anatomical structure $u$ that is taxonomically valid in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ and supported by $n_{\text{sup}}(u) \ge N_{\text{struct}} = 3$ proteins with $P^{(k-1)}(p', f') \ge 0.60$; then with support ratio $\rho_{\text{sup}}(u) = \min(1.0, n_{\text{sup}}(u)/N_{\text{struct}})$,
  \begin{align*}
    B_4(p, f) &= -\mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \wedge P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \ge 0.20\bigr] \\
    &\quad + \frac{\alpha_{\text{boost}}}{\alpha_4}\rho_{\text{sup}}(u)\mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{act}}^{(k-1)}(\tau_{\text{tax}})\bigr] \\
    &\qquad \times \mathbb{I}\bigl[P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \in [0.22, 0.49]\bigr].
  \end{align*}
  \item \textbf{Metabolic gap-filling and RLGC prior ($m=5$, $\alpha_5 = 1.2$, \texttt{GapFillingPrior.groovy} and \texttt{ReactionLocalContextSuggester.groovy}):} Given a missing pathway reaction $r$ mapped to catalytic term $f_r$, the Reaction-Local Genomic Context (RLGC) score for candidate protein $p$ with $P^{(k-1)}(p, f_r) \ge 0.01$ combines four bounded signals:
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, r) &= q_{\text{cat}}(p, r) \\
      &\quad + \lambda_{\text{BF}} \tanh\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{10} \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, r)\bigr) \\
      &\quad + \lambda_{\text{dom}} S_{\text{Pfam}}(p, r) \\
      &\quad + \lambda_{\text{PLM}} \max\bigl(0, S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, r)\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rlgc}
  \end{equation}
  where $q_{\text{cat}}(p, r) \in (0, 1)$ is the calibrated catalytic homology/HMM probability; $\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, r)$ is the empirical Bayes factor (termed the \textit{RLGC kernel density scorer} when evaluated standalone in Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}) measuring Gaussian positional enrichment of genes annotated to pathway $W(r)$ within local neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_w(p)$ ($\pm w$ genes on the same contig, $w=5$, kernel $K_{\text{pos}}(p, p') = \exp(-\Delta g(p, p')^2 / 8)$) relative to the genome-wide background prevalence $|W(r)|/N$ with smoothing $\varepsilon_{\text{bf}} = 0.05$ ($\lambda_{\text{BF}} = 0.35$; in \texttt{ReactionLocalContextSuggester.groovy}, the log-odds suggestion score weights the four standardized signals by $w_{\text{seq}}=0.30, w_{\text{BF}}=0.35, w_{\text{dom}}=0.20, w_{\text{PLM}}=0.15$):
  \begin{align*}
    \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, r) &= \\
    &\!\!\!\!\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{bf}} + \sum\limits_{p' \in \mathcal{N}_w^*(p)} K_{\text{pos}}(p, p')\,\mathbb{I}[p' \in W(r)]}{\varepsilon_{\text{bf}} + (|W(r)|/N)\sum\limits_{p' \in \mathcal{N}_w^*(p)} K_{\text{pos}}(p, p')},
  \end{align*}
  with $\mathcal{N}_w^*(p) = \mathcal{N}_w(p)\setminus\{p\}$; $S_{\text{Pfam}}(p, r) \in [0, 1]$ scores EC top-level class compatibility from Pfam domains ($\lambda_{\text{dom}} = 0.20$); and $S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, r) \in [-1, 1]$ is the cosine similarity between the ESM-2 embedding of protein $p$ and the pre-$t_0$ Swiss-Prot reference centroid of reaction $r$ ($\lambda_{\text{PLM}} = 0.45$). Ranking candidates for reaction $r$ by descending $S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, r)$ with rank $\text{rank}_r(p) \in \{1, 2, \dots\}$ over the top $K_{\text{gap}} = 3$ candidates, the prior shift is $B_5(p, f_r) = \mathbb{I}[\text{rank}_r(p) \le K_{\text{gap}}] \, \min\bigl(1.5, S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, r)\bigr) / \text{rank}_r(p)$ (with $+1.2\,(S_{\text{RLGC}} - 0.5)$ bonus in \texttt{GapFillingPrior.groovy} when $S_{\text{RLGC}} > 0.50$).
  \item \textbf{Operon neighborhood prior ($m=6$, $\alpha_6 = 1.0$, \texttt{GenomicContextPrior.groovy}):} Let $\mathcal{N}_{\text{op}}(p)$ denote adjacent same-strand genes within intergenic distance $d_{p,p'} \le d_{\max} = 150\text{ bp}$ \citep{price2005novel}, weighted by $w_{\text{ig}}(d) = \exp(-d / 75)$ when base-pair coordinates are supplied (or $1 / (1 + \Delta\text{idx})$ when only gene-index order is available; in \texttt{GenomicContextPrior.groovy}, normalized across same-operon pathway partners). When $P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \in [0.05, 0.50)$ for a term $f \in C$,
  \begin{align*}
    B_6(p, f) &= \mathbb{I}\bigl[P^{(k-1)}(p, f) \in [0.05, 0.50)\bigr] \\
    &\; \times \min\Bigl(1.0, \sum\limits_{p' \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{op}}(p)} \max\limits_{f' \in C \setminus \{f\}} w_{\text{ig}}(d_{p,p'}) \\
    &\quad \times P^{(k-1)}(p', f') \, \mathbb{I}\bigl[P^{(k-1)}(p', f') \ge 0.50\bigr]\Bigr).
  \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent \textbf{Hyperparameter protocol and sensitivity analysis.} All 28 pipeline constants across Stage 1 ($\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_6$, $\beta_{\text{single}}$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}}$, $\theta_{\text{ess}}$, $\theta_{\text{coh}}$, $\theta_{\text{het}}$, $M_{\text{ess}}$, $K_{\text{gap}}$, $\lambda_{\text{BF}}$, $\lambda_{\text{dom}}$, $\lambda_{\text{PLM}}$, $d_{\max}$) and Stages 2A--2B ($\gamma_{\text{relax}}$, $\theta_{\text{sub}}$, $\theta_{\text{core}}$, $\alpha_{\text{bio}}$, $\eta_{\text{null}}$, $\gamma_{\text{FBA}}$, $\gamma_{\text{damp}}$, $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_4$, including the individual $\beta_{1,2}$ and $\gamma_{\text{damp}}$ rows) were fitted exclusively on the \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 development proteome via two-stage block coordinate search (first optimizing Stage 1 weights/thresholds over a 5-point coordinate grid maximizing 5-fold cross-validated LRO Hit@1 + GAEF consistency, then optimizing Stage 2A/2B stoichiometric parameters with Stage 1 frozen) and held strictly frozen across all non-\textit{E.~coli} benchmarks. Complete one-at-a-time sensitivity perturbations of $\pm 20\%$ across all 28 parameters on the 8 non-\textit{E.~coli} genomes of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, the 144 \textit{B.~subtilis} LRO reactions, and the 22-genome \texttt{Price-149} panel (\texttt{results/\allowbreak hyperparameter\_\allowbreak sensitivity\_\allowbreak non\_\allowbreak ecoli.tsv}) confirm that mean 8-genome micro $F_{\max}$ varies by $\le 0.0008$, GAEF by $\le 0.0038$, \textit{B.~subtilis} LRO Hit@1 by $\le 1.32$ percentage points, and \texttt{Price-149} CLEAN $F_{\max}$ by $\le 0.0042$.

\subsection{Cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical constraint bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}

To capture taxonomic restrictions entailed by multicellular eukaryotic developmental and physiological GO terms, we programmatically extracted five resource tables from the official GO, Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon}, Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, and NCBI Taxonomy OBO/OWL releases via \texttt{ExtractAnatomicalConstraints.groovy}: (1) 26,911 direct GO taxon constraint rules ($13{,}819$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $13{,}092$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across 18,159 distinct GO terms (\texttt{go-taxon-constraints.tsv}); (2) 2,715 equivalence and subclass cross-product axioms linking 2,674 distinct GO terms to 1,294 Uberon, PO, and CL classes across 30 object properties (\texttt{RO:0002296} \texttt{results\_in\_development\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002298} \texttt{results\_in\_morphogenesis\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002297} \texttt{results\_in\_formation\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002299} \texttt{results\_in\_maturation\_of}, \texttt{BFO:0000066} \texttt{occurs\_in}, and transport/organization relations; \texttt{go-anatomical-constraints.tsv}); (3) 8,331 taxon constraints ($6{,}230$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $2{,}101$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across 1,247 Uberon, PO, and CL classes (\texttt{uberon-po-taxon-constraints.tsv}); and (4) 7,722 anatomical hierarchy edges across 2,855 Uberon/PO/CL classes (\texttt{uberon-po-hierarchy.tsv}) and 1,818 taxonomic hierarchy edges across 1,305 NCBI Taxonomy clades (\texttt{ncbi-taxon-hierarchy.tsv}). A manual audit of a stratified random sample of 100 extracted GO--Uberon/PO/CL taxon-entailment pairs against curated literature confirmed $99/100$ ($99.0\%$) agreement (with 1 ambiguous life-stage term).

\subsection{Stage 2A: Deterministic symbolic enforcement and semantic ancestor relaxation}

In \texttt{IntegrateCommand.groovy} and \texttt{ConsistencyEnforcer.groovy}, Stage 2A executes immediately after Stage 1 (and prior to Stage 2B) as a linear-time ($O(|\mathcal{P}| \cdot |\mathcal{T}|)$) deterministic graph-traversal pass over the Stage 1 probability matrix. When a predicted annotation $(p, t)$ with $P^{(K)}(p, t) \ge \theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$ (or unpenalized posterior $P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t) \ge 0.50$ prior to soft constraint suppression) violates a direct GO taxon constraint or an entailed Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraint in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, Stage 2A performs breadth-first search upward in $\mathcal{T}$ from $t$ to identify the set of nearest valid ancestors $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}}) \subseteq \text{Anc}(t) \setminus \{\text{GO:0008150}, \text{GO:0003674}, \text{GO:0005575}\}$ that satisfy all taxonomic and anatomical constraints for $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ (if multiple incomparable ancestors lie at the minimum shortest-path distance, the probability update is applied to each minimal valid ancestor $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ and propagated upward; if $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}}) = \emptyset$ because all non-root ancestors of $t$ are themselves forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, semantic relaxation defaults to removal with $P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) = 0$):
\begin{align}
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a) &= \max\Bigl(P^{(K)}(p, a), \, \gamma_{\text{relax}} \, P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)\Bigr), \nonumber \\
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) &= 0,
  \label{eq:relax}
\end{align}
with $\gamma_{\text{relax}} = 0.95$, after which $P_{\text{Stage2A}}$ is converted back to log-odds $L_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, f) = \log \frac{\max(\varepsilon, P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, f))}{1 - \min(1-\varepsilon, P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, f))}$. In addition, for any obligate heteromeric complex $C \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ whose subunit support—defined as the fraction of required component functions $f' \in C$ having $\max_{p'} P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p', f') \ge 0.50$—remains below $\theta_{\text{het}} = 0.35$, isolated singleton predictions above $0.50$ are deterministically attenuated to $0.45$ and logged to an auditable action ledger.

\subsection{Stage 2B: Flux-certified stoichiometric locus refinement}

Stage 2B (\texttt{scripts/stoichiometric\_locus\_refiner.py}) operates over a sanitized universal metabolic network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ constructed from the ModelSEED biochemistry database (v2.6.1 cross-referenced to MetaNetX v4.4 and BiGG reaction/EC identifiers, flagging mass- or charge-imbalanced pool reactions with penalty $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 25.0$). For every universal reaction $j \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$, reference EC and KO annotations are compiled into a disjunctive normal form (DNF) of catalytic mechanisms $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j) = \{m_1, \dots, m_{K_j}\}$, where each mechanism $m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)$ is a conjunction of required catalytic roles or complex subunits $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m) = \{u_1, \dots, u_{D_m}\}$ ($D_m = 1$ for monomeric enzymes; $D_m \ge 2$ for multi-subunit complexes in Complex Portal/KEGG Modules such as urease \texttt{EC 3.5.1.5} $\alpha\beta\gamma$). First, the sparse role aggregator computes candidate locus support $s_{p, u} = P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, u) \in (0, 1)$ for $p \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)$ and aggregates across loci using Top-$M_{\text{iso}}$ ($M_{\text{iso}}=3$) Noisy-OR for isozyme disjunctions and a bottleneck-gated geometric mean for subunit conjunctions:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q(u) &= 1 - \prod\limits_{p \in \text{Top-}M_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u))} \bigl(1 - s_{p, u}\bigr), \\
    S_{\text{mech}}(m) &= \begin{cases}
      \Bigl(\prod\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u)\Bigr)^{1 / D_m}, & \!\min\limits_{u} Q(u) \ge \theta_{\text{sub}}, \\
      \min\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u), & \!\text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases} \\
    S_{\text{rxn}}(j) &= \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)} S_{\text{mech}}(m),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gpr_agg}
\end{equation}
with subunit bottleneck gate $\theta_{\text{sub}} = 0.25$.

Second, reactions are partitioned into core draft reactions $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} = \{j : S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge \theta_{\text{core}} = 0.50\}$ and candidate repair reactions with strictly positive addition penalties $c_j^{\text{add}} = \lambda_{\text{add}} (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j)) + w_{\text{imb}}(j) + \epsilon_{\text{par}} > 0$ ($\lambda_{\text{add}} = 1.0$, $\epsilon_{\text{par}} = 10^{-3}$; because $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 25.0 = 25\lambda_{\text{add}}$, mass-imbalanced reactions are effectively forbidden whenever any balanced repair path exists). Splitting reversible fluxes into non-negative forward and backward variables $v_j = v_j^+ - v_j^-$ ($v_j^+, v_j^- \ge 0$, $|v_j| = v_j^+ + v_j^-$) and computing the maximum achievable biomass flux $v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ over the balanced universal network under medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$, Stage 2B solves a two-step lexicographic pFBA linear program \citep{lewis2010omic} in HiGHS (feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$): Step 1 minimizes $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge \alpha_{\text{bio}} \, v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ ($\alpha_{\text{bio}} = 0.10$) to obtain minimum repair cost $C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$; Step 2 minimizes total network flux $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-) \le (1 + \delta) C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ ($\delta = 10^{-6}$) and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge \alpha_{\text{bio}} \, v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$. A non-core reaction $j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$ is certified as an active repair ($j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$) if and only if $|v_j^{\text{pFBA}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})| > \epsilon_{\text{flux}} = 10^{-6}$ ($10^3\times$ above solver feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$; identical counts hold at $10^{-7}$). Each repaired reaction $j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ receives a stoichiometric confidence weight $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 0.5\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}] + 0.3\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}] + 0.2\,\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in [0, 1]$, where $j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}$ if its minimum flux under flux variability analysis at $99\%$ optimal repair cost exceeds $\epsilon_{\text{flux}}$, $j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}$ if constraining $v_j = 0$ reduces biomass below $\alpha_{\text{bio}} v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}$ without increasing repair cost by $>10\%$, and $\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) = 1.0$ if $j$ remains active under a $5\%$ objective perturbation pass and $0.65$ otherwise.

Third, the locus responsibility updater attributes certified reaction flux back to candidate gene loci against an explicit unassigned-gene (null) hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ with prior mass $\eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$:
\begin{align}
  w_{j, u}(p) &= \left(\frac{s_{p, u}}{1 - s_{p, u}}\right)^{\beta_1} \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)^{\beta_2} \nonumber \\
  &\quad \times \exp\bigl(\beta_3 C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) + \beta_4 S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)\bigr), \nonumber \\
  r_{j, u}(p) &= \frac{w_{j, u}(p)}{\eta_{\text{null}} + \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)} w_{j, u}(p')},
  \label{eq:responsibility}
\end{align}
with $\beta_1 = 1.0, \beta_2 = 1.0, \beta_3 = 1.5, \beta_4 = 1.5$, where $C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) \in [0, 1]$ is the co-operonic pathway partner support. When three candidate genes carry only noise-floor sequence support ($s_{p, u} \le 0.01, \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}=1, C_{\text{syn}}=0, S_{\text{ESM2}}=0$), $\sum_{p'} w_{j, u}(p') \le 0.0303 < \eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$, so $h_\varnothing$ absorbs $r_{j, u}(h_\varnothing) \ge 0.62 > 0.50$ and prevents false-positive gene promotion; conversely, a single candidate with moderate homology ($s_{p, u} = 0.30$) already yields $w_{j, u}(p) \ge 0.429$, reducing $r_{j, u}(h_\varnothing)$ to $\le 0.104$ and attributing $r_{j, u}(p) \ge 0.896$ to the supported locus. In outer pass $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, candidate loci receive the stoichiometric log-odds shift $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}^{(r)}(p, u) = \gamma_{\text{FBA}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}^{(r)}(u)} \pi_j^{\text{FBA}}\, r_{j, u}^{(r)}(p)\, (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}^{(r)}(j))$ ($\gamma_{\text{FBA}} = 2.2$). In \textbf{Full GSPA Iter $k$} ($k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), outer pass $k$ updates the Stage 1 anchor log-odds in Eq.~(\ref{eq:stage1}) via geometrically damped accumulation ($\gamma_{\text{damp}} = 0.50$):
\begin{equation}
  L_{\text{anchor}}^{(k)}(p, u) = L^{(0)}(p, u) + \sum_{r=1}^{k-1} \gamma_{\text{damp}}^{r-1} \, \Delta L_{\text{FBA}}^{(r)}(p, u),
  \label{eq:outer_anchor}
\end{equation}
and re-runs one pass of Stage 1 + Stage 2A + Stage 2B, contracting monotonically by outer Iteration 3.

To formalize the theoretical limits of metabolic gene recovery across Strata A, B, and C, we define five binary recoverability indicators for each held-out reaction $j$ and true gene $p^*$: (1) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}}(j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff reaction $j$ is present and mass-balanced in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$; (2) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}}(j, \mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff blocking $j$ requires non-zero repair flux under medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$ ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 1$ on minimal glucose for Stratum A, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 1$ on cognate substrate for Stratum B, and $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 0$ for secondary metabolism Stratum C); (3) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}}(j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff $j$ has no unmasked isozyme sufficient to carry full flux; (4) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) = \mathbb{I}[\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) > 1]$; and (5) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{complex}}(p^*, j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff $j$ is catalyzed by a multi-subunit complex with partner subunits present.

\subsection{Stage 3: Selective neuro-symbolic agentic annotation completion}

Stage 3 (\texttt{AnomalyExtractor.groovy} and \texttt{benchmark/agent/run\_gspa\_agent.py}) operates selectively on post-Stage-2 genome-scale anomalies across four categories: (1) \texttt{PATHWAY\_GAP} (a pathway with completeness $\ge \theta_{\text{path\_anom}}$ missing a step $f$ with sub-threshold candidates in $P_{\text{Stage2}} \in [\theta_{\text{floor}}, 0.50)$, where \texttt{AnomalyExtractor.groovy} defaults to $\theta_{\text{path\_anom}} = 0.50$, $\theta_{\text{floor}} = 0.05$, up to $8$ candidates per anomaly and $150$ anomalies per genome, and the Suite 8 benchmark focuses on high-completeness pathways $\ge 0.65$ with top $5$ candidates in $[0.15, 0.49]$); (2) \texttt{INCOMPLETE\_COMPLEX} (an obligate complex with $\ge 50\%$ subunit coverage missing a required subunit with borderline candidates); (3) \texttt{BORDERLINE\_ESSENTIAL} (an unfulfilled core essential category $f \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ with sub-threshold candidates); and (4) \texttt{BORDERLINE\_CONFLICT} (a claim in $[0.35, 0.62]$ subject to a taxonomic or anatomical conflict where domain inspection can refine the ancestral target).

For each anomaly, Stage 3 runs a bounded multi-turn tool-calling loop ($\le 6$ turns in full mode, $\le 2$ turns in fast 1-step mode) using the open-weight instruction model \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8} \citep{yang2024qwen25} (served as \texttt{qwen3.8-27b} via vLLM on \texttt{unimatrix01} with a 32,768-token context window, temperature $0.0$, maximum 450 output tokens per turn; evaluated across 3 independent random seeds $\text{seed} \in \{42, 43, 44\}$, \texttt{results/pillar4\_multiseed\_summary.tsv}) equipped with five deterministic read-only tools: \texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak operon\_\allowbreak neighborhood}, \texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak pathway\_\allowbreak or\_\allowbreak complex\_\allowbreak gap}, \texttt{query\_\allowbreak protein\_\allowbreak evidence}, \texttt{check\_\allowbreak symbolic\_\allowbreak constraints}, and \texttt{propose\_\allowbreak resolution}. Every proposed resolution is verified by the deterministic \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} against all 26,911 direct GO taxon constraints and 2,715 GO--Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints before acceptance.

\subsection{BacDive Track 1, Track 2 Bayesian Anchor, and GSPA + DiASPora Hybrid protocols}
\label{sec:methods_bacdive}

In Suite 7 (\texttt{Track 1}, \texttt{benchmark/unified/pillar3/run\_bacdive\_benchmark.py}), for each strain $s$ and target carbon substrate or diagnostic enzyme $y$, automated metabolic reconstructions (\texttt{CarveMe} v1.5.2 \citep{machado2018fast}, \texttt{ModelSEED} v2.6.1 \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, and \texttt{gapseq} v1.2 \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}) were executed as released to obtain binary pathway/reaction calls $m_k(s, y) \in \{0, 1\}$. \textbf{GSPA Track 1 (100\% Blind)} takes \texttt{gapseq} as the base stoichiometric indicator ($P_{\text{base}}(s, y) = 0.75$ if $m_{\text{gapseq}}(s, y) = 1$ else $0.20$), adjusts by $+0.10$ when \texttt{ModelSEED} and \texttt{CarveMe} both agree ($m_{\text{mseed}} = m_{\text{carveme}} = 1$) and $-0.08$ when both are absent ($m_{\text{mseed}} = m_{\text{carveme}} = 0$), and on carbon substrates adds $+0.12$ when $\ge 2$ positive feeder assays support the strain's catabolic network (clamped to $[0.02, 0.98]$), using zero experimental phenotype training labels and preserving \texttt{gapseq}'s binary decision boundary at $\theta = 0.50$ while yielding a continuous confidence score for threshold-swept ROC-AUC evaluation. In Suite 8 (\textbf{GSPA Track 2 Bayesian Anchor}), $30\%$ of each strain's experimental assays $\mathcal{Y}_{\text{rev}}(s)$ are revealed; let $\bar{p}_{\text{rev}}(s) = (|\mathcal{Y}_{\text{rev}}(s)| + 2)^{-1}\bigl(1 + \sum_{y' \in \mathcal{Y}_{\text{rev}}(s)} y'(s)\bigr)$ denote the Laplace-smoothed revealed strain activity rate, and let $r_{\text{pb}}(y', y)$ denote the point-biserial correlation between assay $y'$ and assay $y$ across non-test reference folds. The held-out $70\%$ assays $y \in \mathcal{Y}_{\text{hold}}(s)$ are scored by combining $P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y)$ with the empirical Bayes strain prior and correlated revealed assays ($0.50\,P_{\text{Track1}} + 0.35\,\bar{p}_{\text{rev}} + 0.15\,\bar{c}_{\text{corr}}$ when $|\mathcal{Y}_{\text{rev}}(s)| \ge 2$).

For the supervised classifiers (\texttt{Traitar} L1-regularized linear SVM \citep{weimann2016traitar}, \texttt{BacDive RF Baseline} Random Forest with 500 trees \citep{koblitz2025diaspora,schober2025bac}, and \texttt{DiASPora} Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees with 300 trees, max depth 6, learning rate $0.05$ \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}), all models were trained and evaluated under identical 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation splits (where all strains of any NCBI genus are assigned to the same fold; per-fold metrics in \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak bacdive\_\allowbreak per\_\allowbreak fold\_\allowbreak cv.tsv}) over Pfam, KEGG Orthology (KO), and EC feature profiles. In \textbf{GSPA + DiASPora Hybrid}, out-of-genus probabilities from \texttt{DiASPora} ($0.55$ weight), \texttt{Traitar} ($0.20$), and \texttt{GSPA} ($0.25$, using $P_{\text{Track1}}$ in Track 1 and $P_{\text{Track2}}$ in Track 2) are combined strictly across out-of-fold predictions without test-genus leakage. All wall-clock runtimes and peak heap memory were measured on dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 compute nodes (8 CPU threads per genome; NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU for vLLM and PLM embedding extraction).

\subsection{Evaluation metrics, donor-database homology masking, and internal GAEF score}

Across Suites 1--8, functional predictions are evaluated using standard protein-centric CAFA metrics ($F_{\max}$, information-accretion-weighted $WF_{\max}$, pair-level micro $F_{\max}$, and minimum semantic distance $S_{\min}$ across thresholds $\theta \in \{0.01, \dots, 0.99\}$ \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,clark2013information}) with $95\%$ bootstrap or exact Clopper--Pearson confidence intervals and two-sided paired Wilcoxon or McNemar tests. Under donor-database homology masking (\texttt{Protocol H} at sequence-identity cutoffs $\eta_{\text{id}} \in \{100\%, 70\%, 50\%, 30\%\}$ and taxonomic Genus/Family masking \texttt{Protocol D} in \texttt{apply\_protocol\_H.py}), all reference donor proteins $r$ with pairwise sequence identity $\text{pident}(g, r) > \eta_{\text{id}}$ (or sharing the same genus/family; when explicit donor \texttt{pident} metadata is absent on a pre-merged claim, raw homology confidence is conservatively capped at $\eta_{\text{id}}/100$) to any test protein $g$ are excluded from the DIAMOND database, ESM-2/ProtT5 $k$NN donor indices, FoldSeek templates, and probability calibrators; for the neural enzyme predictors in Suite 1C (\texttt{CLEAN}, \texttt{DeepProZyme}, and \texttt{EnzBERT}), reference lookup databases and training-split homologs above $\eta_{\text{id}}$ are filtered per each tool's homology-split evaluation protocol, while the underlying pretrained protein language model backbone weights (ESM-2 and ProtT5) remain frozen as released (which may retain pretraining exposure to UniRef50 sequences and therefore makes strict Track A cosine-masked LRO a more conservative test of de novo context recovery).

Complementing external ground-truth evaluation, the internal Genome-Scale Annotation Evaluation Framework (GAEF; \texttt{QualityReport.groovy}) structural audit score summarizes genome-wide invariants at $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$—Essential Function Completeness ($C_{\text{ess}}$), Functional Coherence ($C_{\text{func}}$, the mean of active Pathway Coherence $C_{\text{path}}$, Complex Coherence $C_{\text{cplx}}$, and Process Coherence $C_{\text{proc}}$, or $\tfrac{1}{2}(C_{\text{path}} + C_{\text{cplx}})$ when process coherence is omitted), and Taxonomic/Anatomical Consistency ($C_{\text{cons}} = \max(0,\, 1 - 0.01\,V(\mathcal{P}))$, where $V(\mathcal{P})$ is the count of formal constraint violations):
\begin{equation}
  \text{GAEF}(\mathcal{P}) = 0.30 \, C_{\text{ess}} + 0.40 \, C_{\text{func}} + 0.30 \, C_{\text{cons}},
  \label{eq:gaef}
\end{equation}
which equals $0.30 \, C_{\text{ess}} + 0.20 \, C_{\text{path}} + 0.20 \, C_{\text{cplx}} + 0.30 \, C_{\text{cons}}$ on the two-component coherence panels. Because $C_{\text{ess}}, C_{\text{func}}$, and $C_{\text{cons}}$ measure the structural invariants targeted by Stages 1 and 2, GAEF is reported strictly as an internal structural diagnostic alongside held-out experimental validation.
