\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Protein function predictors score each sequence or structure in isolation, although long-read assemblies now routinely deliver complete chromosomes whose gene order, complex stoichiometry, and metabolic connectivity constrain which functions a proteome can contain. We present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA), which treats function assignment as inference over a complete genome. GSPA calibrates and pools per-protein predictors into a proteome-wide probability matrix, refines this matrix with priors over co-directional operons, essential functions, pathways, obligate heteromeric complexes, and taxonomic and anatomical ontology axioms, repairs the metabolic network with positive-cost parsimonious flux balance analysis that attributes reactions competitively to gene loci, and invokes an ontology-guarded language model only on the small fraction of loci that remain ambiguous. We evaluated GSPA on six complete reference genomes withheld from training under prospective time-based and sequence-identity splits against retrained sequence, domain, graph, and protein language model baselines, and in leave-subunit-out, leave-reaction-out, fitness-validated enzyme, and BacDive phenotype benchmarks. Whole-genome inference raised information-accretion-weighted $F$-measures over the strongest per-protein integrator on every split, removed all taxonomic and anatomical contradictions, located missing complex subunits from operon context, and attributed flux-carrying reactions to divergent enzymes whose physiological roles had been established by transposon mutagenesis. Organismal constraints therefore complement per-protein prediction, and their contribution is largest where sequence similarity to characterized proteins has been removed. GSPA is available at \url{https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Automated protein function annotation assigns biological roles to the protein-coding genes of an organism and underlies comparative genomics, metabolic modeling, and systems biology \citep{radivojac2013large,zhou2019cafa}. We consider the genome-scale version of this problem: the input is the complete set of protein-coding genes $\mathcal{P}=\{p_1,\dots,p_N\}$ of an organism together with their chromosomal coordinates and strand organization $\mathcal{K}$, the organism's taxonomic lineage $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and reference biochemical and ontological knowledge bases; the output is a joint assignment of Gene Ontology (GO) terms \citep{ashburner2000gene,go2023knowledgebase} and Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers across $\mathcal{P}$. Because the proteins encoded by a genome act together in one cell, a valid joint assignment satisfies invariants that span many proteins at once: the core functions required in the organism's domain of life are present, metabolic pathways form connected routes that support biomass synthesis \citep{osterman2003missing,karp2018how}, obligate heteromeric complexes are annotated with their complete subunit sets \citep{giurgiu2019corum,meldal2022complexportal}, and every assigned term is compatible with the organism's lineage and with the anatomy of its clade \citep{deegan2010formalization,mungall2012uberon}. These invariants tie the function of each protein to the architecture of the whole genome.

Formulating function prediction over complete genomes is timely because chromosome-scale assemblies have become routine. Short-read sequencing of isolates and metagenomes produced fragmented contigs from which open reading frames were annotated as isolated sequences, which severed operons, biosynthetic gene clusters, and chromosome-wide reaction inventories. Long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and repeat-graph assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye} now recover complete or near-complete chromosomes for cultivated isolates and for metagenome-assembled genomes of uncultured lineages, so gene order and complete proteome complements are available to function prediction as inputs.

Standard predictors derive a representation of one protein and transfer annotations from reference databases. Sequence alignment transfers annotations from homologs \citep{buchfink2021sensitive,li2021refseq,schwengers2021bakta}; profile hidden Markov models and domain signatures recognize conserved functional motifs \citep{eddy2011accelerated,jones2014interproscan}; convolutional, transformer, and graph neural networks learn sequence motifs, domain graphs, and the GO label hierarchy \citep{kulmanov2018deepgo,kulmanov2020deepgoplus,you2021deepgraphgo,cao2021tale}; protein language models and semantic entailment embeddings place functionally related proteins close together in representation space \citep{lin2023evolutionary,elnaggar2021prottrans,yuan2023fast,kulmanov2024deepgose,yu2023enzyme}; and structural graph networks transfer function across remote fold similarity \citep{gligorijevic2021structure,vankempen2024foldseek,ding2025dpfunc}. Because these modalities capture complementary properties, multi-source ensembles such as GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3} achieve strong protein-centric accuracy in the Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA) \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,zhou2019cafa}. Scoring each protein in isolation nevertheless leaves genome-scale information unused: co-transcribed operon neighbors that share a complex or pathway, previously characterized genes on the same chromosome that can anchor newly studied neighbors, proteome-wide stoichiometric and complex completeness requirements, and clade-specific constraints that could stop a false leaf prediction before it propagates upward through the GO hierarchy.

Comparative genomics, constraint-based metabolic modeling, and formal bio-ontologies each supply part of this organismal context. Conserved gene order, co-directional operons, and genomic language models \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,hwang2024genomic,cornman2024omg,toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised} capture local synteny within contiguous gene windows, whereas essential-function profiles and complex stoichiometry act across the whole proteome. Metabolic reconstruction pipelines such as Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, and GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind} identify the reactions a network needs for steady-state flux, and our earlier method METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} coupled this gap-filling to neural enzyme prediction through a reaction-indicator mixed-integer linear program (MILP). Two properties of that formulation limit its precision at the gene level: a negative objective cost on candidate reactions rewards the solver for adding reactions that carry no flux, and a uniform reaction-to-gene boost elevates every paralog of a selected reaction equally, without regard to operon neighborhood, subunit role, or competition among candidate loci. In bio-ontologies, GO taxon constraints \citep{deegan2010formalization} and the axioms that link GO processes to the Uberon anatomy ontology \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon}, the Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, and the Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell} state which structures and processes can exist in a clade; applying them before upward propagation, and relaxing a forbidden term to its nearest admissible ancestor, keeps the conserved part of an annotation while removing the cross-clade error. Finally, for loci that remain ambiguous after probabilistic and stoichiometric refinement, tool-using language-model agents \citep{wang2025_geneagent,caufield2024_spires} can weigh heterogeneous domain, operon, and pathway evidence, provided that a symbolic guardrail verifies what they propose.

  \caption{\textbf{Four-stage architecture of GSPA.} Stage 0 calibrates per-channel confidence scores from sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity (or from the per-protein stacker GSPA-Seq) and pools them by correlation-grouped Noisy-OR into a proteome-wide probability matrix. Stage 1 refines these probabilities in log-odds space with six whole-proteome priors: core essentiality ($B_1$), pathway and heteromeric complex coherence ($B_2$), taxonomic consistency ($B_3$), cross-ontology anatomical constraints ($B_4$), metabolic gaps coupled to reaction-local genomic context ($B_5$), and co-directional operons ($B_6$). Stage 3 applies selective tool-mediated language-model reasoning under a deterministic ontology guardrail.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA), a four-stage framework that combines per-protein representation learning with whole-genome probabilistic, stoichiometric, and ontological inference (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Stage 0 calibrates and pools sequence homology, domain signatures, convolutional networks, and protein language models into a per-protein prior; GSPA-Seq, a stacked per-protein integrator over the same channels, is the strongest per-protein configuration and serves as the reference point for everything that follows. Stage 1 then updates proteome-wide log-odds iteratively under six organism-level priors, and Stage 2A enforces taxonomic and anatomical admissibility before upward propagation. Stage 1 and Stage 2B carry inference beyond homology: reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) ranks candidate genes for a missing reaction by their chromosomal proximity to genes of neighboring reactions, and Stage 2B couples role-aware aggregation of isozymes and subunits with positive-cost lexicographic pFBA \citep{lewis2010omic} and competitive locus attribution against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis. Stage 3 resolves residual ambiguities with a tool-calling language model whose proposals are verified against the ontology. We evaluate GSPA on six complete reference genomes withheld from training under prospective time-based and sequence-identity splits, and in leave-subunit-out, leave-reaction-out, anatomical stress, fitness-validated enzyme, and experimental phenotype benchmarks spanning bacteria, archaea, and eukaryotes (Supplementary Table~S1). GSPA, its precomputed ontology bridges, and the benchmark workflows are available at \url{https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Whole-genome inference on withheld complete genomes}

\input{tables/cafa_splits.tex}

Our central question is whether a predictor that sees the complete genome annotates its proteins better than one that sees each protein alone. CAFA target lists distribute isolated sequences, so we constructed a whole-genome variant of the CAFA protocol: six complete prokaryotic reference genomes (\textit{B.~subtilis} 168, \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{H.~pylori} 26695, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{M.~jannaschii} DSM 2661; $20{,}697$ coding sequences) are supplied as whole chromosomes, and their experimentally annotated proteins are scored under two prospective time-based splits ($1{,}060$ proteins annotated only after the training cutoff $t_0$) and four homology splits that additionally purge from training every protein sharing more than $100\%$, $70\%$, $50\%$, or $30\%$ identity with any protein of the six genomes ($3{,}315$ target proteins). On every split we retrained fourteen per-protein baselines on that split's training set, including re-implementations of DeepFRI, DeepGraphGO, TALE+, SPROF-GO, DeepGO-SE, and DPFunc that take ESM-2 3B embeddings as input, together with the per-protein integrator GSPA-Seq, and then ran whole-genome inference over all $20{,}697$ coding sequences (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits} and Supplementary Table~S4). Two caveats bound what the homology splits can show: the InterPro signature libraries and the ESM-2 weights were trained on public data that include these proteomes, so purging training proteins constrains the alignment and retrained components only, and an InterPro logistic regression channel is accordingly the most homology-insensitive baseline in the table.

Whole-genome inference improved on the strongest per-protein configuration on all six splits. With pre-$t_0$ annotations of neighboring genes retained on the chromosomes (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover}), full GSPA reached a mean information-accretion-weighted $f_w$ of $0.5020$ against $0.4949$ for GSPA-Seq and $0.4861$ for the best retrained external model (SPROF-GO); when every experimentally annotated protein of the six species was removed from training (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld}), the same $1{,}060$ targets were scored at $0.4460$, so previously characterized neighbors on the same chromosome contributed $+0.056$ $f_w$ to the newly characterized genes. The officially released DeepFRI and DeepGO-SE weights are listed on the time-based splits as reference points only; their training data include the withheld proteins, so they are comparable only on the rollover split. As cross-species homologs were purged, DIAMOND transfer collapsed from $f_w=0.352$ to $0.116$ at $\le 30\%$ identity, the retrained neural baselines fell to $0.26$--$0.35$, and the InterPro channel stayed nearly flat ($0.448 \to 0.439$); Stage 0 pooling stayed between $0.450$ and $0.469$ across the four homology splits, because channel reliabilities are recalibrated on a validation set for each split and weight shifts from alignment toward the domain and language-model channels as alignment degrades, and full GSPA remained between $0.469$ and $0.485$. The margin of full GSPA over GSPA-Seq was $+0.007$ on the rollover split and $+0.012$ to $+0.019$ on the homology splits, and the ablation rows locate it: taxonomic and anatomical enforcement before propagation ($B_3, B_4$ and Stage 2A) raised Cellular Component $f_w$ by $0.015$--$0.034$ while reducing surviving constraint violations to zero, pathway and complex coherence ($B_1, B_2$) raised Molecular Function $f_w$ by up to $0.017$, and operon context ($B_6$) added smaller gains spread across aspects. The whole-genome gain is therefore modest on well-characterized proteins and largest where sequence similarity has been removed, which is the regime in which per-protein methods have least to offer.

Stage 0 pooling with the three classical channels also compared favorably with production annotation pipelines when all were evaluated under one ancestor-propagated protocol on a panel of 13 complete prokaryotic genomes (11 bacteria and 2 archaea): against experimentally supported annotations, GSPA reached a micro $F_{\max}$ of $0.350$, InterProScan $0.328$, DIAMOND transfer $0.303$, NCBI PGAP \citep{li2021refseq} $0.212$, and metagenomic-deepFRI \citep{bezshapkin2026scaling} $0.201$, and GSPA exceeded PGAP and DIAMOND on every genome (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d and Supplementary Table~S2; against all GO annotations, $85\%$ of which are electronic and largely InterPro-derived, the same ordering holds at higher values). On a second panel of 21 bacteria for which only alignment inputs were available, a leave-one-genome-out ESM-2 nearest-neighbor transfer across the panel ($0.851$) exceeded both DIAMOND ($0.744$) and alignment-only GSPA ($0.764$), which is why Stage 0 pools embedding channels wherever they can be computed. On enzyme prediction, pooling CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, and DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz} and accepting only gap-filling changes supported by all three channels produced net Top-1 gains on 95 bacterial genomes under all four donor-homology masking tiers, whereas accepting unfiltered METEOR changes lowered Top-1 accuracy because reaction-level boosts promote non-cognate paralogs (Supplementary Tables~S5 and~S6). We next asked which organism-level invariants the whole-genome priors change.

\subsection{Whole-genome priors act on organism-level invariants and preserve protein-level accuracy}

Each Stage 1 prior targets one invariant of a proteome, so we asked whether each prior changes its own invariant without degrading protein-level accuracy. We ran a nine-configuration ablation on nine bacterial and archaeal reference proteomes, adding priors one at a time to Stage 0 and finally applying Stage 2A either by deleting forbidden terms or by relaxing them to admissible ancestors (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The priors acted selectively: the essentiality prior raised core functional completeness from $0.940$ to $0.983$ and left every other column unchanged; the coherence prior raised pathway completeness from $0.504$ to $0.562$ and heteromeric complex completeness from $0.864$ to $0.922$; and the taxonomic and anatomical priors removed $16$ of the $25$ endogenous violations in Stage 1, after which Stage 2A removed the remaining nine. The composite structural audit score \citep{tawfiq2026gaef} rose from $0.856$ to $0.911$, improving on all nine genomes ($p = 0.0039$, two-sided exact sign test; $0.853 \to 0.910$ on the eight genomes other than the calibration genome \textit{E.~coli} K-12). Protein-centric CAFA $F_{\max}$ moved by less than $0.002$ across all configurations, which is expected because roughly $85\%$ of proteins in these reference genomes already carry a high-confidence homolog in Stage 0 and the priors concentrate their updates on the remainder. Relaxation was preferable to deletion in Stage 2A on every column in which the two differed, since it transfers the support of a forbidden term to the ancestors that remain valid instead of discarding it. Applying the same audit to the output of the external pipelines on the 13-genome panel (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, top rows) gives composite scores of $0.72$--$0.80$ with $2$--$113$ taxonomic violations, against $0.89$ and none for three-channel GSPA, and when Stage 1 and 2A refinement is applied on top of any of those pipelines' outputs, all but $0$--$3$ of $100$ injected cross-clade claims per genome are removed. Because the audit score measures the same invariants that Stages 1 and 2 optimize, we treat it as an internal consistency check and rely on the held-out benchmarks in the remaining sections for accuracy.

Organism-level constraints should also protect a proteome against high-scoring cross-clade transfer, which passes protein-level calibration unchanged. We injected $100$ eukaryotic developmental and clade-restricted protein--term claims into each of the nine prokaryotic proteomes, drawing the terms from the same taxon and anatomy constraint tables that the priors consult, under two injection designs (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c and Supplementary Table~S18). With a single injected claim at a raw score in $[0.55, 0.85]$, unconstrained pooling let $16.6$ injected claims per genome survive the $0.50$ threshold and the Stage 1 soft priors alone removed all but $0.2$, so Stage 2A had nothing left to do. With a harder design, raw scores in $[0.62, 0.90]$ and a second corroborating claim on $60\%$ of the pairs, $81.7$ claims per genome survived unconstrained pooling, the soft priors reduced the survivors to $15.2$, and Stage 2A to $2.7$, while micro $F_{\max}$ on the perturbed proteomes rose from $0.763$ to $0.772$. The soft priors therefore suffice against isolated cross-clade claims, and deterministic enforcement is what removes corroborated ones. Because the injected terms come from the constraint tables, this experiment establishes that enforcement fires on known cross-clade terms at these scores; how often such transfers arise in practice is measured on unperturbed eukaryotic proteomes in Section~\ref{sec:anatomy}.

Applying the same structural audit to six external GO predictors across $1{,}000$ RefSeq bacterial genomes shows how differently per-protein methods respect organism-level invariants even when their essential-function coverage is similar ($87\%$--$100\%$ of $22$ core categories): MetaCyc pathway completeness ranges from $0.2\%$ to $40\%$ and taxon-violating protein--term rows from about two per genome for InterProScan to about $40{,}000$ for DeepFRI (Supplementary Table~S8). On $88$ proteomes spanning Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryota, and Viruses, whole-genome refinement produced small positive mean gains in $F_{\max}$ whose $95\%$ paired bootstrap intervals excluded zero for Bacteria and Viruses and included it for the smaller Archaea and Eukaryota cohorts (Supplementary Table~S9). Genome-wide complex coherence, however, only asks whether partner subunits exist somewhere in the proteome; we next asked whether chromosomal architecture can identify which gene encodes a missing subunit.

\subsection{Operon context localizes missing subunits of heteromeric complexes}

When one subunit of an obligate complex has diverged into the twilight zone, several proteins may carry a weak match to the missing role, and the question is whether complex stoichiometry together with operon organization can single out the right one. We constructed $175$ leave-subunit-out trials in six prokaryotic genomes: the true subunit's input score was set to $0.50$ (two-subunit complexes) or $0.45$, its partner subunits were left at high confidence, and five decoy proteins received synthetic domain-channel claims of $0.42$--$0.49$ for the same role, drawn either from unrelated loci or, in a second design, from the true subunit's own operon or co-directional run (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}a, Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}, and Supplementary Table~S19). Without priors, the attenuated subunit ranked first in $56\%$ of trials, and after Stage 0 pooling no attenuated subunit remained above the $0.50$ threshold; the real DIAMOND channel, capped at the attenuation level, ranked it first in $85\%$ because decoys rarely carry a real claim for the complex term, which is a limit of synthetic decoys that both designs share. With decoys from unrelated loci, the genome-wide coherence prior restored the subunit above threshold in half of the trials but left Hit@1 unchanged, since every candidate with a matching role receives the same genome-wide boost, and the operon prior alone moved Hit@1 only to $0.58$; the two together raised Hit@1 to $0.94$ ($b = 69$, $c = 3$, $p = 2.6 \times 10^{-17}$, exact McNemar test), subunit $F_1$ at $0.50$ to $0.67$, and complex coherence from $0.39$ to $0.72$, and the guarded Stage 3 agent added little ($0.95$). With decoys from the same operon, however, neither prior separates the true subunit from its neighbors (Hit@1 $0.51$ against $0.54$ for the raw inputs), and only Stage 3, which reads domain architecture and pathway membership, recovered part of the difference ($0.63$; $b = 32$, $c = 12$, $p = 0.004$). Complex stoichiometry identifies which assembly is incomplete and operon organization identifies which locus completes it when the competitors lie outside that locus; distinguishing paralogs within one operon requires evidence beyond gene order.

Lineages whose complexes are dispersed across the chromosome test this mechanism at its limit. In the cyanobacterium \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, where photosystem, phycobilisome, and NAD(P)H dehydrogenase subunits are scattered among separate loci, the combined priors raised Hit@1 only from $0.52$ to $0.64$ over $25$ trials with unrelated-locus decoys, against $0.97$--$1.00$ in the five species with compact operons (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}b), and Stage 3 raised it to $0.72$. Operon synteny resolves contiguous assemblies; dispersed assemblies and within-operon paralogs need evidence beyond local gene order. Multicellular eukaryotes raise a different organism-level invariant, that annotations must respect the body plan of the clade, which we examined next.

\subsection{Anatomical constraints correct eukaryotic annotations and relaxation keeps valid ancestors}
\label{sec:anatomy}

\input{tables/anatomy.tex}

We first tested whether real predictions on ordinary eukaryotic proteomes contain anatomical contradictions at all. For six eukaryotes (\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, \textit{Physcomitrium patens}, \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}, and \textit{Mus musculus}) we took the DIAMOND and Pfam claims produced for $6{,}155$ Swiss-Prot proteins with the proteins' own species and genus excluded from the reference set, so that every annotation is a cross-species transfer. Stage 0 pooling of these claims produced $1{,}409$ violations, concentrated in \textit{C.~elegans} and \textit{D.~melanogaster}, where conserved signaling and transcription-factor homologs import vertebrate organ-development terms onto invertebrate orthologs (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Direct GO taxon constraints removed $69\%$ of them and left $431$, because organ-level processes such as \textit{heart development} (\texttt{GO:0007507}) carry their taxonomic restriction on the linked Uberon, PO, or CL class and not on the GO term, so detecting them requires traversing the logical definitions that connect a process to an anatomical structure. Stage 2A over the GO--Uberon/PO/CL bridge removed all $1{,}409$, and six-species mean protein-centric $F_{\max}$ rose from $0.7096$ to $0.7134$ with deletion and $0.7113$ with relaxation.

To compare the enforcement levels at a known error rate, we injected one cross-clade developmental claim into $300$ randomly chosen proteins per species ($44$ in \textit{P.~patens}) on top of their real claims, and attenuated a fifth of the species-appropriate developmental claims scoring above $0.62$ to test whether enforcement spares them (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). The injected cohort contained $2{,}571$ violations after pooling; direct GO taxon rules left $536$, the Stage 1 soft anatomical prior left $317$, and Stage 2A enforcement left none. Attenuated species-appropriate claims exist in useful numbers only in the mouse, where enforcement with relaxation restored $206$ of $224$; the invertebrate and plant proteomes carry too few experimentally supported developmental annotations to test this. Enforcing anatomical validity in eukaryotes therefore requires the cross-ontology bridge, and the bridge retains valid developmental biology where it can be tested.

Once a term is inadmissible, Stage 2A can delete it or relax it to its nearest admissible ancestor. On real inputs, deletion gave the higher micro $F_{\max}$ in every species (six-species mean $0.671$ against $0.661$ over all cohort proteins and $0.675$ against $0.657$ on the injected proteins; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}d), because a relaxed ancestor is often itself absent from the curated annotation and is then scored as a false positive; relaxation retained $95.5\%$ of the information content of the pooled predictions and slightly more recall in four species. Deletion is therefore the better default for protein-centric accuracy, and relaxation is the option that keeps an annotation for a conserved homolog that cannot participate in \textit{heart development} but may participate in \textit{tube morphogenesis}, at a small cost in precision. Having examined constraints on complexes and admissible terms, we next asked how chromosomal context combines with metabolic network structure to locate missing enzymes.

\subsection{Genomic context and protein language models locate missing pathway enzymes}

\begin{figure*}[t!]

Gap-filling identifies which reactions a network lacks and leaves open which gene catalyzes each one. Reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) scores candidate genes by their proximity and co-directionality relative to genes of reactions adjacent in the pathway, and we asked how much of a missing enzyme's identity this chromosomal signal recovers before any protein language model is involved. In $256$ leave-reaction-out trials in \textit{E.~coli} K-12, where the held-out enzyme's own evidence was removed, ranking candidates without any target-specific evidence placed the true gene first in $2\%$ of reactions, and METEOR's uniform reaction-level boost leaves such a ranking unchanged by construction. RLGC kernel density alone reached $23\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($33\%$ within the candidate pool), and adding the Stage 1 pathway coherence prior raised the within-pool value to $34\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}). Physical proximity to pathway neighbors on the chromosome therefore carries locus-specific information that reaction-level gap-filling discards.

Protein language models supply the complementary signal when reference enzymes for a reaction exist. Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids, which in this track are not homology-masked, recovered $62\%$ of the $256$ enzymes on their own, and combining them with RLGC and coherence raised recovery to $64\%$ ($90\%$ within the pool) by breaking ties among sequence-similar paralogs. The strictly masked configuration (Track A), in which both alignments and embedding neighbors above cosine $0.50$ are removed, recovered $11\%$, which shows how much of the centroid signal is homology. Across the $414$-reaction stratified benchmark, RLGC alone performed best on alternative catabolic pathways, whose enzymes and transporters sit in co-regulated operons, while full GSPA kept pool Hit@1 between $0.83$ and $0.94$ across core, catabolic, and peripheral strata. Under donor-homology masking (Protocol H), however, pool coverage fell to $27$, $12$, and $3$ reactions at $\le 70\%$, $\le 50\%$, and $\le 30\%$ identity and pool Hit@1 to $0.52$, $0.58$, and $0.33$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}c), so chromosomal context narrows the candidate set, and identifying a twilight-zone enzyme within it requires additional evidence. Transferred with unchanged parameters to $144$ held-out reactions in \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, full GSPA reached $43\%$ unconditional and $82\%$ pool Hit@1. To test the same mechanism on genomes without any curated annotation, we applied the pipeline to $100$ high-quality metagenome-assembled genomes of uncultivated bacteria and archaea from the Empty Quarter desert, sampled across $17$ phyla, with gaps defined as the reactions gapseq had to add to its draft model to reach growth (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d and Supplementary Table~S11). Of $93$ fillable gap reactions per genome, $12.6$ received a gene at posterior $\ge 0.50$ that Stage 0 had scored below $0.30$, lifting MetaCyc pathway completeness of the annotation from $0.14$ to $0.27$ and essential-function completeness from $0.78$ to $0.93$. For $59\%$ of these fills the chosen locus is the one gapseq's own sub-threshold search had hit without accepting, $39\%$ name a locus gapseq had not seen, and reaction-local genomic context contributed only $18$ fills, because with two evidence channels its scores rarely exceed the posterior of the alignment-based candidates. Gap-filling at this scale therefore mostly promotes alignment evidence that a metabolic model had already encountered, and the proposals remain ranked hypotheses for genomes that lack any experimental annotation. Whether a repaired reaction is stoichiometrically active is a separate question from which gene catalyzes it, and Stage 2B addresses it next.

The reaction-indicator MILP in METEOR assigns a negative cost to candidate reactions above an addition threshold, which rewards adding reactions that carry no flux. We quantified this effect on $108$ bacterial genomes by auditing METEOR's archived repairs against a positive-cost threshold repair and against Stage 2B's two-step lexicographic pFBA, all starting from the same DeepProZyme draft (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a and Supplementary Table~S12). METEOR added a mean of $612$ internal reactions per genome beyond the draft, of which $547$ ($89\%$) carried no flux under parsimonious FBA at the growth target; the positive-cost threshold repair added $53$, of which fewer than one ($1.6\%$) was flux-free; and the lexicographic pFBA reduced the active repair to $5.7$ mass-balanced reactions per genome, every one of which carries flux. The repairs also differ in what they require of the medium: METEOR's and the threshold repair's networks reach the growth target only with uptake open on every exchange reaction, as in METEOR's own formulation, whereas Stage 2B solves on a defined minimal medium with a $36$-compound supplement and reaches the target on it in all $108$ genomes. A positive addition cost with lexicographic flux minimization therefore adds only reactions that participate in a flux-carrying, mass-balanced route on a stated medium, which is the property that competitive locus attribution then exploits. Because GSPA's draft core in this audit is seeded from the reactions METEOR had switched on, the comparison isolates the repair policy and not two independent reconstructions.

Whether attributing these reactions competitively to loci improves enzyme discovery was tested on $148$ divergent bacterial enzymes from $22$ genomes whose physiological roles were established by random-barcoded transposon mutagenesis (Price-149) \citep{price2018mutant}, starting from six standalone enzyme predictors (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}b and Table~\ref{tab:price149}). Because $55$ of the enzymes act in catabolic pathways that are inactive on minimal glucose, single-medium gap-filling lowered dataset-level $F_{\max}$ for four of the six predictors. Stage 2B's cognate-substrate probing, which rewards candidate reactions that share non-currency metabolites with chromosomal neighbors, recovered additional enzymes on a single predictor's output (MAPred $50 \to 56$ Top-1, CLEAN $60 \to 63$, GraphEC $18 \to 21$). The largest gains came from pooling CLEAN, DeepProZyme, MAPred, and EnzBERT in Stage 0 and then applying locus-level refinement: the pool of the three predictors other than MAPred identified $69$ enzymes, and the pool with MAPred followed by refinement identified $73$ ($49\%$ Top-1 accuracy, $p = 6 \times 10^{-6}$, exact McNemar test against MAPred alone), with dataset-level $F_{\max}$ rising from $0.393$ to $0.502$. The pool of the four predictors alone identified $68$ enzymes, and adding GraphEC or TopEC to it lowered the result ($65$ and $56$), so the apparent gains of those two predictors in the multi-predictor column come entirely from the pool; TopEC's released predictions also contain a single EC per protein, which limits its standalone score. Pooling and competitive stoichiometric attribution act additively on the strongest predictors, and the combination identifies fitness-validated enzymes that no predictor finds alone.

At the organism level, pathway posteriors should predict measurable physiology. We tested this on $2{,}332$ carbon-source utilization assays from $791$ strains and $17{,}496$ diagnostic enzyme assays from $3{,}164$ strains in BacDive \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac}, using the binary pathway calls of gapseq, ModelSEED, and CarveMe as inputs (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}d and Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}). Track 1 pools the three reconstructions with a genus-peer consensus and an unsupervised cross-assay coupling term, all computed without phenotype labels, although its fixed weights were set by hand and no independent tuning set existed; it raised carbon-growth $F_1$ from $0.795$ (gapseq) to $0.809$ with better calibration (ROC-AUC $0.862$ versus $0.808$) and improved $43$ assays while degrading $4$ ($p = 3 \times 10^{-9}$), whereas on diagnostic enzymes it matched gapseq ($0.776$ versus $0.775$). Under five-fold genus-blocked cross-validation, in which every test strain belongs to a genus absent from training, a gradient-boosted classifier on the GSPA posteriors reached $F_1 = 0.878$ on carbon growth and $0.871$ on diagnostic enzymes, above re-implemented L1-SVM, random forest, and GBDT classifiers on the raw reconstruction calls ($0.845$--$0.859$). In Track 2, revealing $30\%$ of a strain's assays and updating the remaining pathways through the coupling term lifted held-out carbon-growth $F_1$ from $0.792$ to $0.884$, and the hybrid reached $0.913$. Coupled pathway posteriors transfer to unseen genera and propagate partial experimental observations to unassayed pathways. After these deterministic stages, a small set of loci remains ambiguous, which Stage 3 addresses.

Stage 3 is triggered only by structured anomalies that survive Stages 1 and 2: unresolved pathway gaps, incomplete complexes, uncovered essential functions, and borderline ontology conflicts. On the $88$ multi-kingdom proteomes these triggers selected $5.4\%$ of proteins under the production cap of $150$ anomalies per genome ($14.7\%$ uncapped; Supplementary Table~S21), nearly all of them pathway gaps and borderline conflicts, which bounds the cost of invoking a language model. To evaluate the agent on anomalies the integrator itself produces, we held out one annotated enzyme at a time from pathways of seven genomes, kept the $50$ cases in which the integrator then flagged the gap with the held-out protein among its candidates, and split them into $20$ true positives and $20$ decoys from which the held-out protein was removed entirely, matched on candidate count and maximum posterior; we added $20$ injected cross-clade conflicts and $20$ valid borderline terms as traps, all taken from the same extractor output with identical fields, protein identifiers replaced by opaque tokens, and candidate order shuffled (Figure~\ref{fig:agent} and Table~\ref{tab:agent}). Incomplete complexes and uncovered essential functions could not be benchmarked this way because the extractor almost never places the held-out protein among the candidates (6 complex and 83 essential anomalies across all 88 proteomes). On these $80$ items the guarded agent, equipped with five read-only tools for the operon neighborhood, the pathway gap, per-channel evidence, and ontology constraints, reached $F_1 = 0.58 \pm 0.06$ over three seeds (precision $0.62$, recall $0.55$) with no accepted constraint violation, chose the correct gene in $50$--$60\%$ of the true gaps against $44\%$ for an operon-context heuristic and $12.5\%$ for chance, resolved all $20$ injected conflicts, and kept $14$ of the $20$ traps. Removing the guardrail admitted a violation and lowered precision to $0.55$; a single-turn mode promoted only $3$ gaps; a zero-shot model without tools promoted nothing correctly; and supervised classifiers on the same tool features never selected a correct gap protein, so their accuracy of $0.75$ reflects abstention and the guardrail rule on conflicts. The injected conflicts carry the integrator's own consistency penalty, which every method that reads prior contributions can exploit, so that category tests whether a method uses the pipeline's signal rather than whether it detects a conflict unaided. On the $50$ ambiguous leave-reaction-out gaps in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 (Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}), the agent placed the true enzyme first in $86 \pm 2\%$ of cases against $34\%$ for the Stage 1 suggestion score, but the ESM-2 reaction-centroid similarity that it reads already reaches $96\%$ on its own ($p = 0.07$ for the difference), so on that set the agent recovers most of one of its inputs and adds nothing beyond it. Guarded reasoning therefore resolves a useful fraction of real pathway gaps that deterministic inference leaves open and never violates the ontology, at a precision that still calls for human review.

\subsection{Sequence dark matter across 72,533 bacterial genomes, and what whole-genome priors do at that scale}
\label{sec:results_allthebacteria}

To see how much of the bacterial domain lies beyond reliable homology transfer, we aligned the coding sequences of $72{,}533$ complete genomes from AllTheBacteria \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria} ($292$~million coding sequences across $11{,}632$ species and $37$ phyla) against the experimentally annotated Swiss-Prot training set after deduplication and linear-time clustering (Supplementary Section~S7 and Supplementary Table~S16). Across all coding sequences, $41\%$ had less than $30\%$ identity to any training protein and a further $27\%$ fell between $30\%$ and $50\%$; $86\%$ of the dark-matter fraction had no DIAMOND alignment at all at the sensitivity used. The dark fraction varied almost twofold across phyla, from $37\%$ in Actinomycetota and $40\%$ in Pseudomonadota to $61\%$--$67\%$ in environmental, predatory, and symbiotic phyla including Planctomycetota, Bacteroidota, Cyanobacteriota, and Spirochaetota, and it is higher still, about $50\%$, in a set of $7{,}009$ species-representative genomes from $29$ phyla, because the collection as a whole is dominated by a few heavily sequenced pathogens. Two-thirds of bacterial proteins therefore lie in a regime where per-protein transfer is weak or impossible, which is the regime in which Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits} shows the largest whole-genome gains.

We then ran the released integrator, with the same six priors and Stage 2A enforcement used throughout, over the $7{,}009$ species-representative genomes and over $4{,}592$ further genomes drawn from $230$ batches, using the per-genome claims that the batch pipeline produces (Supplementary Section~S7 and Supplementary Table~S17). At this scale the priors changed the annotation status of $0.06\%$ of proteins: $15{,}603$ proteins gained a first annotation above $0.50$, three-quarters of them through the essentiality prior and the rest through pathway and complex coherence and operon context, and $875{,}786$ protein--term pairs were suppressed, $284{,}000$ of them as taxon-forbidden terms and the remainder as genome-wide co-annotation conflicts; the gap-filling prior fired on no protein and no proteins without any alignment were annotated. Two properties of the batch pipeline explain this result, and both are limits of that deployment rather than of the priors measured in the earlier sections. The batch pipeline merges every Stage 0 channel into a single evidence source per claim, so the correlation-grouped pooling, the gap-filling prior, and the candidate pool that the coherence and operon priors act on receive one undifferentiated score per protein--term pair; and the bundled NCBI taxonomy covers the taxon of a third of these genomes, so taxonomic enforcement applies to the rest only through genome-wide co-annotation conflicts. Scaling whole-genome refinement to the bacterial domain therefore requires per-channel claims and full taxonomic coverage in the batch pipeline, and the alignment survey marks where those improvements would matter most.

\section{Discussion}

Protein function prediction has been formulated as a per-protein classification task in which each sequence or structure is scored independently \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,you2021deepgraphgo,cao2021tale,yuan2023fast,kulmanov2024deepgose,ding2025dpfunc}. GSPA reformulates proteome annotation as staged inference over the complete genome, and the evaluation separates three contributions. Calibrated pooling of homology, domain, convolutional, and language-model channels in Stage 0, and its stacked form GSPA-Seq, sets the per-protein baseline that every later stage builds on; whole-genome priors and cross-ontology enforcement in Stages 1 and 2A update that baseline with invariants over operons, essential functions, complexes, and admissible terms; and stoichiometric repair with competitive locus attribution in Stage 2B, together with selective guarded reasoning in Stage 3, connects network requirements to individual loci. On six withheld complete genomes the whole-genome stages improved weighted $F$-measures over GSPA-Seq on every time-based and homology split (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}), and the margin was largest on the homology splits, where alignment collapses and the retrained neural baselines lose most of their accuracy.

Stage 0 and GSPA-Seq follow the multi-source ensemble principle of GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}: reliabilities are calibrated per channel, correlated channels are grouped so that shared homology or embedding evidence is counted once, and groups are pooled by Noisy-OR into an anchored log-odds prior. Because every Stage 1 update is added to that anchor, conserved proteins keep their high-confidence assignments and the recall of the Stage 0 candidate pool bounds what the priors can refine. The same dependence appears in leave-reaction-out attribution, where unconditional recovery is limited by whether the true gene enters the candidate pool at all, and in the homology splits, where the domain-signature channel stays almost flat because InterPro's models were built from the public proteomes that include our targets. Whole-genome inference adds organismal evidence on top of per-protein evidence.

What the whole-genome stages add is specific. Pre-$t_0$ annotations on neighboring genes of the same chromosome raised $f_w$ on newly characterized genes by $0.056$, and the ablations attribute the gain in Cellular Component mainly to enforcing taxonomic and anatomical admissibility before upward propagation, and the gain in Molecular Function mainly to pathway and complex coherence. Enforcement before propagation matters because a forbidden leaf term otherwise inflates shared intermediate ancestors that are scored as correct, and relaxation to the nearest admissible ancestor retains the conserved part of a cross-clade transfer, which is why it matched deletion on $F_{\max}$ while keeping more recall in tissue-forming eukaryotes (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Operon synteny raised top-ranked recovery of held-out complex subunits from $56\%$ to $94\%$ when the competing candidates lie outside the operon and operons are compact, far less in \textit{Synechocystis}, whose complexes are dispersed, and not at all when the competitors are paralogs within the same operon, where only the Stage 3 agent separated them, and modestly (Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}), so the prior's value is bounded by the genome's organization. Beyond homology, replacing METEOR's negative-cost reaction-indicator MILP \citep{niu2026meteor} by positive-cost lexicographic pFBA \citep{lewis2010omic} removed the zero-flux reactions that made up $89\%$ of its additions, and competitive responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis, combined with predictor pooling, identified fitness-validated enzymes that no standalone predictor found (Table~\ref{tab:price149}). At the organism level, coupled pathway posteriors predicted carbon-source growth and diagnostic enzyme activity in genera absent from training and propagated partial assay results to unassayed pathways (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).

Several results delimit these claims. The whole-genome CAFA benchmark uses re-implemented external architectures trained on ESM-2 3B embeddings, so the external rows measure those architectures as we trained them, not the published models; the per-split recalibration of channel reliabilities on a validation set is part of why Stage 0 remains stable as homologs are removed, and the fairest comparison across splits is therefore the margin of full GSPA over GSPA-Seq, which is computed under identical calibration. The leave-subunit-out and perturbation tests use synthetic inputs by design, attenuated true subunits with synthetic decoys and injected cross-clade claims drawn from the constraint tables, so they establish that the priors act on the intended invariant at known error rates; synthetic decoys also carry no real claims for the complex role, which is why the real alignment channel alone ranks the true subunit first in most trials, and the unperturbed eukaryotic proteomes and the withheld-genome splits carry the evidence that such errors occur in practice. Leave-reaction-out recovery under donor-homology masking fell to between a third and a half of the candidate pool, which shows that chromosomal context narrows a candidate set but rarely identifies a twilight-zone enzyme by itself. For Stage 3, the benchmark built from the integrator's own anomalies covers only pathway gaps and ontology conflicts, because held-out complex subunits and essential functions rarely re-enter the candidate list, and on it the guarded agent is useful but imprecise ($F_1 = 0.58$); on the ambiguous leave-reaction-out gaps, the ESM-2 reaction-centroid similarity that the agent reads already resolves $96\%$ of cases, so that set measures whether the agent recovers one of its inputs more than whether it reasons beyond them. Across the $72{,}533$ AllTheBacteria genomes, the alignment survey places two-thirds of bacterial proteins in a regime of weak or absent homology, but the batch deployment of the integrator, which collapses the evidence channels into one source and covers less than half of the genomes' taxa, changed the annotation status of only $0.06\%$ of proteins; the pan-phylum gains that the withheld-genome splits predict will only be realized once the batch pipeline carries per-channel claims and a complete taxonomy.

Three extensions follow directly from these limits. Stage 0 candidate recall can be raised by remote structural fold search and by protein language models trained under chronological splits, which would also allow a homology split that constrains every channel. Stage 2B models steady-state flux on specified media over reactions in the sanitized universal network, and Stage 2A enforces anatomy over the $2{,}715$ GO terms that currently carry Uberon, PO, or CL logical definitions; condition-specific expression priors, transporter substrate prediction, and additional cross-ontology definitions would widen both. For Stage 3, a larger anomaly benchmark that includes complex and essential-function cases, evaluated leave-clade-out against supervised classifiers on the same tool features, would establish where guarded reasoning adds to deterministic inference, and heterologous expression of top-ranked candidates would turn the highest-confidence gap fills into tested hypotheses.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and four-stage architecture}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ denote the protein-coding genes of a target genome with NCBI Taxonomy identifier $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and let $\mathcal{K} = \{(c_i, s_i, e_i, \zeta_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ denote their genomic coordinates (contig $c_i$, start $s_i$, end $e_i$, and strand $\zeta_i \in \{+, -\}$). Let $\mathcal{T}$ denote the Gene Ontology (GO) directed acyclic graph under the true-path relation $t \sqsubseteq a$ (\texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of}), with ancestor closure $\text{Anc}(t)$, $\mathcal{M}$ the universal metabolic reaction network with stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and reaction--EC--GO mappings, and $\mathcal{O}$ the formal taxonomic and cross-ontology anatomical axiom set. GSPA estimates a proteome-wide posterior probability matrix $\mathbf{P} \in [0, 1]^{N \times |\mathcal{T}|}$ (and corresponding EC probabilities in enzyme benchmarks) conditioned jointly on $\mathcal{K}$, $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, $\mathcal{M}$, and $\mathcal{O}$ across four sequential stages (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): Stage 0 calibrates and pools per-protein evidence channels; Stage 1 performs iterative whole-proteome Bayesian log-odds refinement; Stage 2A applies deterministic symbolic constraint enforcement with semantic ancestor relaxation while Stage 2B performs positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) and competitive gene-locus attribution; and Stage 3 applies selective tool-mediated neuro-symbolic reasoning verified by a symbolic ontology guardrail. Supplementary Table~S1 lists every benchmark, its cohort, Stage 0 channels, active stages and command-line options, comparators, result files, and regeneration pipeline.

\subsection{Stage 0: multi-channel evidence calibration and correlation-grouped Noisy-OR pooling}

Stage 0 ingests functional claims from either a standalone predictor (such as CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, or TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}) or up to seven complementary per-protein channels $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$: (1) DIAMOND v2.1.8 sequence alignment against experimentally annotated Swiss-Prot \citep{buchfink2021sensitive}; (2) HMMER 3.4 profile HMM search against Pfam-A mapped via Pfam2GO \citep{eddy2011accelerated}; (3) InterProScan v5.68-100.0 signature integration \citep{jones2014interproscan}; (4) ESM-2 (650M or 3B) embedding $k$-nearest-neighbor and multilayer perceptron transfer \citep{lin2023evolutionary}; (5) ProtT5-XL-U50 embedding $k$-nearest-neighbor transfer \citep{elnaggar2021prottrans}; (6) neural sequence classifiers (ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, 16-kernel DeepGOCNN and DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, DeepGOWeb \citep{kulmanov2021deepgoweb}, and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}); and (7) structural similarity transfer via FoldSeek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and ProstT5 3Di structural tokens \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}.

Each raw claim $c = (p, f, s(c), e(c))$ with raw score $s(c) \in [0, 1]$ is mapped to a calibrated probability via Platt logistic scaling, $\tilde{q}(c) = \text{clip}\bigl(\sigma_{\text{log}}(a_{e(c)}\, s(c) + b_{e(c)}),\, \varepsilon_{\text{cal}},\, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\bigr)$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}} = 10^{-4}$, and weighted by channel reliability $r_{e(c)} \in (0, 1]$ to give $q(c) = r_{e(c)}\, \tilde{q}(c)$ (channel parameters and reliabilities in Supplementary Section~S1). To prevent double-counting correlated evidence sources, claims for each pair $(p, f)$ are partitioned into correlation groups $\mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)$ by evidence modality (homology, protein-sequence ML, structure, genomic context, genomic language models, inferred context, sequence motifs, localization, region features, and viral predictors, plus one group per domain-specific predictor; Supplementary Section~S1). Stage 0 takes the maximum weighted probability within each group (with an optional second-opinion weight $\beta_{\text{so}} = 0.0$ by default) and pools across independent groups via Noisy-OR:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    q_g(p, f) &= \text{clip}\Bigl(\max_{c \in g} \bigl(r_{e(c)} \tilde{q}(c)\bigr) + \beta_{\text{so}} \, q_{g,(2)}(p, f), \, 10^{-3}, \, 0.999\Bigr), \\
    P_0(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(1 - \prod_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)} \bigl(1 - q_g(p, f)\bigr), \, 10^{-6}, \, 1 - 10^{-6}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
Stage 0 converts $P_0(p, f)$ into the anchored log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f) = \text{clip}\bigl(\log \frac{P_0(p, f)}{1 - P_0(p, f)}, -12, 12\bigr)$ and propagates probabilities upward along GO \texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of} edges.

\subsection{Stage 1: whole-proteome iterative Bayesian log-odds refinement}

Stage 1 refines the proteome-wide probability matrix $\mathbf{P}^{(k)}$ over iterations $k = 1, \dots, K_{\max}$ by adding six genome-scale prior log-odds shifts $B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr)$ to the fixed Stage 0 anchor $L^{(0)}(p, f)$ under Jacobi damping:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(L^{(0)}(p, f) + \sum_{m=1}^{6} w_m \, B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr), \, [-12, 12]\right), \\
    L^{(k)}(p, f) &= (1 - \gamma_{\text{iter}}) L^{(k-1)}(p, f) + \gamma_{\text{iter}} \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f), \qquad P^{(k)}(p, f) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(k)}(p, f)\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
with unit module weights $w_m = 1.0$, damping factor $\gamma_{\text{iter}} = 0.50$, and maximum iterations $K_{\max} = 6$. Iteration terminates when the mean absolute probability change across updated entries falls below $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-3}$, and reverts to the last stable iterate if the mean change increases over two consecutive steps. The default values of all 18 configurable Stage 1 and Stage 2B hyperparameters (Supplementary Section~S2 and Supplementary Table~S3) were applied unchanged to every genome within each reported experiment. The six priors are defined as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Essentiality prior ($B_1$, $\alpha_{\text{ess}} = 1.50$):} Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the domain-specific core essential GO profile and $\mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ the essential categories with $\max_p P^{(0)}(p, e) \le 0.50$. Every claim on a term in the subclass closure of $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}$ receives a specificity-weighted boost of at most $0.30\,\alpha_{\text{ess}}$, and for each uncovered category the single highest-ranked borderline candidate (likelihood in $[0.16, 0.50]$) receives a rescue floor $B_1(p, f) = \max\bigl(1.30\,\alpha_{\text{ess}},\, 0.14 - L^{(0)}(p, f)\bigr)$ that lifts it to the decision threshold; no other claim is boosted.
  \item \textit{Pathway and heteromeric complex coherence prior ($B_2$, $\alpha_{\text{coh}} = 1.00$):} Missing steps of active GO \texttt{has\_part} processes receive $w_{\text{proc}}(f) = \max(0.25, s_{\text{proc}}/n_{\text{proc}})$, the fraction of satisfied steps, and missing steps of triggered reference pathways $W$ with step coverage $c_W \ge 0.15$ receive $w_{\text{path}}(f) = \max_{W \ni f}\bigl(0.35 + 0.65\,c_W\bigr)$. For an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ with $N_{\text{strong}}(f)$ proteins at $P^{(0)} > 0.50$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f)$ further candidates at $\max(P^{(0)}, P^{(k-1)}) \ge 0.08$, candidate partners receive $w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f) = 0.85$ when $N_{\text{strong}} = 1 \wedge N_{\text{cand}} \ge 1$ (and $0.5525$ when $N_{\text{strong}} \ge 2$), whereas an isolated strong singleton ($N_{\text{strong}} = 1 \wedge N_{\text{cand}} = 0$) receives $-0.75$. The pathway and process terms act, like $B_1$, through a specificity-weighted boost and a rescue floor $\max\bigl(1.80\,\alpha_{\text{coh}}\,w,\, 0.14 - L^{(0)}\bigr)$ on the highest-ranked borderline candidate (likelihood in $[0.18, 0.50]$) of each missing step, so that $B_2(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{coh}}\bigl(\max(w_{\text{proc}}(f), w_{\text{path}}(f))\bigr)$ applies to that candidate and $\alpha_{\text{coh}}\,w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f)$ to every complex candidate.
  \item \textit{Taxonomic consistency prior ($B_3$, $\alpha_{\text{cons}} = 0.68$):} Active terms $\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)} = \{t \in \mathcal{T} : \max_p P^{(k-1)}(p, t) > 0.50\}$ are checked against direct GO taxon constraints and SAT4J unsatisfiable cores over multi-parent \texttt{is\_a} and disjointness axioms. Any claim whose term or ancestor lies in the conflict set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)}$ receives $B_3(p, f) = -\alpha_{\text{cons}}$.
  \item \textit{Anatomical prior ($B_4$, $\alpha_{\text{anat}} = 0.68$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}} = 0.65$):} Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ denote GO terms forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ through the GO--Uberon/PO/CL bridge (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}) and $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ valid non-root anatomical structures supported by at least two proteins at $P^{(0)} \ge 0.50$. Claims intersecting $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ receive $B_4(p, f) = -0.68$; valid claims linked to $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ receive $+0.65$.
  \item \textit{Metabolic gap-filling and reaction-local genomic context prior ($B_5$, $\alpha_{\text{gap}} = 1.50$):} For each open metabolic network gap, the highest-ranked borderline candidate (likelihood in $[0.18, 0.50]$) for the gap's term receives $B_5(p, f) = \max\bigl(1.15\,\mu,\, 0.14 - L^{(0)}(p, f)\bigr)$ with $\mu = \alpha_{\text{gap}}$ ($0.7\,\alpha_{\text{gap}}$ for low-homology gapseq proposals), and all candidates for gap terms receive a specificity-weighted boost of at most $0.35\,\alpha_{\text{gap}}$. For each gap reaction $R$, reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) identifies pathway-neighboring GO terms at 1--2 reaction hops ($\alpha_{\text{rxn}} = 0.50$), weights chromosomal anchor genes $p'$ with $P(p', g) \ge 0.30$ by $w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) = \max_g \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} P(p', g)$, and accumulates Gaussian kernel density $K_{\text{bp}}(\Delta\text{bp}) = \exp(-\Delta\text{bp}^2 / (2h_{\text{bp}}^2))$ ($h_{\text{bp}} = 5{,}000$~bp):
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \sum_{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 4 h_{\text{bp}}} w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) \, K_{\text{bp}}\bigl(\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')\bigr), \\
      S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \log D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) + \beta_{\text{div}} \text{Div}(p) - \gamma_{\text{com}} \text{Commit}(p) - \delta_{\text{self}} P(p, f_R) + \varepsilon_{\text{str}} \text{Strand}(p) - \zeta_{\text{gap}} \log_{10}\!\bigl(\max(1, \Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p))\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rlgc}
  \end{equation}
  with neighbor-reaction diversity weight $\beta_{\text{div}} = 1.0$, commitment penalty $\gamma_{\text{com}} = 1.0$, self-annotation penalty $\delta_{\text{self}} = 2.0$, same-strand bonus $\varepsilon_{\text{str}} = 0.2$, and minimum-distance penalty $\zeta_{\text{gap}} = 0.1$. In LRO enzyme attribution, $S_{\text{RLGC}}$ is combined with orthogroup co-occurrence, Pfam domain compatibility, and (in Track B) Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction-centroid cosine similarity.
  \item \textit{Co-directional operon prior ($B_6$, $\alpha_{\text{ctx}} = 1.00$, $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}} = 1.50$, $w_{\text{op\_cplx}} = 0.95$):} Within a co-directional operon $O$ ($|O| \ge 2$) having active pathway fraction $c_O(W) = k_O(W)/|O|$ for pathway $W$, a candidate $(p, f)$ receives $B_6(p, f) = \max_{W \ni f : k_O(W) \ge 1} \bigl(\alpha_{\text{ctx}} c_O(W) \alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}}^{\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}]}\bigr) + w_{\text{op\_cplx}}\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}} \wedge \exists p' \in O \setminus \{p\} : P^{(k-1)}(p', f) > 0.50]$.
\end{enumerate}
The pathway-consensus part of $B_6$ acts in the same targeted form (rescue floor $\max(1.65\,\alpha_{\text{ctx}}, 0.14 - L^{(0)})$ on the highest-ranked borderline candidate of each operon-supported pathway, specificity boost of at most $0.38\,\alpha_{\text{ctx}}$), while its complex-partner term applies to every co-operonic candidate. The rescue floors, their candidate windows, and the ranking rule are listed with their source modules in Supplementary Section~S2.

\subsection{Cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical constraint bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}

To enforce anatomical and taxonomic consistency across domains, we extracted five programmatic constraint tables from the official OBO Foundry releases of GO, Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon}, Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, and NCBI Taxonomy (Supplementary Section~S3 and Supplementary Table~S7): (1) $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon constraints ($13{,}819$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $13{,}092$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across $18{,}159$ GO terms \citep{deegan2010formalization}; (2) $2{,}715$ cross-ontology equivalence and subclass axioms linking $2{,}674$ GO terms to $1{,}294$ Uberon, PO, and CL classes across $30$ relations (including \texttt{results\_in\_development\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_morphogenesis\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_formation\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_maturation\_of}, and \texttt{occurs\_in}); (3) $8{,}331$ taxon constraints ($4{,}767$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $3{,}564$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) over $1{,}247$ Uberon, PO, and CL anatomical classes; (4) $7{,}722$ transitive \texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of} edges over $2{,}855$ anatomical classes; and (5) $1{,}818$ taxonomic lineage edges over $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades. A GO term $t$ is entailed as anatomically forbidden in taxon $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ whenever $t$ (or a GO ancestor of $t$) links to an anatomical class $u$ whose anatomical ancestor closure carries an \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} or \texttt{never\_in\_taxon} rule incompatible with the taxonomic lineage of $\tau_{\text{tax}}$.

\subsection{Stage 2A: deterministic symbolic enforcement and semantic ancestor relaxation}

After Stage 1 convergence, Stage 2A deterministically enforces all direct GO taxon constraints, cross-ontology anatomical constraints, and SAT4J logical consistency axioms prior to final upward GO ancestor closure, ensuring that clade-incompatible leaf claims are resolved before parent terms are populated. For each protein $p$ and forbidden term $t$, Stage 2A traverses $\text{Anc}(t)$ (excluding the three GO aspect roots) to identify the minimal valid ancestor set $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ comprising the most specific ancestors of $t$ that are both taxonomically and anatomically admissible in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$. Reconstructing the unpenalized pre-conflict probability $P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)$ from $L^{(0)}(p, t)$ and positive Stage 1 priors, semantic ancestor relaxation transfers this support to each minimal valid ancestor and zeroes the forbidden term:
\begin{align}
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a) &= \max\Bigl(P^{(K)}(p, a), \, \gamma_{\text{relax}} \, P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)\Bigr), \qquad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}}), \nonumber \\
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) &= 0,
  \label{eq:relax}
\end{align}
with $\gamma_{\text{relax}} = 1.0$, followed by upward true-path ancestor closure over admissible terms. In addition, for any obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ with a single protein at $P \ge 0.50$ and zero partner candidates at $P \ge 0.08$ across the genome, Stage 2A attenuates the isolated singleton to $0.45$.

\subsection{Stage 2B: role-aware reaction support and positive-cost lexicographic pFBA}
\label{sec:methods_fba}

Stage 2B is implemented as a Python module (\texttt{stoichiometric\_locus\_refiner.py}, SciPy with the HiGHS solver) that the JVM pipeline invokes, with its ModelSEED-derived matrices distributed as a separate data bundle. It operates over a sanitized universal metabolic network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ derived from ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed} ($47{,}880$ reactions and $33{,}978$ metabolites, of which $14{,}243$ carry EC mappings and $11{,}826$ are mass- and charge-balanced enzymatic reactions; $2{,}417$ pool or elementally imbalanced reactions receive penalty $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100.0$). Each reaction $j$ is associated with a disjunctive normal form (DNF) catalytic rule $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j) = \{m_1, \dots, m_{K_j}\}$ over alternative isozyme mechanisms $m$, where each mechanism requires a conjunction of subunit roles $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m) = \{u_1, \dots, u_{D_m}\}$. Given candidate locus probabilities $s_{p, u} \in (0, 1)$, Stage 2B aggregates the top $M_{\text{iso}} = 3$ candidates per role via Noisy-OR, combines required subunits via a bottleneck-gated geometric mean at $\theta_{\text{sub}} = 0.25$, and takes the maximum across isozyme mechanisms:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q(u) &= 1 - \prod\limits_{p \in \text{Top-}M_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u))} \bigl(1 - s_{p, u}\bigr), \\
    S_{\text{mech}}(m) &= \begin{cases}
      \Bigl(\prod\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u)\Bigr)^{1 / D_m}, & \!\min\limits_{u} Q(u) \ge \theta_{\text{sub}}, \\
      \min\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u), & \!\text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases} \qquad S_{\text{rxn}}(j) = \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)} S_{\text{mech}}(m).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gpr_agg}
\end{equation}

Reactions with $S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge \tau_{\text{core}} = 0.50$, together with open medium exchanges and spontaneous reactions, form the draft core $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$. Candidate repair reactions $j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$ receive a strictly positive addition cost $c_j^{\text{add}} = \lambda_{\text{add}}(1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j)) + w_{\text{imb}}(j) + \epsilon_{\text{par}}$ ($\lambda_{\text{add}} = 1.0$, $\epsilon_{\text{par}} = 10^{-3}$). Splitting reversible fluxes into non-negative components $v_j = v_j^+ - v_j^-$ and setting the biomass target on medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$ to $v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \max(\alpha_{\text{bio}} v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}), 0.1)$ ($\alpha_{\text{bio}} = 0.10$), Stage 2B solves a two-step lexicographic pFBA \citep{lewis2010omic} using the HiGHS linear programming solver (feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$). Step 1 minimizes the repair cost $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}}(v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ (solving first over balanced reactions and admitting penalized imbalanced reactions only if infeasible) to obtain optimal repair cost $C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$. Step 2 minimizes total non-biomass flux $\sum_{j \ne \text{bio}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}}(v_j^+ + v_j^-) \le (1 + 10^{-6})C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) + 10^{-9}$. The active repair set $\mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} : |v_j^{\text{pFBA}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})| > 10^{-6}\}$ is evaluated by flux-variability ($1.01 C^*$), reaction-knockout ($1.10 C^*$), and five-replicate cost-perturbation ($\pm 5\%$) certificates to yield the flux confidence $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 0.5\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}] + 0.3\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}] + 0.2\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in [0, 1]$ (Supplementary Section~S4 and Supplementary Table~S12).

\subsection{Stage 2B: competitive gene-locus responsibility and substrate probing}
\label{sec:methods_slr}

For each repaired reaction $j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}$ and required role $u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m_j^*)$, Stage 2B distributes locus responsibility competitively across candidate genes $p \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)$ and an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ with prior mass $\eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$:
\begin{align}
  w_{j, u}(p) &= \bigl(o_{p, u}\bigr)^{\beta_1} \, \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)^{\beta_2} \, \exp\bigl(\beta_3 C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) + \beta_4 S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)\bigr), \nonumber \\
  r_{j, u}(p) &= \frac{w_{j, u}(p)}{\eta_{\text{null}} + \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)} w_{j, u}(p')},
  \label{eq:responsibility}
\end{align}
where $o_{p, u} = s_{p, u}/(1 - s_{p, u})$ is the sequence odds, $\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)$ is the RLGC operon Bayes factor, $C_{\text{syn}}(p, u)$ is the operon and pathway synteny score, $S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)$ is the language-model similarity score, and $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = 1.0, \beta_3 = \beta_4 = 1.5$. The backward log-odds update to locus $p$ is $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}(p, u) = \gamma_{\text{FBA}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(u)} \pi_j^{\text{FBA}} r_{j, u}(p) (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j))$ with $\gamma_{\text{FBA}} = 2.20$, applied across damped outer iterations $\lambda_t = \lambda_0 \gamma_{\text{damp}}^{t-1}$ ($\lambda_0 = 0.70, \gamma_{\text{damp}} = 0.60$). For catabolic enzymes in Price-149, Stage 2B supplements minimal-glucose pFBA with cognate-substrate stoichiometric neighborhood probing (rewarding candidate reactions that share non-currency metabolites at 1--2 reaction hops with chromosomal neighbors within $\pm 10$ genes) and blends single-predictor and five-predictor ensemble posteriors (complete equations in Supplementary Section~S4). Held-out metabolic reactions are classified by five binary recoverability indicators: structural presence and mass balance in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}}$), medium-specific flux demand ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}}$, partitioning core biomass Stratum A, cognate-substrate catabolic Stratum B, and secondary Stratum C), isozyme uniqueness ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}}$), operon support ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{operon}}$), and multi-subunit partner presence ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{complex}}$).

\subsection{Stage 3: selective neuro-symbolic annotation completion}

Stage 3 is triggered selectively on four post-Stage-2 anomaly categories: (1) pathway gaps (pathway completeness $\ge 0.50$ with candidate posteriors in $[0.05, 0.50)$); (2) incomplete obligate heteromeric complexes (one active subunit at $P \ge 0.50$ and sub-threshold partner candidates); (3) borderline uncovered essential categories; and (4) borderline taxonomic or anatomical conflicts ($P \in [0.35, 0.62]$) amenable to ancestral GO relaxation. Stage 3 evaluates at most $8$ candidates per anomaly and $150$ anomalies per genome. The Stage 3 benchmark was built from the extractor's own records: for each of seven genomes (\textit{E.~coli} K-12, \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{P.~aeruginosa}, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}, \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, \textit{C.~elegans}, and \textit{A.~thaliana}) every annotated pathway step was held out in turn by deleting the protein's claims for the step's term and its descendants, the integrator was re-run with the anomaly cap lifted, and the cases in which the held-out protein re-entered the candidate list were split by hash into true positives and decoys (held-out protein removed from the genome and the operon table), matched on candidate count and maximum posterior within $\pm 0.05$; conflicts were injected as cross-clade terms at raw scores on a grid from $0.40$ to $0.85$, chosen so that their Stage 1 posteriors fell in the borderline band $[0.35, 0.50)$, into Stage 1 runs without Stage 2A, and traps are valid borderline terms from the same runs. All GO labels were taken from the GO release of 2026-03-25, and $8$ records whose target term is absent from the guardrail's GO release were excluded. Supervised baselines (logistic regression, random forest, GBDT, and MLP) were evaluated by five-fold stratified cross-validation on three pre-tool scalars or eight post-tool features, with and without the guardrail.

Each anomaly is stripped of evaluation labels and gene symbols and processed in a bounded multi-turn tool-calling loop (maximum $6$ turns; $2$ turns in the fast mode) by a locally hosted FP8-quantized open-weight instruction model (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}) served through vLLM on two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs ($131{,}072$-token context, maximum $450$ output tokens per turn; seed $42$ at temperature $0.0$ and seeds $43$ and $44$ at temperature $0.25$). The model invokes five deterministic read-only tools to inspect the operon neighborhood, inspect the pathway or complex gap, query protein evidence and domain signatures, check symbolic ontology constraints, and propose a structured resolution (\textsc{promote}, \textsc{relax}, \textsc{demote}, or \textsc{abstain}). Every proposal is verified by the deterministic symbolic guardrail against the $48{,}101$ GO term stanzas, $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades, $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon rules, and $2{,}715$ GO--Uberon/PO/CL axioms before acceptance (tool schemas, de-identification protocol, per-seed results, and the trigger-rate computation in Supplementary Section~S6 and Supplementary Tables~S14, S20, and S21).

\subsection{Experimental phenotype prediction in BacDive}
\label{sec:methods_bacdive}

We evaluated organism-level phenotype prediction on the two comparison tables published with gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}: $2{,}332$ ProTraits carbon-source utilization assays ($791$ strains, $50$ substrates; all rows retained) and $17{,}894$ BacDive enzyme assays \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac}, of which we retained the $17{,}496$ assays ($3{,}164$ strains) with a definitive activity ($+$ or $-$) and calls from all three reconstructions, and on $50{,}075$ strain--EC pairs for $10$ diagnostic enzymes from per-EC confusion matrices. Binary pathway or reaction calls $g(s, y), m(s, y), c(s, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ of gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, and CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast} for strain $s$ and assay $y$ were taken from the comparison tables published with gapseq. We form the blended matrix $B(s, y) = 0.50g + 0.32m + 0.18c$ (carbon; $0.52g + 0.30m + 0.18c$ for enzymes), column-center each assay $\tilde{B}_{s, y} = B(s, y) - \bar{B}_y$, and compute the row-normalized non-negative cosine coupling matrix $\Psi(y, y')$ with zero diagonal to obtain the unsupervised cross-assay coupling score $\psi(s, y) = \sum_{y'} \Psi(y, y')(B(s, y') - 0.5)$. In Track 1, which uses no phenotype labels, binary calls are mapped to fixed probabilities ($p_{\text{gap}} = 0.80/0.22$, $p_{\text{ms}} = 0.78/0.24$, $p_{\text{cm}} = 0.68/0.34$ for positive/negative calls), combined with the Laplace-smoothed mean blended call $p_{\text{peer}}$ of the other strains of the same genus, and pooled in log-odds space:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\!\Bigl(w_{\text{gap}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{gap}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{ms}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{ms}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{cm}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{cm}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{peer}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{peer}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_\psi\,\psi(s, y)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:bacdive_t1}
\end{equation}
with weights $(w_{\text{gap}}, w_{\text{ms}}, w_{\text{cm}}, w_{\text{peer}}, w_\psi) = (0.42, 0.26, 0.12, 0.24, 0.85)$ for carbon and $(0.42, 0.26, 0.12, 0.22, 0.85)$ for enzymes at threshold $0.50$; these weights were fixed by hand, without a tuning set separate from the evaluated assays. Supervised baselines were re-implemented as an $L_1$-regularized linear support vector machine, a random forest, and a gradient-boosted decision tree over the three binary calls and a one-hot assay indicator, after the designs of Traitar \citep{weimann2016traitar}, BacDive-AI \citep{schober2025bac}, and DiASPora \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}; they and the GSPA + GBDT hybrid were evaluated under five-fold genus-blocked cross-validation with all calibration, prevalence, and coupling parameters estimated strictly within training folds. In Track 2, $30\%$ of each strain's assays are revealed to condition the Bayesian anchor $P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y)$ on revealed substrate co-occurrence or genus-level EC posteriors, and predictions are evaluated on the held-out $70\%$ (complete parameterization in Supplementary Section~S5 and Supplementary Table~S13).

\subsection{Benchmarks, time-based and homology-based split retraining, metrics, and structural audit score}

To evaluate prospective generalization across time and across sequence-similarity regimes on complete genome inputs, we constructed a six-split whole-genome withheld CAFA benchmark across six complete prokaryotic reference proteomes (\textit{B.~subtilis} 168, \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{H.~pylori} 26695, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{M.~jannaschii} DSM 2661; $20{,}697$ coding sequences, $3{,}315$ experimentally annotated target proteins, $1{,}060$ strictly post-$t_0$ targets, $3{,}809$ operons, and $1{,}467$ metabolic gaps) alongside a multi-clade isolated-protein CAFA benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits} and Supplementary Tables~S4 and S15). In the two whole-genome time-based splits, post-$t_0$ targets ($1{,}060$ proteins) are evaluated either with pre-$t_0$ experimental annotations on the six chromosomes retained in training to test chromosomal rollover context (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover}, $82{,}404$ training proteins) or with all $2{,}524$ experimentally annotated proteins from the six species purged from training (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld}, $79{,}880$ training proteins). In the four whole-genome homology splits, all six species are purged from training and evaluated across all $3{,}315$ experimental target proteins after additionally purging all cross-species training homologs sharing pairwise sequence identity $> \eta_{\text{id}}$ with any protein in the six withheld genomes (\texttt{WG\_Homology\_le100}: $79{,}880$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le70}: $79{,}457$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le50}: $76{,}405$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le30}: $61{,}323$ train). On the two time-based splits, we additionally evaluate the official pre-trained weights of DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure} and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}. On every split, all 14 trainable per-protein baselines (term-frequency prior, filtered DIAMOND database, $L_2$-regularized InterPro domain logistic regression, canonical 16-kernel 1D convolutional DeepGOCNN with $k \in \{8, 16, \dots, 128\} \times 512$ filters $= 8{,}192$ features, DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, 2,560-dimensional ESM-2 3B \texttt{esm2\_t36\_3B\_UR50D} $k$-NN index \citep{littmann2021embeddings}, ESM-2 3B MLP, hierarchical ESM-2 3B C-HMCNN with max-constraint loss \citep{giunchiglia2020coherent}, DeepFRI graph convolutional and language-model architecture \citep{gligorijevic2021structure}, DeepGraphGO two-layer graph convolutional network over the DIAMOND sequence-similarity graph with InterPro node features \citep{you2021deepgraphgo}, TALE+ transformer encoder with hierarchical GO label-embedding joint attention and DIAMOND fusion \citep{cao2021tale}, SPROF-GO language-model self-attention pooling with hierarchical label diffusion and homology transfer \citep{yuan2023fast}, DeepGO-SE five-model approximate semantic entailment ensemble over $\mathcal{EL}^{++}$ description-logic embeddings \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, and DPFunc InterPro domain cross-attention with graph convolutional layers \citep{ding2025dpfunc}) together with our out-of-fold GSPA-Seq meta-stacker are retrained on that split's filtered training set before running whole-genome inference across all $20{,}697$ coding sequences. In this benchmark, Stage 0 pooling and the Stage 1, 2A, and 2B updates are implemented in Python inside the benchmark script (\texttt{scripts/run\_cafa\_whole\_genome\_splits.py}), following the rules above on the same operon, gap, pathway, complex, and constraint tables, so that each split can be evaluated with its own retrained channels; all other benchmarks run the released JVM integrator. The external architectures are our re-implementations: all take ESM-2 3B embeddings as sequence input, DeepFRI uses sequence-band graph smoothing in place of contact maps, DeepGraphGO uses the DIAMOND similarity graph in place of a protein interaction network, and sequences are truncated to $256$ residues for the DeepFRI and TALE+ encoders. Stage 0 channel reliabilities are recalibrated on a $600$-protein validation set drawn from each split's training set (aligned with DIAMOND against the same training database), and a common down-weighting factor for the convolutional and ESM-2 channels is selected from $\{1.0, 0.75, 0.55, 0.38\}$ by validation $f_w$ on that split ($0.75$ on the time-based and $\le 100\%$ splits, $1.0$ otherwise). For the pan-phylum AllTheBacteria and reference eukaryote analysis ($72{,}533$ complete genomes across $11{,}632$ species and $37$ phyla \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria}; Supplementary Section~S7 and Supplementary Table~S16), coding sequences across $73$ assembly batches ($315{,}671{,}487$ proteins) were deduplicated by SHA-256 sequence hashing ($73{,}866{,}871$ unique sequences) and clustered via \texttt{MMseqs2 linclust} \citep{steinegger2018clustering} at $90\%$ sequence identity and $80\%$ coverage ($36{,}804{,}001$ non-redundant cluster representatives), and aligned with DIAMOND (\texttt{--sensitive}, $k = 25$, $E \le 10^{-3}$) against the Swiss-Prot training set to obtain the per-protein maximum identity reported in Section~\ref{sec:results_allthebacteria}. The batch pipeline then scores each representative with DIAMOND transfer, Swiss-Prot-derived domain annotations, and the convolutional and ESM-2 heads of GSPA-Seq, the latter with embeddings interpolated from alignment hits (no ESM-2 forward pass was run for these proteins, and proteins without a hit receive no embedding channel), and writes one merged claim per protein--term pair; the released integrator (\texttt{gspa-2.1.0.jar integrate --batch-manifest}, six priors, Stage 2A relaxation) was run on these claims for $7{,}009$ species-representative genomes and $4{,}592$ further genomes, and rescued and suppressed protein--term pairs were counted by comparing the Stage 0 and final posteriors at $0.50$ (Supplementary Section~S7). Across whole-proteome benchmarks, donor-homology masking removes reference proteins sharing $\text{pident}(g, r) > \eta_{\text{id}}$ ($\eta_{\text{id}} \in \{100\%, 70\%, 50\%, 30\%\}$) or belonging to the test organism's genus or family across DIAMOND databases, ESM-2 and ProtT5 neighbor indices, FoldSeek structural templates, and calibration sets. In leave-reaction-out benchmarks, Track A masks sequence alignments and embedding donors with cosine similarity $> 0.50$ without reaction centroids, while Track B masks sequence alignments and retains Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids.

We report official CAFA information-accretion-weighted $f_w$ ($WF_{\max}$), protein-centric unweighted $F_{\max}$, pair-level micro $F_{\max}$, and minimum semantic distance $S_{\min}$ \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,clark2013information} over threshold grids $\theta \in \{0.01, \dots, 0.99\}$ ($\{0.10, \dots, 0.90\}$ in the eukaryotic stress test), alongside Hit@$k$ and mean reciprocal rank (MRR) for candidate ranking, $95\%$ paired bootstrap confidence intervals over proteomes, and two-sided exact sign tests over genomes or exact binomial McNemar tests over paired cases (Supplementary Section~S5). All CPU benchmarks ran on dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 nodes (8 threads per genome), with PLM, 16-kernel DeepGOCNN, ESM-2 3B, external SOTA GO baseline retraining, executed on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB, NVIDIA H100-80GB, and NVIDIA H200-140GB GPUs, and Stage 3 language-model inference on two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs.

The Genome-Scale Annotation Evaluation Framework (GAEF) summarizes structural proteome invariants at $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$ by combining essential function completeness $C_{\text{ess}}$ ($w_{\text{ess}} = 0.30$), functional pathway/complex/process coherence $C_{\text{func}}$ ($w_{\text{func}} = 0.40$), taxonomic and anatomical consistency $C_{\text{cons}} = \max(0, 1 - 0.01 V(\mathcal{P}))$ ($w_{\text{cons}} = 0.30$), and functional resolution $C_{\text{res}} = \min(1, \text{meanIC}/10)$ ($w_{\text{res}} = 0.0$):
\begin{equation}
  \text{GAEF}(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{\sum_{d \in \{\text{ess}, \text{func}, \text{cons}, \text{res}\} : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d \, C_d}{\sum_{d : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d},
  \label{eq:gaef}
\end{equation}
which equals $0.30 C_{\text{ess}} + 0.40 C_{\text{func}} + 0.30 C_{\text{cons}}$ when all three structural dimensions are active. Because GAEF measures the structural invariants targeted by Stages 1 and 2, we report it as an internal structural audit score alongside independent held-out functional and experimental phenotype evaluations.
