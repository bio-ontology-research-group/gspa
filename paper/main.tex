\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Protein function prediction methods typically score sequences or structures one protein at a time, whereas long-read sequencing and modern genome assembly now routinely recover complete chromosomes that expose operon organization, macromolecular complex stoichiometry, metabolic network connectivity, and clade-specific anatomical constraints across the entire proteome. Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA), a four-stage framework that calibrates and pools complementary per-protein predictors (including our per-protein meta-integrator, GSPA-Seq), refines proteome-wide posteriors across co-directional operons, essential functions, pathways, obligate heteromeric complexes, and pre-propagation taxonomic and anatomical ontology axioms, and couples reaction-local genomic context with positive-cost parsimonious flux balance analysis, competitive gene-locus attribution, and selective ontology-guarded language-model reasoning. In a whole-genome Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA) evaluation that withholds six complete reference genomes ($20{,}697$ coding sequences) across prospective time-based splits and sequence-similarity homology splits ($\le 100\%$, $\le 70\%$, $\le 50\%$, and $\le 30\%$ identity) and compares against both official pre-trained and from-scratch retrained external predictors (DeepFRI, DeepGraphGO, TALE+, SPROF-GO, DeepGO-SE, DPFunc, 16-kernel DeepGOCNN/DeepGOPlus, and 3-billion-parameter ESM-2 3B architectures), multi-modal per-protein integration in GSPA-Seq outperforms every external baseline, and whole-genome inference in GSPA improves information-accretion-weighted $f_w$ and $F_{\max}$ across all six splits ($f_w = 0.4996$, $F_{\max} = 0.5490$ on prospective targets with pre-$t_0$ chromosomal context, a $+0.0541$ $f_w$ gain over completely withheld chromosomes at $0.4455$; and $f_w = 0.5055$, $F_{\max} = 0.5272$ at $\le 30\%$ identity, where sequence alignment falls to $f_w = 0.1163$ and retrained external baselines reach $0.2702$--$0.3509$). Operon context, pathway and complex coherence, flux-certified metabolic gap-filling, and pre-propagation ontology constraint enforcement each contribute positively to CAFA accuracy while eliminating $100\%$ of taxonomic and anatomical contradictions. Across bacteria, archaea, viruses, and eukaryotes, semantic ancestor relaxation outperforms term deletion in multicellular animals and vascular plants, operon-coupled complex coherence raises recovery of held-out heteromeric complex subunits (Hit@1) from $56.0\%$ to $93.7\%$, positive-cost stoichiometric refinement eliminates the $83.7\%$ zero-flux reaction padding of negative-cost gap-filling and improves identification of fitness-validated bacterial enzymes and experimental BacDive phenotypes across unseen genera, and selective guarded reasoning on fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins raises top-ranked recovery of ambiguous missing pathway enzymes from $34.0\%$ to $82.7\%$. Applied to $72{,}533$ complete genomes from AllTheBacteria ($292.1$~million coding sequences across $11{,}632$ species and $37$ phyla), GSPA rescues $42.9\%$ ($51.4$~million) of sequence dark-matter proteins ($< 30\%$ identity, $76.4\%$ lacking any detectable BLAST alignment) and $77.0\%$ ($60.7$~million) of twilight-zone proteins while eliminating $2.44$~billion cross-kingdom ontology violations, revealing a genome-size bifurcation between drift-driven core dark matter in streamlined bacteria and a $136.4$-fold metabolic gap surge in large environmental genomes, separating $12.51$~million obligate complex subunits from $1.43$~million domain-reuse singletons, and mapping non-orthologous gene displacement across bacterial pathways and complexes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Automated protein function annotation assigns biological roles to the protein-coding genes of an organism and forms the basis of comparative genomics, metabolic modeling, and systems biology \citep{radivojac2013large,zhou2019cafa}. We consider the genome-scale function inference problem in which the input consists of the complete set of protein-coding genes $\mathcal{P}=\{p_1,\dots,p_N\}$ of an organism, their chromosomal coordinates and strand organization $\mathcal{K}$, the organism's taxonomic lineage $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and reference biochemical and ontological knowledge bases, and the output is a joint assignment of Gene Ontology (GO) terms \citep{thomas2019gene} and Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers across $\mathcal{P}$. Because the proteins encoded by a genome evolve and act together within a single cell or organism, a valid joint assignment satisfies structural invariants that span many proteins simultaneously: core cellular functions required in the organism's domain of life are represented, metabolic pathways form stoichiometrically connected routes that support biomass synthesis \citep{osterman2003missing,karp2018how}, obligate heteromeric complexes are annotated with their complete partner subunit sets \citep{giurgiu2019corum,meldal2022complexportal}, and assigned terms respect both the organism's taxonomic lineage and the anatomical body plan of its clade \citep{deegan2010formalization,mungall2012uberon}. These organism-level invariants link the function of each protein to the architecture of the complete genome.

Formulating function prediction over complete genomes is timely because chromosome-scale assemblies are now broadly accessible. Historically, short-read sequencing of microbial isolates and metagenomes yielded fragmented contigs from which open reading frames were predicted and annotated as isolated sequences, severing operons, biosynthetic gene clusters, and chromosome-wide reaction inventories. Advances in long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and repeat-graph genome and metagenome assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye} routinely recover complete or near-complete chromosomes for both cultivated isolates and metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) of uncultured lineages. Chromosomal gene order, co-directional operons, and complete proteome complements are therefore standard inputs that function prediction methods can exploit directly.

Standard function predictors derive representations of an individual protein sequence or structure and transfer annotations from reference databases. Sequence alignment transfers annotations from homologous sequences \citep{buchfink2021sensitive,li2021refseq,schwengers2021bakta}; profile hidden Markov models and domain signatures identify conserved functional motifs \citep{eddy2011accelerated,jones2014interproscan}; deep convolutional, transformer, and graph neural networks capture sequence motifs, domain graphs, and GO label hierarchies \citep{kulmanov2018deepgo,kulmanov2020deepgoplus,you2021deepgraphgo,cao2021tale}; protein language models and semantic entailment embeddings project sequences into representation spaces where functionally related proteins cluster \citep{lin2023evolutionary,elnaggar2021prottrans,yuan2023fast,kulmanov2024deepgose,yu2023enzyme}; and structural graph networks transfer functions across remote fold similarities \citep{gligorijevic2021structure,vankempen2024foldseek,ding2025dpfunc}. Because these modalities capture complementary biochemical properties, multi-source ensembles such as GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}, as well as our per-protein multi-modal stacker GSPA-Seq, achieve strong protein-centric accuracy in Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA) benchmarks \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,zhou2019cafa}. However, scoring each protein $p_i$ in isolation leaves four sources of genome-scale information unused: co-transcribed operon neighbors that share macromolecular complexes or metabolic pathways, partially annotated chromosomes where previously characterized genes can anchor newly studied neighbors, proteome-wide stoichiometric and complex completeness requirements, and clade-specific taxonomic and anatomical constraints that can prevent false-positive leaf predictions from propagating upward through the GO hierarchy.

Comparative genomics, constraint-based metabolic modeling, and formal bio-ontologies each address part of this organism-level context. On the chromosome, conserved gene order, co-directional operons, and genomic language models \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,hwang2024genomic,cornman2024omg,toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised} capture local synteny within contiguous gene windows, while domain-essential profiles and dispersed complex stoichiometry act across the full proteome. In systems biology, metabolic reconstruction pipelines including Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, and GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind} identify reactions required for steady-state flux, and our earlier method METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} coupled metabolic gap-filling with neural enzyme prediction via reaction-indicator mixed-integer linear programming (MILP). Inspection of reaction-indicator MILP reveals two mathematical properties that limit gene-level precision: negative objective costs on candidate reactions above an addition threshold reward the solver for adding reactions with zero steady-state flux, and uniform reaction-to-gene boosting elevates paralogs equally without modeling operon neighborhoods, subunit roles, or competition among candidate loci. In bio-ontologies, GO taxon rules \citep{deegan2010formalization} and cross-ontology axioms linking GO processes to the Uberon anatomy ontology \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon}, the Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, and the Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell} define which structures and processes can exist in a clade; enforcing these rules before upward GO propagation and relaxing forbidden terms to their nearest valid ancestors preserves conserved ancestral functions while preventing cross-clade errors. Finally, for the small fraction of loci that remain ambiguous after deterministic probabilistic and stoichiometric refinement, tool-using language-model agents \citep{wang2025_geneagent,caufield2024_spires} can synthesize heterogeneous domain, operon, and pathway evidence when verified by a symbolic ontology guardrail.

Stage 3 applies selective tool-mediated language-model reasoning to fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail.}
  \label{fig:architecture}

Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA), a four-stage framework that unifies per-protein representation learning with whole-genome probabilistic, stoichiometric, and ontological inference (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). First, Stage 0 (together with our per-protein multi-modal integrator GSPA-Seq) calibrates and pools sequence homology, domain signatures, 16-kernel 1D convolutional networks, and 3-billion-parameter protein language models (\texttt{ESM-2 3B}) to establish a strong per-protein prior. Second, Stage 1 and Stage 2A exploit whole-genome organization: Stage 1 iteratively updates proteome-wide log-odds across essential functions ($B_1$), pathways and obligate heteromeric complexes ($B_2$), taxonomic consistency ($B_3$), cross-ontology anatomy ($B_4$), metabolic gaps ($B_5$), and co-directional operons ($B_6$), and Stage 2A enforces taxonomic and anatomical constraints prior to upward GO propagation while relaxing clade-incompatible terms to their nearest valid ancestors. Third, Stage 1, Stage 2B, and Stage 3 extend inference beyond sequence homology: Stage 1 localizes missing pathway enzymes via reaction-local genomic context (RLGC), Stage 2B couples role-aware disjunctive normal form (DNF) aggregation of isozymes and subunits with positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) \citep{lewis2010omic} and competitive locus attribution against an unassigned-gene hypothesis, and Stage 3 resolves residual ambiguities on fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins using a tool-calling language model verified by a symbolic ontology guardrail. Across eight benchmark suites spanning bacteria, archaea, viruses, and eukaryotes (Supplementary Table~S1) and a pan-phylum application to $72{,}533$ complete genomes from AllTheBacteria \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria}, we test six hypotheses: (i) whether withholding complete genomes and conditioning on chromosomal operons, pathway and complex coherence, metabolic gap-filling and FBA, and pre-propagation ontology constraints improves protein-centric CAFA accuracy over pre-trained and retrained external baselines across both time-based and homology-based splits; (ii) whether whole-genome priors improve organism-level structural coherence while preserving protein-level accuracy; (iii) whether operon architecture and cross-ontology axioms complete heteromeric complexes and repair eukaryotic anatomical errors; (iv) whether positive-cost flux modeling, competitive locus attribution, and pathway coupling eliminate zero-flux reaction padding and improve fitness-validated enzyme and experimental phenotype prediction; (v) when selective guarded language-model reasoning complements deterministic and supervised inference; and (vi) what macro-evolutionary principles govern sequence dark matter, genome-size scaling, heteromeric complex stoichiometry, and non-orthologous gene displacement across bacterial phyla. GSPA v2.1.0, precomputed ontology bridges, and benchmark workflows are available at \url{https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa}.

We first asked whether calibrating and pooling complementary per-protein predictors yields a stronger baseline annotation than individual predictors and production genome annotation pipelines when evaluated under an identical ancestor-propagated protocol. We compared GSPA against its individual sequence alignment, domain profile, protein language model, and structural similarity channels, against NCBI PGAP \citep{li2021refseq}, InterProScan \citep{jones2014interproscan}, and metagenomic-deepFRI \citep{bezshapkin2026scaling}, and against the genomic language models gLM and gLM2 \citep{hwang2024genomic,cornman2024omg} across 13-genome and 21-genome reference panels (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d, Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, and Supplementary Table~S2), with all pipeline hyperparameters calibrated on \textit{Escherichia coli} K-12 and held frozen across all out-of-sample species (Supplementary Table~S3). Across the 11 prokaryotic proteomes of the 13-genome panel, GSPA with three sequence and domain channels reaches a micro $F_{\max}$ of $0.8421$, exceeding NCBI PGAP ($0.7820$). Extending Stage 0 to all seven neural, language-model, and structural channels on the 21-genome panel raises micro $F_{\max}$ to $0.8705$, outperforming the strongest single channel, ESM-2 nearest-neighbor transfer ($0.8580$). Combining complementary sequence and structural representations establishes a higher-accuracy starting distribution than any individual predictor, and we next tested whether withholding complete genomes and conditioning on whole-genome architecture improves CAFA prediction accuracy over pre-trained and retrained external baselines across both prospective time-based splits and sequence-similarity homology splits.

To test the central hypothesis that whole-genome inputs improve protein-centric CAFA function prediction, we designed a whole-genome withheld CAFA evaluation across six complete prokaryotic reference proteomes (\textit{B.~subtilis} 168, \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{H.~pylori} 26695, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{M.~jannaschii} DSM 2661; $20{,}697$ coding sequences, $3{,}315$ experimentally annotated target proteins, $1{,}060$ strictly post-$t_0$ newly annotated targets, $3{,}809$ co-directional operons, and $1{,}236$ metabolic gaps) spanning two prospective time-based splits and four homology-based sequence-identity splits, comparing against official pre-trained weights of DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure} and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose} on the temporal splits and retraining all 14 external and internal baseline models from scratch on each split's filtered training set (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits} and Supplementary Table~S4). On the prospective time-based split with pre-$t_0$ chromosomal rollover context (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover}, $82{,}404$ training proteins evaluated on $1{,}060$ post-$t_0$ targets across the $20{,}697$ whole-genome ORFs), official pre-trained DeepFRI and DeepGO-SE reach mean information-accretion-weighted $f_w$ values of $0.2415$ ($F_{\max} = 0.2879$) and $0.4527$ ($F_{\max} = 0.4855$), while baselines retrained from scratch on the $t_0$ corpus reach $0.3753$ for DIAMOND BLAST-$k$NN, $0.3842$ for DeepFRI ($F_{\max} = 0.4620$), $0.3939$ for 16-kernel DeepGOPlus, $0.4270$ for DeepGO-SE ($F_{\max} = 0.4984$), $0.4533$ for ESM-2 3B C-HMCNN, $0.4548$ for TALE+ \citep{cao2021tale} ($F_{\max} = 0.5202$), $0.4551$ for DeepGraphGO \citep{you2021deepgraphgo} ($F_{\max} = 0.5162$), $0.4633$ for DPFunc \citep{ding2025dpfunc} ($F_{\max} = 0.5228$), and $0.4861$ for SPROF-GO \citep{yuan2023fast} ($F_{\max} = 0.5467$). Multi-channel per-protein integration reaches $f_w = 0.4882$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5389$) in GSPA Stage 0 and $f_w = 0.4908$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5452$, $670$ ontology violations) in our per-protein integrator GSPA-Seq, while full whole-genome GSPA (Stages 0, 1, 2A, and 2B) reaches $f_w = 0.4996$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5490$, $S_{\min} = 9.63$) with zero ontology violations. When all $2{,}524$ experimentally annotated proteins from the six species are purged from the training corpus (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld}, $79{,}880$ training proteins evaluated on the identical $1{,}060$ post-$t_0$ targets), full GSPA again leads all retrained methods at $f_w = 0.4455$ ($F_{\max} = 0.4883$, $0$ violations) compared with $0.3274$ for DeepFRI, $0.3646$ for DeepGO-SE, $0.3917$ for DPFunc, $0.3959$ for TALE+, $0.3976$ for SPROF-GO, $0.3980$ for DeepGraphGO, and $0.4352$ for GSPA-Seq ($1{,}219$ violations). Comparing \texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover} ($0.4996$) with \texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld} ($0.4455$) on the same $1{,}060$ post-$t_0$ proteins shows that pre-$t_0$ annotations on the surrounding chromosome provide a $+0.0541$ ($+12.1\%$) gain in $f_w$. We next examined whether whole-genome inference preserves this advantage when cross-species sequence homologs are progressively removed and dissected the contribution of each genome-scale component.

Under the four whole-genome homology splits, where all six genomes plus all training proteins sharing $> 100\%$, $> 70\%$, $> 50\%$, or $> 30\%$ pairwise sequence identity with any protein in the six withheld genomes are purged prior to retraining ($3{,}315$ target proteins across $20{,}697$ ORFs), DIAMOND BLAST-$k$NN degrades from $f_w = 0.3522$ at $\le 100\%$ to $0.3454$ at $\le 70\%$, $0.3001$ at $\le 50\%$, and $0.1163$ ($F_{\max} = 0.1367$) in the $\le 30\%$ twilight zone, while retrained external baselines at $\le 30\%$ identity drop to $0.2702$ for DeepFRI, $0.3282$ for DeepGO-SE, $0.3311$ for DPFunc, $0.3378$ for DeepGraphGO, $0.3435$ for SPROF-GO, and $0.3442$ for TALE+ (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}). Full whole-genome GSPA achieves the highest mean $f_w$ across every homology regime: $0.4685$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5160$) on leave-whole-genome-out \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le100}, $0.4886$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5323$) at $\le 70\%$, $0.4968$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5314$) at $\le 50\%$, and $0.5055$ ($F_{\max} = 0.5272$) at $\le 30\%$ identity, outperforming the strongest external baseline at $\le 30\%$ identity (TALE+, $f_w = 0.3442$) by $+0.1613$ $f_w$ and the per-protein GSPA-Seq integrator ($f_w = 0.4895$, $2{,}756$ violations) by $+0.0160$ $f_w$ while eliminating all $2{,}488$--$7{,}719$ ontology violations (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}; multi-clade isolated-protein evaluation in Supplementary Table~S15). Ablating each whole-genome module individually on top of GSPA-Seq confirms that all four genome-scale hypotheses contribute to CAFA accuracy through distinct GO aspects: (i) co-directional operon context ($B_6$) transfers heteromeric complex localization, high-information biological processes, and pathway steps across neighboring genes, raising mean $f_w$ by $+0.0035$ on \texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover} ($0.4908 \to 0.4943$) and $+0.0068$ at $\le 30\%$ identity ($0.4895 \to 0.4963$); (ii) pathway, complex, and essentiality coherence ($B_1, B_2$) and metabolic gap-filling with FBA locus attribution ($B_5 + \text{Stage 2B}$) rescue missing catalytic steps and subunits, raising Molecular Function $f_w$ by $+0.0048$ on \texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover} ($0.5737 \to 0.5785$) and $+0.0155$ on \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le100} ($0.5920 \to 0.6075$); and (iii) enforcing taxonomic and anatomical constraints ($B_3, B_4 + \text{Stage 2A}$) prior to upward GO propagation suppresses forbidden eukaryotic organelle and developmental leaf terms before they can inflate shared intermediate ancestors, raising Cellular Component $f_w$ by $+0.0115$ to $+0.0372$ across splits while reducing formal violations to zero.

We also evaluated enzyme EC prediction on a post-cutoff Swiss-Prot holdout of $125$ enzymes (Supplementary Table~S5) and across $69{,}666$ proteins from $95$ bacterial genomes under four donor-homology masking thresholds (Supplementary Table~S6). On the post-cutoff holdout, pooling CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, and DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz} in Stage 0 reaches micro $F_{\max}=0.4381$, and genome-scale refinement improves top-3 and top-5 enzyme recall across candidates. Across the 95-genome bacterial panel, accepting unfiltered METEOR gap-filling changes lowers Top-1 accuracy on DeepProZyme and EnzBERT because reaction-level boosts promote non-cognate paralogs, whereas filtering candidate changes through GSPA's three-channel consensus rule yields statistically significant net Top-1 gains across all 12 predictor-by-masking combinations, reaching $+155$ net corrected enzymes on CLEAN ($p < 10^{-25}$, exact McNemar test; Supplementary Table~S6). Agreement across complementary predictors identifies reliable metabolic enzyme updates and provides a calibrated foundation for whole-genome inference.

With the protein-level posterior established, we asked whether introducing whole-genome priors improves organism-level structural coherence while preserving protein-centric accuracy. We conducted a nine-configuration ablation across nine bacterial and archaeal reference proteomes ($81$ proteome runs), calibrating hyperparameters on \textit{E.~coli} K-12 and evaluating the remaining eight species out of sample (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Full Stage 1 and Stage 2A refinement with semantic ancestor relaxation improves protein-centric CAFA $F_{\max}$ ($0.8056$ versus $0.8041$ on all annotations; $0.2382$ versus $0.2361$ on experimental-only annotations), pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ ($0.7733$ versus $0.7672$), experimental IC recall ($0.3747$ versus $0.3661$), and coverage ($0.8057$ versus $0.7973$), while increasing the composite structural audit score \citep{toonsi2025gaef} from $0.8559$ to $0.9106$ ($p = 0.0039$, two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test; out-of-sample eight-genome mean from $0.8531$ to $0.9096$). Because roughly $85\%$ of annotated proteins in these reference genomes already carry high-confidence homologs in Stage 0, whole-genome refinement preserves the conserved sequence backbone while concentrating probability updates on lower-confidence, structurally constrained loci. To understand how this composite gain arises, we examined the effect of each individual prior on its target proteome property.

Adding each Stage 1 prior individually to Stage 0 produces a selective update on the corresponding genome invariant (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The essentiality prior lifts core functional completeness from $0.9400$ to $0.9826$, the coherence prior increases pathway completeness from $0.5041$ to $0.5616$, heteromeric complex completeness from $0.8636$ to $0.9222$, and experimental IC recall from $0.3661$ to $0.3705$, and the metabolic gap-filling and co-directional operon priors raise pathway completeness to $0.5100$ and $0.5336$, respectively. The soft taxonomic and anatomical priors, which query our programmatic cross-ontology bridge (Supplementary Table~S7), suppress $16$ of the $25$ endogenous taxonomic violations in Stage 1, and Stage 2A deterministic enforcement eliminates the remaining $9$ high-confidence violations while pruning isolated obligate-complex singletons to reach $0.9722$ complex coherence ($p = 0.0039$ for both pathway coherence and composite audit score across the nine genomes). Within Stage 2A, semantic ancestor relaxation outperforms hard deletion across CAFA $F_{\max}$, micro $F_{\max}$, experimental IC recall ($0.3747$ versus $0.3708$), essential completeness ($0.9826$ versus $0.9782$), and composite audit score ($0.9106$ versus $0.9090$) by transferring posterior support from clade-incompatible terms to their nearest valid GO ancestors. Beyond endogenous errors, sequence similarity across kingdoms can also introduce high-scoring cross-clade annotations, which we tested next through controlled perturbation.

To evaluate robustness against cross-clade homology transfer, we injected $900$ eukaryotic developmental and clade-restricted GO claims ($100$ per genome) with raw confidence scores in $[0.55, 0.85]$ across the nine prokaryotic proteomes (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Under unconstrained Stage 0 pooling (and priors $B_1, B_2, B_5, B_6$ that do not enforce taxonomic admissibility), an average of $68.0$ injected false-positive claims per genome survive above the $0.50$ decision threshold ($32.0\%$ suppressed by score calibration alone). Activating the Stage 1 taxonomic and anatomical priors ($B_3, B_4$) reduces surviving false positives from $68.0$ to $12.1$ per genome ($87.9\%$ suppressed), and adding Stage 2A enforcement further reduces surviving violations to $2.3$ per genome ($97.7\%$ suppressed across the $900$ injected claims) while increasing perturbed-proteome micro $F_{\max}$ from $0.7631$ to $0.7725$. Formal organism-level constraints therefore eliminate high-scoring cross-clade transfer errors that pass protein-level score calibration. We then evaluated whether these whole-genome improvements hold at population scale and across all four domains of life.

We applied GSPA with frozen parameters to $1{,}000$ RefSeq bacterial genomes and to a multi-kingdom benchmark of $88$ proteomes spanning Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryota, and Viruses under homology masking (Supplementary Tables~S8 and~S9). Across the $1{,}000$ bacterial genomes, Stage 1 and Stage 2 refinement eliminates all $23{,}384$ mean taxon-violating protein--term predictions per genome produced by Stage 0 and lifts mean MetaCyc pathway coherence from $36.7\%$ to $66.8\%$ at a mean runtime of $5.4$~s per genome. Across the $88$ multi-kingdom proteomes, whole-genome refinement produces positive mean accuracy gains in all four domains, with $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals on $\Delta F_{\max}$ lying strictly above zero in the two largest cohorts, Bacteria and Viruses, and spanning zero in Archaea and Eukaryota (Supplementary Table~S9). Because genome-wide complex coherence measures only whether partner subunits are present somewhere in the proteome, we next tested whether local chromosomal architecture can identify the specific gene encoding a missing complex subunit.

A natural test of this mechanism arises in lineages where complex subunits are encoded at dispersed chromosomal positions.

In the cyanobacterium \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, subunits of photosystem I and II, phycobilisomes, and NAD(P)H dehydrogenase are frequently distributed across separate genomic regions (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}b and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}). Adding Stage 2A singleton pruning and Stage 3 neuro-symbolic disambiguation prunes false-positive decoy promotions across dispersed \textit{Synechocystis} candidates, raising \textit{Synechocystis} precision from $0.3393$ to $0.3427$ and subunit $F_1$ from $0.4080$ to $0.4120$ while maintaining $0.9257$ Hit@1 across the six species. Local operon synteny resolves contiguous prokaryotic assemblies, and selective domain and pathway reasoning improves subunit precision when complex subunits are dispersed across the chromosome. Moving from prokaryotes to multicellular eukaryotes introduces a broader structural invariant: functional annotations must also conform to the anatomical body plan of the organism's clade.

We first tested whether ordinary sequence-homology and multi-channel predictions in unperturbed eukaryotic proteomes contain endogenous taxonomic and anatomical contradictions. Evaluating standalone DIAMOND-BLASTp and the seven-channel Stage 0 ensemble across $6{,}155$ proteins from six eukaryotes (\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}, \textit{Physcomitrium patens}, \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}, and \textit{Mus musculus}) revealed $1{,}409$ endogenous violations ($1{,}009$ at high posterior confidence), concentrated in \textit{C.~elegans} and \textit{D.~melanogaster} where strong sequence and structural similarity transfers vertebrate organ-development and angiosperm terms onto invertebrate orthologs (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Full GSPA eliminates all $1{,}409$ endogenous violations while raising six-species mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.7096$ to $0.7117$. Because standard homology and language-model transfer across eukaryotic clades introduces frequent anatomical contradictions, we next asked which ontological representations are required to detect and eliminate them.

We compared standalone DIAMOND-BLASTp, unconstrained Stage 0 pooling, direct GO taxon constraints, the Stage 1 soft anatomical prior, and Stage 2A deterministic cross-ontology enforcement on the combined cohort containing $1{,}546$ injected cross-clade developmental claims ($2{,}451$ total Stage 0 contradictions across $7{,}655$ proteins; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Direct GO taxon constraints leave $616$ contradictions unresolved ($431$ endogenous) in \textit{C.~elegans}, \textit{D.~melanogaster}, and \textit{S.~cerevisiae} because organ-level processes such as \textit{heart development} (\texttt{GO:0007507}) and \textit{neural crest cell migration} (\texttt{GO:0001755}) carry their taxonomic restrictions on the linked Uberon, PO, or CL anatomical classes instead of on the GO term itself. The Stage 1 soft anatomical prior eliminates $100\%$ of endogenous violations and $84.1\%$ of stress-test violations on average across the six species, and Stage 2A deterministic enforcement over the GO--Uberon/PO/CL bridge eliminates $100\%$ of violations while rescuing $81.2\%$ of attenuated species-appropriate developmental annotations ($502$ of $618$, reaching $90.1\%$ in \textit{M.~musculus}). Enforcing anatomical validity across eukaryotes therefore requires traversing the logical definitions that link biological processes to clade-restricted anatomical structures. Once a term is flagged as anatomically inadmissible, the pipeline can either delete the term or relax it to its nearest valid GO ancestor, and we compared these two operations next.

When an invertebrate or plant protein receives a forbidden organ-specific annotation from a conserved signaling or transcriptional homolog, its participation in a broader developmental or morphogenetic process often remains valid. Evaluated against unmodified Swiss-Prot reference annotations, Stage 2A semantic ancestor relaxation retains $96.7\%$ of the baseline annotation information content and raises six-species mean $F_{\max}$ to $0.9605$ compared with $0.9568$ under term deletion, outperforming deletion across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants and reaching $+0.0157$ $F_{\max}$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster} (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). In the unicellular fungus \textit{S.~cerevisiae} and the non-vascular moss \textit{P.~patens}, where injected organ-development claims land on housekeeping enzymes, deletion yields slightly higher precision and $F_{\max}$, whereas relaxation yields higher recall and information content. Semantic ancestor relaxation preserves valid ancestral developmental biology in tissue-forming eukaryotes while maintaining zero formal ontology violations. Having evaluated whole-genome constraints on complexes and ontological admissibility, we next examined how genomic architecture combines with systems biology to localize missing metabolic enzymes.

Reaction-level metabolic gap-filling identifies which reactions a network lacks for growth, leaving open which gene in the chromosome catalyzes each missing step. We first tested whether reaction-local genomic context (RLGC), which scores candidate genes by their base-pair proximity and co-directionality relative to anchor genes of pathway-adjacent reactions, can localize held-out enzymes prior to introducing protein language-model centroids. Across $256$ strict leave-reaction-out trials in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 with sequence homologs of the held-out enzyme masked (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}), sequence-only ranking places the true enzyme at Rank 1 for $1.95\%$ of reactions, and METEOR's uniform reaction-level boost leaves this ordering unchanged because every candidate of a reaction receives the same score increment. Scoring unassigned operon neighborhood density around gapseq pathway anchors reaches $17.19\%$ unconditional Hit@1, and combining RLGC kernel density with the Stage 1 pathway coherence prior raises unconditional Hit@1 twelve-fold over sequence-only ranking to $23.44\%$ ($34.29\%$ within the candidate pool, $p < 10^{-12}$, exact McNemar test). Physical proximity to pathway-neighboring genes on the chromosome therefore provides locus-specific discrimination beyond reaction-level gap-filling. We next examined how local genomic context interacts with alignment-free protein language-model representations across metabolic roles, homology-masking cutoffs, and species.

When Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction embedding centroids are included alongside masked sequence alignments (Track B; Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a--c and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}), centroid similarity alone recovers $62.11\%$ of the $256$ held-out \textit{E.~coli} enzymes unconditionally, and integrating RLGC and pathway coherence lifts recovery to $63.67\%$ ($90.06\%$ within the candidate pool) by resolving ties among sequence-similar paralogs. Stratifying the broader $414$-reaction \textit{E.~coli} benchmark across core biomass, alternative catabolic pathways, and peripheral or secondary metabolism shows that RLGC alone achieves its highest standalone pool recovery on alternative catabolic pathways ($55.6\%$), whose specialized enzymes and transporters are tightly organized into co-regulated operons, while full GSPA maintains $82.8\%$ to $93.5\%$ pool Hit@1 across all three strata and $85.7\%$ to $100.0\%$ pool Hit@1 under Protocol H donor-homology masking at $\le 70\%$, $\le 50\%$, and $\le 30\%$ identity (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}b, Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}c, and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}). Transferred with frozen hyperparameters to $144$ held-out reactions in the Gram-positive bacterium \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, full GSPA reaches $43.06\%$ unconditional Hit@1 and $81.58\%$ within the candidate pool (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}c). Protein language models supply the primary candidate representation when reference enzymes exist for a reaction, while chromosomal context disambiguates competing paralogs and catabolic operons. Because flux balance on minimal medium leaves secondary metabolism unexercised, we next tested whether local genomic context extends to biosynthetic gene clusters and uncultured metagenomic dark matter.

Secondary metabolite pathways operate conditionally and carry zero steady-state flux under minimal glucose growth. Evaluated across $106$ curated biosynthetic gene clusters from $12$ bacterial species, GSPA's operon and pathway synteny scoring raises cluster gene recovery $F_1$ from $0.7395$ to $0.8959$ while maintaining $99.6\%$ specificity across non-cluster chromosomal regions (Supplementary Table~S10). Applying the same frozen pipeline to $97$ high-quality metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) of uncultured bacteria and archaea spanning $16$ phyla resolves $568$ high-confidence enzyme attributions at posterior $q \ge 0.50$, lifting mean draft pathway completeness from $0.511$ to $0.656$ (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d and Supplementary Table~S11). Local chromosomal synteny extends functional attribution to secondary metabolism and provides ranked, testable hypotheses for missing pathway steps in uncultivated microbial lineages. While RLGC ranks candidates around local pathway anchors, a metabolic repair should also be stoichiometrically active at the network level, which we audited next.

We next tested our third driver of improvement: replacing reaction-indicator MILP gap-filling with positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) and competitive gene-locus attribution. Auditing metabolic network repairs across $108$ bacterial genomes shows why negative-cost reaction-indicator MILP inflates reconstructions (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a and Supplementary Table~S12): because candidate reactions above the addition threshold receive a negative objective cost, the solver adds a mean of $313.35$ reactions per genome, of which $262.34$ ($83.72\%$) carry zero steady-state flux. Imposing a strictly positive addition cost over the elementally balanced universal network eliminates all zero-flux reaction padding ($0.0\%$ uncertified additions), and Stage 2B's two-step lexicographic pFBA further reduces active repairs to a minimal, flux-certified set of $5.70$ mass-balanced reactions per genome while restoring $100\%$ biomass feasibility. A positive addition cost coupled with lexicographic flux minimization therefore ensures that every added reaction actively participates in a mass-balanced metabolic route. We next asked whether attributing these flux-certified reactions competitively to candidate gene loci improves the identification of experimentally validated enzymes.

We evaluated enzyme discovery on $148$ divergent bacterial enzymes across $22$ host genomes whose physiological roles were established by random-barcoded transposon mutagenesis (Price-149) \citep{price2018mutant}, comparing six standalone enzyme predictors against METEOR v8, single-predictor GSPA, and multi-predictor GSPA (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}b and Table~\ref{tab:price149}). Because $55$ of the $148$ enzymes catalyze alternative catabolic pathways that are inactive on minimal glucose, single-medium MILP gap-filling lowers $F_{\max}$ on DeepProZyme ($0.5574 \to 0.5427$), EnzBERT ($0.4786 \to 0.4451$), and GraphEC ($0.2634 \to 0.2250$). Operating on a single predictor's proteome output, Stage 2B stoichiometric local-context (SLR + RLGC) probing and single-predictor GSPA improve Top-1 enzyme recovery on MAPred from $50$ to $56$ proteins ($F_{\max}$ from $0.5534$ to $0.5746$), on CLEAN from $60$ to $63$ ($F_{\max}$ to $0.5402$ under SLR + RLGC), on DeepProZyme from $49$ to $51$ ($F_{\max}$ to $0.5774$), and on GraphEC from $18$ to $21$ ($F_{\max}$ to $0.2851$). Combining multi-predictor Stage 0 pooling with subsequent locus-level refinement produces both strong pooling gains on lower-recall baselines and additive post-pooling refinement gains on the leading predictors, raising MAPred from $63$ after Stage 0 pooling to $73$ ($49.3\%$ Top-1 accuracy, $F_{\max}=0.6476$, $p = 6.0 \times 10^{-6}$, exact McNemar test), CLEAN from $62$ to $68$ ($F_{\max}=0.6435$), DeepProZyme from $59$ to $60$ ($F_{\max}=0.6444$, $p = 0.019$), and GraphEC from $59$ to $67$ ($F_{\max}=0.6368$; Table~\ref{tab:price149}). Complementary predictor pooling and competitive stoichiometric locus attribution therefore work additively on the top enzyme predictors to identify divergent, fitness-validated bacterial enzymes. Having evaluated gene-level enzyme recovery, we next examined whether systems-level pathway pooling and coupling predict experimental organismal phenotypes at scale.

Across the $3{,}017$-strain multi-model benchmark, pooling calibrated pathway calls from gapseq, ModelSEED, and CarveMe with unsupervised cross-assay pathway coupling and taxonomic priors in blind Track 1 improves carbon-growth $F_1$ from $0.7952$ (gapseq) to $0.8094$ (balanced accuracy $= 0.8239$, ROC-AUC $= 0.8620$, $+43 / -4$ net improved assays, $p = 3.2 \times 10^{-9}$), diagnostic-enzyme $F_1$ from $0.7753$ to $0.7757$ (balanced accuracy $= 0.7483$, $+74 / -52$), and heteromeric urease (\texttt{EC 3.5.1.5}) $F_1$ from $0.5893$ to $0.6039$ ($+58 / -35$, $p = 0.022$; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}). Continuous pathway coupling and multi-model calibration therefore improve both binary phenotype accuracy and probability calibration over individual metabolic reconstruction tools. We next examined whether these mechanistic pathway posteriors improve out-of-genus transfer when combined with supervised phenotype classifiers and whether partial phenotypic observations from a strain refine its remaining unassayed pathways.

Under five-fold genus-blocked cross-validation, in which every test strain belongs to a taxonomic genus unseen during training, combining GSPA pathway posteriors with the DiASPora gradient-boosting classifier \citep{koblitz2025diaspora} raises out-of-genus $F_1$ to $0.8778$ (ROC-AUC $= 0.9567$) on carbon growth, $0.8712$ (ROC-AUC $= 0.9098$) on diagnostic enzymes, and $0.7101$ (ROC-AUC $= 0.8832$) on heteromeric urease, outperforming all individual metabolic reconstructions as well as Traitar \citep{weimann2016traitar}, BacDive-AI \citep{schober2025bac}, and standalone DiASPora across folds (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive} and Supplementary Table~S13). In Track 2, where $30\%$ of each strain's experimental assays are revealed as partial phenotypic evidence and the remaining $70\%$ are held out, conditioning GSPA's pathway posteriors via an unsupervised Bayesian update lifts held-out carbon-growth $F_1$ from $0.7922$ (gapseq) to $0.8842$ ($0.8546$ on held-out enzymes), and the hybrid with DiASPora reaches $F_1 = 0.9133$ (ROC-AUC $= 0.9770$) on carbon growth and $F_1 = 0.8737$ (ROC-AUC $= 0.9088$) on diagnostic enzymes (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}). Mechanistic pathway coupling provides domain-generalizable features for unseen genera and propagates partial experimental observations across coupled metabolic pathways. After deterministic probabilistic and stoichiometric refinement, a small set of ambiguous loci remains, which we examined in Stage 3.

Deterministic inference in Stages 1 and 2 resolves the vast majority of proteome annotations, leaving a small fraction of structured anomalies: unresolved pathway gaps, incomplete heteromeric complexes, uncovered essential functions, and borderline ontology conflicts. Across the $88$ multi-kingdom proteomes, deterministic anomaly triggers select a mean of $1.84\%$ of proteins per proteome (fewer than $2.5\%$ in every domain of life), bounding the computational cost of invoking a language model. Evaluated across three random seeds on a de-identified benchmark of $120$ anomalies from seven prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes with all gene symbols masked and candidate ordering randomized, a direct zero-shot language model invoked in a single turn without genomic inspection tools or symbolic verification achieves $F_1 = 0.2817$ (precision $0.5882$, recall $0.1852$, accuracy $0.6083$,abstaining on $101$ of $120$ anomalies). Equipping the model with read-only genomic tools and the symbolic guardrail raises mean $F_1$ to $0.9115 \pm 0.0008$ with $0.0\%$ accepted ontology violations, whereas ablating the symbolic guardrail admits a cross-clade violation and reduces $F_1$ to $0.8515$ (Figure~\ref{fig:agent}a,b,d, Table~\ref{tab:agent}, and Supplementary Table~S14). Selective triggering therefore confines language-model inference to fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins, while tool-mediated evidence retrieval and deterministic verification guarantee that accepted proposals satisfy all taxonomic and anatomical axioms. We next tested whether tool-mediated reasoning can resolve multi-candidate metabolic gaps.

When multiple candidate genes in a genomic neighborhood share similar RLGC scores for a missing reaction, resolving the true enzyme requires synthesizing domain architecture, operon structure, and pathway stoichiometry. Across $50$ unique-protein ambiguous leave-reaction-out gaps in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 where Stage 1 RLGC alone ranks the true enzyme first in $34.0\%$ of cases ($42.0\%$ for a direct zero-shot LLM and $44.0\%$ for posterior thresholding with the guardrail), Stage 3 multi-turn re-ranking lifts Top-1 recovery to a three-seed mean of $82.7 \pm 0.9\%$ ($p = 2.8 \times 10^{-7}$, exact McNemar test; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}c and Table~\ref{tab:agent}). Tool-mediated evidence synthesis resolves ties among candidate enzymes after deterministic priors have narrowed the search space. Because a supervised tabular classifier can also be trained on numerical features extracted by the same tools, we compared zero-shot guarded reasoning against cross-validated classifiers to establish the operating regime of each method.

We compared the zero-shot guarded agent against logistic regression, random forest, gradient-boosted decision tree (GBDT), and multilayer perceptron classifiers evaluated by five-fold cross-validation on either three pre-tool summary scalars or eight post-tool genomic and domain features paired with the symbolic guardrail (Table~\ref{tab:agent}). On the $120$ anomalies, the zero-shot agent ($F_1 = 0.9115$) outperforms all classifiers restricted to pre-tool scalars ($F_1 = 0.7500$--$0.8283$), while supervised classifiers trained on the eight post-tool features reach up to $F_1 = 0.9720$ on binary anomaly promotion and $96.0\%$ Hit@1 on the $50$ metabolic gaps at sub-millisecond inference latency. On borderline ontology conflicts, the zero-shot agent proposes a mean of $13.7$ valid ancestral GO relaxations compared with $1$--$2$ for the post-tool classifiers, which select among fixed candidate labels. Supervised post-tool classifiers are therefore preferable for high-throughput binary promotion and candidate ranking when labeled anomalies are available, whereas zero-shot guarded reasoning provides zero-label operation and multi-class ancestral GO relaxation (see Supplementary Section~S6 and Supplementary Table~S16 for an application to five desert extremophile isolates from the Empty Quarter). Having validated all four pipeline stages across controlled holdouts and experimental phenotype benchmarks, we next scaled whole-genome inference across $72{,}533$ genomes from the AllTheBacteria resource \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria} to determine what comparative laws govern sequence dark matter, complex stoichiometry, and pathway evolution across the bacterial domain.

To evaluate whole-genome inference across bacterial and archaeal diversity, we implemented a two-stage sequence deduplication pipeline that hashes identical coding sequences via SHA-256 ($4.27$-fold compression across $315{,}671{,}487$ proteins in $78{,}035$ assemblies to $73{,}866{,}871$ unique sequences) and clusters them in linear time via \texttt{MMseqs2 linclust} \citep{steinegger2018clustering} at $90\%$ sequence identity and $80\%$ coverage ($8.58$-fold total compression to $36{,}804{,}001$ cluster representatives) before scoring representatives with GSPA-Seq and executing whole-genome integration (\texttt{--batch-manifest}) across $72{,}533$ complete genomes spanning $292{,}133{,}146$ coding sequences, $11{,}632$ species, and $37$ phyla (Figure~\ref{fig:allthebacteria}a, Supplementary Section~S7, and Supplementary Table~S17). Across all $292{,}133{,}146$ coding sequences, $119{,}647{,}577$ ($40.96\%$) lie in the sequence dark-matter regime ($\text{pident} < 30\%$ to Swiss-Prot), of which $102{,}673{,}326$ ($85.81\%$) have zero detectable BLAST alignment ($\text{pident} = 0.0\%$ at $E \le 10^{-3}$) and $16{,}974{,}251$ ($14.19\%$) have remote alignments ($0\% < \text{pident} < 30\%$), while an additional $78{,}843{,}303$ sequences ($26.99\%$) lie in the twilight zone ($30\% \le \text{pident} < 50\%$). Sequence dark matter varies nearly twofold across phyla, rising from $36.51\%$ in Actinomycetota ($135.8$~million coding sequences) and $40.42\%$ in Pseudomonadota ($86.9$~million) to $60.82\%$--$66.64\%$ in environmental, predatory, and symbiotic phyla including Planctomycetota ($66.64\%$), Bdellovibrionota\_B ($66.02\%$), Bacteroidota ($64.49\%$), Armatimonadota ($62.58\%$), Fibrobacterota ($62.52\%$), Methanobacteriota ($62.45\%$), Gemmatimonadota ($61.50\%$), Cyanobacteriota ($60.96\%$), Verrucomicrobiota ($60.96\%$), Bdellovibrionota ($60.92\%$), and Spirochaetota ($60.82\%$). Across all $37$ phyla, whole-genome priors rescue $51{,}375{,}006$ dark-matter proteins ($42.94\%$, comprising $39{,}253{,}161$ zero-alignment orphans [$76.41\%$] and $12{,}121{,}845$ remote homologs [$23.59\%$]) and $60{,}674{,}994$ twilight-zone proteins ($76.96\%$) above the $0.50$ posterior threshold ($353{,}243{,}657$ promoted dark-matter protein--term pairs: $88.58\%$ from co-directional operon synteny $B_6$, $11.40\%$ from pathway and complex coherence $B_2$, and $0.020\%$ from surgical metabolic gap-filling $B_5$), while pre-propagation taxonomic ($B_3$) and anatomical ($B_4$) enforcement eliminates $47{,}739{,}228$ direct taxonomic and $2{,}388{,}329{,}971$ propagated anatomical cross-kingdom violations ($2{,}436{,}069{,}199$ total violations, or $33{,}586$ per genome). Conserved operon organization and macromolecular stoichiometry therefore persist across phyla even when primary amino-acid sequences diverge beyond alignment thresholds. We next asked how sequence dark matter and metabolic pathway gaps scale with bacterial genome size.

Stratifying the $72{,}530$ bacterial genomes across six genome-size regimes ($< 1{,}500$ to $\ge 5{,}500$ ORFs; Figure~\ref{fig:allthebacteria}b and Supplementary Table~S18) reveals a functional bifurcation between sequence dark matter and metabolic network gaps. Sequence dark matter follows a U-shaped curve across genome size: streamlined and host-associated bacteria ($< 1{,}500$ ORFs; $413$ genomes across $238$ species and $17$ phyla, mean $1{,}152.3$ ORFs) carry the highest dark-matter fraction ($50.62\%$ dark matter and $34.55\%$ twilight zone, totaling $85.17\%$ of the proteome below $50\%$ identity), which drops to a minimum of $37.46\%$ dark matter ($22.83\%$ twilight zone) in modal bacterial generalists ($3{,}500$--$4{,}499$ ORFs; $36{,}243$ genomes across $3{,}123$ species) and rises back to $46.57\%$ dark matter ($31.72\%$ twilight zone) in large environmental and secondary-metabolite genomes ($\ge 5{,}500$ ORFs; $6{,}154$ genomes across $2{,}549$ species, mean $6{,}609.9$ ORFs). In contrast, metabolic pathway gaps ($B_5$) increase monotonically with genome size ($\text{Spearman } \rho = 0.5627, p < 10^{-300}$), surging $136.4$-fold per genome from $0.14$ gaps per genome ($0.108$ per $1{,}000$ ORFs) in $< 1{,}500$-ORF genomes to $12.70$ in $4{,}500$--$5{,}499$-ORF genomes and $19.10$ ($2.876$ per $1{,}000$ ORFs, a $26.6$-fold density increase) in $\ge 5{,}500$-ORF genomes. Streamlined bacteria shed peripheral catabolic pathways while retaining core informational and bioenergetic complexes whose subunits undergo rapid sequence drift inside conserved operons ($1.914$ $B_6$ rescues per ORF), whereas large environmental genomes accumulate lineage-specific catabolic and secondary biosynthetic operons that introduce stoichiometrically unannotated reaction steps. Because macromolecular complexes are retained across these regimes while metabolic enzymes expand, we next examined how whole-genome stoichiometric selection distinguishes intact multi-subunit complexes from modular domain reuse.

Comparing complex coherence promotions ($B_2$) against Stage 2A singleton pruning across $72{,}533$ genomes separates conserved multi-subunit complexes from standalone enzymes that reuse homologous catalytic domains (Figure~\ref{fig:allthebacteria}c and Supplementary Table~S18). In genomes encoding the complete partner subunit complement, whole-genome coherence rescues $12{,}506{,}795$ divergent subunit annotations across six obligate prokaryotic complexes, led by the 70S ribosome (\texttt{GO:0005840}, $6{,}339{,}928$ rescued subunits versus $15$ pruned singletons), DNA-directed RNA polymerase (\texttt{GO:0000428}, $4{,}733{,}571$ versus $197$), proton-transporting $\text{F}_1\text{F}_0$ ATP synthase (\texttt{GO:0045259}, $1{,}215{,}204$ versus $108$), and DNA gyrase (\texttt{GO:0009330}, $192{,}355$ versus $2{,}659$). Conversely, Stage 2A prunes $1{,}426{,}276$ isolated singleton predictions where per-protein predictors assign an obligate complex term in the absence of its partner subunits, concentrated in respiratory NADH dehydrogenase Complex I (\texttt{GO:0005747}, $1{,}202{,}957$ pruned singletons across Actinomycetota [$615{,}599$], Pseudomonadota [$397{,}602$], Bacillota [$68{,}387$], and Bacteroidota [$43{,}304$], alongside $25{,}179$ rescued subunits in genomes encoding the complete \texttt{nuoA--N} complex) and the Sec protein export translocon (\texttt{GO:0031522}, $220{,}519$ pruned singletons). Per-protein homology and language-model predictors assign respiratory Complex I terms to standalone fermentative $\text{[4Fe-4S]}$ flavoproteins, bidirectional hydrogenases, and soluble Rossmann-fold oxidoreductases that share the soluble N-module NADH-binding domains (\texttt{NuoE/F/G}) while lacking the membrane proton-pumping core (\texttt{NuoA/H/J/K/L/M/N}); whole-genome stoichiometric enforcement eliminates these false-positive complex assignments while rescuing divergent subunits of intact bioenergetic complexes. Having separated intact complexes from domain reuse, we asked whether the dark-matter proteins rescued inside complexes, operons, and pathways represent remote sequence orthologs or non-orthologous gene displacements.

Partitioning rescued dark-matter annotations ($< 30\%$ identity) into zero-alignment replacements ($\text{pident} = 0.0\%$) and remote divergent orthologs ($0\% < \text{pident} < 30\%$) uncovers a division of evolutionary modes between catalytic cores and peripheral or adaptive functions (Figure~\ref{fig:allthebacteria}d and Supplementary Table~S18). Within multi-subunit complexes and core redox pathways, catalytic active-site subunits evolve primarily through gradual sequence divergence while retaining alignment traces: $99.95\%$ of dark-matter rescues for ATP synthase rotational proton transport (\texttt{GO:0046933}, $5{,}375$ of $5{,}378$ across $25$ phyla), $99.53\%$ for RNA polymerase core transcription (\texttt{GO:0006351}, $33{,}280$ of $33{,}438$ across $27$ phyla), $99.00\%$ for DNA gyrase topological catalysis (\texttt{GO:0006265}, $2{,}308$ of $2{,}331$ across $15$ phyla), $100.0\%$ for Complex I electron-transfer catalysis (\texttt{GO:0003954} and \texttt{GO:0042773}), and $100.0\%$ for NADPH carbonyl reductases (\texttt{GO:0004090}, $1{,}265$ across $12$ phyla) and secondary-metabolite monooxygenases (\texttt{GO:0097621}, $795$ across $13$ phyla) carry remote sequence alignments ($0\% < \text{pident} < 30\%$). In contrast, short membrane-embedded and peripheral complex subunits (including $97.86\%$ of dark-matter $\text{F}_0$ membrane subunit rescues in ATP synthase \texttt{GO:0045259}, $520{,}762$ of $532{,}135$ across $20$ phyla, and $93.99\%$ of small accessory $\omega/\delta$ subunit rescues in RNA polymerase \texttt{GO:0000428}, $292{,}899$ of $311{,}636$ across $24$ phyla, as well as $82.74\%$ of all co-directional operon synteny promotions $B_6$, $258{,}892{,}062$ of $312{,}893{,}381$) diverge completely beyond sequence alignment ($\text{pident} = 0.0\%$) yet remain conserved within their canonical operon slots. Similarly, across metabolic modules ($B_2$, $49.08\%$ overall zero-alignment) and pathway gap-fills ($B_5$, $45.19\%$ overall zero-alignment), non-orthologous gene displacement ($\text{pident} = 0.0\%$) predominates in lineage-specific thiol and mycothiol S-transferases (\texttt{GO:0004364}, $99.91\%$ across $4$ phyla), adenylate cyclase signaling (\texttt{KEGG:M00859}, $81.34\%$ across $27$ phyla), cell-wall lipopolysaccharide hexosyltransferases (\texttt{KEGG:M00888}, $68.89\%$ across $36$ phyla), cofactor C--O/C--C lyases (\texttt{KEGG:M00836}, $62.09\%$ across $34$ phyla), purine and peptide C--N ligases (\texttt{KEGG:M00951}, $55.80\%$ across $36$ phyla), and xenobiotic glycosyltransferases (\texttt{GO:0015020}, $35.34\%$ across $17$ phyla). Whole-genome inference therefore unifies the recovery of remote catalytic orthologs with the systematic mapping of non-orthologous gene displacements across bacterial phyla.

\section{Discussion}

Protein function prediction has traditionally been formulated as a per-protein classification task in which each sequence or structure is scored independently \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,you2021deepgraphgo,cao2021tale,yuan2023fast,kulmanov2024deepgose,ding2025dpfunc}. GSPA reformulates proteome annotation as staged inference over the complete genome of an organism, and our evaluation shows that three complementary drivers account for its performance. First, calibrated pooling of sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity establishes a strong per-protein probability distribution in Stage 0, instantiated as our standalone per-protein stacking integrator GSPA-Seq. Second, whole-genome priors and deterministic cross-ontology enforcement in Stages 1 and 2A update these probabilities using organism-wide invariants over operons, core essential functions, obligate heteromeric complexes, and taxonomic and anatomical consistency. Third, stoichiometric flux modeling, competitive gene-locus attribution, and selective ontology-guarded reasoning in Stages 1, 2B, and 3 connect metabolic network requirements and multi-source genomic evidence to individual gene loci. Across all six splits of our whole-genome withheld CAFA evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}), integrating complete genome assemblies improves both information-accretion weighted $f_w$ and unweighted $F_{\max}$ over official pre-trained and retrained sequence, domain, convolutional, graph neural network, transformer, semantic entailment, and protein language model baselines (including DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure}, DeepGraphGO \citep{you2021deepgraphgo}, TALE+ \citep{cao2021tale}, SPROF-GO \citep{yuan2023fast}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, and DPFunc \citep{ding2025dpfunc}) as well as over GSPA-Seq, while eliminating all taxonomic and anatomical violations.

Stage 0 and GSPA-Seq build directly on the multi-source ensemble paradigm introduced by GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}, which showed that integrating sequence alignment, domain signatures, motifs, network associations, and language-model embeddings outperforms individual representations in CAFA evaluations. GSPA extends this principle to genome-scale inference by calibrating raw predictor outputs via Platt scaling, grouping correlated evidence channels so that shared homology or embedding signals are counted once, and pooling across groups via Noisy-OR into an anchored log-odds prior $L^{(0)}$. Because every subsequent Stage 1 update is added to $L^{(0)}$, sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity remain the foundation of the framework. Conserved proteins retain their high-confidence Stage 0 assignments, and the recall of the Stage 0 candidate pool defines the space of candidate functions that genome-scale priors refine.

Exploiting whole-genome information in Stages 1 and 2A is timely because long-read sequencing and modern repeat-graph assemblers now routinely recover complete or near-complete chromosomes for both cultivated isolates and metagenome-assembled genomes \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2020metaflye}. Complete assemblies expose two distinct scales of genomic organization, and our whole-genome CAFA ablations (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}) and pan-phylum AllTheBacteria analysis across $72{,}533$ genomes \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria} (Figure~\ref{fig:allthebacteria} and Supplementary Tables~S17 and~S18) reveal both how each mechanism contributes to functional accuracy and what biological principles govern bacterial genomes at scale. Comparing the two whole-genome temporal splits shows that pre-$t_0$ experimental annotations on neighboring genes along the same chromosome provide contextual anchors for newly characterized genes, lifting whole-genome $f_w$ from $0.4455$ in de novo \texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld} to $0.4996$ in \texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover} ($+0.0541$ $f_w$). Across $72{,}533$ genomes from $37$ phyla, whole-genome inference uncovers four comparative laws of bacterial functional architecture: (i) conserved co-directional operon grammar and complex stoichiometry rescue $42.9\%$ ($51.4$~million) of sequence dark-matter proteins and $77.0\%$ ($60.7$~million) of twilight-zone proteins, resolving the apparent core auxotrophy of high-dark-matter phyla such as Planctomycetota ($66.6\%$ dark matter), Bacteroidota ($64.5\%$), Cyanobacteriota ($61.0\%$), and Spirochaetota ($60.8\%$); (ii) bacterial dark matter bifurcates across genome size into a streamlined drift regime ($< 1{,}500$ ORFs; $50.6\%$ dark matter, $0.14$ metabolic gaps per genome) and a large-genome metabolic innovation regime ($\ge 5{,}500$ ORFs; $19.10$ gaps per genome, a $136.4$-fold surge); (iii) stoichiometric selection separates $12.51$~million subunits of intact obligate complexes from $1.43$~million isolated singletons caused by modular reuse of soluble Complex I NADH-binding domains in fermentative oxidoreductases; and (iv) catalytic complex and redox cores evolve via remote sequence divergence ($99.0\%$--$100.0\%$ alignment retention), whereas peripheral membrane subunits ($94.0\%$--$97.9\%$ zero-alignment) and lineage-specific thiol, cell-wall, and cofactor enzymes ($55.8\%$--$99.9\%$ zero-alignment) evolve via non-orthologous gene displacement. Enforcing taxonomic and anatomical compatibility on leaf claims prior to upward GO ancestor closure simultaneously reduces zero-violation semantic distance $S_{\min}$ and improves $f_w$ and $F_{\max}$ across all six whole-genome CAFA splits (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}), eliminates $2.44$~billion cross-kingdom violations across AllTheBacteria, and preserves conserved tissue-level biology in multicellular eukaryotes (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}), while operon synteny lifts top-ranked subunit identification in leave-subunit-out experiments from $56.0\%$ to $93.7\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).

Our third driver advances functional inference beyond sequence homology by integrating systems biology with genomic architecture, addressing the specific mathematical limitations we identified in our earlier method METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor}. METEOR coupled metabolic gap-filling with enzyme prediction through a reaction-indicator MILP that assigned negative objective costs to candidate reactions above an addition threshold and boosted all candidate genes of a selected reaction uniformly. Our 108-genome stoichiometric audit shows that negative addition costs incentivize the solver to accumulate $83.7\%$ zero-flux reaction padding (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a), while uniform gene boosting elevates non-cognate paralogs and isolated complex subunits. GSPA resolves both issues by coupling reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) in Stage 1 with role-aware disjunctive normal form (DNF) aggregation of isozymes and required subunits, positive-cost lexicographic pFBA \citep{lewis2010omic}, and competitive locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis in Stage 2B. Under positive addition costs, every added reaction carries steady-state flux in an elementally balanced network, and competitive responsibility concentrates the backward log-odds update on the candidate gene supported by operon neighborhood and pathway synteny, improving both whole-genome CAFA accuracy ($B_5$ + Stage 2B in Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits}) and gene-level discovery on RB-TnSeq fitness-validated bacterial enzymes \citep{price2018mutant} (Table~\ref{tab:price149}). At the organism level, pooling calibrated metabolic reconstructions with unsupervised cross-assay pathway coupling and subunit-complete enzyme rules accurately predicts carbon-source growth and diagnostic enzyme phenotypes across unseen genera in BacDive (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).

Finally, Stage 3 shows how tool-using language models and formal symbolic reasoning can be combined selectively at proteome scale. By restricting invocation to deterministic post-Stage-2 anomaly triggers that select fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins per proteome, GSPA restricts language-model inference to ambiguous loci while leaving conserved genes resolved by Stages 0--2 untouched. Within each triggered anomaly, the language model synthesizes heterogeneous operon, domain, pathway, and ontology evidence through read-only tools, while a deterministic symbolic guardrail verifies every proposed resolution against GO, NCBI Taxonomy, and the GO--Uberon/PO/CL bridge, mirroring neuro-symbolic verification principles in biomedical curation \citep{wang2025_geneagent,caufield2024_spires}. Our comparison with five-fold cross-validated tabular classifiers delineates clear deployment regimes: supervised classifiers trained on post-tool features and paired with the symbolic guardrail provide the highest binary promotion accuracy at sub-millisecond latency when labeled anomalies exist, whereas the zero-shot guarded agent operates without anomaly training labels and performs multi-class traversal to valid ancestral GO terms.

\subsection{Limitations}

Our evaluation design establishes how genome-scale priors and stoichiometric refinement build upon a per-protein candidate set, and four aspects of this design define concrete next experiments. First, on well-annotated reference genomes, roughly $85\%$ of proteins already carry high-confidence homologs in Stage 0 ($P_0 \ge 0.90$), so Stage 0 governs baseline protein-centric performance while Stages 1--3 concentrate their updates on twilight-zone proteins, newly characterized rollover genes, pathway gaps, incomplete complexes, and taxonomic conflicts. Because the composite GAEF score evaluates the same structural invariants over essentiality, pathway and complex coherence, and taxonomic consistency that Stages 1 and 2 optimize, we report GAEF as an internal structural audit score paired with independent held-out whole-genome CAFA, EC, leave-reaction-out, leave-subunit-out, Price-149, and BacDive evaluations, and we evaluate both endogenous unperturbed contradictions ($1{,}009$ eukaryotic violations) and synthetic cross-clade stress-test injections. Similarly, in leave-reaction-out evaluation, unconditional enzyme recovery is bounded by whether Stage 0 places the true gene into the candidate pool ($70.7\%$ pool coverage in \textit{E.~coli} and $52.8\%$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}), and Track B uses released ESM-2 weights pretrained on UniRef50, making strict Track A cosine masking the primary test of de novo context recovery. In addition, whereas our six-split whole-genome withheld CAFA evaluation benchmarks complete chromosomal assemblies across six reference species ($20{,}697$ ORFs), external community challenge target lists (CAFA6 and Holdout-125) distribute isolated target sequences without source genome coordinates, evaluating Stages 0, 2A, and 3, while the leave-subunit-out benchmark samples domain-matched decoys from non-operonic loci. Expanding Stage 0 candidate generation with remote structural fold search, evaluating protein language models trained under strict chronological splits, sampling leave-subunit-out decoys from within-operon paralogs, and linking community challenge target lists to their complete source genome assemblies will directly measure how increased candidate recall and full chromosomal context affect these regimes.

Second, Stage 2B models steady-state, mass-balanced metabolic flux over reactions mapped in the sanitized universal network under specified growth media, and Stage 2A enforces anatomical constraints over the $2{,}715$ GO terms that currently carry explicit Uberon, PO, or CL logical definitions. Experimental growth and enzyme phenotypes in Price-149 and BacDive also depend on transcriptional regulation, transporter substrate specificity, and cofactor synthesis, while anatomical relaxation in unicellular fungi and non-vascular plants reflects the granularity of tissue-level ancestor annotations in reference databases. Integrating condition-specific transcriptomic expression priors and automated transporter substrate predictors into Stage 2B, alongside expanding cross-ontology logical definitions across additional GO biological processes and eukaryotic clades, will extend stoichiometric and ontological coverage to broader physiological states.

Third, Stage 3 was evaluated using a single locally hosted open-weight instruction model across three random seeds, and the $568$ high-confidence pathway gap fills across $97$ uncultured MAGs (as well as the extremophile pathway completions in the Empty Quarter cohort) represent ranked computational hypotheses. Benchmarking additional open-weight model families on the de-identified 120-anomaly suite, evaluating zero-shot Stage 3 against supervised post-tool classifiers under a leave-clade-out anomaly split, implementing an adaptive router that dispatches binary in-distribution promotions to the $>100$-fold faster supervised classifier while reserving multi-turn language-model reasoning for novel clades and ancestral GO relaxations, and validating top-ranked MAG and extremophile enzyme candidates via heterologous expression and mutant fitness assays will establish both computational generality and experimental confirmation.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and four-stage architecture}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ denote the protein-coding genes of a target genome with NCBI Taxonomy identifier $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and let $\mathcal{K} = \{(c_i, s_i, e_i, \zeta_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ denote their genomic coordinates (contig $c_i$, start $s_i$, end $e_i$, and strand $\zeta_i \in \{+, -\}$). Let $\mathcal{T}$ denote the Gene Ontology (GO) directed acyclic graph under the true-path relation $t \sqsubseteq a$ (\texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of}), with ancestor closure $\text{Anc}(t)$, $\mathcal{M}$ the universal metabolic reaction network with stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and reaction--EC--GO mappings, and $\mathcal{O}$ the formal taxonomic and cross-ontology anatomical axiom set. GSPA estimates a proteome-wide posterior probability matrix $\mathbf{P} \in [0, 1]^{N \times |\mathcal{T}|}$ (and corresponding EC probabilities in enzyme benchmarks) conditioned jointly on $\mathcal{K}$, $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, $\mathcal{M}$, and $\mathcal{O}$ across four sequential stages (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): Stage 0 calibrates and pools per-protein evidence channels; Stage 1 performs iterative whole-proteome Bayesian log-odds refinement; Stage 2A applies deterministic symbolic constraint enforcement with semantic ancestor relaxation while Stage 2B performs positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) and competitive gene-locus attribution; and Stage 3 applies selective tool-mediated neuro-symbolic reasoning verified by a symbolic ontology guardrail. Supplementary Table~S1 summarizes all eight benchmark suites, cohorts, Stage 0 channel configurations, and active pipeline stages.

\subsection{Stage 0: multi-channel evidence calibration and correlation-grouped Noisy-OR pooling}

Stage 0 ingests functional claims from either a standalone predictor (such as CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, or TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}) or up to seven complementary per-protein channels $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$: (1) DIAMOND v2.1.8 sequence alignment against experimentally annotated Swiss-Prot \citep{buchfink2021sensitive}; (2) HMMER 3.4 profile HMM search against Pfam-A mapped via Pfam2GO \citep{eddy2011accelerated}; (3) InterProScan v5.68-100.0 signature integration \citep{jones2014interproscan}; (4) ESM-2 (650M or 3B) embedding $k$-nearest-neighbor and multilayer perceptron transfer \citep{lin2023evolutionary}; (5) ProtT5-XL-U50 embedding $k$-nearest-neighbor transfer \citep{elnaggar2021prottrans}; (6) neural sequence classifiers (ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, 16-kernel DeepGOCNN and DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, DeepGOWeb \citep{kulmanov2021deepgoweb}, and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}); and (7) structural similarity transfer via FoldSeek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and ProstT5 3Di structural tokens \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}.

Each raw claim $c = (p, f, s(c), e(c))$ with raw score $s(c) \in [0, 1]$ is mapped to a calibrated probability via Platt logistic scaling, $\tilde{q}(c) = \text{clip}\bigl(\sigma_{\text{log}}(a_{e(c)}\, s(c) + b_{e(c)}),\, \varepsilon_{\text{cal}},\, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\bigr)$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}} = 10^{-4}$, and weighted by channel reliability $r_{e(c)} \in (0, 1]$ to give $q(c) = r_{e(c)}\, \tilde{q}(c)$ (channel parameters and reliabilities in Supplementary Section~S1). To prevent double-counting correlated evidence sources, claims for each pair $(p, f)$ are partitioned into correlation groups $\mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)$ by evidence modality (homology, protein-sequence ML, structure, genomic context, genomic language models, inferred context, sequence motifs, localization, and domain-specific predictors; Supplementary Section~S1). Stage 0 takes the maximum weighted probability within each group (with an optional second-opinion weight $\beta_{\text{so}} = 0.0$ by default) and pools across independent groups via Noisy-OR:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    q_g(p, f) &= \text{clip}\Bigl(\max_{c \in g} \bigl(r_{e(c)} \tilde{q}(c)\bigr) + \beta_{\text{so}} \, q_{g,(2)}(p, f), \, 10^{-3}, \, 0.999\Bigr), \\
    P_0(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(1 - \prod_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)} \bigl(1 - q_g(p, f)\bigr), \, 10^{-6}, \, 1 - 10^{-6}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
Stage 0 converts $P_0(p, f)$ into the anchored log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f) = \text{clip}\bigl(\log \frac{P_0(p, f)}{1 - P_0(p, f)}, -12, 12\bigr)$ and propagates probabilities upward along GO \texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of} edges.

\subsection{Stage 1: whole-proteome iterative Bayesian log-odds refinement}

Stage 1 refines the proteome-wide probability matrix $\mathbf{P}^{(k)}$ over iterations $k = 1, \dots, K_{\max}$ by adding six genome-scale prior log-odds shifts $B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr)$ to the fixed Stage 0 anchor $L^{(0)}(p, f)$ under Jacobi damping:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(L^{(0)}(p, f) + \sum_{m=1}^{6} w_m \, B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr), \, [-12, 12]\right), \\
    L^{(k)}(p, f) &= (1 - \gamma_{\text{iter}}) L^{(k-1)}(p, f) + \gamma_{\text{iter}} \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f), \qquad P^{(k)}(p, f) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(k)}(p, f)\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
with unit module weights $w_m = 1.0$, damping factor $\gamma_{\text{iter}} = 0.50$, and maximum iterations $K_{\max} = 6$. Iteration terminates when the mean absolute probability change across updated entries falls below $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-3}$, and reverts to the last stable iterate if the mean change increases over two consecutive steps. All 18 configurable Stage 1 and Stage 2B hyperparameters were calibrated on \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 and held frozen across all other species (Supplementary Section~S2 and Supplementary Table~S3). The six priors are defined as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Essentiality prior ($B_1$, $\alpha_{\text{ess}} = 1.50$):} Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the domain-specific core essential GO profile and $\mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ the essential categories with $\max_p P^{(0)}(p, e) \le 0.50$. For any term $f$ in the downward subclass closure $\mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) = \bigcup_{e \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})} \{t \in \mathcal{T} : t \sqsubseteq e\}$, candidate claims receive $B_1(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{ess}} \, \mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})]$.
  \item \textit{Pathway and heteromeric complex coherence prior ($B_2$, $\alpha_{\text{coh}} = 1.00$):} Missing steps of active GO \texttt{has\_part} processes receive $w_{\text{proc}}(f) = \max(0, 1 - c_{\text{proc}})$, and missing steps of triggered reference pathways $W$ with step coverage $c_W \in (0, 1)$ receive $w_{\text{path}}(f) = \max_{W \ni f} \max(0.25, 1 - c_W)$. For an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ with $N_{\text{strong}}(f)$ proteins at $P^{(0)} > 0.50$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f)$ candidates at $\max(P^{(0)}, P^{(k-1)}) \ge 0.08$, candidate partners receive $w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f) = 0.85$ when $N_{\text{strong}} = 1 \wedge N_{\text{cand}} \ge 2$ (and $0.5525$ when $N_{\text{strong}} \ge 2$), whereas isolated strong singletons ($N_{\text{strong}} = 1 \wedge N_{\text{cand}} \le 1$) receive $-0.75$. Therefore, $B_2(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{coh}}\bigl(\max(w_{\text{proc}}(f), w_{\text{path}}(f)) + w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f)\bigr)$.
  \item \textit{Taxonomic consistency prior ($B_3$, $\alpha_{\text{cons}} = 0.68$):} Active terms $\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)} = \{t \in \mathcal{T} : \max_p P^{(k-1)}(p, t) > 0.50\}$ are checked against direct GO taxon constraints and SAT4J unsatisfiable cores over multi-parent \texttt{is\_a} and disjointness axioms. Any claim whose term or ancestor lies in the conflict set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)}$ receives $B_3(p, f) = -\alpha_{\text{cons}}$.
  \item \textit{Anatomical prior ($B_4$, $\alpha_{\text{anat}} = 0.68$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}} = 0.65$):} Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ denote GO terms forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ through the GO--Uberon/PO/CL bridge (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}) and $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ valid non-root anatomical structures supported by at least two proteins at $P^{(0)} \ge 0.50$. Claims intersecting $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ receive $B_4(p, f) = -0.68$; valid claims linked to $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ receive $+0.65$.
  \item \textit{Metabolic gap-filling and reaction-local genomic context prior ($B_5$, $\alpha_{\text{gap}} = 1.50$):} Candidate terms matching open metabolic network gaps receive $B_5(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{gap}}$ ($1.05$ for low-homology gapseq proposals). For each gap reaction $R$, reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) identifies pathway-neighboring GO terms at 1--2 reaction hops ($\alpha_{\text{rxn}} = 0.50$), weights chromosomal anchor genes $p'$ with $P(p', g) \ge 0.30$ by $w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) = \max_g \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} P(p', g)$, and accumulates Gaussian kernel density $K_{\text{bp}}(\Delta\text{bp}) = \exp(-\Delta\text{bp}^2 / (2h_{\text{bp}}^2))$ ($h_{\text{bp}} = 5{,}000$~bp):
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \sum_{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 4 h_{\text{bp}}} w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) \, K_{\text{bp}}\bigl(\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')\bigr), \\
      S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \log D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) + \beta_{\text{div}} \text{Div}(p) - \gamma_{\text{com}} \text{Commit}(p) - \delta_{\text{self}} P(p, f_R) + \varepsilon_{\text{str}} \text{Strand}(p) - \zeta_{\text{gap}} \log_{10}\!\bigl(\max(1, \Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p))\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rlgc}
  \end{equation}
  with neighbor-reaction diversity weight $\beta_{\text{div}} = 1.0$, commitment penalty $\gamma_{\text{com}} = 1.0$, self-annotation penalty $\delta_{\text{self}} = 2.0$, same-strand bonus $\varepsilon_{\text{str}} = 0.2$, and minimum-distance penalty $\zeta_{\text{gap}} = 0.1$. In LRO enzyme attribution, $S_{\text{RLGC}}$ is combined with orthogroup co-occurrence, Pfam domain compatibility, and (in Track B) Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction-centroid cosine similarity.
  \item \textit{Co-directional operon prior ($B_6$, $\alpha_{\text{ctx}} = 1.00$, $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}} = 1.50$, $w_{\text{op\_cplx}} = 0.95$):} Within a co-directional operon $O$ ($|O| \ge 2$) having active pathway fraction $c_O(W) = k_O(W)/|O|$ for pathway $W$, a candidate $(p, f)$ receives $B_6(p, f) = \max_{W \ni f : k_O(W) \ge 1} \bigl(\alpha_{\text{ctx}} c_O(W) \alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}}^{\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}]}\bigr) + w_{\text{op\_cplx}}\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}} \wedge \exists p' \in O \setminus \{p\} : P^{(k-1)}(p', f) > 0.50]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical constraint bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}

To enforce anatomical and taxonomic consistency across domains, we extracted five programmatic constraint tables from the official OBO Foundry releases of GO, Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon}, Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, and NCBI Taxonomy (Supplementary Section~S3 and Supplementary Table~S7): (1) $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon constraints ($13{,}819$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $13{,}092$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across $18{,}159$ GO terms \citep{deegan2010formalization}; (2) $2{,}715$ cross-ontology equivalence and subclass axioms linking $2{,}674$ GO terms to $1{,}294$ Uberon, PO, and CL classes across $30$ relations (including \texttt{results\_in\_development\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_morphogenesis\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_formation\_of}, \texttt{results\_in\_maturation\_of}, and \texttt{occurs\_in}); (3) $8{,}331$ taxon constraints ($4{,}767$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $3{,}564$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) over $1{,}247$ Uberon, PO, and CL anatomical classes; (4) $7{,}722$ transitive \texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of} edges over $2{,}855$ anatomical classes; and (5) $1{,}818$ taxonomic lineage edges over $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades. A GO term $t$ is entailed as anatomically forbidden in taxon $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ whenever $t$ (or a GO ancestor of $t$) links to an anatomical class $u$ whose anatomical ancestor closure carries an \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} or \texttt{never\_in\_taxon} rule incompatible with the taxonomic lineage of $\tau_{\text{tax}}$.

\subsection{Stage 2A: deterministic symbolic enforcement and semantic ancestor relaxation}

After Stage 1 convergence, Stage 2A deterministically enforces all direct GO taxon constraints, cross-ontology anatomical constraints, and SAT4J logical consistency axioms prior to final upward GO ancestor closure, ensuring that clade-incompatible leaf claims are resolved before parent terms are populated. For each protein $p$ and forbidden term $t$, Stage 2A traverses $\text{Anc}(t)$ (excluding the three GO aspect roots) to identify the minimal valid ancestor set $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ comprising the most specific ancestors of $t$ that are both taxonomically and anatomically admissible in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$. Reconstructing the unpenalized pre-conflict probability $P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)$ from $L^{(0)}(p, t)$ and positive Stage 1 priors, semantic ancestor relaxation transfers this support to each minimal valid ancestor and zeroes the forbidden term:
\begin{align}
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a) &= \max\Bigl(P^{(K)}(p, a), \, \gamma_{\text{relax}} \, P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)\Bigr), \qquad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}}), \nonumber \\
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) &= 0,
  \label{eq:relax}
\end{align}
with $\gamma_{\text{relax}} = 1.0$, followed by upward true-path ancestor closure over admissible terms. In addition, for any obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ with a single protein at $P \ge 0.50$ and zero partner candidates at $P \ge 0.08$ across the genome, Stage 2A attenuates the isolated singleton to $0.45$.

\subsection{Stage 2B: role-aware reaction support and positive-cost lexicographic pFBA}
\label{sec:methods_fba}

Stage 2B operates over a sanitized universal metabolic network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ derived from ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed} ($47{,}880$ reactions and $33{,}978$ metabolites, of which $14{,}243$ carry EC mappings and $11{,}826$ are mass- and charge-balanced enzymatic reactions; $2{,}417$ pool or elementally imbalanced reactions receive penalty $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100.0$). Each reaction $j$ is associated with a disjunctive normal form (DNF) catalytic rule $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j) = \{m_1, \dots, m_{K_j}\}$ over alternative isozyme mechanisms $m$, where each mechanism requires a conjunction of subunit roles $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m) = \{u_1, \dots, u_{D_m}\}$. Given candidate locus probabilities $s_{p, u} \in (0, 1)$, Stage 2B aggregates the top $M_{\text{iso}} = 3$ candidates per role via Noisy-OR, combines required subunits via a bottleneck-gated geometric mean at $\theta_{\text{sub}} = 0.25$, and takes the maximum across isozyme mechanisms:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q(u) &= 1 - \prod\limits_{p \in \text{Top-}M_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u))} \bigl(1 - s_{p, u}\bigr), \\
    S_{\text{mech}}(m) &= \begin{cases}
      \Bigl(\prod\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u)\Bigr)^{1 / D_m}, & \!\min\limits_{u} Q(u) \ge \theta_{\text{sub}}, \\
      \min\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u), & \!\text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases} \qquad S_{\text{rxn}}(j) = \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)} S_{\text{mech}}(m).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gpr_agg}
\end{equation}

Reactions with $S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge \tau_{\text{core}} = 0.50$, together with open medium exchanges and spontaneous reactions, form the draft core $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$. Candidate repair reactions $j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$ receive a strictly positive addition cost $c_j^{\text{add}} = \lambda_{\text{add}}(1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j)) + w_{\text{imb}}(j) + \epsilon_{\text{par}}$ ($\lambda_{\text{add}} = 1.0$, $\epsilon_{\text{par}} = 10^{-3}$). Splitting reversible fluxes into non-negative components $v_j = v_j^+ - v_j^-$ and setting the biomass target on medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$ to $v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \max(\alpha_{\text{bio}} v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}), 0.1)$ ($\alpha_{\text{bio}} = 0.10$), Stage 2B solves a two-step lexicographic pFBA \citep{lewis2010omic} using the HiGHS linear programming solver (feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$). Step 1 minimizes the repair cost $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}}(v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ (solving first over balanced reactions and admitting penalized imbalanced reactions only if infeasible) to obtain optimal repair cost $C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$. Step 2 minimizes total non-biomass flux $\sum_{j \ne \text{bio}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}}(v_j^+ + v_j^-) \le (1 + 10^{-6})C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) + 10^{-9}$. The active repair set $\mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} : |v_j^{\text{pFBA}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})| > 10^{-6}\}$ is evaluated by flux-variability ($1.01 C^*$), reaction-knockout ($1.10 C^*$), and five-replicate cost-perturbation ($\pm 5\%$) certificates to yield the flux confidence $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 0.5\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}] + 0.3\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}] + 0.2\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in [0, 1]$ (Supplementary Section~S4 and Supplementary Table~S12).

\subsection{Stage 2B: competitive gene-locus responsibility and substrate probing}
\label{sec:methods_slr}

For each repaired reaction $j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}$ and required role $u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m_j^*)$, Stage 2B distributes locus responsibility competitively across candidate genes $p \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)$ and an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ with prior mass $\eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$:
\begin{align}
  w_{j, u}(p) &= \bigl(o_{p, u}\bigr)^{\beta_1} \, \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)^{\beta_2} \, \exp\bigl(\beta_3 C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) + \beta_4 S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)\bigr), \nonumber \\
  r_{j, u}(p) &= \frac{w_{j, u}(p)}{\eta_{\text{null}} + \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)} w_{j, u}(p')},
  \label{eq:responsibility}
\end{align}
where $o_{p, u} = s_{p, u}/(1 - s_{p, u})$ is the sequence odds, $\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)$ is the RLGC operon Bayes factor, $C_{\text{syn}}(p, u)$ is the operon and pathway synteny score, $S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)$ is the language-model similarity score, and $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = 1.0, \beta_3 = \beta_4 = 1.5$. The backward log-odds update to locus $p$ is $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}(p, u) = \gamma_{\text{FBA}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(u)} \pi_j^{\text{FBA}} r_{j, u}(p) (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j))$ with $\gamma_{\text{FBA}} = 2.20$, applied across damped outer iterations $\lambda_t = \lambda_0 \gamma_{\text{damp}}^{t-1}$ ($\lambda_0 = 0.70, \gamma_{\text{damp}} = 0.60$). For catabolic enzymes in Price-149, Stage 2B supplements minimal-glucose pFBA with cognate-substrate stoichiometric neighborhood probing (rewarding candidate reactions that share non-currency metabolites at 1--2 reaction hops with chromosomal neighbors within $\pm 10$ genes) and blends single-predictor and five-predictor ensemble posteriors (complete equations in Supplementary Section~S4). Held-out metabolic reactions are classified by five binary recoverability indicators: structural presence and mass balance in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}}$), medium-specific flux demand ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}}$, partitioning core biomass Stratum A, cognate-substrate catabolic Stratum B, and secondary Stratum C), isozyme uniqueness ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}}$), operon support ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{operon}}$), and multi-subunit partner presence ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{complex}}$).

\subsection{Stage 3: selective neuro-symbolic annotation completion}

Stage 3 is triggered selectively on four post-Stage-2 anomaly categories: (1) pathway gaps (pathway completeness $\ge 0.50$ with candidate posteriors in $[0.05, 0.50)$; $\ge 0.65$ and $[0.15, 0.49]$ in the controlled 120-anomaly benchmark); (2) incomplete obligate heteromeric complexes (one active subunit at $P \ge 0.50$ and sub-threshold partner candidates); (3) borderline uncovered essential categories; and (4) borderline taxonomic or anatomical conflicts ($P \in [0.35, 0.62]$) amenable to ancestral GO relaxation. Stage 3 evaluates at most $8$ candidates per anomaly and $150$ anomalies per genome.

Each anomaly is stripped of evaluation labels and gene symbols and processed in a bounded multi-turn tool-calling loop (maximum $6$ turns; $2$ turns in the fast mode) by a locally hosted FP8-quantized 27-billion-parameter instruction model from the Qwen open-weight family \citep{yang2024qwen25} served via vLLM ($32{,}768$-token context, temperature $0.0$, maximum $450$ output tokens per turn, evaluated across seeds $42, 43$, and $44$). The model invokes five deterministic read-only tools to inspect the operon neighborhood, inspect the pathway or complex gap, query protein evidence and domain signatures, check symbolic ontology constraints, and propose a structured resolution (\textsc{promote}, \textsc{relax}, \textsc{demote}, or \textsc{abstain}). Every proposal is verified by the deterministic symbolic guardrail against the $48{,}101$ GO term stanzas, $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades, $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon rules, and $2{,}715$ GO--Uberon/PO/CL axioms before acceptance (tool schemas, de-identification protocol, and per-seed results in Supplementary Section~S6 and Supplementary Table~S14).

\subsection{Experimental phenotype prediction in BacDive}
\label{sec:methods_bacdive}

We evaluated organism-level phenotype prediction across $2{,}332$ carbon-source utilization assays in $529$ ProTraits-matched bacterial strains, $17{,}496$ diagnostic enzyme assays in $3{,}017$ BacDive type strains, and $50{,}075$ assays for $10$ diagnostic enzymes across $6{,}313$ strains \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac}. Given binary pathway or reaction calls $g(s, y), m(s, y), c(s, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ from gapseq v1.2 \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, ModelSEED v2.6.1 \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, and CarveMe v1.5.2 \citep{machado2018fast} for strain $s$ and assay $y$, we form the blended matrix $B(s, y)$ ($0.55g + 0.30m + 0.15c$ for carbon; $0.55g + 0.28m + 0.17c$ for enzymes), column-center each assay $\tilde{B}_{s, y} = B(s, y) - \bar{B}_y$, and compute the row-normalized non-negative cosine coupling matrix $\Psi(y, y')$ with zero diagonal to obtain the unsupervised cross-assay coupling score $\psi(s, y) = \sum_{y'} \Psi(y, y')(B(s, y') - 0.5)$. In blind Track 1 ($0\%$ phenotype labels), binary tool calls are mapped to calibrated probabilities $p_{\text{gap}}, p_{\text{ms}}, p_{\text{cm}}$ and pooled in log-odds space:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\!\Bigl(w_{\text{gap}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{gap}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{ms}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{ms}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{cm}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{cm}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_\psi\,\psi(s, y)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:bacdive_t1}
\end{equation}
with weights $(0.56, 0.28, 0.16, 0.65)$ for carbon and $(0.55, 0.27, 0.18, 0.55)$ for enzymes at threshold $0.50$. Supervised baselines (Traitar L1-SVM \citep{weimann2016traitar}, BacDive Random Forest \citep{schober2025bac}, and DiASPora GBDT \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}) and the GSPA + DiASPora hybrid were evaluated under five-fold genus-blocked cross-validation with all calibration, prevalence, and coupling parameters estimated strictly within training folds. In Track 2, $30\%$ of each strain's assays are revealed to condition the Bayesian anchor $P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y)$ on revealed substrate co-occurrence or genus-level EC posteriors, and predictions are evaluated on the held-out $70\%$ (complete parameterization in Supplementary Section~S5 and Supplementary Table~S13).

\subsection{Benchmarks, time-based and homology-based split retraining, metrics, and structural audit score}

To evaluate prospective generalization across time and across sequence-similarity regimes on complete genome inputs, we constructed a six-split whole-genome withheld CAFA benchmark across six complete prokaryotic reference proteomes (\textit{B.~subtilis} 168, \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{H.~pylori} 26695, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{M.~jannaschii} DSM 2661; $20{,}697$ coding sequences, $3{,}315$ experimentally annotated target proteins, $1{,}060$ strictly post-$t_0$ targets, $3{,}809$ operons, and $1{,}236$ metabolic gaps) alongside a multi-clade isolated-protein CAFA benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:cafa_splits} and Supplementary Tables~S4 and S15). In the two whole-genome time-based splits, post-$t_0$ targets ($1{,}060$ proteins) are evaluated either with pre-$t_0$ experimental annotations on the six chromosomes retained in training to test chromosomal rollover context (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Rollover}, $82{,}404$ training proteins) or with all $2{,}524$ experimentally annotated proteins from the six species purged from training (\texttt{WG\_Time\_Withheld}, $79{,}880$ training proteins). In the four whole-genome homology splits, all six species are purged from training and evaluated across all $3{,}315$ experimental target proteins after additionally purging all cross-species training homologs sharing pairwise sequence identity $> \eta_{\text{id}}$ with any protein in the six withheld genomes (\texttt{WG\_Homology\_le100}: $79{,}880$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le70}: $79{,}457$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le50}: $76{,}405$ train; \texttt{WG\_Homology\_le30}: $61{,}323$ train). On the two time-based splits, we additionally evaluate the official pre-trained weights of DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure} and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}. On every split, all 14 trainable per-protein baselines (term-frequency prior, filtered DIAMOND database, $L_2$-regularized InterPro domain logistic regression, canonical 16-kernel 1D convolutional DeepGOCNN with $k \in \{8, 16, \dots, 128\} \times 512$ filters $= 8{,}192$ features, DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, 2,560-dimensional ESM-2 3B \texttt{esm2\_t36\_3B\_UR50D} $k$-NN index \citep{littmann2021embeddings}, ESM-2 3B MLP, hierarchical ESM-2 3B C-HMCNN with max-constraint loss \citep{giunchiglia2020coherent}, DeepFRI graph convolutional and language-model architecture \citep{gligorijevic2021structure}, DeepGraphGO two-layer graph convolutional network over the DIAMOND sequence-similarity graph with InterPro node features \citep{you2021deepgraphgo}, TALE+ transformer encoder with hierarchical GO label-embedding joint attention and DIAMOND fusion \citep{cao2021tale}, SPROF-GO language-model self-attention pooling with hierarchical label diffusion and homology transfer \citep{yuan2023fast}, DeepGO-SE five-model approximate semantic entailment ensemble over $\mathcal{EL}^{++}$ description-logic embeddings \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, and DPFunc InterPro domain cross-attention with graph convolutional layers \citep{ding2025dpfunc}) together with our out-of-fold GSPA-Seq meta-stacker are retrained from scratch on that split's filtered training set before running whole-genome inference across all $20{,}697$ coding sequences in GSPA Stages 0--2. For the pan-phylum AllTheBacteria and reference eukaryote analysis ($72{,}533$ complete genomes across $11{,}632$ species and $37$ phyla \citep{hunt2024allthebacteria}; Supplementary Section~S7 and Supplementary Table~S17), coding sequences across $73$ assembly batches ($315{,}671{,}487$ proteins) were deduplicated by SHA-256 sequence hashing ($73{,}866{,}871$ unique sequences) and clustered via \texttt{MMseqs2 linclust} \citep{steinegger2018clustering} at $90\%$ sequence identity and $80\%$ coverage ($36{,}804{,}001$ non-redundant cluster representatives), scored with our full GSPA-Seq stacker, mapped back to each chromosome's co-directional operon layout, and integrated in shared-ontology multi-genome batch mode (\texttt{gspa-unified.jar integrate --batch-manifest}). Across whole-proteome benchmarks, donor-homology masking removes reference proteins sharing $\text{pident}(g, r) > \eta_{\text{id}}$ ($\eta_{\text{id}} \in \{100\%, 70\%, 50\%, 30\%\}$) or belonging to the test organism's genus or family across DIAMOND databases, ESM-2 and ProtT5 neighbor indices, FoldSeek structural templates, and calibration sets. In leave-reaction-out benchmarks, Track A masks sequence alignments and embedding donors with cosine similarity $> 0.50$ without reaction centroids, while Track B masks sequence alignments and retains Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids.

We report official CAFA information-accretion-weighted $f_w$ ($WF_{\max}$), protein-centric unweighted $F_{\max}$, pair-level micro $F_{\max}$, and minimum semantic distance $S_{\min}$ \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,clark2013information} over threshold grids $\theta \in \{0.01, \dots, 0.99\}$ ($\{0.10, \dots, 0.90\}$ in the eukaryotic stress test), alongside Hit@$k$ and mean reciprocal rank (MRR) for candidate ranking, $95\%$ bootstrap or exact Clopper--Pearson confidence intervals, and two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank or exact binomial McNemar tests (Supplementary Section~S5). All CPU benchmarks ran on dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 nodes (8 threads per genome), with PLM, 16-kernel DeepGOCNN, ESM-2 3B, external SOTA GO baseline retraining, and Stage 3 LLM inference executed on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB, NVIDIA H100-80GB, and NVIDIA H200-140GB GPUs.

The Genome-Scale Annotation Evaluation Framework (GAEF) summarizes structural proteome invariants at $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$ by combining essential function completeness $C_{\text{ess}}$ ($w_{\text{ess}} = 0.30$), functional pathway/complex/process coherence $C_{\text{func}}$ ($w_{\text{func}} = 0.40$), taxonomic and anatomical consistency $C_{\text{cons}} = \max(0, 1 - 0.01 V(\mathcal{P}))$ ($w_{\text{cons}} = 0.30$), and functional resolution $C_{\text{res}} = \min(1, \text{meanIC}/10)$ ($w_{\text{res}} = 0.0$):
\begin{equation}
  \text{GAEF}(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{\sum_{d \in \{\text{ess}, \text{func}, \text{cons}, \text{res}\} : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d \, C_d}{\sum_{d : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d},
  \label{eq:gaef}
\end{equation}
which equals $0.30 C_{\text{ess}} + 0.40 C_{\text{func}} + 0.30 C_{\text{cons}}$ when all three structural dimensions are active. Because GAEF measures the structural invariants targeted by Stages 1 and 2, we report it as an internal structural audit score alongside independent held-out functional and experimental phenotype evaluations.
