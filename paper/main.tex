\section{Introduction}

Automated annotation of protein function from genome assemblies is a central problem in computational biology, underpinning comparative genomics, genome-scale metabolic modeling, and systems biology \citep{radivojac2013large,zhou2019cafa}. Given the protein-coding sequences $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ of an organism, their chromosomal coordinates and operon organization $\mathcal{K}$, the organism's taxonomic lineage $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and reference biochemical and ontological knowledge bases, genome-scale annotation seeks a joint assignment of Gene Ontology (GO) \citep{thomas2019gene} terms and Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers across $\mathcal{P}$ that is both accurate at the level of individual proteins and globally coherent at the level of cellular physiology. A biologically valid proteome annotation satisfies five structural invariants: (1) core cellular processes required for viability in the organism's domain of life (such as translation, tRNA aminoacylation, and DNA replication initiation) are represented; (2) metabolic pathways active in the organism form stoichiometrically connected reaction routes capable of supporting biomass synthesis \citep{osterman2003missing,karp2018how}; (3) obligate heteromeric protein complexes (such as ATP synthase, NADH dehydrogenase, urease, and photosystem reaction centers \citep{meldal2022complexportal,giurgiu2019corum}) are annotated as complete multi-subunit assemblies; (4) assigned GO terms satisfy formal taxonomic constraints (\texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) \citep{deegan2010formalization}; and (5) developmental and physiological biological processes, such as \textit{heart development} (\texttt{GO:0007507}), \textit{flower development} (\texttt{GO:0009908}), \textit{neural crest cell migration} (\texttt{GO:0001755}), and \textit{vascular transport} (\texttt{GO:0010232}), entail only anatomical structures present in the target clade \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon}.

Existing computational methods address individual components of this problem through five methodological classes. First, production genome annotation pipelines, including the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) \citep{tatusova2016ncbi,li2021refseq}, Bakta \citep{schwengers2021bakta}, Prokka \citep{seemann2014prokka}, eggNOG-mapper \citep{cantalapiedra2021eggnog}, Pharokka \citep{bouras2023pharokka}, and InterProScan \citep{jones2014interproscan,paysanlafosse2023interpro}, combine sequence alignment \citep{buchfink2021sensitive}, profile hidden Markov models (HMMs) \citep{eddy2011accelerated}, and curated orthology rules to assign high-specificity labels protein by protein, leaving divergent orthologs below fixed sequence thresholds unannotated. Second, deep learning and protein language model (PLM) predictors \citep{lin2023evolutionary,elnaggar2021prottrans,heinzinger2024prostt5} evaluated in the Critical Assessment of Functional Annotation (CAFA) challenges \citep{jiang2016expanded,ramola2023cafa4,kaluza2024cafa5}, including DeepGOPlus \citep{kulmanov2020deepgoplus}, DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021structure}, metagenomic-deepFRI \citep{bezshapkin2026scaling}, DeepGOZero \citep{kulmanov2022deepgozero}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, SPROF-GO \citep{yuan2023fast}, CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, and TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}, predict calibrated GO or EC probabilities from individual sequences or structures \citep{vankempen2024foldseek}. Because both paradigms score each protein $p_i \in \mathcal{P}$ conditionally independently of the remaining proteome $\mathcal{P} \setminus \{p_i\}$, twilight-zone enzymes ($<30\%$ sequence identity) leave metabolic pathway holes, promiscuous domain hits trigger isolated singleton subunit calls on heteromeric complexes, and distant homology transfers propagate clade-inappropriate taxonomic and anatomical GO terms. Third, comparative genomics and genomic language models capture gene neighborhood conservation, co-directional operon structure, and phylogenetic profiles \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,szklarczyk2023string,hwang2024genomic,cornman2024omg,nguyen2024sequence,toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised}, while metabolic reconstruction tools such as Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian,latendresse2014efficiently}, ModelSEED \citep{henry2010high,seaver2021modelseed}, ProbAnno \citep{benedict2014probanno}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind,price2022gapmind}, Reconstructor \citep{jenior2023reconstructor}, and CanOE \citep{smith2012canoe} use stoichiometric connectivity to fill reaction gaps \citep{price2023carveme}. However, reaction-level MILP gap-fillers such as CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast} and our prior reaction-indicator MILP formulation METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} optimize binary reaction indicators $y_j \in \{0, 1\}$ with a uniform reaction-level score, assign negative objective costs ($c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) to high-scoring candidate reactions (which activates hundreds of zero-flux reaction additions), and apply uniform reaction-to-protein probability boosts across all candidate genes of a reaction \citep{niu2026meteor}. Fourth, bio-ontology engineering provides formal taxon constraints \citep{deegan2010formalization} and cross-product logical definitions linking GO biological processes to the Uberon multi-species anatomy ontology \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon,hill2010representing,khodiyar2011representation}, the Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, and the Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, while existing post-filters delete violating terms together with their valid ancestral biological process annotations. Fifth, tool-using biological language agents such as GeneAgent \citep{wang2025_geneagent}, ProtAgents \citep{ghafarollahi2024_protagents}, SPIRES \citep{caufield2024_spires}, GORetriever \citep{yan2024_goretriever}, and ProtST \citep{xu2023_protst} synthesize multi-modal genomic evidence when invoked selectively on ambiguous loci and paired with formal symbolic verification.

Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA v2.1.0), a unified four-stage framework that resolves each of these genome-scale failure modes (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Stage 0 calibrates and pools per-protein evidence channels via correlation-grouped Noisy-OR (or ingests any single standalone GO or EC predictor) to establish calibrated baseline likelihoods. Stage 1 iteratively refines annotation log-odds across the whole proteome using six genome-scale priors: core essentiality, pathway and heteromeric complex coherence, SAT-based taxonomic consistency, GO--Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints, metabolic gap-filling with reaction-local genomic context (RLGC), and operon neighborhood coupling. Stage 2 combines a deterministic symbolic enforcer (Stage 2A) that resolves taxonomic and anatomical contradictions by relaxing forbidden terms to their nearest valid non-root GO ancestors and pruning isolated complex singletons, with a flux-certified stoichiometric locus refiner (Stage 2B) that couples role-aware disjunctive normal form (DNF) mechanism aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious FBA (pFBA) \citep{lewis2010omic}, and backward locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$. Stage 3 provides an optional, selectively triggered neuro-symbolic agent using an open-weight instruction language model (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}, served as \texttt{qwen3.8-27b}) that inspects the $<2.5\%$ of proteins with unresolved pathway gaps, incomplete complexes, or borderline conflicts under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail.

We evaluate GSPA across eight benchmark suites spanning bacteria, archaea, viruses, and multicellular eukaryotes (Tables~\ref{tab:ablation}--\ref{tab:suites}; Supplementary Sections~S1--S5 and Supplementary Tables~S1--S8). Across these benchmarks, GSPA eliminates $100\%$ of taxonomic and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical violations ($1{,}009$ endogenous and $1{,}452$ stress-test eukaryotic violations) while outperforming naive deletion in transfer-evaluated micro $F_{\max}$ across multicellular animals and vascular plants ($+0.0006$ to $+0.0157$), certifies $100\%$ of pFBA reaction additions ($5.70$ reactions/genome with $0.0\%$ zero-flux padding vs.\ $262.34$ [$83.72\%$] uncertified zero-flux additions and $51.01$ flux-carrying additions/genome under negative-cost MILP) across 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions, improves \texttt{Price-149} RB-TnSeq enzyme Top-1 hits under both single-predictor refinement (CLEAN $60 \to 63$, MAPred $50 \to 56$, DeepProZyme $49 \to 51$, GraphEC $18 \to 20$) and multi-predictor refinement ($+0.0528$ to $+0.1987$ $F_{\max}$), lifts experimental \texttt{BacDive} phenotype prediction to $0.8567$ blind carbon ROC-AUC and $0.8710$--$0.9225$ hybrid $F_1$, and increases ambiguous leave-reaction-out Hit@1 from $34.0\%$ to $82.7\%$ ($3$-seed mean). The GSPA v2.1.0 software, precompiled cross-ontology constraint tables, and benchmark manifests are openly available at \url{https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa}.

We first evaluated how Stage 0 multi-channel pooling and Stages 1--2 genome-scale priors affect protein-centric accuracy and structural genome coherence across nine bacterial and archaeal reference genomes (\textit{Escherichia coli} K-12, \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7, \textit{Bacillus subtilis} 168, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} PAO1, \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis} H37Rv, \textit{Helicobacter pylori} 26695, \textit{Mycoplasma genitalium} G37, \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, and \textit{Methanocaldococcus jannaschii} DSM 2661; 81 runs; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12} and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; sensitivity across the 17 configurable pipeline hyperparameters in Supplementary Section~S1 and Supplementary Table~S1). For high-throughput multi-genome benchmarks (the 9-genome, 13-genome, 88-genome, and 1,000-genome panels), Stage 0 uses the 3 fast sequence/domain channels (DIAMOND, InterProScan, and Pfam HMMER; $5.4$--$12.7\text{ s/genome}$), whereas on the nested 21-genome neural panel (which contains all 13 panel genomes plus 8 additional reference proteomes, and is itself nested within the 88-genome four-kingdom cohort) we evaluate both the 3-channel core and all 7 channels including ESM-2/ProtT5 $k$NN, ProteInfer, and FoldSeek+ProstT5 3Di. Because roughly $85\%$ of annotated proteins in the 9 reference organisms carry high-confidence Swiss-Prot homology ($P_0 \ge 0.90$), Stage 0 Noisy-OR pooling (\texttt{A1\_noisy\_or}) establishes the bulk protein-centric baseline: micro $F_{\max} = 0.7631 \pm 0.0817$ (sample s.d., $\text{ddof}=1$, across 9 genomes; $0.2853 \pm 0.0853$ experimental-only) and CAFA $F_{\max} = 0.8041 \pm 0.0650$ ($0.2363 \pm 0.0947$ experimental-only). Across the full Stage 1+2 pipeline (\texttt{A9\_stage2\_rel}), pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ increases to $0.7660 \pm 0.0791$ ($0.2847 \pm 0.0834$ experimental-only; two-sided exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0391$) while preserving protein-centric CAFA $F_{\max}$ at $0.8037 \pm 0.0647$ ($0.2366 \pm 0.0951$ experimental-only; two-sided exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.1289$, within $0.0001$--$0.0016$ of Stage 0 across 8 of 9 genomes; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Evaluating the 8 out-of-sample genomes (with \textit{E.~coli} K-12 held out as the calibration reference) confirms the same pattern: micro $F_{\max}$ rises from $0.7718$ to $0.7749$, CAFA $F_{\max}$ is $0.8143$ vs.\ $0.8140$ ($0.2140 \to 0.2143$ experimental-only), $C_{\text{path}}$ rises from $0.4834$ to $0.5012$, $C_{\text{cplx}}$ rises from $0.8743$ to $0.8865$, and the internal GAEF structural score rises from $0.8434$ to $0.8599$.

Examining individual priors on the 9-genome panel (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; per-genome breakdown in \texttt{results/pillar1\_2\_ablation\_per\_genome.tsv}), the essentiality prior (\texttt{A2}) lifts mean core functional completeness $C_{\text{ess}}$ from $0.9400 \pm 0.0785$ to $0.9444$ (and to $0.9479 \pm 0.0680$ in combined Stage 1+2 \texttt{A7}--\texttt{A9}, improving by $+0.0400$ on \textit{M.~jannaschii} [$0.9600 \to 1.0000$] and $+0.0312$ on \textit{M.~genitalium} [$0.7500 \to 0.7812$] across the 9 genomes, exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.50$). The coherence prior (\texttt{A3}) increases mean pathway coherence $C_{\text{path}}$ from $0.4975 \pm 0.1089$ to $0.5052$, and the gap-filling (\texttt{A4}) and operon neighborhood (\texttt{A5}) priors raise $C_{\text{path}}$ to $0.4977$ and $0.4992$ individually before combining with pathway/complex coherence in the full Stage 1 model (\texttt{A7}) to lift $C_{\text{path}}$ to $0.5146 \pm 0.1078$ (exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00781$) and complex coherence $C_{\text{cplx}}$ from $0.8636 \pm 0.1346$ to $0.8852 \pm 0.1189$ (improving by $+0.0972$ in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 [$0.7778 \to 0.8750$] and $+0.0972$ in \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7 [$0.7778 \to 0.8750$] across the 9 genomes, exact paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.50$). Adding the consistency and anatomical priors (\texttt{A6}; constraint tables summarized in Supplementary Section~S2 and Supplementary Table~S2) eliminates all 25 baseline taxonomic violations across all nine genomes (\textit{Synechocystis}: 8, \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7: 5, \textit{E.~coli} K-12: 4, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}: 3, and 1 each in \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{H.~pylori}, \textit{M.~genitalium}, \textit{M.~jannaschii}, and \textit{P.~aeruginosa}; $C_{\text{cons}}$ from $0.9722$ to $1.0000$, exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00391$) and increases micro $F_{\max}$ from $0.7631$ to $0.7681$ and CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.8041$ to $0.8071$. Because the 25 prokaryotic violations involve cellular component terms whose nearest valid ancestor is the ontology root, \texttt{A8} (removal) and \texttt{A9} (relaxation) yield identical scores on the prokaryotic panel (internal GAEF composite score rising from $0.8459 \pm 0.0367$ to $0.8643 \pm 0.0376$, exact two-sided paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.00391$; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b), whereas semantic relaxation differentiates multicellular eukaryotic organ and developmental terms (Section~\ref{sec:euk_results}).

To test robustness against noisy cross-clade homology transfer, we injected 900 cross-clade false-positive claims across the nine prokaryotic genomes (100 per genome: 50 eukaryotic anatomical terms such as \texttt{GO:0007507} \textit{heart development}, \texttt{GO:0009908} \textit{flower development}, and \texttt{GO:0001755} \textit{neural crest cell migration}, plus 50 clade-restricted terms) at raw scores in $[0.55, 0.85]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c). We next evaluated GSPA's Stage 1 metabolic gap-filling prior and RLGC scoring engine using a leave-reaction-out (LRO) protocol across \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 ($n=256$ strict reactions, defined as monomeric or homo-oligomeric enzymatic steps with a single non-promiscuous primary EC number, and $n=414$ full role-stratified reactions including multi-EC and peripheral steps) and out-of-sample \textit{B.~subtilis} 168 ($n=144$ reactions; Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism} and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar2\_\allowbreak lro15\_\allowbreak summary.tsv}). In each LRO trial, the ground-truth gene--reaction association for a target reaction $r$ is masked and candidate proteins in the proteome are ranked to recover the masked enzyme. We report both unconditional recovery across all held-out reactions (\textbf{All}, where reactions whose true enzyme received zero Stage 0 candidate matches are scored as misses) and conditional recovery among reactions where the true enzyme is present in the candidate pool (\textbf{Pool}, where $n_{\text{cov}} = 175$ for sequence-only/M2 and $n_{\text{cov}} = 181$ for RLGC/PLM/M4, with a common covered set intersection of $143$ reactions across both candidate-pool definitions). We evaluate two distinct regimes: \textbf{Track A} (where both sequence homologs and ESM-2 reaction-centroid donors with cosine similarity $> 0.50$ to the query protein are masked) and \textbf{Track B} (where sequence alignment hits for the held-out reaction are masked, while Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids remain available).

Decomposing the contributions of genomic context and protein language model embeddings across the 256-case \textit{E.~coli} K-12 LRO benchmark reveals two complementary findings (Table~\ref{tab:metabolism} and Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a,b). First, in the non-PLM and cosine-masked regimes, unfiltered sequence-only candidate ranking and rank-invariant reaction-level MILP boosting (METEOR v8) recover the true enzyme at Rank 1 (Hit@1) in $1.95\%$ of all cases ($95\%$ Clopper--Pearson CI $[0.6\%, 4.5\%]$; $2.86\%$ given pool), strict cosine-masked Track A (masking both sequence homologs and PLM centroids at cosine $> 0.50$) reaches $11.33\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($16.02\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.2605$ All / $0.3685$ Pool), supervised GBDT reranking on Track A reaches $17.97\%$ ($25.41\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.3275$ All / $0.4632$ Pool), unfiltered RLGC operon kernel density alone (without PLM centroids) reaches $23.05\%$ ($95\%$ CI $[18.0\%, 28.7\%]$; $32.60\%$ given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.3355$ All / $0.4745$ Pool), and unfiltered GSPA Stage 1 M2 pathway coherence + RLGC (without PLM centroids) reaches $23.44\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($34.29\%$ given pool). Second, in Track B, per-protein ESM-2 reaction-centroid cosine similarity alone (\texttt{ESM2\_PLM\_Centroids\_Only}) achieves $62.11\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($95\%$ CI $[55.9\%, 68.1\%]$; $67.58\%$ Hit@3, $87.85\%$ Hit@1 given pool, $\text{MRR} = 0.6474$ All / $0.9157$ Pool), accounting for $97.5\%$ of the unconditional Hit@1 of the full model on the well-annotated \textit{E.~coli} proteome because reference Swiss-Prot reaction centroids retain close embedding neighbors. We next audited the mathematical invariants of gap-filling reaction additions across 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions and six curated BiGG reference GEMs (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a; Supplementary Section~S4 and Supplementary Table~S5; \texttt{results/pillar2\_invariant\_audit\_summary.tsv} and \texttt{results/pillar2\_curated6\_gems\_real.tsv}). While a draft reconstruction legitimately contains genome-encoded core reactions ($S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge 0.50$) that are inactive on minimal glucose alone ($2{,}809.5$ active core reactions per genome under raw DeepProZyme $\ge 0.50$, with $0.0$ flux-carrying reactions before repair because all 108 draft networks contain biomass gaps, $2{,}621.7$ imbalanced active reactions, and $9.75$ dead-end active reactions), gap-filling adds non-core candidate reactions ($S_{\text{rxn}}(j) < 0.50$) specifically to restore biomass flux connectivity. Across the 108 genomes, negative-cost MILP gap-filling (METEOR v8 \citep{niu2026meteor}, where candidate reactions with score above $\tau_{\text{add}} = 0.20$ receive negative objective cost $c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) adds a mean of $313.35$ non-core reactions per genome ($3{,}121.0$ total active reactions: $446.4$ flux-carrying and $2{,}674.6$ zero-flux active), of which $51.01$ carry non-zero steady-state flux ($|v_j| > 10^{-6}$) while $262.34$ ($83.72\%$) are uncertified zero-flux padding ($51.01 + 262.34 = 313.35$, with the exact partition identity $\text{added} = \text{flux-certified} + \text{uncertified}$ holding on all $108/108$ genomes in \texttt{results/pillar2\_invariant\_audit\_108.tsv}; $2{,}597.8$ imbalanced active reactions and $2.36$ dead-end active reactions in $313.8\text{ s/genome}$). To evaluate external biological accuracy on experimental strain characterization data, we compared GSPA against automated metabolic reconstruction pipelines (CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, and negative-cost MILP gap-filling METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor}) and supervised genome-to-phenotype classifiers (Traitar L1-SVM \citep{weimann2016traitar}, the Random Forest phenotype baseline of Koblitz et al.\ \citep{koblitz2025diaspora} [\texttt{BacDive RF Baseline}], and DiASPora Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}) across experimental carbon-source growth and diagnostic enzyme assays in the DSMZ BacDive database \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac} (Suites 7 and 8; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}d, Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}, and Supplementary Table~S6; per-fold CV metrics and runtimes in \texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_per\_fold\_cv.tsv}; architectural details in Section~\ref{sec:methods_bacdive}).

First, across $50{,}075$ strain--enzyme assay pairs spanning 10 diagnostic ECs in $6{,}313$ strains (\texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_track1\_summary.tsv} and \texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_enzyme\_per\_ec.tsv}), negative-cost minimal-glucose MILP gap-filling across three parameterizations (\texttt{METEOR v6} with default addition threshold $\tau_{\text{add}}=0.20, n=50{,}075$; \texttt{METEOR a025} with stricter $\tau_{\text{add}}=0.25, n=50{,}075$; and \texttt{METEOR v7} with relaxed core threshold including 30 additional borderline strain--EC pairs, $n=50{,}105$) trades precision for recall: precision drops from $0.6442$ (DeepProZyme baseline, recall $= 0.8105$, $F_1 = 0.7178$, balanced accuracy $= 0.6792$) to $0.6194$--$0.6285$ (recall $= 0.8285$--$0.8640$, $F_1 = 0.7148$--$0.7215$, balanced accuracy dropping to $0.6631$--$0.6670$). Similarly, on the 108-genome non-glucose catabolic panel ($n=1{,}622$ assays, evaluated with \texttt{METEOR v8} using updated MetaNetX reaction mappings), minimal-glucose MILP gap-filling reduces precision from $0.6254$ to $0.6051$, balanced accuracy from $0.5386$ to $0.5101$, and ROC-AUC from $0.5716$ to $0.5484$ ($F_1 = 0.6711$ vs.\ $0.6722$). On obligate heteromeric urease (\texttt{EC 3.5.1.5}, requiring UreA, UreB, and UreC/D/F/G subunits across $n=5{,}026$ assayed strains in the 6,313-strain panel and $n=871$ in the 3,017 type-strain panel), negative-cost MILP reduces precision from $0.4520$ to $0.4320$ and $F_1$ from $0.5748$ to $0.5610$ ($\text{BAcc} = \text{binary ROC-AUC} = 0.7294 \to 0.7184$) by boosting isolated singleton subunits, whereas complex-aware pathway reconstruction (\texttt{gapseq} / \texttt{GSPA Track 1} on the type-strain panel) achieves $F_1 = 0.7626$.

Second, in Track 1 ($2{,}332$ experimental carbon-source growth assays across $529$ strains and $17{,}496$ diagnostic enzyme assays across $3{,}017$ type strains), we separate 100\% blind unsupervised prediction (zero phenotype training labels) from supervised 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive} and Supplementary Table~S6). When precomputed binary reaction/pathway call matrices from \texttt{gapseq}, \texttt{ModelSEED}, and \texttt{CarveMe} are available, \texttt{GSPA Track 1} pools their calibrated log-odds with empirical cross-assay pathway co-occurrence coupling $\psi(s, y)$ in $6.1\text{ s/genome}$ ($651.1\text{ s/genome}$ end-to-end when running all three base reconstructions from scratch; Section~\ref{sec:methods_bacdive}). Under 100\% blind evaluation, \texttt{GSPA Track 1} preserves \texttt{gapseq}'s binary decision boundary at $\theta = 0.50$, matching \texttt{gapseq} on carbon-source binary calls ($\text{Precision} = 0.8071$, $\text{Recall} = 0.7836$, $F_1 = 0.7952$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8082$, vs.\ $F_1 = 0.6474$ for ModelSEED and $0.5132$ for CarveMe) and on the 17,496-assay blind enzyme panel ($\text{Precision} = 0.8393$, $\text{Recall} = 0.7204$, $F_1 = 0.7753$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.7461$, vs.\ $0.5943$ for ModelSEED and $0.4319$ for CarveMe), while providing calibrated continuous confidence scores that lift threshold-swept $\text{ROC-AUC}$ from $0.8082$ (binary) to $0.8567$ ($+0.0485$) on carbon growth and from $0.7461$ to $0.7520$ on blind enzymes. Under strict 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (\texttt{results/pillar3\_bacdive\_per\_fold\_cv.tsv} and \texttt{pillar3\_bacdive\_track1\_summary.tsv}), combining GSPA's continuous posteriors with the supervised classifiers (\texttt{GSPA + DiASPora Hybrid}) improves pooled out-of-genus carbon-source $F_1$ from $0.8448$ (BacDive RF; 5-fold mean $0.8329 \pm 0.0659$), $0.8483$ (DiASPora alone; 5-fold mean $0.8408 \pm 0.0390$), and $0.8571$ (Traitar; 5-fold mean $0.8467 \pm 0.0438$) to $0.8710$ (5-fold mean $0.8616 \pm 0.0495$, $\text{Precision} = 0.8766$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8783$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9567$) and improves diagnostic enzyme $F_1$ from $0.8458$ (BacDive RF; 5-fold mean $0.8456 \pm 0.0076$), $0.8550$ (DiASPora; 5-fold mean $0.8550 \pm 0.0075$), and $0.8593$ (Traitar; 5-fold mean $0.8595 \pm 0.0076$) to $0.8713$ (5-fold mean $0.8712 \pm 0.0083$, $\text{Precision} = 0.8652$, $\text{Recall} = 0.8776$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8257$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9097$).

Third, in Track 2 (where $30\%$ of each strain's assays are revealed to calibrate strain metabolic activity and co-occurring pathway posteriors, and evaluation is performed strictly on the held-out $70\%$ assays), \texttt{GSPA Track 2 Bayesian Anchor} improves unsupervised held-out carbon-source $F_1$ ($n=1{,}641$) from $0.7922$ (gapseq and unassisted GSPA Track 1 on the same 70\% split) to $0.8871$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8362$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9447$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8889$, continuous $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9534$) and held-out enzyme $F_1$ ($n=12{,}316$) from $0.7737$ (gapseq) to $0.8545$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8067$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9084$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.7749$, continuous $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.8738$). Combining Track 2 posteriors with DiASPora (\texttt{GSPA Track 2 + DiASPora Hybrid}) reaches $F_1 = 0.9225$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.9048$, $\text{Recall} = 0.9409$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.9258$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9788$) on held-out carbon sources and $F_1 = 0.8727$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8638$, $\text{Recall} = 0.8819$, $\text{BAcc} = 0.8264$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9080$) on held-out diagnostic enzymes, outperforming DiASPora ($0.8854$ / $0.8507$), BacDive RF ($0.8625$ / $0.8492$), and Traitar ($0.8386$ / $0.8569$; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).

We next evaluated completing obligate heteromeric protein complexes when individual subunits have twilight-zone homology while suppressing false-positive singleton subunit predictions (Suite 5; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}; \texttt{results/pillar3\_complex\_lso.tsv}). Across curated heteromeric complexes from Complex Portal and GO (including proton-transporting ATP synthase \texttt{GO:0045259}, respiratory chain complex I \texttt{GO:0045271}, cytochrome $bo_3$/$c$ oxidase, DNA-directed RNA polymerase, 30S/50S ribosomal subunits, and the SecYEG translocon), we performed 175 leave-subunit-out (LSO) trials across six prokaryotic proteomes (\textit{E.~coli} K-12 [$n=30$], \textit{B.~subtilis} 168 [$n=30$], \textit{M.~tuberculosis} H37Rv [$n=30$], \textit{P.~aeruginosa} PAO1 [$n=30$], \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7 [$n=30$], and \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803 [$n=25$]). In each trial, a true complex subunit's primary evidence was attenuated into the twilight zone ($q_e \in [0.22, 0.38]$) alongside five matched decoy proteins carrying comparable raw domain scores in unrelated genomic loci (6 candidates per trial; random-chance Hit@1 $= 1/6 = 0.1667$; Precision@0.5, Recall@0.5, and $F_1\text{@0.5}$ are evaluated per trial and macro-averaged across all trials).

Under baseline Noisy-OR pooling (\texttt{M1}), all 175 attenuated subunits remain below the $\theta = 0.50$ threshold ($\text{Recall@0.5} = 0.0000$, $F_{1}\text{@0.5} = 0.0000$, genome Complex Coherence $= 0.3901$), and Hit@1 among the 6 candidates is $0.5600$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.483, 0.635]$). Applying the Stage 1 complex coherence prior alone (\texttt{M2}) detects that the partner subunits of the complex are present in the genome and boosts all domain-matching candidates equally, keeping Hit@1 at $0.5600$ while lifting Recall@0.5 to $0.5029$, Precision@0.5 to $0.1729$, and per-trial $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ to $0.2459$. Coupling complex coherence with the Stage 1 operon neighborhood prior (\texttt{M3}) disambiguates the true co-operonic subunit from non-operonic decoys: across all 175 trials, Hit@1 rises from $0.5600$ to $0.9371$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.890, 0.968]$), Recall@0.5 reaches $0.9257$, Precision@0.5 rises more than three-fold to $0.5951$, and $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ reaches $0.6680$ ($0.9310$ Hit@1, $0.9103$ Recall@0.5, $0.5886$ Precision@0.5, and $0.6595$ $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ across the 145 non-\textit{E.~coli} K-12 trials; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).

Comparing per-genome recovery across the six species highlights the complementary roles of operon context and Stage 3 agentic reasoning (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}b). In \textit{E.~coli} K-12, \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}, \textit{P.~aeruginosa}, and \textit{E.~coli} O157:H7, most heteromeric complex subunits reside in contiguous operons, allowing \texttt{M3} to achieve $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ between $0.6862$ (\textit{P.~aeruginosa}) and $0.7279$ (\textit{E.~coli} O157:H7; $0.7089$ in \textit{E.~coli} K-12, $0.7090$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}, and $0.7244$ in \textit{M.~tuberculosis}). In the cyanobacterium \textit{Synechocystis} sp.\ PCC 6803, subunits of photosystem I/II, phycobilisomes, and NADH dehydrogenase are frequently split across dispersed chromosomal loci: baseline \texttt{M1} achieves $0.5200$ Hit@1 (Complex Coherence $= 0.3587$), \texttt{M2} reaches $0.4800$ Recall@0.5 and $0.2507$ $F_1\text{@0.5}$ (Complex Coherence $= 0.6125$), and operon context (\texttt{M3}) reaches $0.6400$ Hit@1 and $0.4080$ $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$. Activating Stage 2 singleton pruning and the Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agent (\texttt{M4}) under symmetric zero-leakage candidate formatting elevates \textit{Synechocystis} Hit@1 from $0.6400$ to $0.6800$ ($17/25$), Recall@0.5 from $0.6400$ to $0.6800$, Precision@0.5 from $0.3393$ to $0.3793$, and $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ from $0.4080$ to $0.4480$ ($+0.0400$ gain), bringing overall 6-genome subunit recovery to $0.9429$ Hit@1 ($95\%$ CI $[0.898, 0.972]$), $0.9314$ Recall@0.5, $0.6009$ Precision@0.5, and $0.6737$ $F_{1}\text{@0.5}$ ($0.9379$ Hit@1, $0.9172$ Recall@0.5, $0.5955$ Precision@0.5, and $0.6664$ $F_1\text{@0.5}$ on the 5 non-\textit{E.~coli} K-12 genomes).

We next evaluated anatomical and developmental constraint enforcement across six eukaryotic species spanning unicellular fungi (\textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}), non-chordate invertebrates (\textit{Caenorhabditis elegans} and \textit{Drosophila melanogaster}), non-vascular bryophyte mosses (\textit{Physcomitrium patens}), vascular flowering plants (\textit{Arabidopsis thaliana}), and vertebrates (\textit{Mus musculus}), evaluating both (i) the unperturbed endogenous homology-transfer proteome ($6{,}155$ curated proteins across the 6 species; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak eukaryote\_\allowbreak endogenous\_\allowbreak vs\_\allowbreak injected.tsv}) and (ii) a cross-clade perturbation stress-test adding $1{,}546$ cross-clade developmental claims across $7{,}655$ proteins ($300$ in 5 species and $46$ in \textit{P.~patens}), scored strictly against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground-truth annotations (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c,d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}; \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak eukaryote\_\allowbreak anatomy.tsv} and \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak relax\_\allowbreak vs\_\allowbreak remove.tsv}). On the unperturbed endogenous proteomes alone, unconstrained Noisy-OR (\texttt{E1}) already produces $1{,}009$ formal cross-clade anatomical and taxonomic violations transferred from mammalian or angiosperm homologs ($3$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, $289$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, and $717$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}; $0$ in \textit{P.~patens}, \textit{A.~thaliana}, and \textit{M.~musculus}; mean $168.2$ violations/species); GSPA eliminates $100\%$ ($1{,}009/1{,}009$) of these endogenous violations and increases endogenous 6-species mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.7096$ (\texttt{E1}) to $0.7117$ at Iter 1 ($+0.0021$ mean gain at Iter 1: $+0.0086$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae} [$0.6644 \to 0.6730$], $+0.0032$ in \textit{C.~elegans} [$0.5797 \to 0.5829$], $+0.0010$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster} [$0.5863 \to 0.5873$], $+0.0010$ in \textit{P.~patens} [$0.8841 \to 0.8851$], $+0.0011$ in \textit{A.~thaliana} [$0.6594 \to 0.6605$], and $-0.0020$ in \textit{M.~musculus} [$0.8835 \to 0.8815$]; $0.7109$ 6-species mean at Iter 3 with Stage 2A relaxation). Across the combined stress-test panel ($1{,}452$ total baseline violations at $\theta = 0.50$: $66$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, $389$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, $894$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}, $3$ in \textit{P.~patens}, $59$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}, and $41$ in \textit{M.~musculus}), applying direct GO taxon constraints alone (\texttt{E2}, using the $26{,}911$ rules in \texttt{go-taxon-constraints.tsv}) eliminates $810/1{,}452$ ($55.79\%$) of violations while leaving $642/1{,}452$ ($44.21\%$) unresolved ($190$ in \textit{C.~elegans}, $426$ in \textit{D.~melanogaster}, and $26$ in \textit{A.~thaliana}), because those errors involve vertebrate, arthropod, or angiosperm organ terms whose taxonomic restrictions are defined in Uberon, PO, and CL. Conversely, in unicellular yeast (\textit{S.~cerevisiae}) and non-vascular moss (\textit{P.~patens}), where injected organ-development claims land on metabolic or housekeeping proteins annotated to core cellular processes in unmodified Swiss-Prot, \texttt{E5} recovers higher Recall ($+1.00$ and $+0.99$ percentage points) and higher Mean IC ($12.07$ vs.\ $12.02$ and $9.79$ vs.\ $9.64$ bits) than \texttt{E4}, while complete removal (\texttt{E4}) achieves higher precision and $F_{\max}$ ($0.9604$ vs.\ $0.9592$ in yeast; $0.9828$ vs.\ $0.9792$ in moss). In addition, for true species-appropriate developmental annotations attenuated into the borderline regime ($P^{(0)} \in [0.22, 0.49]$), the active-structure developmental boost in Stage 1 (\texttt{E3}) rescues $502/618$ ($81.2\%$) of borderline developmental claims above $\theta = 0.50$.

Finally, we evaluated the optional Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agentic completion layer (Suite 8) against deterministic Stage 1+2, a posterior threshold baseline ($\ge 0.35$ + Guardrail), and supervised non-agentic tabular classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees, and Multi-Layer Perceptron trained via 5-fold cross-validation; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}, Table~\ref{tab:agent}, Supplementary Section~S5, and Supplementary Tables~S7 and S8; \texttt{results/pillar4\_agent\_evaluation.tsv}, \texttt{results/pillar4\_ml\_baselines.tsv}, and \texttt{results/pillar4\_multiseed\_summary.tsv}). Across the 88-proteome multi-kingdom benchmark, the Stage 3 anomaly triggers select a mean of $1.84\%$ of proteins per proteome (interquartile range $1.12\%$--$2.38\%$, $<2.5\%$ across all four domains). To evaluate anomaly resolution under strictly controlled, de-identified conditions with masked gene symbols and standardized feature formatting, we constructed a 120-anomaly benchmark ($30$ pathway gaps, $30$ incomplete complexes, $30$ borderline essentiality gaps, and $30$ borderline conflicts across 7 genomes: \textit{E.~coli} K-12 [$n=29$], \textit{P.~aeruginosa} [$n=18$], \textit{B.~subtilis} [$n=17$], \textit{M.~tuberculosis} [$n=16$], \textit{S.~cerevisiae} [$n=16$], \textit{C.~elegans} [$n=13$], and \textit{A.~thaliana} [$n=11$]; \texttt{results/pillar4\_agent\_benchmark\_120.jsonl}). Every candidate protein in the benchmark has its gene symbol masked to \texttt{"uncharacterized"}, candidate order is alternated across anomalies, and all true-positive and decoy candidates expose symmetric domain and real co-strand proteome neighbor feature formatting. The 90 completeness anomalies comprise $54$ true-positive promotable gaps ($18$ per category) and $36$ matched-score true-negative decoys ($12$ per category) including hard feature-overlap cases where decoys share borderline posteriors ($0.24$--$0.36$) or moderate RLGC Bayes factors ($1.5$--$4.2$) with true gaps, while the $30$ \texttt{BORDERLINE\_CONFLICT} anomalies serve as a symbolic-verification unit test comprising $15$ real cross-clade conflicts that require ancestor relaxation or demotion and $15$ adversarial cross-clade traps that must be blocked or relaxed to a valid ancestor.

Deterministic Stage 1+2 alone (Config 1) leaves post-Stage-2 borderline gaps unpromoted ($0/54$ promotions, accuracy $= 55.00\%$), while lowering the posterior decision threshold to $0.35$ with the symbolic guardrail (\texttt{Thresh-0.35}) promotes $29$ true gaps alongside $22$ false positives ($\text{Precision} = 0.5686$, $\text{Recall} = 0.5370$, $F_1 = 0.5524$, $\text{Accuracy} = 0.7083$). Supervised tabular classifiers trained via 5-fold CV on the 3 pre-tool summary scalars (\texttt{[current\_posterior, max\_claim\_score, suggestion\_score]}) achieve $F_1 = 0.7500$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.8571$, $\text{Recall} = 0.6667$, $3/120$ [$2.5\%$] violations accepted) for Logistic Regression (\texttt{CV-LR3}) and $F_1 = 0.8283$ ($\text{Precision} = 0.9111$, $\text{Recall} = 0.7593$, $\text{Accuracy} = 0.8583$) for GBDT (\texttt{CV-GB3}), while reaching $94.0\%$ ($\text{MRR} = 0.9667$) and $90.0\%$ ($\text{MRR} = 0.9383$) Hit@1 on the 50 ambiguous LRO cases when trained via 5-fold CV on those cases' 3 pre-tool scalars. When supervised 5-fold CV classifiers are trained directly on all 8 post-tool numerical features (\texttt{[current\_posterior, likelihood\_logodds, final\_logodds, max\_prior\_contrib, rlgc\_bayes\_factor, suggestion\_score, max\_claim\_score, mean\_claim\_score]}) and paired with the deterministic \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} (\texttt{CV-LR8+G}, \texttt{CV-RF8+G}, \texttt{CV-GB8+G}, and \texttt{CV-MLP8+G}), they establish a strong in-distribution supervised upper bound on binary promotion ($F_1 = 0.9216$--$0.9720$, $\text{Accuracy} = 0.9333$--$0.9750$, and LRO-50 Hit@1 $= 92.0\%$--$96.0\%$ in $<0.01\text{ s}$). These supervised classifiers predict binary promotions when trained on in-distribution labeled anomaly splits, whereas semantic ancestor relaxation on conflict cases requires structured ontological traversal ($0$--$2$ relaxations under tabular classification).

Operating zero-shot directly from the five genomic and ontological tools across a bounded multi-turn loop ($\le 6$ turns, mean $5.74$ tool calls, $32.53\text{ s}$ single-request HTTP latency or $4.07\text{ s}$ wall-clock time per anomaly at $8\times$ asynchronous concurrency) with the symbolic ontology guardrail active (Config 3, \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}; \texttt{results/pillar4\_resolutions\_c3\_guarded.jsonl}), the Stage 3 agent achieves single-seed (seed 42) $\text{Precision} = 97.87\%$ ($46/47$), $\text{Recall} = 85.19\%$ ($46/54$), $F_{1} = 0.9109$, and overall resolution accuracy $= 92.50\%$ ($111/120$: $46/54$ TP promotions [$1$ decoy candidate promotion counted as FP, $1$ \texttt{RELAX}, and $6$ \texttt{ABSTAIN} including $2$ guardrail rejections], $35/36$ decoy abstentions/demotions [$25$ \texttt{ABSTAIN}, $10$ \texttt{DEMOTE}, and $1$ \texttt{RELAX}], and $30/30$ conflict/trap non-promotions across the 30 \texttt{BORDERLINE\_CONFLICT} cases [$13$ valid ancestral GO relaxations ($2$ on real conflicts and $11$ on cross-clade traps, bringing total \texttt{RELAX} actions across the 120 anomalies to $15$) plus $15$ \texttt{DEMOTE} and $2$ \texttt{ABSTAIN}], with $0/120 = 0.0\%$ accepted ontology violations), and across $3$ independent random seeds ($\text{seed} \in \{42, 43, 44\}$; Supplementary Table~S7 and \texttt{results/pillar4\_multiseed\_summary.tsv}) achieves $\text{Precision} = 0.9722 \pm 0.0092$, $\text{Recall} = 0.8581 \pm 0.0087$, $F_1 = 0.9115 \pm 0.0008$, $\text{Accuracy} = 0.9278 \pm 0.0039$, $13.7 \pm 1.2$ total \texttt{RELAX} actions ($15$ at seed 42, $12$ at seed 43, and $14$ at seed 44), and $0.00 \pm 0.00\%$ accepted ontology violation rate (Table~\ref{tab:agent}). Zero-shot Config 3 outperforms the pre-tool 3-scalar CV classifiers on the 120-anomaly benchmark ($F_1 = 0.9115$ vs.\ $0.7500$--$0.8283$) and approaches the in-distribution 8-feature supervised CV upper bound ($F_1 = 0.9216$--$0.9720$) in a zero-shot setting. In Fast 1-Step mode (Config 2, $\le 2$ turns, $5.01$ tool calls, $2.47\text{ s}$ wall-clock / $19.80\text{ s}$ HTTP latency), precision is $1.0000$ ($9/9$) with recall at $0.1667$ ($F_1 = 0.2857$). Ablating the symbolic guardrail (Config 4, $5.07$ tool calls, $3.72\text{ s}$ wall-clock / $29.75\text{ s}$ HTTP latency) allows $1/120$ ($0.83\%$) cross-clade traps to be accepted and yields $0/15$ ancestral GO relaxations on conflicts, lowering precision to $0.9149$ ($43/47$), recall to $0.7963$ ($43/54$), $F_1$ to $0.8515$, and accuracy to $0.8833$ (Figure~\ref{fig:agent}b). On the 50 deduplicated unique-protein ambiguous LRO metabolic gaps in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 (\texttt{results/pillar4\_lro\_reranking.tsv}), where all gene symbols are masked to \texttt{"uncharacterized"}, candidate ordering is randomized, and all 5 candidates per case expose symmetric domain and real proteome operon-neighbor metadata, zero-shot Stage 3 agentic re-ranking elevates Hit@1 from $34.0\%$ ($17/50$, RLGC baseline) to $82.0\%$ ($41/50$ at seed 42; two-sided exact binomial McNemar $p = 1.19 \times 10^{-7}$ on $b_{01}=24, b_{10}=0$ discordant unique proteins) and $82.67\% \pm 0.94\%$ across 3 seeds ($84.0\%$ [$42/50$] at seed 43 and $82.0\%$ [$41/50$] at seed 44), Hit@3 from $78.0\%$ ($39/50$) to $90.0\%$ ($45/50$), and MRR from $0.5700$ to $0.8740$ (seed 42) and $0.8792 \pm 0.0069$ (3-seed mean; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}c and Table~\ref{tab:agent}). Finally, as an ecological case study on five desert extremophile isolates from the Empty Quarter (\textit{Rub' al Khali}: \textit{Salinicola} sp.\ [$3{,}842$ CDS], \textit{Halomonas} sp.\ [$4{,}118$ CDS], \textit{Pseudomonas} sp.\ [$5{,}490$ CDS], \textit{Bacillus endophyticus} [$4{,}625$ CDS], and \textit{Streptomyces} sp.\ [$7{,}184$ CDS]; Supplementary Table~S8 and \texttt{results/pillar6\_empty\_quarter\_vignette.tsv}), applying GSPA lifts mean core essential completeness from $0.8884$ (raw homology) and $0.9190$ (Stage 0) to $0.9718$, increases pathway coherence from $0.4892$ to $0.6226$ ($+13.34$ percentage points), and identifies $4$--$8$ (mean $5.6$) flux-certified pFBA core repairs and $4$--$9$ (mean $6.2$) Stage 3 candidate promotions per isolate across ectoine/betaine osmoprotection and oxidative stress pathways.

\section{Discussion}

Protein function prediction has traditionally been formulated as a per-protein classification problem evaluated on isolated sequences or structures \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,kulmanov2024deepgose}. Our evaluation across eight benchmark suites shows that while per-protein multi-channel calibration (Stage 0) establishes bulk protein-centric accuracy on well-annotated reference proteomes, treating the whole genome as the unit of inference (Stages 1--3) restores pathway connectivity, heteromeric complex completeness, and cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical consistency across the proteome.

Four main conclusions emerge from our study. First, our audit of 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions clarifies why negative-cost MILP gap-filling (including our prior METEOR formulation \citep{niu2026meteor}) degrades gene-level enzyme precision on diagnostic and catabolic panels: assigning negative addition costs ($c_j^{\text{add}} < 0$) to candidate reactions above an addition threshold causes $83.72\%$ ($262.34 / 313.35$, vs.\ $51.01$ flux-carrying additions) of added reactions to carry zero steady-state flux, while uniform reaction-to-gene boosting inflates false-positive calls across paralogs. While any positive-cost LP formulation over the sanitized universal network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ eliminates uncertified zero-flux additions ($81.31/81.31$ flux-certified additions under threshold LP repair and $5.70/5.70$ under two-step lexicographic pFBA with FVA/KO/perturbation certification), combining role-aware DNF mechanism aggregation (Top-$M_{\text{iso}}$ isozyme OR and bottleneck-gated geometric-mean subunit AND), two-step lexicographic pFBA repair, cognate substrate flux probing, and competitive locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ eliminates the $2.23$--$2.36$ dead-end active reactions per genome left by unregularized LP/MILP repair, improves \texttt{Price-149} enzyme Top-1 hits under both single-predictor refinement (CLEAN $60 \to 63$, MAPred $50 \to 56$, DeepProZyme $49 \to 51$ with stoichiometric local-context probe, GraphEC $18 \to 20$) and multi-predictor refinement ($+0.0528$ to $+0.1987$ $F_{\max}$), and improves \texttt{BacDive} phenotype predictions across blind, Track 2 phenotype-anchored, and genus-blocked evaluation tracks.

Second, our ablation of the leave-reaction-out (LRO) benchmark separates the contribution of protein language model embeddings from that of local genomic synteny. On the well-studied \textit{E.~coli} K-12 proteome (Track B), per-protein ESM-2 reaction-centroid similarity alone recovers $62.11\%$ of masked reactions at Rank 1 ($87.85\%$ given the candidate pool), with Stage 1 pathway coherence and RLGC operon neighborhood density adding $+1.56$ percentage points unconditionally ($+2.21$ percentage points given pool, to $63.67\%$ / $90.06\%$). Conversely, when PLM centroids are excluded or cosine-masked (Track A, cosine $> 0.50$) or when multiple candidate genes in a proteome share similar domain and embedding scores ($n=50$ unique-protein ambiguous LRO gaps and $n=175$ heteromeric complex LSO trials), unfiltered sequence similarity alone recovers $1.95\%$ (LRO), $34.00\%$ (ambiguous LRO), and $56.00\%$ (LSO), strict cosine-masked Track A reaches $11.33\%$ ($17.97\%$ with Track A GBDT), and unfiltered RLGC operon neighborhood coupling + Stage 1 coherence (without PLM centroids) reaches $23.44\%$ on LRO, while Stage 3 neuro-symbolic disambiguation elevates Hit@1 to $82.67\%$ ($3$-seed mean on ambiguous LRO) and $94.29\%$ (LSO across 6 candidates per trial).

Third, our multi-kingdom eukaryotic evaluation shows that enforcing ontology consistency in multicellular organisms requires bridging the Gene Ontology with external anatomy ontologies (Uberon, PO, and CL). On unperturbed 7-channel predictions across six model eukaryotes, GSPA eliminates all $1{,}009$ endogenous homology-transferred violations while improving mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ by $+0.0021$ ($+0.0086$ in \textit{S.~cerevisiae}). Under cross-clade perturbation stress-testing evaluated against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground truth ($1{,}452$ total baseline violations across fungi, nematodes, insects, mosses, flowering plants, and mammals), direct GO taxon constraints leave $44.21\%$ ($642/1{,}452$) of errors unresolved and soft Stage 1 log-odds penalties still leave $201$--$559$ high-score violations in invertebrate proteomes, whereas Stage 2A semantic relaxation over our 2,715 GO--Uberon/PO/CL cross-product axioms eliminates $100\%$ of violations, retains $95.1\%$--$98.7\%$ of unconstrained mean IC, and outperforms naive deletion across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants ($\Delta F_{\max}(\text{E5}-\text{E4}) = +0.0006$ to $+0.0157$; Recall $+0.67$ to $+3.05$ percentage points), while recovering higher Recall and Mean IC than deletion in unicellular yeast and non-vascular moss.

Fourth, comparing the zero-shot Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agent (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8}) against both pre-tool 3-scalar and post-tool 8-feature supervised 5-fold CV classifiers on the de-identified 120-anomaly and 50-LRO benchmarks clarifies when an LLM agent is warranted over a lightweight tabular classifier. When in-distribution labeled anomaly data and pre-extracted tool features are available and only binary promotion or candidate ranking is needed, an 8-feature supervised classifier paired with \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} achieves $F_1 = 0.9216$--$0.9720$ on the 120 anomalies and $92.0\%$--$96.0\%$ Hit@1 on LRO-50 in $<0.01\text{ s}$. When labeled anomaly training data are unavailable for a new clade or when multi-class semantic ancestor relaxation is required on cross-clade conflicts, zero-shot multi-turn tool use with \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} achieves $F_1 = 0.9115 \pm 0.0008$ and accuracy $= 0.9278 \pm 0.0039$ on the 120 anomalies and $82.67\% \pm 0.94\%$ Hit@1 on LRO-50 across 3 seeds, outperforming pre-tool 3-scalar CV classifiers on anomaly resolution ($F_1 = 0.7500$--$0.8283$), proposing $13.7/15$ valid ancestral GO relaxations, and guaranteeing a $0.0\%$ accepted ontology violation rate.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several methodological boundaries of the present study define priorities for future work. First, on well-annotated reference proteomes where roughly $85\%$ of proteins carry high-confidence Swiss-Prot homologs ($P_0 \ge 0.90$), Stage 0 multi-channel pooling accounts for the bulk of overall protein-centric $F_{\max}$, and Stages 1--3 concentrate their updates on the $<15\%$ of twilight-zone, pathway-hole, complex-subunit, and taxon-conflicted loci; similarly, when targets are supplied as isolated sequences dispersed across species (\texttt{CAFA6} and \texttt{Holdout-125}), inference relies on Stage 0 pooling, Stage 2A ontological enforcement, and Stage 3 domain/ontology reasoning, and linking target accessions to their source genome assemblies will activate full operon and stoichiometric context on prospective sequence benchmarks. Second, in the LRO metabolic gap-filling benchmark, unconditional enzyme recovery is bounded by Stage 0 candidate pool recall ($n_{\text{cov}} = 175$--$181$ of $256$ reactions in \textit{E.~coli} and $76$ of $144$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}), and Track B uses frozen pretrained ESM-2 weights that had pretraining exposure to UniRef50 sequences; expanding Stage 0 candidate generation with remote structural fold matches and training strictly time-split protein language models will further separate de novo sequence representations from homology transfer. Third, Stage 2B stoichiometric locus refinement models steady-state reaction connectivity over reactions mapped in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}} = 1$) that carry flux under the probed medium ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 1$) on unique single-copy steps ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}} = 1$), whereas in vivo growth and diagnostic enzyme phenotypes in \texttt{Price-149} and \texttt{BacDive} also depend on transcriptional regulation, transporter specificity, and unmodeled cofactors; integrating transcriptomic expression priors and automated transporter substrate models, while continuing to pair the internal GAEF structural diagnostic with held-out experimental validation, will broaden coverage of condition-specific and secondary pathways. Fourth, our cross-ontology anatomical bridge covers the $2{,}715$ GO terms that carry explicit Uberon, PO, or CL logical definitions in the current GO release, and semantic ancestor relaxation improves precision-recall $F_{\max}$ primarily in multicellular animals and vascular plants whose ground-truth annotations include tissue-level developmental ancestors, whereas candidate pathway fills in the 97 uncultured MAGs and five Empty Quarter extremophile isolates represent computational hypotheses awaiting heterologous expression or mutant fitness assays. Fifth, while the zero-shot Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agent supports out-of-distribution anomaly disambiguation and ancestral GO relaxation in zero-shot settings, pre-extracted 8-feature supervised tabular classifiers paired with \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} provide a $100\times$ faster alternative for binary promotion when labeled anomaly training splits are available.

\subsection{Stage 0: Multi-channel per-protein evidence calibration and Noisy-OR pooling}

GSPA can refine either a single standalone predictor (such as CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, or TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}) or integrate claims from up to seven per-protein evidence channels $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$: (1) sequence alignment via DIAMOND \citep{buchfink2021sensitive} against Swiss-Prot GOA entries; (2) profile HMM domain architecture via HMMER 3 \citep{eddy2011accelerated} against Pfam-A/Pfam2GO; (3) domain signatures via InterProScan v5 \citep{jones2014interproscan}; (4) ESM-2 (650M) embedding $k$NN transfer \citep{lin2023evolutionary}; (5) ProtT5-XL-U50 embedding $k$NN transfer \citep{elnaggar2021prottrans}; (6) sequence-based neural classifiers (ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, DeepGOWeb \citep{kulmanov2021deepgoweb}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}); and (7) structural similarity transfer via FoldSeek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and ProstT5 3Di \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}.

For each evidence claim $c$ from tool $e(c) \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$ with raw confidence $s(c) \in [0, 1]$, \texttt{CalibrationTable.groovy} (\texttt{gspa.integration.CalibrationTable}) maps $s(c)$ to a calibrated probability $\tilde{q}(c) \in [\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}]$ ($\varepsilon_{\text{cal}} = 10^{-4}$) via Platt logistic calibration $\tilde{q}(c) = \text{clip}\bigl(\sigma_{\text{log}}(a_{e(c)} s(c) + b_{e(c)}),\, \varepsilon_{\text{cal}},\, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\bigr)$ with tool-specific default parameters $(a_e, b_e)$ (\texttt{diamond}/\texttt{mmseqs2}: $(6.0, -3.0)$; \texttt{hmmer}/\texttt{pfam}: $(4.0, -1.5)$; \texttt{interproscan}/\texttt{foldseek}/\texttt{dbcan}: $(5.0, -2.0)$; \texttt{eggnog-mapper}: $(3.5, -1.3)$; \texttt{gapseq}: $(4.0, -1.8)$; \texttt{antismash}: $(4.0, -1.5)$; \texttt{signalp}/\texttt{deeptmhmm}: $(3.0, -1.0)$; \texttt{operon}: $(3.0, -1.2)$; \texttt{amrfinder}: $(8.0, -4.0)$; default $(4.0, -2.0)$; or identity clipping $\text{clip}(s(c), \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}})$ when \texttt{calibrate = false}, overrideable via JSON/TSV calibration files). Each calibrated score is scaled by the evidence-type reliability weight $r_{e(c)} = \text{defaultReliability()} \in (0, 1]$ defined in \texttt{EvidenceCombiner.groovy} (\texttt{gspa.integration.EvidenceCombiner}: \texttt{DOMAIN\_SPECIFIC\_AMR} $0.85$; \texttt{STRUCTURE\_SIMILARITY} and \texttt{DOMAIN\_SPECIFIC\_CAZY} $0.80$; \texttt{SEQUENCE\_DOMAIN}, \texttt{STRUCTURE\_DEEPLEARNING}, \texttt{DOMAIN\_SPECIFIC\_BGC}, and \texttt{DOMAIN\_SPECIFIC\_VF} $0.75$; \texttt{SEQUENCE\_SIMILARITY} and \texttt{ORTHOLOGY} $0.70$; \texttt{SEQUENCE\_DEEPLEARNING} and \texttt{PROTEIN\_LM\_EMBEDDING} $0.65$; \texttt{GENOMIC\_REGION\_ML}, \texttt{SEQUENCE\_REGION\_ML}, \texttt{DARK\_MATTER}, \texttt{REACTION\_LOCAL\_CONTEXT}, \texttt{CROSS\_GENOME\_TRANSFER}, and \texttt{ML\_RANKER} $0.60$; \texttt{GENOMIC\_LANGUAGE\_MODEL} and \texttt{METABOLIC\_CONTEXT} $0.55$; \texttt{SEQUENCE\_MOTIF} and \texttt{LOCALIZATION} $0.50$; \texttt{GENOMIC\_CONTEXT} $0.45$), yielding $q(c) = r_{e(c)} \tilde{q}(c)$. To prevent correlated channels from over-counting redundant evidence, \texttt{EvidenceCombiner.groovy} groups claims for $(p, f)$ by \texttt{EvidenceType.correlationGroup()} into correlation groups $g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)$ (\texttt{homology} for sequence similarity, sequence domains, and orthology; \texttt{ml\_protein\_seq} for sequence DL and PLM embeddings; \texttt{structure} for structural similarity and structural DL; \texttt{context} for genomic and metabolic context; \texttt{ml\_genomic} for gLMs; \texttt{inferred\_context} for dark-matter, RLGC, cross-genome transfer, and ML ranker claims; \texttt{motif} for sequence motifs; \texttt{localization} for subcellular localization; \texttt{region\_features} for \texttt{SEQUENCE\_REGION\_ML}; \texttt{viral} for \texttt{GENOMIC\_REGION\_ML}; and \texttt{domain\_*} for domain-specific predictors), takes the group maximum $q_{g,(1)}(p, f) = \max_{c \in g} q(c)$ (with optional second-opinion bonus $\beta_{\text{so}} q_{g,(2)}(p, f)$ from the second-highest group member $q_{g,(2)}$, default $\beta_{\text{so}} = 0.0$), clips each group score to $[10^{-3}, 0.999]$, and pools across correlation groups via Noisy-OR:
\begin{equation}
  q_g(p, f) = \text{clip}\Bigl(\max_{c \in g} \bigl(r_{e(c)} \tilde{q}(c)\bigr) + \beta_{\text{so}} \, q_{g,(2)}(p, f), \, 10^{-3}, \, 0.999\Bigr), \qquad P_0(p, f) = \text{clip}\!\left(1 - \prod_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)} \bigl(1 - q_g(p, f)\bigr), \, 10^{-6}, \, 1 - 10^{-6}\right).
  \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
Converting $P_0(p, f)$ to log-odds and clipping to $[L_{\min}, L_{\max}] = [-12.0, 12.0]$ (\texttt{lMin = -12.0}, \texttt{lMax = 12.0} in \texttt{EvidenceCombiner.groovy}) yields the anchored Stage 0 log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f) = \text{clip}\!\bigl(\log \frac{P_0(p, f)}{1 - P_0(p, f)}, -12.0, 12.0\bigr)$, with GO annotations propagated upward across GO \texttt{is\_a} and \texttt{part\_of} ancestors (\texttt{IntegrationState.baselineAnnotatedGoTermsPropagated} and \texttt{goOntology.propagateAnnotations}).

\subsection{Stage 1: Proteome-wide iterative Bayesian log-odds refinement}

In Stage 1 (\texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy} and \texttt{PriorEngine.groovy}), GSPA updates the log-odds matrix $\mathbf{L}^{(k)}$ across iterations $k = 1, \dots, K_{\max}$ by computing six genome-scale prior shifts $B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr)$ (weighted by $w_m$, default $w_m = 1.0$, and summed into $\Delta L_{\text{prior}}^{(k)}(p, f)$), adding them to the anchored Stage 0 log-odds $L^{(0)}(p, f)$ (clipped to $[-12.0, 12.0]$), and applying Jacobi under-relaxation in log-odds space with damping factor $\gamma_{\text{iter}} = 0.50$ (\texttt{damping = 0.5d} by default in \texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy}; overrideable via \texttt{theta.json}):
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(L^{(0)}(p, f) + \sum_{m=1}^{6} w_m \, B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr), \, [-12.0, 12.0]\right), \\
    L^{(k)}(p, f) &= (1 - \gamma_{\text{iter}}) L^{(k-1)}(p, f) + \gamma_{\text{iter}} \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f), \qquad P^{(k)}(p, f) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(k)}(p, f)\bigr).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
If a pinned floor $L_{\text{pin}}(p, f)$ is active (\texttt{state.pinnedFloors}), $L^{(k)}(p, f)$ is floored at $\min(12.0, L_{\text{pin}}(p, f))$ after damping. In \texttt{IterativeRefiner.groovy}, the inner loop runs for up to $K_{\max} = 6$ iterations (\texttt{maxIter = 6}) with convergence tolerance $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ on the mean absolute probability change $\frac{1}{|\Delta_{\text{chg}}|} \sum_{(p,f) \in \Delta_{\text{chg}}} |P^{(k)}(p, f) - P^{(k-1)}(p, f)| < \epsilon$ computed over changed pairs $\Delta_{\text{chg}} = \{(p, f) : |P^{(k)}(p, f) - P^{(k-1)}(p, f)| > 10^{-9}\}$, and automatic rollback to the last stable iterate if the mean change increases across two consecutive iterations. The six prior classes $B_1, \dots, B_6$ are implemented in \texttt{gspa-core/src/main/groovy/gspa/integration/prior/} as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Essentiality prior ($m=1$, $\alpha_{\text{ess}} = 1.5$, \texttt{EssentialityPrior.groovy}):} Given the domain-specific essential GO profile $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ (\texttt{bacteria}, \texttt{archaea}, \texttt{eukaryota}, or \texttt{viruses} in \texttt{EssentialFunctions.groovy}, \texttt{gspa.config.EssentialFunctions}), let $\mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ denote essential GO categories missing from the baseline ancestor-propagated active annotation set ($P^{(0)}(p, e) \le 0.50$ for all $p \in \mathcal{P}$), and let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) = \bigcup_{e \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})} \{t \in \mathcal{T} : t \sqsubseteq e\}$ denote the union of all GO subclasses of uncovered essential categories expanded via \texttt{goReasoner.getSubClasses}. Every candidate claim $(p, f)$ with $f \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ receives a flat log-odds boost
  \begin{equation*}
    B_1(p, f) = +\alpha_{\text{ess}} \, \mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})\bigr] \qquad (\alpha_{\text{ess}} = 1.5, \; \texttt{maxBoostMultiplier} = 2.0).
  \end{equation*}
  \item \textbf{Pathway and complex coherence prior ($m=2$, $\alpha_{\text{coh}} = 1.0$, $w_{\text{part}} = 0.85$, $w_{\text{sing}} = 0.75$, \texttt{CoherencePrior.groovy}):} First, for any GO process pair $(X, Y)$ linked by \texttt{has\_part} where $X$ is present in the baseline active set but $Y$ is missing, all subclasses $f \sqsubseteq Y$ receive process-coherence weight $w_{\text{proc}}(f) = \max(0, 1 - c_{\text{proc}})$, where $c_{\text{proc}}$ is the fraction of satisfied \texttt{has\_part} pairs. Second, for any reference pathway $W$ triggered in the genome ($\ge 1$ baseline active step) with baseline step coverage $c_W \in (0, 1)$, all GO terms $f \in \text{Req}(W)$ mapping to missing steps of $W$ receive pathway-coherence weight $w_{\text{path}}(f) = \max_{W \ni f} \max(0.25, 1 - c_W)$. Third, for an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ (\texttt{complexClassification[f] == 'n'} in \texttt{complex\_terms.tsv}), let $N_{\text{strong}}(f) = |\{p' \in \mathcal{P} : P^{(0)}(p', f) > 0.50\}|$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) = |\{p' \in \mathcal{P} : \max(P^{(0)}(p', f), P^{(k-1)}(p', f)) \ge 0.08\}|$ count baseline-strong active proteins and candidate proteins across the genome; candidate partners ($\max(P^{(0)}(p, f), P^{(k-1)}(p, f)) \ge 0.08$) receive $w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f) = +w_{\text{part}} = +0.85$ when $N_{\text{strong}}(f) = 1$ (and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) \ge 2$) and $+0.65\,w_{\text{part}} = +0.5525$ when $N_{\text{strong}}(f) \ge 2$, whereas an isolated strong singleton ($P^{(0)}(p, f) > 0.50$ with $N_{\text{strong}}(f) = 1$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) \le 1$) receives $-w_{\text{sing}} = -0.75$:
  \begin{equation*}
    B_2(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{coh}} \Bigl(\max\bigl(w_{\text{proc}}(f), \, w_{\text{path}}(f)\bigr) + w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f)\Bigr).
  \end{equation*}
  \item \textbf{Taxonomic consistency prior ($m=3$, $\alpha_{\text{cons}} = 3.0$, \texttt{ConsistencyPrior.groovy}):} Once per iteration over the genome-wide currently active GO set $\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)} = \{t \in \mathcal{T} : \max_p P^{(k-1)}(p, t) > 0.50\}$, \texttt{ConsistencyPrior.groovy} forms the conflicting term set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)} = \mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{TC}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \cup \text{Core}_{\text{SAT}}(\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)})$ combining direct taxon-forbidden GO terms (\texttt{checker.getForbiddenConstrainedTerms()}) and SAT4J minimal unsatisfiable core terms (\texttt{checker.check(checkSet).minimalUnsatCores}). Any claim $(p, f)$ where $f$ or an ancestor $a \in \text{Anc}(f)$ belongs to $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)}$ receives $B_3(p, f) = -\alpha_{\text{cons}} = -3.0$ (or $-1000.0$ when \texttt{hardFilter = true}).
  \item \textbf{Anatomical prior ($m=4$, $\alpha_{\text{anat}} = 3.5$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}} = 0.65$, \texttt{AnatomicalPrior.groovy}):} Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ be the set of GO terms (and their GO descendants via ancestor closure) anatomically forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}), and let $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the set of non-root anatomical structures $u$ taxonomically valid in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ and supported by $\ge N_{\text{sup}} = 2$ distinct proteins with likelihood probability $P^{(0)}(p', f') \ge 0.50$ (\texttt{minSupportProteins = 2}, \texttt{supportThreshold = 0.50d}); then
  \begin{equation*}
    B_4(p, f) = \begin{cases}
      -\alpha_{\text{anat}} = -3.5, & \text{if } (\{f\} \cup \text{Anc}(f)) \cap \mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \ne \emptyset, \\
      +\alpha_{\text{boost}} = +0.65, & \text{else if } \text{Anat}(f) \cap \mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \ne \emptyset, \\
      0, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}
  \item \textbf{Metabolic gap-filling prior ($m=5$, $\alpha_{\text{gap}} = 1.5$, \texttt{GapFillingPrior.groovy}) and post-refinement RLGC suggester (\texttt{ReactionLocalContextSuggester.groovy}):} Inside the Stage 1 loop, \texttt{GapFillingPrior.groovy} maps open metabolic gaps $r \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}$ to gap-filler EC numbers and GO terms (using explicit EC-to-GO mappings plus prefix expansion for partial ECs), applying $B_5(p, f) = +\alpha_{\text{gap}} = +1.5$ to direct gap matches and $+0.7\,\alpha_{\text{gap}} = +1.05$ (\texttt{gapseqGuessedFactor = 0.7d}) when the gap reaction was flagged as a low-homology \texttt{gapseq} guess. Following Stage 1 refinement, \texttt{ReactionLocalContextSuggester.groovy} scores candidate genes $p$ for each target gap reaction $R$ via a $K_{\text{hop}} = 2$ breadth-first search over the metabolic reaction adjacency graph $\mathcal{G}_{\text{rxn}}$ (graph decay $\alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)}$ with $\alpha_{\text{rxn}} = 0.50$ for hop distance $d(g) \in \{1, 2\}$ of neighbor reaction GO terms $g \in \mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R)$). Each anchor gene $p'$ on the same contig with $\max_{g \in \mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R)} P(p', g) \ge \tau_{\text{anc}} = 0.30$ receives anchor weight $w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) = \max_{g \in \mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R) : P(p', g) \ge \tau_{\text{anc}}} \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} P(p', g)$, and \texttt{GenomicDensityField.groovy} accumulates support within $\pm 4 h_{\text{bp}}$ via the Gaussian base-pair distance kernel $K_{\text{bp}}(\Delta\text{bp}) = \exp\bigl(-\Delta\text{bp}^2 / (2 h_{\text{bp}}^2)\bigr)$ ($h_{\text{bp}} = 5{,}000\text{ bp}$):
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \sum_{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 4 h_{\text{bp}}} w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) \, K_{\text{bp}}\bigl(\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')\bigr), \\
      S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \log D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) + \beta_{\text{div}} \text{Div}(p) - \gamma_{\text{com}} \text{Commit}(p) - \delta_{\text{self}} P(p, f_R) + \varepsilon_{\text{str}} \text{Strand}(p) - \zeta_{\text{gap}} \log_{10}\!\bigl(\max(1, \Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p))\bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rlgc}
  \end{equation}
  where the top $Q = 50$ candidates with $D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) \ge 0.01$ are evaluated using neighbor reaction diversity $\text{Div}(p) = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R)} \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} \bigl(1 - \prod_{p' \in W_2(p) \setminus \{p\}} (1 - P(p', g))\bigr)$ over window $W_2(p) = \{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 2 h_{\text{bp}}\}$ ($\beta_{\text{div}} = 1.0$, \texttt{computeDiversity}), non-target commitment penalty $\text{Commit}(p) = -\log\max\bigl(10^{-4},\, 1 - \max_{g \in (\mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R)) \setminus \{f_R\}} P(p, g)\bigr)$ ($\gamma_{\text{com}} = 1.0$, \texttt{computeCommitmentPenalty}), self-annotation penalty $\delta_{\text{self}} = 2.0$, strand concordance fraction $\text{Strand}(p) \in [0, 1]$ ($\varepsilon_{\text{str}} = 0.2$), and minimum anchor base-pair distance $\Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p)$ ($\zeta_{\text{gap}} = 0.1$), and normalized over the top 20 candidates via softmax $P_{\text{RLGC}}(p \mid R) = \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R)) / \sum_{p''} \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p'', R))$ with Bayes factor $\text{BF}_{\text{RLGC}} = \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R))$. In the M2/M4 Track A and Track B LRO rankers (\texttt{CrossGenomeReScorer} in \texttt{ConditionalLRScorer.groovy} and \texttt{benchmark/unified/pillar2\_fba\_lro\_price149/run\_pillar2\_all.py}), $S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R)$ is combined with cross-genome orthogroup co-occurrence ($\lambda_{\text{cg}} = 0.5$ in \texttt{CrossGenomeReScorer}), Pfam domain compatibility, and (in Track B) ESM-2 PLM reaction centroid cosine similarity.
  \item \textbf{Operon neighborhood prior ($m=6$, $\alpha_{\text{ctx}} = 1.0$, $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}} = 1.5$, $w_{\text{op\_cplx}} = 0.95$, \texttt{GenomicContextPrior.groovy}):} For each co-directional operon $O$ ($|O| \ge 2$) and reference pathway $W$ with $k_O(W) = |\{p' \in O : \max_{f' \in \text{Req}(W)} P^{(k-1)}(p', f') > 0.50\}| \ge 1$ active members, let $c_O(W) = k_O(W) / |O|$ denote the operon's pathway consensus strength. Claims $(p, f)$ with $p \in O$ and $f \in \text{Req}(W)$ receive $\alpha_{\text{ctx}} c_O(W)$ (multiplied by $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}} = 1.5$ when $f$ is also an open metabolic gap-filler GO term), and claims for an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ (\texttt{'n'}) where another member $p' \in O \setminus \{p\}$ carries active evidence $P^{(k-1)}(p', f) > 0.50$ for the same complex $f$ receive an additional $+w_{\text{op\_cplx}} = +0.95$:
  \begin{equation*}
    B_6(p, f) = \max_{W \ni f : k_O(W) \ge 1} \Bigl(\alpha_{\text{ctx}} \, c_O(W) \, \bigl(\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}}\bigr)^{\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}]}\Bigr) + w_{\text{op\_cplx}} \, \mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}} \wedge \exists p' \in O \setminus \{p\} \text{ with } P^{(k-1)}(p', f) > 0.50\bigr].
  \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}

All 17 configurable pipeline hyperparameters across Stage 1 and Stages 2A--2B ($12$ CLI parameters in \texttt{IntegrateCommand.groovy} and $5$ stoichiometric refiner parameters in \texttt{scripts/stoichiometric\_locus\_refiner.py}) were fitted exclusively on the \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 development proteome via two-stage block coordinate search (first optimizing Stage 1 weights and thresholds over a 5-point coordinate grid maximizing 5-fold cross-validated LRO Hit@1 plus GAEF consistency, then optimizing Stage 2A and Stage 2B stoichiometric parameters with Stage 1 frozen) and held strictly frozen across all non-\textit{E.~coli} benchmarks (Supplementary Section~S1 and Supplementary Table~S1). Complete one-at-a-time sensitivity perturbations of $\pm 20\%$ across all 17 parameters on the 8 non-\textit{E.~coli} genomes of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, the 144 \textit{B.~subtilis} LRO reactions, and the 22-genome \texttt{Price-149} panel (\texttt{scripts/run\_hyperparameter\_sensitivity.py}, \texttt{results/\allowbreak hyperparameter\_\allowbreak sensitivity\_\allowbreak non\_\allowbreak ecoli.tsv}) confirm that mean 8-genome micro $F_{\max}$ varies by $\le 0.0009$, GAEF by $\le 0.0031$, \textit{B.~subtilis} LRO Hit@1 by $\le 1.32$ percentage points, and \texttt{Price-149} CLEAN $F_{\max}$ by $\le 0.0036$.

\subsection{Cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical constraint bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}

To capture taxonomic restrictions entailed by multicellular eukaryotic developmental and physiological GO terms, we programmatically extracted five resource tables from the official GO, Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon}, Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, and NCBI Taxonomy OBO/OWL releases via \texttt{scripts/extract\_anatomical\_and\_taxon\_constraints.py} (and validated via \texttt{AnatomicalConstraints.groovy}): (1) 26,911 direct GO taxon constraint rules ($13{,}819$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $13{,}092$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across 18,159 distinct GO terms (\texttt{go-taxon-constraints.tsv}); (2) 2,715 equivalence and subclass cross-product axioms linking 2,674 distinct GO terms to 1,294 Uberon, PO, and CL classes across 30 object properties (\texttt{RO:0002296} \texttt{results\_in\_development\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002298} \texttt{results\_in\_morphogenesis\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002297} \texttt{results\_in\_formation\_of}, \texttt{RO:0002299} \texttt{results\_in\_maturation\_of}, \texttt{BFO:0000066} \texttt{occurs\_in}, and transport/organization relations; \texttt{go-anatomical-constraints.tsv}); (3) 8,331 taxon constraints ($4{,}767$ \texttt{only\_in\_taxon} and $3{,}564$ \texttt{never\_in\_taxon}) across 1,247 Uberon, PO, and CL classes (\texttt{uberon-po-taxon-constraints.tsv}); and (4) 7,722 anatomical hierarchy edges across 2,855 Uberon/PO/CL classes (\texttt{uberon-po-hierarchy.tsv}) and 1,818 taxonomic hierarchy edges across 1,305 NCBI Taxonomy clades (\texttt{ncbi-taxon-hierarchy.tsv}).

\subsection{Stage 2A: Deterministic symbolic enforcement and semantic ancestor relaxation}

In \texttt{IntegrateCommand.groovy} and \texttt{ConsistencyEnforcer.groovy} (invoked when \texttt{--enforce-stage2} is active), Stage 2A executes immediately after Stage 1 (and prior to Stage 2B) as a deterministic pass over all GO provenance entries in \texttt{IntegratedAnnotationSet} (\texttt{IntegrationState}). First, \texttt{computeForbiddenTerms()} identifies all GO terms among the proteome's provenance entries that violate a direct GO taxon constraint or an entailed Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraint in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ (including upward GO ancestor closure via \texttt{goReasoner.getSuperClasses}; when \texttt{organismTaxon} is absent or \texttt{mode == "minimal-flip"}, \texttt{TaxonConsistencyMaxSat} / \texttt{SatConsistencyChecker} extracts the unsatisfiable or minimal-weight flip set). In \texttt{relax} mode (\texttt{--enforce-consistency-mode relax}), for each protein $p$ and forbidden term $t$, \texttt{AnatomicalConstraints.findValidRelaxedAncestors()} traverses $\text{Anc}(t) \setminus \{\text{GO:0008150}, \text{GO:0003674}, \text{GO:0005575}\}$ to select the most specific valid non-root ancestors $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ (retaining only ancestors $a$ that have no valid descendant $b \in \text{Anc}(t)$ with $b \sqsubset a$). Reconstructing the unpenalized posterior $P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(0)}(p, t) + \sum_{m \notin \{\text{cons}, \text{anat}\} \vee B_m(p, t) > 0} B_m(p, t)\bigr)$ prior to negative consistency and anatomical penalties, each minimal valid ancestor $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ is updated directly (and if $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}}) = \emptyset$, $t$ is removed):
\begin{align}
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a) &= \max\Bigl(P^{(K)}(p, a), \, \gamma_{\text{relax}} \, P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)\Bigr), \nonumber \\
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) &= 0,
  \label{eq:relax}
\end{align}
with default relaxation factor $\gamma_{\text{relax}} = 1.0$ (\texttt{relaxFactor = 1.0d} in \texttt{ConsistencyEnforcer.groovy}), updating the provenance entry of $a$ via \texttt{logit}$(P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a))$ and removing $(p, t)$ from \texttt{state.provenance}. Second, in \texttt{IntegrateCommand.groovy}, for any obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ (\texttt{complexClassification[f] == 'n'}) that has exactly one active protein ($\ge 0.50$) and zero candidate partners ($\ge 0.08$) across the proteome, the isolated singleton prediction is deterministically attenuated to $0.45$ and logged as \texttt{PRUNED\_SINGLETON} in \texttt{EnforcementReport}.

\subsection{Stage 2B: Flux-certified stoichiometric locus refinement}

Stage 2B (\texttt{scripts/stoichiometric\_locus\_refiner.py}, invoked from the CLI via \texttt{StoichiometricLocusRefiner.groovy} when \texttt{--enable-fba-refiner} is passed) operates over a sanitized universal metabolic network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ constructed from the ModelSEED biochemistry database ($47{,}880$ total universal reactions across $33{,}978$ metabolites, of which $14{,}243$ reactions carry EC mappings and $11{,}826$ are strictly mass- and charge-balanced enzymatic reactions after flagging $2{,}417$ pool or imbalanced reactions with \texttt{imbalance\_penalty} $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100.0$, together with basal inorganic and cofactor exchange bounds). For each universal reaction $j \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ with catalytic mechanisms $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j) = \{m_1, \dots, m_{K_j}\}$ and required roles or subunits $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m) = \{u_1, \dots, u_{D_m}\}$ (where multi-role conjunctions $D_m \ge 2$ apply when explicit multi-subunit complex rules are supplied in \texttt{rxn\_mechanisms}, while single-EC mappings in the default universal metadata reduce to $D_m = 1$ Top-$M_{\text{iso}}$ isozyme disjunctions), \texttt{SparseRoleAggregator.compute\_reaction\_probabilities} (and \texttt{SparseRoleAggregator.groovy}) aggregates locus support $s_{p, u} \in (0, 1)$ over the top $M_{\text{iso}} = 3$ candidate genes per role with bottleneck gate $\theta_{\text{sub}} = 0.25$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q(u) &= 1 - \prod\limits_{p \in \text{Top-}M_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u))} \bigl(1 - s_{p, u}\bigr), \\
    S_{\text{mech}}(m) &= \begin{cases}
      \Bigl(\prod\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u)\Bigr)^{1 / D_m}, & \!\min\limits_{u} Q(u) \ge \theta_{\text{sub}}, \\
      \min\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u), & \!\text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases} \\
    S_{\text{rxn}}(j) &= \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)} S_{\text{mech}}(m).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gpr_agg}
\end{equation}

Second, reactions are partitioned into the draft core $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} = \{j : S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge \tau_{\text{core}} = 0.50\}$ (plus open medium exchanges and spontaneous reactions) and candidate repair reactions $j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$ restricted to the active solver domain $\mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}}$ comprising reactions with $S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge 10^{-3}$ or belonging to the 108-genome universal flux support skeleton \texttt{univ\_sup}, with strictly positive addition penalties $c_j^{\text{add}} = \lambda_{\text{add}} (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j)) + w_{\text{imb}}(j) + \epsilon_{\text{par}} > 0$ ($\lambda_{\text{add}} = 1.0$, $\epsilon_{\text{par}} = 10^{-3}$, $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100.0$ if $j$ is imbalanced and $0$ otherwise). Splitting reversible fluxes into non-negative variables $v_j = v_j^+ - v_j^-$ and setting the biomass target $v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \max\bigl(\alpha_{\text{bio}} \, v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}),\, 0.1\bigr)$ ($\alpha_{\text{bio}} = 0.10$), Stage 2B solves a two-step lexicographic pFBA linear program \citep{lewis2010omic} in HiGHS (feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$): Step 1 minimizes $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ (first constraining all imbalanced candidate bounds to $(0, 0)$ via \texttt{balanced\_bounds\_split} so that imbalanced reactions are strictly excluded whenever a balanced route exists, and falling back to the penalized bounds $w_{\text{imb}} = 100.0$ only if the strictly balanced LP is infeasible) to obtain minimum repair cost $C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$; Step 2 minimizes total non-biomass network flux $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \{\text{bio}\}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-) \le (1 + \delta_{\text{opt}}) C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) + 10^{-9}$ ($\delta_{\text{opt}} = 10^{-6}$). A non-core reaction $j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$ is certified as an active repair ($j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$) iff $|v_j^{\text{pFBA}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})| > \epsilon_{\text{flux}} = 10^{-6}$. When full certificate auditing is active (\texttt{compute\_certificates=True}), each repaired reaction receives $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 0.5\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}] + 0.3\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}] + 0.2\,\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in [0, 1]$, where $j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}$ iff $\min (v_j^+ + v_j^-) > \epsilon_{\text{flux}}$ subject to $\sum c_{j'}^{\text{add}} (v_{j'}^+ + v_{j'}^-) \le 1.01\,C^* + 10^{-7}$, $j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}$ iff constraining $v_j = 0$ either renders $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} \cup \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}$ growth-infeasible or increases the minimum repair cost on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}}$ above $1.10\,C^* + 10^{-6}$, and $\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in \{1.0, 0.65, 0.0\}$ is the perturbation stability score across $N_{\text{ens}} = 5$ repair LPs with $\pm 5\%$ uniform multiplicative cost noise ($1.0$ if active in $\ge 80\%$ of perturbed solves, $0.65$ if active in $> 0\%$, and $0.0$ otherwise; when \texttt{compute\_certificates=False}, $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 1.0$ for all pFBA-active repairs).

Third, \texttt{attribute\_locus\_responsibility} in \texttt{scripts/stoichiometric\_locus\_refiner.py} (and \texttt{LocusResponsibilityUpdater.groovy}) attributes certified reaction flux back to candidate gene loci against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$ with prior mass $\eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$, using each locus's sequence odds $o_{p, u} = \frac{s_{p, u}}{1 - s_{p, u}}$:
\begin{align}
  w_{j, u}(p) &= \bigl(o_{p, u}\bigr)^{\beta_1} \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)^{\beta_2} \nonumber \\
  &\quad \times \exp\bigl(\beta_3 C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) + \beta_4 S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)\bigr), \nonumber \\
  r_{j, u}(p) &= \frac{w_{j, u}(p)}{\eta_{\text{null}} + \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)} w_{j, u}(p')},
  \label{eq:responsibility}
\end{align}
with $\beta_1 = 1.0, \beta_2 = 1.0, \beta_3 = 1.5, \beta_4 = 1.5$, yielding the stoichiometric log-odds shift $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}(p, u) = \gamma_{\text{FBA}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(u)} \pi_j^{\text{FBA}}\, r_{j, u}(p)\, (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j))$ ($\gamma_{\text{FBA}} = 2.2$), applied with step weight $\lambda_t = \lambda_0 \gamma_{\text{damp}}^{t-1}$ ($\lambda_0 = 0.70, \gamma_{\text{damp}} = 0.60$) in \texttt{refine\_iterative} (in the CLI bridge \texttt{StoichiometricLocusRefiner.groovy}, the three auxiliary covariates passed to Python are extracted from \texttt{IntegratedAnnotationSet} as $\text{BF}_{\text{operon}} = \exp(B_{\text{gap}}(p, u)/1.5)$, $C_{\text{syn}} = \text{clip}(B_{\text{ctx}}(p, u) + B_{\text{coh}}(p, u), 0, 1)$, and $S_{\text{ESM2}}$ equal to the maximum raw claim score \texttt{sc.rawScore} over ESM/ProtT5/PLM/CLEAN supporting claims on $(p, u)$). In the standalone \texttt{Price-149} evaluation script (\texttt{scripts/eval\_price149\_real\_all6.py}), which evaluates both target-level and whole-genome predictions across 6 neural EC predictors, locus responsibility is computed as $r_j(p) = w_h / (0.05 + w_h)$ where $w_h = \text{odds}_{\text{seq}}^{0.5}\,\exp(0.85\,S_{\text{1hop}} + 0.40\,S_{\text{2hop}})\,\exp(1.2\,C_{\text{syn}})\,g_{\text{need}}$ and $g_{\text{need}} = \max(0.10,\, 1 - 0.85\,s_{\text{other},\max})$; \textbf{+ GSPA SLR (Minimal Glucose)} solves the glucose pFBA repair LP on each of the 22 host genomes and boosts candidate ECs in active or repaired reactions via $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}} = 2.2\,\pi_j\,r_j(p)\,(1 - s)$ ($\pi_j \in \{0.85, 0.45\}$); \textbf{+ GSPA SLR (Cognate Substrate)} (\texttt{+ SLR Stoich.\ Probe} in Table~\ref{tab:price149}) augments glucose pFBA repair with a 100\%-blind stoichiometric local-context reaction probe that scores 1-hop and 2-hop non-currency metabolite-sharing reaction adjacency $\mathcal{G}_{\text{rxn}}$ derived directly from $\mathbf{S}$ across chromosomal neighbors ($\pm 5$ genes with distance decay $\exp(-|\Delta g|/2.0)$) using exclusively predicted stoichiometry and chromosomal adjacency; and \textbf{+ Full GSPA Iter $t$} ($t \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) evaluates the recursive damped log-odds outer loop combining the Stage 1 operon/coherence shift $\Delta L_{\text{Stage1}}(p, u)$ and stoichiometric probe shift $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}^{\text{rxn}}(p, u)$ with $\Delta_{\text{tot}}(p, u) = \Delta L_{\text{Stage1}}(p, u) + \Delta L_{\text{FBA}}^{\text{rxn}}(p, u)$ and $L_{\text{target}}(p, u) = L^{(0)}(p, u) + \Delta_{\text{tot}}(p, u)$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    L_{\text{outer}}^{(1)}(p, u) &= (1 - 0.70)\,L^{(0)}(p, u) + 0.70\,L_{\text{target}}(p, u), \\
    L_{\text{outer}}^{(2)}(p, u) &= (1 - 0.42)\,L_{\text{outer}}^{(1)}(p, u) + 0.42\,\bigl(L_{\text{target}}(p, u) + 0.25\,\Delta_{\text{tot}}(p, u)\bigr), \\
    L_{\text{outer}}^{(3)}(p, u) &= (1 - 0.252)\,L_{\text{outer}}^{(2)}(p, u) + 0.252\,\bigl(L_{\text{target}}(p, u) + 0.35\,\Delta_{\text{tot}}(p, u)\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:outer_anchor}
\end{equation}
where multi-predictor refinement blends the single-predictor outer-pass probability $P_{\text{single}}^{(t)}(p, u) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L_{\text{outer}}^{(t)}(p, u)\bigr)$ with the 5-predictor Noisy-OR ensemble probability $P_{\text{ens}}(p, u)$ via $P_{\text{multi}}^{(t)}(p, u) = 0.55\,P_{\text{single}}^{(t)}(p, u) + 0.45\,P_{\text{ens}}(p, u)$.

To formalize the theoretical limits of metabolic gene recovery across Strata A, B, and C, we define five binary recoverability indicators for each held-out reaction $j$ and true gene $p^*$: (1) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}}(j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff reaction $j$ is present and mass-balanced in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$; (2) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}}(j, \mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff blocking $j$ requires non-zero repair flux under medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$ ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 1$ on minimal glucose for Stratum A, $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 1$ on cognate substrate for Stratum B, and $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}} = 0$ for secondary metabolism Stratum C); (3) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}}(j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff $j$ has no unmasked isozyme sufficient to carry full flux; (4) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) = \mathbb{I}[\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) > 1]$; and (5) $\mathcal{C}_{\text{complex}}(p^*, j) \in \{0, 1\}$, equal to $1$ iff $j$ is catalyzed by a multi-subunit complex with partner subunits present.

\subsection{Stage 3: Selective neuro-symbolic agentic annotation completion}

Stage 3 (\texttt{AnomalyExtractor.groovy} and \texttt{benchmark/agent/run\_gspa\_agent.py}) operates selectively on post-Stage-2 genome-scale anomalies across four categories: (1) \texttt{PATHWAY\_GAP} (a pathway with completeness $\ge \theta_{\text{path\_anom}}$ missing a step $f$ with sub-threshold candidates in $P_{\text{Stage2}} \in [\theta_{\text{floor}}, 0.50)$, where \texttt{AnomalyExtractor.groovy} defaults to $\theta_{\text{path\_anom}} = 0.50$, $\theta_{\text{floor}} = 0.05$, up to $8$ candidates per anomaly and $150$ anomalies per genome, and the Suite 8 benchmark focuses on high-completeness pathways $\ge 0.65$ with top $5$ candidates in $[0.15, 0.49]$); (2) \texttt{INCOMPLETE\_COMPLEX} (an obligate heteromeric complex \texttt{'n'} where \texttt{AnomalyExtractor.groovy} triggers when exactly one active anchor protein has posterior $\ge 0.50$ and $\ge 1$ sub-threshold candidate lies in $[\theta_{\text{floor}}, 0.50)$, and the Suite 8 benchmark samples incomplete complexes with $\ge 50\%$ partner subunit coverage); (3) \texttt{BORDERLINE\_ESSENTIAL} (an unfulfilled core essential category $f \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ with sub-threshold candidates); and (4) \texttt{BORDERLINE\_CONFLICT} (a claim in $[0.35, 0.62]$ subject to a taxonomic or anatomical conflict where domain inspection can refine the ancestral target).

For each anomaly, Stage 3 strips all evaluation metadata (\texttt{\_strip\_ground\_truth}) and runs a bounded multi-turn tool-calling loop ($\le 6$ turns in full mode, $\le 2$ turns in fast 1-step mode) using our locally hosted FP8-quantized 27B open-weight instruction checkpoint \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8} (derived from the Qwen2.5/Qwen3 open-weight model family \citep{yang2024qwen25} and served as \texttt{qwen3.8-27b} via vLLM on \texttt{unimatrix01} with a 32,768-token context window, temperature $0.0$, maximum 450 output tokens per turn; evaluated across 3 independent random seeds $\text{seed} \in \{42, 43, 44\}$, \texttt{results/pillar4\_multiseed\_summary.tsv}) equipped with five deterministic read-only tools: \texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak operon\_\allowbreak neighborhood}, \texttt{inspect\_\allowbreak pathway\_\allowbreak or\_\allowbreak complex\_\allowbreak gap}, \texttt{query\_\allowbreak protein\_\allowbreak evidence}, \texttt{check\_\allowbreak symbolic\_\allowbreak constraints}, and \texttt{propose\_\allowbreak resolution}. Every proposed resolution is verified by the fail-closed deterministic \texttt{SymbolicGuardrail} against all 48,101 GO term stanzas ($40{,}122$ active and $7{,}979$ obsolete) in \texttt{go-basic.obo}, 1,305 NCBI Taxonomy clades, 26,911 direct GO taxon constraints, and 2,715 GO--Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints before acceptance.

\subsection{BacDive Track 1, Track 2 Bayesian Anchor, and GSPA + DiASPora Hybrid protocols}
\label{sec:methods_bacdive}

In Suites 7 and 8 (\texttt{benchmark/unified/pillar3\_bacdive/run\_bacdive\_tracks1\_2.py}), we evaluate experimental carbon-source growth ($n = 2{,}332$ strain--substrate assays across $529$ ProTraits-matched bacterial genomes [$791$ total distinct \texttt{genome\_id} entries in \texttt{bacdive\_carbon\_2332\_real.tsv}]) and diagnostic enzyme activity ($n = 17{,}496$ strain--EC assays across $3{,}017$ BacDive type-strain assemblies [$3{,}170$ total distinct \texttt{strain\_id} entries in \texttt{bacdive\_enzyme\_17496\_real.tsv}]). For each strain $s$ and assay $y$ ($M = 37$ carbon substrates or $M = 10$ diagnostic enzymes), given binary reaction/pathway calls $g(s, y), m(s, y), c(s, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ from \texttt{gapseq} v1.2 \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, \texttt{ModelSEED} v2.6.1 \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, and \texttt{CarveMe} v1.5.2 \citep{machado2018fast}, \texttt{build\_cooccurrence\_coupling} forms the blended call matrix $B(s, y) = 0.55\,g(s, y) + 0.30\,m(s, y) + 0.15\,c(s, y)$ on carbon and $B(s, y) = 0.55\,g(s, y) + 0.28\,m(s, y) + 0.17\,c(s, y)$ on enzymes, column-centers $\tilde{B}_{s, y} = B(s, y) - \bar{B}_y$, computes the nonnegative cosine similarity $\Psi_0(y, y') = \max\bigl(0,\, \frac{\tilde{B}_{:,y}^\top \tilde{B}_{:,y'}}{\|\tilde{B}_{:,y}\|_2 \|\tilde{B}_{:,y'}\|_2 + 10^{-9}}\bigr)$ with $\Psi_0(y, y) = 0$, row-normalizes $\Psi(y, y') = \Psi_0(y, y') / (\sum_{y''} \Psi_0(y, y'') + 10^{-9})$, and defines the unsupervised cross-assay coupling score $\psi(s, y) = \sum_{y'} \Psi(y, y')\bigl(B(s, y') - 0.5\bigr)$. In \textbf{GSPA Track 1 (100\% Blind)}, binary calls are mapped to calibrated tool probabilities $p_{\text{gap}}(s, y) \in \{0.12, 0.88\}$, $p_{\text{ms}}(s, y) \in \{0.22, 0.80\}$, and $p_{\text{cm}}(s, y) \in \{0.30, 0.72\}$ on carbon ($p_{\text{gap}} \in \{0.14, 0.86\}$, $p_{\text{ms}} \in \{0.24, 0.78\}$, and $p_{\text{cm}} \in \{0.28, 0.74\}$ on enzymes) and pooled in log-odds space:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\!\Bigl(w_{\text{gap}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{gap}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{ms}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{ms}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{cm}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{cm}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_\psi\,\psi(s, y)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:bacdive_t1}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\text{gap}}, w_{\text{ms}}, w_{\text{cm}}, w_\psi) = (0.56, 0.28, 0.16, 0.65)$ on carbon and $(0.55, 0.27, 0.18, 0.55)$ on enzymes, evaluated at $\theta = 0.50$.

For the supervised classifiers in Track 1 (\texttt{Traitar} L1-regularized \texttt{LinearSVC} wrapped in \texttt{CalibratedClassifierCV} with $C = 0.3$ on carbon and $C = 0.2$ on enzymes \citep{weimann2016traitar}, \texttt{BacDive RF Baseline} Random Forest with $200$ trees on carbon and $150$ trees on enzymes at max depth $8$ \citep{koblitz2025diaspora,schober2025bac}, and \texttt{DiASPora} \texttt{GradientBoostingClassifier} with $150$ trees, max depth $4$, and learning rate $0.05$ on carbon and \texttt{HistGradientBoostingClassifier} with $100$ iterations, max depth $4$, and learning rate $0.08$ on enzymes \citep{koblitz2025diaspora}), all models are trained and evaluated under deterministic 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (\texttt{fold(s) = int(md5(genus)[:8], 16) mod 5}; per-fold metrics in \texttt{results/\allowbreak pillar3\_\allowbreak bacdive\_\allowbreak per\_\allowbreak fold\_\allowbreak cv.tsv}). Baseline classifiers use the $(3+M)$-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{x}_{\text{base}}(s, y) = [g(s, y), m(s, y), c(s, y), \mathbf{e}_y] \in \mathbb{R}^{3+M}$, where $\mathbf{e}_y \in \{0, 1\}^M$ is the assay one-hot indicator. Using the training-fold tool precision/false-alarm rates $(p_g^{\text{tr}}, p_m^{\text{tr}}, p_c^{\text{tr}})$ and Laplace-smoothed training-fold assay prevalence $\pi_{\text{prior}}(s, y)$, GSPA forms the fold-local pooled score $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(w_g^{\text{tr}}\operatorname{logit}(p_g^{\text{tr}}) + w_m^{\text{tr}}\operatorname{logit}(p_m^{\text{tr}}) + w_c^{\text{tr}}\operatorname{logit}(p_c^{\text{tr}}) + w_\pi^{\text{tr}}\operatorname{logit}(\pi_{\text{prior}}) + w_\psi^{\text{tr}}\psi(s, y)\bigr)$ (with $(0.45, 0.25, 0.15, 0.15, 0.50)$ on carbon and $(0.48, 0.24, 0.14, 0.14, 0.45)$ on enzymes), constructs the $(10+4M)$-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{x}_{\text{GSPA}}(s, y)$ (appending tool consensus, weighted sum, tool disagreement, $\psi(s, y)$, strain metabolic breadth, $\pi_{\text{prior}}(s, y)$, $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$, and tool--assay interactions $g\mathbf{e}_y, m\mathbf{e}_y, c\mathbf{e}_y$), trains the hybrid classifier (\texttt{GradientBoostingClassifier} with $180$ trees, max depth $4$, learning rate $0.05$ on carbon; \texttt{HistGradientBoostingClassifier} with $160$ iterations, max depth $5$, learning rate $0.08$ on enzymes), and blends the out-of-fold classifier probability $P_{\text{ML}}(s, y)$ with $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$ as $P_{\text{Hyb1}}(s, y) = 0.88\,P_{\text{ML}}(s, y) + 0.12\,p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$ (evaluated at $\theta = 0.50$ on carbon and at the training-fold $F_1$-optimal threshold on the enzyme panel). In Track 2, $30\%$ of assay rows (\texttt{int(md5(f"\{id\}\_\{assay\}\_\{idx\}")[:8], 16) mod 100 < 30}) are revealed ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{rev}}$) and evaluation is restricted to the held-out $70\%$ ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{hold}}$). \textbf{GSPA Track 2 Bayesian Anchor} combines $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$ with the revealed substrate prevalence $s_{b,\text{emp}}(y)$ and revealed-assay co-occurrence shift $\psi_{\text{rev}}(s, y)$ on carbon via $P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(0.72\operatorname{logit}(p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)) + 0.28\operatorname{logit}(s_{b,\text{emp}}(y)) + 0.85\,\psi_{\text{rev}}(s, y)\bigr)$, and combines $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$ with the revealed genus--EC posterior $g_{e,\text{emp}}(s, y)$ on enzymes via $P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(0.70\operatorname{logit}(p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)) + 0.30\operatorname{logit}(g_{e,\text{emp}}(s, y))\bigr)$ (with decision thresholds tuned strictly on the revealed $30\%$ split), while \textbf{GSPA Track 2 + DiASPora Hybrid} blends the Track 2 classifier $P_{\text{ML2}}(s, y)$ with $P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y)$ as $P_{\text{Hyb2}}(s, y) = 0.65\,P_{\text{ML2}}(s, y) + 0.35\,P_{\text{Track2}}(s, y)$ evaluated at $\theta = 0.50$. All wall-clock runtimes and peak heap memory were measured on dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 compute nodes (8 CPU threads per genome; NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU for vLLM and PLM embedding extraction).

\subsection{Evaluation metrics, donor-database homology masking, and internal GAEF score}

Across Suites 1--8, functional predictions are evaluated using standard protein-centric CAFA metrics ($F_{\max}$, information-accretion-weighted $WF_{\max}$, pair-level micro $F_{\max}$, and minimum semantic distance $S_{\min}$ across thresholds $\theta \in \{0.01, \dots, 0.99\}$ \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,clark2013information}) with $95\%$ bootstrap or exact Clopper--Pearson confidence intervals and two-sided paired Wilcoxon or McNemar tests. Under donor-database homology masking (\texttt{Protocol H} at sequence-identity cutoffs $\eta_{\text{id}} \in \{100\%, 70\%, 50\%, 30\%\}$ and taxonomic Genus/Family masking \texttt{Protocol D} in \texttt{apply\_protocol\_H.py}), all reference donor proteins $r$ with pairwise sequence identity $\text{pident}(g, r) > \eta_{\text{id}}$ (or sharing the same genus/family; when explicit donor \texttt{pident} metadata is absent on a pre-merged claim, raw homology confidence is conservatively capped at $\eta_{\text{id}}/100$) to any test protein $g$ are excluded from the DIAMOND database, ESM-2/ProtT5 $k$NN donor indices, FoldSeek templates, and probability calibrators; for the neural enzyme predictors in Suite 1C (\texttt{CLEAN}, \texttt{DeepProZyme}, and \texttt{EnzBERT}), reference lookup databases and training-split homologs above $\eta_{\text{id}}$ are filtered per each tool's homology-split evaluation protocol, while the underlying pretrained protein language model backbone weights (ESM-2 and ProtT5) remain frozen as released (which may retain pretraining exposure to UniRef50 sequences and therefore makes strict Track A cosine-masked LRO a more conservative test of de novo context recovery).

Complementing external ground-truth evaluation, the internal Genome-Scale Annotation Evaluation Framework (GAEF; \texttt{QualityReport.groovy}) structural audit score summarizes genome-wide invariants at $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$ across four weighted dimensions: Essential Function Completeness ($C_{\text{ess}}$, default weight $w_{\text{ess}} = 0.30$), Functional Coherence ($C_{\text{func}}$, default weight $w_{\text{func}} = 0.40$, computed as the mean of non-null Pathway Coherence $C_{\text{path}}$, Complex Coherence $C_{\text{cplx}}$, and Process Coherence $C_{\text{proc}}$), Taxonomic/Anatomical Consistency ($C_{\text{cons}} = \max(0,\, 1 - 0.01\,V(\mathcal{P}))$, default weight $w_{\text{cons}} = 0.30$, where $V(\mathcal{P})$ is the count of formal constraint violations), and Functional Resolution ($C_{\text{res}} = \min(1.0,\, \text{meanIC}/10.0)$, default weight $w_{\text{res}} = 0.00$), renormalized over all non-null evaluated dimensions:
\begin{equation}
  \text{GAEF}(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{\sum_{d \in \{\text{ess}, \text{func}, \text{cons}, \text{res}\} : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d \, C_d}{\sum_{d : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d},
  \label{eq:gaef}
\end{equation}
which reduces to $0.30 \, C_{\text{ess}} + 0.40 \, C_{\text{func}} + 0.30 \, C_{\text{cons}}$ under default weights when completeness, coherence, and consistency are all evaluated. Because $C_{\text{ess}}, C_{\text{func}}$, and $C_{\text{cons}}$ measure the structural invariants targeted by Stages 1 and 2, GAEF is reported strictly as an internal structural diagnostic alongside held-out experimental validation.
