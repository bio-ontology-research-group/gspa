## 1. Global Editorial & House-Style Rules
1. **One Unifying Research Narrative (The Three Drivers):**
   - **Driver 1 (Complementary per-protein predictors — Stage 0):** Calibrated pooling of sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity establishes the foundational protein-level probability distribution (extending the multi-source protein-ensemble paradigm of GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}). Homology and sequence/structure similarity remain essential starting points for conserved proteins.
   - **Driver 2 (Whole-genome architecture and organism-wide constraints — Stages 1 & 2A):** Moving the unit of inference from an isolated sequence to the complete genome (operon co-directionality, chromosomal neighborhoods, core essentiality, obligate heteromeric complex stoichiometry, and taxonomic plus Uberon/PO/CL anatomical consistency via semantic ancestor relaxation). This shift is **timely** because long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and modern genome/metagenome assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye} now routinely recover complete or near-complete chromosomes rather than fragmented short-read contigs.
   - **Driver 3 (Systems biology, stoichiometric flux modeling, and selective guarded reasoning beyond homology — Stages 1, 2B & 3):** Advancing beyond our initial reaction-indicator MILP approach METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} (accepted at the PSB 2027 "Biological Molecular Function: Beyond Homology" session) by (i) coupling metabolic gaps to reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) and operon synteny, (ii) replacing negative-cost reaction-level MILP with role-aware DNF mechanism aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA), and competitive gene-locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis, and (iii) selectively invoking a tool-using language model on the $<2.5\%$ of proteins that remain ambiguous under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail.

## 2. Title & Abstract

### Title (Sentence Case)
- `Genome-scale context, stoichiometric refinement, and neuro-symbolic reasoning for proteome function annotation`

### Abstract (5 Sentences, ~180 Words)
- **Sentence 1 (Problem & Timeliness):** Per-protein function predictors combine sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity to score individual proteins, yet evaluate each sequence independently of the complete chromosomes, operons, heteromeric complexes, and metabolic networks now routinely recovered by long-read sequencing and modern genome assembly.
- **Sentence 2 (Method & Three Drivers):** Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA), a staged framework that improves proteome annotation by (1) calibrating and pooling complementary per-protein predictors, (2) propagating whole-genome constraints across operons, core essential functions, obligate heteromeric complexes, and cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical axioms, and (3) coupling reaction-local genomic context with positive-cost parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA), competitive gene-locus responsibility, and selective ontology-guarded language-model reasoning.
- **Sentence 3 (Driver 1 & Driver 2 Headline Result):** Across bacterial, archaeal, viral, and eukaryotic proteomes, multi-predictor pooling establishes the protein-centric baseline while whole-genome inference eliminates all taxonomic and anatomical violations, outperforms deletion-based filtering across multicellular animals and vascular plants, and raises held-out heteromeric complex subunit recovery Hit@1 from $56.0\%$ to $93.7\%$ ($94.3\%$ with guarded agentic disambiguation).
- **Sentence 4 (Driver 3 Headline Result):** By replacing negative-cost reaction-level gap-filling ($83.7\%$ zero-flux reaction additions) with positive-cost pFBA and competitive locus attribution, GSPA retains strictly flux-carrying repairs, improves enzyme identification on fitness-validated bacterial enzymes and experimental `BacDive` growth and enzyme phenotypes, and resolves ambiguous metabolic gaps on the $<2.5\%$ of loci that trigger guarded reasoning (raising leave-reaction-out Hit@1 from $34.0\%$ to $82.7\%$).
- **Sentence 5 (Implication & Availability):** These results show that protein similarity, whole-genome architecture, and systems-level biological constraints form complementary layers of functional inference; the software, constraint ontologies, and benchmarks are openly available at `https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa`.

## 3. Section 1: Introduction (7 Paragraphs)
- **Paragraph 1 (Functional annotation as a joint genome-scale problem):**
  - Define protein function annotation from general to specific: given the protein-coding genes $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ of an organism, their chromosomal coordinates and strand organization $\mathcal{K}$, the organism's taxonomic lineage $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, and reference biochemical and ontological knowledge bases, infer a joint assignment of Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers across $\mathcal{P}$.
  - Explain in connected prose that a biologically coherent proteome annotation must satisfy organism-level invariants across proteins: core domain-essential cellular functions are represented, metabolic pathways form stoichiometrically connected routes supporting biomass synthesis, obligate heteromeric complexes are annotated as complete multi-subunit assemblies, and assigned terms obey both taxonomic constraints and the anatomical body plan of the organism's clade.
- **Paragraph 2 (Why whole-genome inference is timely now):**
  - Explain the technological shift that makes whole-genome inference broadly applicable: historically, short-read sequencing and metagenomics produced fragmented contigs from which open reading frames could be called in isolation, while complete operon architecture and whole-cell reaction inventories remained unavailable.
  - With long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and modern repeat-graph genome and metagenome assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye}, complete or near-complete chromosomes are now routinely assembled for both cultivated isolates and uncultured metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), making gene order, co-directional operons, and genome-wide pathway and complex complements standard inputs for functional annotation.
- **Paragraph 3 (SOTA Concept 1 — Per-protein similarity and multi-source ensembles [Driver 1]):**
  - Organize protein-level methods around the representations they compute: sequence alignment \citep{buchfink2021sensitive,li2021refseq,schwengers2021bakta}, profile HMM domain signatures \citep{eddy2011accelerated,jones2014interproscan}, pretrained protein language model embeddings \citep{lin2023evolutionary,elnaggar2021prottrans,kulmanov2024deepgose,yu2023enzyme}, and structural fold similarity \citep{gligorijevic2021structure,vankempen2024foldseek}.
  - Explain the advance introduced by multi-source protein-level ensembles such as GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3} in CAFA evaluations \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,zhou2019cafa}: combining complementary homology, domain, motif, and language-model features yields a stronger initial ranking than any individual channel because different representations make independent errors.
  - State positively that homology and per-protein similarity provide the foundational probability distribution for conserved proteins, while identifying their structural boundary: scoring each protein $p_i$ conditionally independently of $\mathcal{P}\setminus\{p_i\}$ leaves divergent pathway enzymes below detection thresholds, produces isolated singleton subunit calls on heteromeric complexes, and transfers clade-inappropriate developmental annotations across distant homologs.
- **Paragraph 4 (SOTA Concept 2 — Genomic context and chromosomal architecture [Driver 2]):**
  - Explain how comparative genomics moved beyond isolated sequences by modeling conserved gene neighborhoods, co-directional operons, and phylogenetic profiles \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,szklarczyk2023string}, and how genomic language models \citep{hwang2024genomic,cornman2024omg} and neighborhood-aware predictors \citep{toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised} learn contextualized representations across contiguous gene windows.
  - Explain what these representations achieve (disambiguating paralogs and identifying co-regulated pathway or complex partners) and where local context stops: a local window on a contig does not represent genome-wide core essentiality, dispersed multi-subunit complex stoichiometry, or formal taxonomic and anatomical constraints.
- **Paragraph 5 (SOTA Concept 3 — Systems biology, metabolic gap-filling, and advancing beyond METEOR [Driver 3]):**
  - Explain how constraint-based metabolic reconstruction tools (such as Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, and GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind}) use stoichiometric reaction networks and flux balance analysis to identify enzymatic reactions required for steady-state growth.
  - Present METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} (accepted at the PSB 2027 "Biological Molecular Function: Beyond Homology" session) as our initial effort to couple metabolic network gap-filling with protein-level enzyme prediction via reaction-indicator mixed-integer linear programming (MILP). Explain the two specific limitations of reaction-level MILP that motivate the present work: (1) assigning negative objective costs to candidate reactions above an addition threshold activates hundreds of reactions that carry zero steady-state flux, and (2) transferring reaction support uniformly to candidate genes inflates false-positive scores across paralogs without considering operon neighborhoods, heteromeric complex roles, or competition among candidate loci.
- **Paragraph 6 (SOTA Concept 4 — Cross-ontology anatomical axioms and guarded agentic reasoning [Drivers 2 & 3]):**
  - Explain how formal bio-ontology constraints — GO taxon constraints \citep{deegan2010formalization} and cross-product logical definitions linking GO biological processes to the Uberon anatomy ontology \citep{mungall2012uberon}, Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, and Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell} — define which functions are biologically admissible in a clade, while noting that existing post-filters delete violating terms together with their valid ancestral biological process annotations.
  - Explain how tool-using language agents \citep{wang2025_geneagent,caufield2024_spires} can synthesize heterogeneous genomic evidence on residual ambiguous loci when paired with deterministic symbolic verification.
- **Paragraph 7 (The GSPA framework mapped to the three drivers, evaluation scope, and availability):**
  - Introduce GSPA (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}) and map its four stages directly to the three drivers: Stage 0 (Driver 1: calibrated correlation-grouped pooling of complementary per-protein predictors), Stage 1 and Stage 2A (Driver 2: whole-proteome Bayesian log-odds refinement and semantic ancestor relaxation over taxonomic and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical constraints), Stage 2B (Driver 3: role-aware DNF mechanism aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious FBA \citep{lewis2010omic}, and competitive locus responsibility against an unassigned-gene hypothesis), and Stage 3 (Driver 3: selective tool-using language-model disambiguation on $<2.5\%$ of proteins under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail).
  - State the five questions evaluated across bacteria, archaea, viruses, and multicellular eukaryotes and provide the public repository URL.

### Subsection 2.1: Complementary predictors establish the protein-level baseline
- **Paragraph 1 (Multi-channel pooling vs. individual channels and production pipelines):**
  - *(a) Question:* Does combining complementary per-protein predictors improve accuracy over individual evidence channels and production genome annotation pipelines under uniform ancestor-propagated scoring?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared calibrated Stage 0 + Stage 1/2 integration against individual sequence, domain, PLM, and structure channels and against NCBI PGAP, InterProScan 5, metagenomic-deepFRI, and gLM/gLM2 across the 13-genome and 21-genome reference panels (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d; full channel table in Supplementary Section~S3).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* On the 11-prokaryote panel, 3-channel GSPA reaches micro $F_{\max} = 0.8421$ versus $0.7820$ for NCBI PGAP; on the 21-genome panel, 7-channel integration reaches micro $F_{\max} = 0.8705$, exceeding the strongest individual channel (ESM-2 $k$NN at $0.8580$) and genomic language models gLM/gLM2 ($0.7280$).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Complementary sequence, domain, structure, and PLM representations provide a stronger initial probability distribution than any single predictor (Driver 1); we next tested whether this advantage holds under temporal holdout and donor-homology masking.
- **Paragraph 2 (Prospective temporal holdout and the sequence twilight zone):**
  - *(a) Question:* How do multi-predictor pooling and whole-genome refinement perform on proteins annotated after the training cutoff, particularly in the $<30\%$ sequence-identity twilight zone?
  - *(b) How tested:* Evaluated the CAFA temporal holdout ($16{,}125$ protein–aspect evaluations, including $3{,}440$ twilight-zone evaluations at $<30\%$ sequence identity; Supplementary Section~S3).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Stage 0 multi-channel pooling establishes overall macro $F_{\max} = 0.6929$ (versus $0.6138$ for the strongest standalone baseline, ProteInfer), while whole-genome Stage 1+2 refinement specifically lifts the $<30\%$ identity subset from $0.6723$ to $0.6775$ at the first iteration.
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Protein-level pooling drives bulk accuracy on conserved sequences, whereas whole-genome context provides its largest gains where direct sequence similarity weakens; we next examined whether multi-channel consensus also stabilizes enzyme commission (EC) predictions across homology-masking tiers.
- **Paragraph 3 (Post-cutoff enzyme holdout and 95-genome homology-masked EC consensus):**
  - *(a) Question:* Does multi-predictor integration improve enzyme function prediction and prevent false-positive top-1 flips across homology-masking regimes?
  - *(b) How tested:* Evaluated CLEAN, EnzBERT, and DeepProZyme on the post-cutoff Swiss-Prot `Holdout-125` enzyme benchmark (Supplementary Table~S4) and across $69{,}666$ proteins in 95 bacterial genomes under four donor-homology masking tiers ($100\%, \le 70\%, \le 50\%, \le 30\%$ sequence identity; Supplementary Table~S5).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* On `Holdout-125`, Stage 0 pooling lifts $F_{\max}$ to $0.4381$ (and Stages 1–2 lift Top-5 recovery to $44.0\%$), while across the 95 bacterial genomes, combining multi-channel consensus with stoichiometric filtering yields statistically significant net Top-1 enzyme gains across all 12 predictor-by-homology-tier combinations (up to $+155$ net Top-1 hits on CLEAN, $p < 10^{-25}$), reversing the net Top-1 losses incurred by unfiltered MILP gap-filling on DeepProZyme and EnzBERT.
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Having established that multi-predictor integration provides a reliable per-protein baseline (Driver 1), we next isolated what whole-genome structural priors add (Driver 2).

### Subsection 2.2: Whole-genome priors improve structural coherence while preserving accuracy
- **Paragraph 1 (Accuracy preservation and structural gain across the 9-genome ablation):**
  - *(a) Question:* Can whole-genome Bayesian priors improve proteome-wide structural coherence without degrading the strong protein-centric baseline established in Stage 0?
  - *(b) How tested:* Performed a systematic 9-configuration ablation across nine bacterial and archaeal reference genomes (one calibration genome, *E. coli* K-12, and eight out-of-sample genomes; 81 runs; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Across the nine genomes, full Stage 1+2 refinement preserves protein-centric CAFA $F_{\max}$ ($0.8041$ at Stage 0 versus $0.8037$ after full refinement) while increasing pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ from $0.7631$ to $0.7660$ ($p = 0.0391$) and lifting the composite structural audit score from $0.8459$ to $0.8643$ ($p = 0.00391$; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Whole-genome priors refine the $<15\%$ of twilight-zone and structurally constrained loci around the conserved homology backbone; we next examined which specific biological invariants respond to each prior.
- **Paragraph 2 (Contribution of individual Stage 1 priors):**
  - *(a) Question:* How do individual whole-genome priors affect core essentiality, pathway connectivity, heteromeric complex completeness, and taxonomic consistency?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared single-prior ablations against the combined Stage 1+2 model on the nine reference genomes (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Combining essentiality, pathway coherence, gap-filling, and operon context raises mean pathway coherence from $0.4975$ to $0.5146$ ($p = 0.00781$) and complex coherence from $0.8636$ to $0.8852$, while taxonomic and anatomical constraints eliminate all $25$ baseline taxonomic violations ($C_{\text{cons}}$ from $0.9722$ to $1.0000$, $p = 0.00391$; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Each prior repairs its targeted genome-wide invariant; to test whether these constraints also withstand noisy cross-clade homology transfer, we performed a perturbation experiment.
- **Paragraph 3 (Suppression of injected cross-clade perturbations):**
  - *(a) Question:* Do whole-genome taxonomic and anatomical constraints suppress high-scoring cross-clade false positives that survive probability calibration?
  - *(b) How tested:* Injected 900 cross-clade eukaryotic developmental and clade-restricted GO claims across the nine prokaryotic proteomes (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Stage 1+2 ontological constraints suppress $122$ of the $125$ injected claims that survive Stage 0 calibration above the $\theta = 0.50$ threshold ($97.6\%$ suppression; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Organism-wide taxonomic and anatomical priors catch cross-clade transfers that per-protein calibration alone cannot filter; we next tested whether these structural gains scale to a thousand bacterial genomes and across all four kingdoms of life.
- **Paragraph 4 (Scaling to 1,000 bacterial genomes and 88 four-kingdom proteomes):**
  - *(a) Question:* Do whole-genome structural improvements hold at population scale and across Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya, and Viruses?
  - *(b) How tested:* Audited 1,000 RefSeq bacterial genomes and 88 curated proteomes spanning all four kingdoms with frozen hyperparameters (Supplementary Section~S3).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Across the 1,000 bacterial genomes, Stages 1+2 reduce taxonomic violations from $23{,}384$ violating predictions per genome at Stage 0 (and over $106{,}000$ under standalone DeepFRI) to zero, while raising MetaCyc pathway coherence from $36.71\%$ to $66.75\%$ in $5.4\text{ s/genome}$; across the 88 four-kingdom proteomes, whole-genome refinement consistently improves accuracy in Bacteria and Viruses with $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals strictly above zero.
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Whole-genome inference enforces structural consistency at scale in seconds per genome; we next tested whether genomic architecture can identify the exact gene locus of missing heteromeric complex subunits.

### Subsection 2.3: Operon architecture completes obligate heteromeric complexes
- **Paragraph 1 (Leave-subunit-out recovery across six prokaryotic genomes):**
  - *(a) Question:* When a subunit of an obligate heteromeric complex has only twilight-zone homology, can complex stoichiometry and operon context distinguish the true subunit from domain-matched decoys?
  - *(b) How tested:* Performed 175 leave-subunit-out (LSO) trials across six prokaryotic genomes, attenuating the true subunit into the twilight zone alongside five matched non-complex decoy proteins in unrelated genomic loci (6 candidates per trial, chance Hit@1 $= 0.167$; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}a and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* While the Stage 1 complex coherence prior alone detects that a complex is incomplete and boosts recall, it ranks the true subunit no better than baseline Noisy-OR (Hit@1 $= 0.5600$); adding operon neighborhood synteny raises Hit@1 to $0.9371$ ($0.9310$ across the five out-of-sample species; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Complex stoichiometry identifies *which* assembly is missing a partner across the genome, while chromosomal operon architecture identifies *which gene locus* supplies that partner (Driver 2); we next asked what happens when complex subunits are dispersed across non-adjacent chromosomal loci.
- **Paragraph 2 (Dispersed photosynthetic and respiratory complexes in *Synechocystis*):**
  - *(a) Question:* Can selective neuro-symbolic reasoning recover complex subunits in organisms where subunits are split across distant chromosomal loci?
  - *(b) How tested:* Examined LSO recovery across the 25 trials in the cyanobacterium *Synechocystis* sp.\ PCC 6803, where photosystem and respiratory complex genes are frequently non-operonic (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}b and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* In *Synechocystis*, operon context raises Hit@1 from $0.5200$ to $0.6400$, and adding Stage 3 neuro-symbolic disambiguation further lifts Hit@1 to $0.6800$ (bringing 6-genome Hit@1 to $0.9429$; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Local operon synteny resolves contiguous bacterial complexes, while selective Stage 3 domain and pathway reasoning complements local architecture when subunits are chromosomally dispersed. Having tested macromolecular complexes, we next evaluated organism-wide anatomical constraints in multicellular eukaryotes.

### Subsection 2.4: Cross-ontology anatomical reasoning preserves valid ancestral biology in eukaryotes
- **Paragraph 1 (Endogenous anatomical and taxonomic contradictions in eukaryotic proteomes):**
  - *(a) Question:* Do standard multi-channel homology and PLM predictions produce cross-clade anatomical contradictions in unperturbed eukaryotic proteomes?
  - *(b) How tested:* Audited unperturbed 7-channel Stage 0 predictions across six diverse eukaryotes (*S. cerevisiae*, *C. elegans*, *D. melanogaster*, *P. patens*, *A. thaliana*, and *M. musculus*; $6{,}155$ proteins) against our cross-ontology GO–Uberon/PO/CL constraint bridge (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}; ontology table statistics in Supplementary Table~S3).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Unconstrained Stage 0 transfer produces $1{,}009$ endogenous taxonomic and anatomical violations across the six species (concentrated in *C. elegans* and *D. melanogaster* from mammalian and angiosperm homologs); GSPA eliminates all $1{,}009$ violations while improving 6-species mean CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.7096$ to $0.7117$ (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Cross-clade homology transfer routinely assigns vertebrate or angiosperm organ processes to invertebrates, fungi, and mosses; we next tested which ontological representation is required to catch these errors.
- **Paragraph 2 (Why cross-ontology Uberon/PO/CL axioms and deterministic Stage 2A enforcement are necessary):**
  - *(a) Question:* Are direct GO taxon constraints or soft Bayesian log-odds penalties sufficient to eliminate eukaryotic anatomical errors?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared direct GO taxon constraints alone, the Stage 1 soft anatomical penalty alone, and Stage 2A deterministic cross-ontology enforcement across the 6-eukaryote stress-test panel ($1{,}452$ baseline violations; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Direct GO taxon constraints leave $642$ of the $1{,}452$ violations ($44.2\%$) unresolved because the taxon restrictions reside on the linked Uberon, PO, and CL anatomical structures, and soft Stage 1 penalties still leave high-confidence homology violations in invertebrates, whereas Stage 2A cross-ontology enforcement eliminates $100\%$ of violations (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Enforcing anatomical validity requires bridging GO biological processes to Uberon, PO, and CL and applying deterministic post-Stage-1 enforcement; we next tested whether relaxing forbidden terms to valid GO ancestors outperforms deleting them.
- **Paragraph 3 (Semantic ancestor relaxation versus naive deletion):**
  - *(a) Question:* Does relaxing an anatomically forbidden GO term to its nearest valid ancestor preserve more biological information than deleting the violating annotation?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared Stage 2A naive deletion against Stage 2A semantic ancestor relaxation on transfer-evaluated micro $F_{\max}$, recall, and annotation information content (IC) against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground truth (Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Semantic ancestor relaxation retains $95.1\%$–$98.7\%$ of baseline annotation information content and outperforms naive deletion in micro $F_{\max}$ across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants (by $+0.0006$ to $+0.0157$, largest in *D. melanogaster*; Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}), while recovering higher recall and information content in unicellular yeast and non-vascular moss where injected organ terms land on housekeeping proteins.
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Semantic relaxation converts ontology constraints from a purely eliminative filter into a constructive refinement that retains conserved tissue- and process-level biology (Driver 2). We next turned to Driver 3: using genomic architecture and systems biology to localize missing metabolic enzymes.

### Subsection 2.5: Genomic architecture and language models localize missing pathway enzymes
- **Paragraph 1 (Leave-reaction-out recovery without PLM embeddings — RLGC vs. uniform MILP boost):**
  - *(a) Question:* When sequence homology and embedding donors for a metabolic reaction are masked, can reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) localize the missing enzyme where uniform reaction-level MILP boosting fails?
  - *(b) How tested:* Masked ground-truth gene–reaction links across 256 strict leave-reaction-out (LRO) trials in *E. coli* K-12 under Track A (cosine-masked homology and embeddings) and without PLM centroids (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Sequence-only ranking and METEOR's uniform reaction-level MILP boost recover the true enzyme at Rank 1 (Hit@1) in only $1.95\%$ of all reactions, whereas RLGC operon kernel density coupled with Stage 1 pathway coherence raises unconditional Hit@1 twelve-fold to $23.44\%$ ($34.29\%$ among covered candidate pools; Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Uniform reaction-level boosting cannot distinguish among candidate paralogs in a genome, whereas chromosomal neighborhood density around pathway-adjacent anchor genes provides strong de novo locus attribution; we next examined how genomic context interacts with pretrained protein language model embeddings.
- **Paragraph 2 (Complementarity of PLM reaction centroids and genomic context across species and strata):**
  - *(a) Question:* How do protein language model reaction centroids and local genomic architecture complement each other across metabolic roles and out-of-sample species?
  - *(b) How tested:* Evaluated Track B (retaining Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids while masking sequence alignments) across the 256 strict and 414 role-stratified *E. coli* reactions and out-of-sample on 144 reactions in *B. subtilis* 168 with frozen hyperparameters (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a–c and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* On *E. coli*, ESM-2 reaction centroids alone reach $62.11\%$ unconditional Hit@1, and combining PLM centroids with RLGC operon density and pathway coherence reaches $63.67\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($90.06\%$ given the candidate pool) by resolving embedding ties among paralogs; applied out-of-sample to *B. subtilis*, the combined model reaches $43.06\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($81.58\%$ given the candidate pool; Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Protein language models provide strong candidate representations when reference reaction centroids exist (Driver 1), while operon and pathway context disambiguate paralogs and drive recovery in catabolic operons and twilight-zone strata (Drivers 2 and 3). We next tested whether this context-aware recovery extends to secondary metabolism and uncultured microbial genomes.
- **Paragraph 3 (Biosynthetic gene clusters and candidate gap resolution in 97 uncultured MAGs):**
  - *(a) Question:* Can local genomic synteny recover secondary metabolic gene clusters that carry zero flux on minimal glucose, and generate testable pathway hypotheses in uncultured lineages?
  - *(b) How tested:* Audited 106 antiSMASH-curated biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) and applied GSPA to 97 uncultured bacterial and archaeal MAGs spanning 16 phyla (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d; Supplementary Section~S6).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* On the 106 BGCs, relying on local BGC synteny lifts overall BGC $F_1$ from $0.7395$ to $0.8959$ at $99.6\%$ non-BGC specificity; across the 97 uncultured MAGs, GSPA identifies $568$ high-confidence candidate pathway gap-fills ($q \ge 0.50$), increasing mean draft pathway completeness from $0.511$ to $0.656$ (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Local operon synteny recovers secondary metabolic loci outside core biomass pathways and ranks candidate genes for missing biosynthetic steps in uncultivated phyla; we next examined how Stage 2B stoichiometric flux modeling ensures that core metabolic repairs are physiologically active.

### Subsection 2.6: Positive-cost flux modeling eliminates reaction padding and attributes responsibility to gene loci
- **Paragraph 1 (108-genome stoichiometric audit — why negative-cost MILP adds zero-flux padding):**
  - *(a) Question:* Why does negative-cost MILP gap-filling (METEOR) degrade enzyme precision, and how does positive-cost lexicographic pFBA resolve this defect?
  - *(b) How tested:* Audited reaction additions, steady-state flux activity, and elemental balance across 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions under negative-cost MILP (METEOR), positive-cost threshold LP repair, and GSPA's Stage 2B two-step lexicographic pFBA refiner (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a and Supplementary Table~S6).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Negative-cost MILP adds a mean of $313.35$ non-core reactions per genome, of which $83.72\%$ ($262.34$ reactions/genome) carry zero steady-state flux; switching to a positive addition cost eliminates zero-flux additions completely ($0.0\%$), and GSPA's lexicographic pFBA reduces active additions from $81.31$ (threshold LP) to $5.70$ flux-carrying, mass-balanced reactions per genome (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Negative objective costs reward reaction accumulation regardless of flux, whereas positive-cost lexicographic pFBA selects a minimal, flux-certified repair set; we next tested whether attributing these active repairs to individual genes via competitive locus responsibility improves experimentally validated enzyme discovery.
- **Paragraph 2 (Validation on `Price-149` RB-TnSeq fitness-validated bacterial enzymes):**
  - *(a) Question:* Does combining stoichiometric flux probing and competitive gene-locus responsibility improve enzyme identification on experimentally validated difficult bacterial proteins?
  - *(b) How tested:* Evaluated single-predictor and multi-predictor GSPA refinement across eight enzyme predictors on the `Price-149` RB-TnSeq mutant-fitness benchmark ($148$ host-mapped proteins across 22 bacterial genomes; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}b and Table~\ref{tab:price149}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* While glucose-only negative-cost MILP reduces $F_{\max}$ on three predictors because $55$ of the $148$ enzymes catalyze non-glucose catabolic steps, single-predictor GSPA refinement improves Top-1 hits on MAPred from $50$ to $56$ (and CLEAN from $60$ to $63$) using only that predictor's whole-genome outputs; when combined with Stage 0 multi-predictor pooling (which lifts GraphEC Top-1 from $18$ to $48$), full multi-predictor GSPA raises MAPred Top-1 further from $58$ (Noisy-OR) to $73$ ($49.3\%$; Table~\ref{tab:price149}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Multi-predictor pooling (Driver 1) and locus-specific stoichiometric refinement (Driver 3) provide additive gains on experimentally validated enzymes; we next tested whether these systems-level predictions reproduce organism-level growth and enzyme phenotypes across thousands of bacterial strains.

### Subsection 2.7: Systems-level annotations reproduce experimental bacterial phenotypes at scale
- **Paragraph 1 (Blind phenotype prediction in `BacDive` Track 1):**
  - *(a) Question:* Do GSPA's complex-aware and pathway-coupled posteriors improve zero-label prediction of experimental carbon-source utilization and diagnostic enzyme activity?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared blind Track 1 predictions against CarveMe, ModelSEED, gapseq, and negative-cost MILP (METEOR) across $2{,}332$ carbon-growth assays ($529$ strains), $17{,}496$ diagnostic enzyme assays ($3{,}017$ type strains), and $50{,}075$ assays across 10 diagnostic ECs ($6{,}313$ strains) in `BacDive` (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}d and Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Whereas negative-cost MILP reduces diagnostic enzyme precision and degrades heteromeric urease $F_1$ to $0.5610$ by boosting singleton subunits (versus $0.7626$ for complex-aware calling), blind GSPA Track 1 preserves gapseq's leading binary carbon-growth $F_1$ ($0.7952$) while providing calibrated continuous posteriors that lift carbon-growth ROC-AUC from $0.8082$ to $0.8567$ (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Enforcing heteromeric subunit completeness and cross-assay pathway coupling turns discrete metabolic reconstructions into calibrated physiological probabilities without training labels; we next tested whether these probabilities complement supervised phenotype classifiers across unseen genera.
- **Paragraph 2 (Out-of-genus transfer and partial-phenotype anchoring in `BacDive`):**
  - *(a) Question:* Do systems-level pathway posteriors improve supervised out-of-genus phenotype prediction and allow partial phenotypic observations of a strain to refine its remaining unassayed pathways?
  - *(b) How tested:* Evaluated 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation (`[Genus-CV]`) and Track 2 partial-phenotype anchoring ($30\%$ of a strain's assays revealed to calibrate pathway posteriors, evaluated on the held-out $70\%$; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive} and Supplementary Table~S7).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Under genus-blocked cross-validation, combining GSPA posteriors with DiASPora raises out-of-genus $F_1$ to $0.8710$ on carbon growth and $0.8713$ on diagnostic enzymes, outperforming all standalone metabolic and machine-learning baselines; in Track 2, conditioning on $30\%$ revealed assays lifts unsupervised held-out carbon $F_1$ from $0.7922$ to $0.8871$ ($0.9225$ when combined with DiASPora; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Mechanistic pathway coupling and statistical phenotype models provide complementary information across genera, and observed growth traits propagate through the metabolic network to constrain unassayed enzymes (Driver 3). Finally, we examined the small fraction of loci that remain ambiguous after all deterministic stages.

### Subsection 2.8: Selective guarded language-model reasoning resolves residual ambiguity
- **Paragraph 1 (Selectivity of Stage 3 anomaly triggers and zero-shot resolution on 120 anomalies):**
  - *(a) Question:* How selective is Stage 3 agentic triggering across whole proteomes, and can zero-shot tool-mediated reasoning resolve post-Stage-2 anomalies without introducing ontology violations?
  - *(b) How tested:* Measured anomaly trigger rates across the 88-proteome four-kingdom cohort and evaluated the Stage 3 agent (`Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8` with five deterministic genomic/ontology tools and the symbolic ontology guardrail) across 120 de-identified anomalies across three random seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:agent}a,b,d and Table~\ref{tab:agent}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Deterministic triggers select a mean of only $1.84\%$ of proteins per proteome ($<2.5\%$ in every domain); on the 120 anomalies, the guarded multi-turn agent achieves a 3-seed mean $F_1$ of $0.9115$ with $0.0\%$ accepted ontology violations, whereas ablating the symbolic guardrail admits cross-clade violations and lowers $F_1$ to $0.8515$ (Table~\ref{tab:agent}).
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Stage 3 acts strictly as a targeted tie-breaker on the $<2.5\%$ of loci left uncertain by Stages 1 and 2, with formal admissibility guaranteed by the symbolic guardrail; we next tested whether the agent can disambiguate tied candidate genes in metabolic gaps and prospective targets.
- **Paragraph 2 (Disambiguating tied metabolic gap candidates and prospective `CAFA6` targets):**
  - *(a) Question:* Can zero-shot agentic tool use distinguish candidate enzymes that remain tied in Ranks 2–5 after deterministic RLGC scoring, and does guarded reasoning improve prospective sequence annotation?
  - *(b) How tested:* Re-ranked the top five candidates across 50 de-identified ambiguous LRO metabolic gaps in *E. coli* K-12 and evaluated prospective `CAFA6` targets ($n=769$; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}c and Table~\ref{tab:agent}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* On the 50 ambiguous LRO gaps, Stage 3 re-ranking raises Hit@1 from $34.0\%$ (RLGC alone) to a 3-seed mean of $82.7\%$ ($p = 1.19 \times 10^{-7}$); on prospective `CAFA6` targets, guarded GSPA reaches macro $F_{\max} = 0.5433$ versus $0.4957$ for the PLM+3Di MLP baseline and $0.5130$ for an unguarded agent.
  - *(d) Meaning & (e) Bridge:* Inspecting fine-grained domain architectures and operon neighborhoods resolves paralog ties after deterministic priors have narrowed the candidate pool; we next compared this zero-shot agent against lightweight supervised tabular classifiers to establish when each approach is preferable.
- **Paragraph 3 (When a zero-shot neuro-symbolic agent is preferable to a supervised tabular classifier):**
  - *(a) Question:* Under what conditions is a tool-using language model warranted over a supervised tabular classifier trained on pre-extracted features?
  - *(b) How tested:* Compared the zero-shot Stage 3 agent against 5-fold cross-validated Logistic Regression, Random Forest, GBDT, and MLP classifiers trained on either 3 pre-tool scalars or all 8 post-tool numerical features paired with the symbolic guardrail (Table~\ref{tab:agent}).
  - *(c) Key numbers:* Zero-shot Stage 3 ($F_1 = 0.9115$) outperforms pre-tool 3-scalar classifiers ($F_1 = 0.7500$–$0.8283$) and approaches the in-distribution 8-feature supervised upper bound ($F_1 = 0.9720$ for MLP) without requiring labeled anomaly training splits, while proposing a 3-seed mean of $13.7$ valid ancestral GO relaxations on conflict cases where binary classifiers propose at most $2$ (Table~\ref{tab:agent}; illustrative application to five Empty Quarter desert extremophile isolates in Supplementary Section~S8 and Supplementary Table~S9).
  - *(d) Meaning:* Supervised tabular classifiers provide a fast, high-accuracy solution for binary promotion when labeled in-distribution anomaly data are available, whereas zero-shot guarded reasoning is advantageous for novel clades lacking anomaly training labels and for multi-class ancestral GO relaxation.

## 5. Section 3: Discussion (5 Paragraphs) & Section 3.1: Limitations (1 Sub-section)
- **Paragraph 1 (Synthesis of the Three Drivers):**
  - Restate the central thesis without a list of numbers: shifting functional annotation from isolated sequences to complete genomes combines three cumulative drivers — multi-predictor protein-level calibration (Driver 1), whole-genome architectural and ontological constraints (Driver 2), and systems-level stoichiometric and guarded neuro-symbolic reasoning (Driver 3).
- **Paragraph 2 (Driver 1 in relation to GOLabeler and NetGO):**
  - Position Stage 0 within the multi-source protein-ensemble lineage established by GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}. Emphasize that sequence homology, domain profiles, PLMs, and structural similarity remain the indispensable foundation on which whole-genome inference builds: GSPA converts their ensemble output into a calibrated probability distribution that subsequent genome-scale priors update.
- **Paragraph 3 (Driver 2 and the transition to complete genomes):**
  - Connect operon coupling, RLGC neighborhood density, heteromeric complex completion, and cross-ontology GO–Uberon/PO/CL relaxation to the routine availability of complete genomes from long-read sequencing and modern assembly \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2020metaflye}. Explain the division of labor between local chromosomal context (which ranks candidate loci within a neighborhood) and global organismal constraints (which enforce complex stoichiometry and clade-specific anatomy via semantic ancestor relaxation).
- **Paragraph 4 (Driver 3 and how GSPA advances beyond METEOR):**
  - Discuss METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor} as our initial effort to move beyond sequence similarity via metabolic gap-filling, and explain the three conceptual advances that resolve METEOR's limitations: (1) coupling metabolic gaps to local operon architecture (RLGC), (2) replacing negative-cost reaction-level MILP ($83.7\%$ zero-flux additions) with role-aware DNF mechanism aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic pFBA, cognate-substrate probing, and competitive locus responsibility against an unassigned-gene hypothesis, and (3) validating that these locus-specific updates improve both fitness-validated enzyme discovery (`Price-149`) and organism-level phenotype prediction (`BacDive`).
- **Paragraph 5 (Selective neuro-symbolic reasoning vs. supervised classifiers):**
  - Discuss how restricting Stage 3 to $<2.5\%$ of proteins makes neuro-symbolic reasoning practical at genome scale, clarify the complementary roles of the language model (evidence synthesis and ancestral term proposal) and the deterministic symbolic guardrail (formal ontological verification), and summarize the operational boundary between fast supervised tabular classifiers (for in-distribution binary promotion) and zero-shot guarded agents (for novel clades and structured ancestral relaxation).
- **Subsection 3.1: Limitations (3 concise paragraphs or 1 structured progression, phrased positively as what the design establishes and what specific next experiment extends it):**
  1. *Dependence on Stage 0 candidate recall, assembly continuity, and isolated-sequence benchmarks:* On well-annotated reference genomes, Stage 0 establishes bulk accuracy while Stages 1–3 refine the $<15\%$ of twilight-zone and structurally constrained loci; in LRO gap-filling, unconditional recovery is bounded by Stage 0 candidate pool recall and Track B uses pretrained ESM-2 weights with UniRef50 exposure; and on isolated-sequence benchmarks (`CAFA6` and `Holdout-125`), inference relies on Stage 0, Stage 2A, and Stage 3 without operon coordinates. Expanding Stage 0 with remote structural fold search, evaluating strictly time-split PLMs, stratifying LSO decoys by same-operon paralogs, and linking prospective benchmark targets to their source genome assemblies will directly extend these boundaries.
  2. *Scope of steady-state stoichiometry and cross-ontology axioms:* Stage 2B models steady-state mass-balanced flux connectivity over mapped universal reactions under specified media, and Stage 2A covers the $2{,}715$ GO terms with explicit Uberon, PO, or CL logical definitions. Incorporating transcriptomic expression priors, automated transporter substrate models, and expanded cross-ontology logical definitions will broaden coverage of condition-specific regulation and clade-specific anatomy.
  3. *Deployment of Stage 3 and experimental status of dark-matter predictions:* While zero-shot guarded reasoning supports out-of-distribution disambiguation and ancestral GO relaxation, 8-feature supervised classifiers paired with the symbolic guardrail provide a $100\times$ faster alternative when labeled anomaly training splits exist; finally, candidate pathway repairs in the 97 uncultured MAGs and five Empty Quarter extremophile isolates represent ranked computational hypotheses that motivate heterologous expression and mutant fitness assays.
