# Section-by-Section and Paragraph-by-Paragraph Narrative & Structural Plan (Self)

## 1. Overarching Research Narrative (The Three Drivers of Functional Annotation)
Every section of the paper (Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, and Methods) is organized around a single coherent research argument explaining **why** GSPA improves protein function prediction across organisms:
1. **Driver 1 — Multi-modal per-protein evidence integration (Stage 0):** Combining sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity into a calibrated probability baseline (building on the multi-source ensemble paradigm pioneered by GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}). Homology- and similarity-based predictors are not obsolete; they establish the foundational per-protein likelihood distribution across the well-conserved fraction of the proteome.
2. **Driver 2 — Exploiting whole-genome architecture and organism-wide constraints (Stages 1 & 2A):** Moving the unit of inference from an isolated polypeptide sequence to the complete genome (co-directional operons, chromosomal gene neighborhoods, obligate heteromeric complex stoichiometry, core cellular essentiality, and formal taxonomic and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical consistency). This shift is **timely**: historically, short-read sequencing produced fragmented contigs from which individual proteins could be called without intact chromosomal context; advances in long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and modern genome and metagenome assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye} now routinely yield complete or near-complete genomes, making whole-genome context broadly available.
3. **Driver 3 — Going beyond homology through systems biology, stoichiometric flux modeling, and selective neuro-symbolic reasoning (Stages 1, 2B & 3):** Our initial effort to move beyond homology, METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor}, coupled metabolic network gap-filling with enzyme prediction via negative-cost reaction-indicator MILP, which introduced zero-flux reaction padding and uniform paralog boosting without genomic architecture. GSPA advances beyond METEOR by integrating systems biology with genomic architecture: (i) coupling metabolic gaps to reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) and operon synteny in Stage 1; (ii) replacing negative-cost MILP with role-aware disjunctive normal form (DNF) complex/isozyme aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA), and competitive gene-locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis in Stage 2B; and (iii) selectively invoking a tool-using language model under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail on the $<2.5\%$ of proteins where deterministic evidence remains ambiguous in Stage 3.

## 3. Abstract (5 Sentences, ~190 Words — Narrative Arc, Zero Number Dumps)
- **Sentence 1 (Problem & Timeliness):** Protein function predictors typically evaluate sequences in isolation, even though long-read sequencing and modern genome assembly now routinely recover complete genomes with intact operon architecture, heteromeric protein complexes, and genome-scale metabolic networks.
- **Sentence 2 (The Method & Three Drivers):** Here we present Genome-Scale Protein Annotation (GSPA v2.1.0), a four-stage framework that improves proteome annotation through three mechanisms: (1) calibrating and pooling complementary sequence, domain, structure, and protein language model predictors at the protein level (Stage 0); (2) coupling predictions across the whole genome via iterative Bayesian priors for operon synteny, core essentiality, heteromeric complex coherence, and cross-ontology taxonomic and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical ancestor relaxation (Stages 1 and 2A); and (3) going beyond sequence similarity through systems-level parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) with competitive gene-locus attribution (Stage 2B) and selective guarded language-model disambiguation on the $<2.5\%$ of proteins that remain ambiguous (Stage 3).
- **Sentence 3 (Results on Drivers 1 & 2 — Whole-Genome Coherence & Anatomy):** Across multi-kingdom benchmarks spanning bacteria, archaea, viruses, and eukaryotes, Stage 0 pooling establishes strong protein-centric accuracy while whole-genome refinement eliminates $100\%$ of taxonomic and anatomical ontology violations, outperforming naive violation deletion across multicellular animals and plants and raising heteromeric complex subunit recovery Hit@1 from $56.0\%$ to $94.3\%$.
- **Sentence 4 (Results on Driver 3 — Systems Biology & Beyond Homology):** By enforcing positive-cost pFBA and gene-locus responsibility, GSPA eliminates the $83.7\%$ zero-flux reaction padding introduced by negative-cost MILP gap-filling across 108 metabolic models, improves experimentally validated enzyme identification on the `Price-149` fitness benchmark and across $19{,}828$ experimental `BacDive` growth and enzyme assays (reaching $0.9225$ hybrid carbon $F_1$), and raises ambiguous leave-reaction-out enzyme recovery Hit@1 from $34.0\%$ to $82.7\%$.
- **Sentence 5 (Availability):** GSPA v2.1.0 is openly available at `https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa`.

## 4. Section 1: Introduction (6 Connected Paragraphs)
- **Paragraph 1 (Problem formulation & timeliness of whole-genome annotation):**
  - Define genome-scale protein function annotation from general to specific: given the complete set of protein-coding genes $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ of an organism, their chromosomal coordinates and operon organization $\mathcal{K}$, and the organism's taxonomic lineage $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, assign Gene Ontology (GO) \citep{thomas2019gene} terms and Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers across $\mathcal{P}$ that are accurate for individual proteins and coherent at the level of cellular physiology.
  - Explain **why whole-genome inference is timely now**: historically, short-read sequencing and metagenomics produced fragmented contigs from which open reading frames were predicted in isolation without complete chromosomal coordinates or intact operons; the widespread adoption of long-read sequencing \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread} and repeat-graph assemblers \citep{kolmogorov2020metaflye} now routinely yields complete or near-complete genomes across cultivated isolates and metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs).
  - State the five structural invariants that a complete proteome annotation must satisfy: (1) core domain-essential cellular functions, (2) stoichiometrically connected metabolic pathways \citep{osterman2003missing,karp2018how}, (3) complete obligate heteromeric complexes \citep{meldal2022complexportal,giurgiu2019corum}, (4) formal GO taxonomic constraints \citep{deegan2010formalization}, and (5) multicellular anatomical consistency with the organism's body plan \citep{mungall2011cross,mungall2012uberon}.
- **Paragraph 2 (SOTA Part 1 — Per-protein similarity and multi-source protein-level ensembles [Mirrors Driver 1]):**
  - Explain how traditional annotation pipelines (such as NCBI PGAP \citep{li2021refseq}, Bakta \citep{schwengers2021bakta}, and InterProScan \citep{jones2014interproscan}) transfer annotations from sequence alignments \citep{buchfink2021sensitive} and domain profile HMMs \citep{eddy2011accelerated}, and how deep learning predictors and protein language models (such as ESM-2 \citep{lin2023evolutionary}, DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}, and CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}) extend similarity detection into the sequence twilight zone using learned sequence, structure \citep{gligorijevic2021structure,vankempen2024foldseek}, or ontology-axiomatic representations.
  - Explain the conceptual advance of multi-source protein-level integration: methods such as GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3} showed in CAFA evaluations \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded,zhou2019cafa} that combining complementary homology, domain, motif, and language-model features substantially improves protein-centric accuracy over any single predictor.
  - State the fundamental limitation: even when multiple channels are combined per protein, scoring each protein $p_i \in \mathcal{P}$ conditionally independently of the remaining proteome $\mathcal{P} \setminus \{p_i\}$ leaves metabolic pathway holes where individual enzymes fall below detection thresholds, produces isolated singleton subunit calls on multi-subunit complexes, and transfers clade-inappropriate developmental GO terms across distant homologs.
- **Paragraph 3 (SOTA Part 2 — Genomic architecture and local chromosomal context [Mirrors Driver 2]):**
  - Explain how comparative genomics moved beyond isolated sequences by exploiting gene neighborhood conservation, co-directional operon structure, and phylogenetic profiles \citep{dandekar1998conservation,overbeek1999use,pellegrini1999assigning,price2005novel,szklarczyk2023string}, and how recent genomic language models (such as gLM \citep{hwang2024genomic} and gLM2 \citep{cornman2024omg}) and context-aware predictors \citep{toibazar2024context,wiatrak2025contextualised} learn contextualized representations across multi-gene windows.
  - State the limitation: local gene neighborhoods capture co-occurrence on a contig, but they do not represent global cellular requirements — namely which core functions are still missing across the chromosome, whether all required subunits of a dispersed heteromeric complex are present, or whether transferred GO biological processes entail anatomical structures (such as a chambered heart in Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon} or floral organs in the Plant Ontology \citep{cooper2013plant}) that are absent from the organism's clade. Moreover, existing taxonomic post-filters \citep{deegan2010formalization} simply delete violating terms, discarding valid ancestral biological process information.
- **Paragraph 4 (SOTA Part 3 — Systems biology, metabolic gap-filling, and going beyond METEOR [Mirrors Driver 3]):**
  - Explain how systems biology and constraint-based metabolic reconstruction tools (such as Pathway Tools \citep{green2004bayesian}, ModelSEED \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, CarveMe \citep{machado2018fast}, gapseq \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, GapMind \citep{price2020gapmind}, and Reconstructor \citep{jenior2023reconstructor}) use reaction networks and flux balance analysis to identify missing enzymatic steps required for biomass growth.
  - Explain our initial attempt to couple genome-scale metabolic reconstruction with enzyme function prediction, METEOR \citep{niu2026meteor}: METEOR formulated a reaction-indicator mixed-integer linear program (MILP) that jointly selects reactions for growth and boosts the probabilities of their candidate genes. However, reaction-level MILP formulations assign negative objective costs to high-scoring candidate reactions — which activates hundreds of reactions that carry zero steady-state flux — and apply a uniform probability boost across all candidate paralogs of a reaction without accounting for heteromeric subunit stoichiometry, operon neighborhoods, or competition among candidate gene loci. Similarly, emerging tool-using biological language agents \citep{wang2025_geneagent,caufield2024_spires} can synthesize genomic evidence for ambiguous genes, but require selective invocation and formal symbolic verification to prevent ontology violations at proteome scale.
- **Paragraph 5 (How GSPA addresses these limitations via three drivers across four stages):**
  - Introduce GSPA v2.1.0 (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}) and explicitly map its four stages to the three drivers:
    1. **Driver 1 (Multi-predictor foundation):** Stage 0 calibrates and combines up to seven sequence, domain, structure, and PLM channels via correlation-grouped Noisy-OR (or refines any single standalone predictor), preserving the high-confidence homology backbone.
    2. **Driver 2 (Whole-genome architecture and ontology relaxation):** Stage 1 iteratively updates proteome-wide log-odds using priors for core essentiality, pathway and heteromeric complex coherence, taxonomic consistency, and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical validity, while Stage 2A enforces $100\%$ taxonomic and anatomical consistency by relaxing forbidden terms to their most specific valid GO ancestors and pruning isolated complex singletons.
    3. **Driver 3 (Systems biology and selective neuro-symbolic reasoning beyond homology):** Stage 1 couples metabolic gaps to reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) and operon synteny; Stage 2B replaces reaction-level negative-cost MILP with role-aware disjunctive normal form (DNF) complex/isozyme aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious FBA (pFBA) \citep{lewis2010omic}, and backward gene-locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis; and Stage 3 selectively invokes an open-weight language model (`Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8`) on the $<2.5\%$ of proteins with unresolved gaps or borderline conflicts under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail.
- **Paragraph 6 (Evaluation scope, main findings, and availability):**
  - Summarize the eight benchmark suites across bacteria, archaea, viruses, and multicellular eukaryotes (Table~\ref{tab:suites}), state what the evaluation establishes for each of the three drivers without a wall of numbers, and state software and data availability at `https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/gspa`.

## 5. Section 2: Results (6 Subsections, 18 Focused Narrative Paragraphs)

### Subsection 2.1: Multi-predictor pooling and whole-genome priors across reference proteomes (Suites 1 and 2)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: How do Driver 1 [multi-predictor pooling] and Driver 2 [whole-genome priors] partition credit on well-annotated reference genomes?):**
  - *How:* Ablated Stage 0 Noisy-OR pooling and all six Stage 1 priors plus Stage 2A enforcement across nine bacterial and archaeal reference proteomes (81 runs; Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; hyperparameter settings in Supplementary Section~S1 and Supplementary Table~S1).
  - *Finding:* Because roughly $85\%$ of annotated proteins in these model organisms share close sequence similarity with curated references, Stage 0 multi-predictor pooling establishes the baseline protein-centric accuracy (micro $F_{\max} = 0.7631$), while adding whole-genome priors and Stage 2A enforcement improves pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ to $0.7660$ ($p = 0.0391$) and raises the genome-scale structural audit score from $0.8459$ to $0.8643$ ($p = 0.00391$; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). The same pattern holds across the eight out-of-sample genomes when *E. coli* K-12 is excluded as the calibration genome.
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Multi-channel pooling captures the conserved homology core (Driver 1), leaving the remaining twilight-zone and structurally constrained loci for whole-genome refinement (Driver 2). To understand how each whole-genome constraint contributes to this structural gain, we examined the individual Stage 1 priors.
- **Paragraph 2 (Question: Which structural defects do individual whole-genome priors resolve, and how robust are they to noisy cross-clade transfers?):**
  - *How:* Compared single-prior ablations (`A2`–`A6`) and injected 900 cross-clade eukaryotic developmental and clade-restricted GO claims across the nine prokaryotic proteomes (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}c and Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; constraint tables in Supplementary Section~S2 and Supplementary Table~S2).
  - *Finding:* Each prior targets its corresponding genome-wide invariant: essentiality, pathway gap-filling, and operon neighborhood coupling jointly lift pathway coherence ($p = 0.00781$) and complex coherence (to $0.8852$), while the taxonomic and anatomical priors eliminate $100\%$ of baseline taxonomic violations ($p = 0.00391$) and suppress $97.6\%$ ($122/125$) of active post-calibration cross-clade perturbations ($99.7\%$ of all 900 injected claims; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Whole-genome priors act selectively on incomplete pathways, fragmented complexes, and cross-clade errors without degrading well-supported homology calls. This raises the question of whether whole-genome integration outperforms standalone annotation pipelines and individual predictors across broader taxonomic panels.
- **Paragraph 3 (Question: How does combining Driver 1 and Driver 2 compare against production genome annotators, deep-learning predictors, and genomic language models at scale?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated GSPA under uniform ancestor-propagated GO scoring on the 13-genome and 21-genome reference panels (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d), across 1,000 RefSeq bacterial genomes, and across 88 proteomes spanning Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya, and Viruses (Supplementary Section~S3).
  - *Finding:* On the 11-prokaryote panel, GSPA reaches a micro $F_{\max}$ of $0.8421$, outperforming NCBI PGAP ($0.7820$), InterProScan 5 ($0.7410$), and metagenomic-deepFRI ($0.6010$); on the 21-genome panel, 7-channel integration ($0.8705$ micro $F_{\max}$) outperforms every constituent channel including ESM-2 $k$NN ($0.8580$) as well as genomic language models gLM and gLM2 ($0.7280$). Across 1,000 RefSeq bacterial genomes, standalone neural predictors produce thousands of taxonomic violations per genome and incomplete pathways, whereas GSPA eliminates $100\%$ of taxonomic violations while raising full bacterial essentiality to $95.3\%$ and MetaCyc pathway coherence to $66.8\%$ in $5.4\text{ s/genome}$, with consistent accuracy gains across all four kingdoms on the 88-genome panel (Supplementary Section~S3).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Combining complementary predictors (Driver 1) with whole-genome structural priors (Driver 2) scales across domains of life in seconds per genome. Next, we tested whether these gains persist under prospective temporal holdouts where test annotations were deposited after database freeze dates.
- **Paragraph 4 (Question: Does GSPA improve function and enzyme prediction on prospective temporal holdouts and under strict donor homology masking?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated GSPA on the Suite 1 CAFA temporal holdout ($16{,}125$ protein–aspect evaluations, including $3{,}440$ twilight-zone targets at $<30\%$ sequence identity), on prospective `CAFA6` targets ($n=769$), on the Swiss-Prot `Holdout-125` enzyme benchmark (Supplementary Table~S3), and across $69{,}666$ proteins in 95 bacterial genomes under homology masking tiers from $100\%$ down to $\le 30\%$ identity (Supplementary Table~S4).
  - *Finding:* On the CAFA temporal holdout, Stage 0 pooling reaches macro $F_{\max} = 0.6929$ (outperforming standalone ProteInfer at $0.6138$ and DeepGOZero+BLAST at $0.6053$), and whole-genome Stage 1+2 refinement specifically lifts the $<30\%$ sequence-identity twilight-zone subset from $0.6723$ to $0.6775$; on prospective `CAFA6` targets, GSPA reaches macro $F_{\max} = 0.5433$ compared with $0.4957$ for the ESM-2 + ProstT5 baseline. On the 95-genome enzyme benchmark, whereas unfiltered MILP gap-filling (METEOR) degrades Top-1 accuracy on DeepProZyme and EnzBERT by promoting false paralogs, GSPA's stoichiometric and 3-channel consensus filters yield statistically significant net Top-1 enzyme gains across all predictors and homology tiers ($+126$ to $+155$ net Top-1 hits on CLEAN, $p < 10^{-25}$; Supplementary Table~S4).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* While multi-predictor pooling drives accuracy on conserved sequences, whole-genome and stoichiometric constraints specifically rescue twilight-zone proteins and prevent false-positive enzyme flips. To isolate how genomic architecture and systems biology recover missing enzymes when sequence homology is completely removed, we turned to leave-reaction-out metabolic gap-filling.

### Subsection 2.2: Genomic architecture and systems biology in metabolic gap-filling (Suites 3--5)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: When sequence homology for a metabolic reaction is masked, how much recovery comes from local genomic architecture [Driver 2] versus protein language model representations [Driver 1]?):**
  - *How:* Performed leave-reaction-out (LRO) trials across 256 strict reactions and 414 role-stratified reactions in *E. coli* K-12 MG1655 and 144 out-of-sample reactions in *B. subtilis* 168 under two regimes: Track A (masking both sequence homologs and PLM reaction centroids with cosine similarity $> 0.50$) and Track B (masking sequence alignments while retaining Swiss-Prot ESM-2 reaction centroids; Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}a–c and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *Finding:* Without PLM centroids, sequence-only ranking and uniform reaction-level MILP boosting (METEOR) recover only $1.95\%$ of masked *E. coli* enzymes at Rank 1 (Hit@1), whereas exploiting operon neighborhood kernel density (RLGC) and Stage 1 pathway coherence lifts unconditional Hit@1 twelve-fold to $23.44\%$ ($34.29\%$ among covered candidate pools; Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}). In Track B, ESM-2 reaction-centroid similarity provides a strong sequence-embedding baseline ($62.11\%$ Hit@1), and combining RLGC operon context, pathway coherence, and PLM centroids in Full GSPA (`M4`) reaches $63.67\%$ unconditional Hit@1 ($90.06\%$ given the candidate pool).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Reaction-level MILP without genomic context cannot distinguish the true enzyme from paralogs, whereas local operon architecture provides strong de novo gene attribution that complements PLM embeddings. We next examined how this recovery varies across metabolic roles, out-of-sample species, and uncultured microbial dark matter.
- **Paragraph 2 (Question: How does context-aware enzyme recovery generalize across core, catabolic, and secondary metabolic strata, out-of-sample species, and uncultured MAGs?):**
  - *How:* Stratified the 414 *E. coli* reactions into Core Biomass (Stratum A), Alternative Catabolic (Stratum B), and Secondary/Peripheral metabolism (Stratum C), evaluated out-of-sample *B. subtilis* 168 (with hyperparameters fixed on *E. coli*), audited 106 antiSMASH biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs), and applied GSPA to 97 uncultured bacterial and archaeal MAGs spanning 16 phyla (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}b–d and Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}).
  - *Finding:* Operon neighborhood density alone is particularly effective in Stratum B catabolic pathways ($55.56\%$ pool Hit@1) where catabolic genes cluster into tightly co-regulated operons, while Full GSPA reaches $82.8\%$–$93.5\%$ pool Hit@1 across all three strata and $81.6\%$ pool Hit@1 ($43.1\%$ unconditional) out-of-sample on *B. subtilis* (Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}). On the 106 antiSMASH BGCs, where minimal-glucose flux is inactive and inference relies on local BGC synteny, GSPA lifts overall BGC $F_1$ from $0.7395$ to $0.8959$ while maintaining $99.6\%$ non-BGC specificity; across the 97 uncultured MAGs, it identifies $568$ high-confidence candidate pathway gap-fills ($q \ge 0.50$), raising mean draft pathway completeness from $0.511$ to $0.656$ (Figure~\ref{fig:metabolism}d).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Exploiting operon and pathway architecture recovers missing enzymatic steps across both core and secondary metabolism without requiring growth on minimal glucose. Next, we investigated how Stage 2B stoichiometric flux modeling advances beyond our earlier METEOR formulation on whole-genome metabolic reconstructions and experimentally validated enzyme benchmarks.

### Subsection 2.3: Advancing beyond negative-cost MILP via parsimonious flux and locus responsibility (Suite 6)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: Why does negative-cost MILP gap-filling [METEOR] degrade gene-level precision, and how does Stage 2B positive-cost pFBA resolve this defect?):**
  - *How:* Audited reaction additions, steady-state flux activity, elemental balance, and essentiality certificates across 108 bacterial metabolic reconstructions and six curated BiGG reference GEMs (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a and Supplementary Section~S4 / Supplementary Table~S5).
  - *Finding:* Because negative-cost MILP gap-filling (METEOR) assigns negative objective costs to candidate reactions above an addition threshold, it adds a mean of $313.35$ non-core reactions per genome, of which $83.72\%$ ($262.34$ reactions/genome) carry zero steady-state flux and act as uncertified network padding. By contrast, GSPA's Stage 2B stoichiometric locus refiner solves a positive-cost lexicographic pFBA problem over a mass-balanced universal network and retains only reactions carrying non-zero pFBA flux, adding just $5.70$ flux-certified reactions per genome with $0.00\%$ zero-flux padding and zero unbalanced or dead-end additions (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Restricting gap-filling to positive-cost pFBA repairs ensures that every added reaction participates in an active, mass-balanced flux route. We next tested whether coupling this stoichiometric refinement with competitive gene-locus responsibility improves enzyme discovery on experimentally validated mutant fitness data.
- **Paragraph 2 (Question: Does Stage 2B stoichiometric locus refinement improve enzyme identification on experimentally validated difficult bacterial proteins?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated eight sequence, structure, and PLM enzyme predictors (including CLEAN, MAPred, DeepProZyme, EnzBERT, GraphEC, and TopEC) on the `Price-149` RB-TnSeq benchmark ($148$ host-mapped enzymes with mutant fitness phenotypes across 22 bacterial genomes \citep{price2018mutant}), separating single-predictor whole-genome refinement from multi-predictor ensemble refinement (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}b and Table~\ref{tab:price149}).
  - *Finding:* Because more than one-third of `Price-149` enzymes catalyze assay-specific catabolic steps outside minimal-glucose biomass, glucose-only MILP gap-filling (METEOR) reduces $F_{\max}$ on three of the six whole-genome predictors (Table~\ref{tab:price149}). Conversely, single-predictor GSPA refinement — which combines operon synteny, stoichiometric local-context probing, and competitive locus responsibility against an unassigned-gene hypothesis — improves Top-1 hits on CLEAN ($60 \to 63$), MAPred ($50 \to 56$), DeepProZyme ($49 \to 51$ with stoichiometric probing), and GraphEC ($18 \to 20$) without consulting any other predictor, while combining multi-predictor pooling (Driver 1) with whole-genome and stoichiometric refinement (Drivers 2 and 3) lifts Top-1 accuracy to $73/148$ ($49.3\%$, $p = 2.9 \times 10^{-4}$) on MAPred and $66/148$ on CLEAN (Table~\ref{tab:price149}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Systems-level stoichiometric refinement improves single-predictor enzyme calls when paired with cognate-substrate probing and competitive locus attribution, and compounds with multi-predictor pooling when multiple channels are available. To test whether these systems-level gains translate to organism-level phenotypes across thousands of bacterial strains, we evaluated experimental growth and enzyme assays in `BacDive`.

### Subsection 2.4: Predicting experimental growth and diagnostic enzyme phenotypes at scale (Suites 7 and 8)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: How do stoichiometric gap-filling and multi-model systems integration perform on large-scale experimental strain phenotypes in `BacDive`?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated GSPA across $50{,}075$ diagnostic enzyme assays in $6{,}313$ bacterial strains, $2{,}332$ carbon-source utilization assays across $529$ strains, and $17{,}496$ enzyme assays across $3{,}017$ type strains in `BacDive` \citep{reimer2022bacdive,schober2025bac}, comparing against automated metabolic reconstruction tools (CarveMe, ModelSEED, gapseq, and METEOR) and supervised genome-to-phenotype classifiers (Traitar, Random Forest, and DiASPora GBDT; Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}d, Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}, and Supplementary Table~S6).
  - *Finding:* Across the $6{,}313$-strain panel, minimal-glucose negative-cost MILP (METEOR) reduces enzyme precision (from $0.6442$ to $0.6194$) and degrades heteromeric urease (\texttt{EC 3.5.1.5}) $F_1$ to $0.5610$ by boosting isolated singleton subunits, whereas complex-aware reconstruction achieves $F_1 = 0.7626$ (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}). In Track 1 blind prediction (zero phenotype training labels), pooling calibrated reconstructions with unsupervised cross-assay pathway co-occurrence matches gapseq's binary $F_1$ while providing continuous posteriors that lift carbon-growth ROC-AUC from $0.8082$ to $0.8567$; under 5-fold genus-blocked cross-validation, integrating GSPA's posteriors with DiASPora raises out-of-genus $F_1$ to $0.8710$ on carbon growth and $0.8713$ on diagnostic enzymes, outperforming all standalone metabolic and machine-learning baselines (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Enforcing heteromeric subunit completeness and cross-pathway coupling improves zero-label phenotype prediction and provides informative systems-level features that generalize across unseen bacterial genera.
- **Paragraph 2 (Question: Can partial experimental phenotyping of a strain be propagated through the metabolic network to improve annotation of its remaining unassayed pathways?):**
  - *How:* In Track 2, revealed $30\%$ of each strain's experimental `BacDive` assays to anchor strain-level pathway activity posteriors and evaluated predictions strictly on the held-out $70\%$ assays ($1{,}641$ carbon and $12{,}316$ enzyme assays; Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *Finding:* Conditioning on the revealed $30\%$ phenotypes via GSPA's Track 2 Bayesian anchor lifts unsupervised held-out carbon $F_1$ from $0.7922$ to $0.8871$ ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9534$) and enzyme $F_1$ from $0.7737$ to $0.8545$, and reaches $F_1 = 0.9225$ ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.9788$) when combined with DiASPora (Table~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Because metabolic pathways are coupled at the cellular level, a small set of observed growth phenotypes constrains the probability of unassayed catabolic enzymes across the rest of the genome. Beyond metabolic pathways, whole-genome inference also constrains macromolecular complexes and multicellular developmental ontologies, which we evaluated next.

### Subsection 2.5: Heteromeric complex completion and multicellular anatomical constraints (Suites 2 and 5)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: How effectively does whole-genome context recover twilight-zone subunits of obligate heteromeric complexes while rejecting false-positive decoys?):**
  - *How:* Conducted 175 leave-subunit-out (LSO) trials across six prokaryotic genomes, where each true subunit's primary evidence was attenuated into the twilight zone ($q_e \in [0.22, 0.38]$) alongside five matched decoy proteins carrying comparable domain scores in unrelated genomic loci (6 candidates per trial, chance Hit@1 $= 0.167$; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}a,b and Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *Finding:* Baseline Noisy-OR leaves all 175 attenuated subunits below the $\theta = 0.50$ decision threshold ($F_1\text{@0.5} = 0.0000$, Hit@1 $= 0.5600$), and complex coherence alone boosts true subunits and domain-matched decoys equally ($F_1\text{@0.5} = 0.2459$; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}). Coupling complex coherence with operon neighborhood synteny disambiguates the true co-operonic partner, raising Hit@1 to $0.9371$ and $F_1\text{@0.5}$ to $0.6680$, while adding Stage 3 neuro-symbolic disambiguation further rescues dispersed non-operonic photosynthetic and respiratory subunits in *Synechocystis* sp.\ PCC 6803 ($F_1\text{@0.5}$ from $0.4080$ to $0.4480$) to reach $0.9429$ Hit@1 overall ($0.9379$ across the five out-of-sample species; Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Subunit stoichiometry identifies *which* complex is incomplete in the genome, while operon architecture and Stage 3 domain reasoning identify *which gene locus* encodes the missing subunit. We next tested how whole-genome ontological constraints operate in multicellular eukaryotes, where homology transfer frequently introduces cross-clade developmental errors.
- **Paragraph 2 (Question: Can cross-ontology GO–Uberon/PO/CL reasoning eliminate cross-clade anatomical errors in eukaryotes while preserving valid ancestral developmental annotations?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated six diverse eukaryotes (*S. cerevisiae*, *C. elegans*, *D. melanogaster*, *P. patens*, *A. thaliana*, and *M. musculus*) against unmodified Swiss-Prot ground truth under both unperturbed endogenous 7-channel predictions ($6{,}155$ proteins) and a cross-clade perturbation stress-test ($7{,}655$ proteins; Figure~\ref{fig:complex_anatomy}c,d and Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *Finding:* Unperturbed homology and PLM transfer already introduces $1{,}009$ endogenous taxonomic and anatomical violations across the six eukaryotic proteomes ($289$ in *C. elegans* and $717$ in *D. melanogaster*), and direct GO taxon constraints alone miss $44.2\%$ of stress-test violations because the forbidden organ structures (such as vertebrate heart or angiosperm flower development) are defined in Uberon, PO, and CL rather than direct GO taxon rules (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). By bridging GO with Uberon, PO, and CL and relaxing forbidden terms to their nearest valid GO ancestors (Stage 2A), GSPA eliminates $100\%$ of endogenous and stress-test violations, retains $95.1\%$–$98.7\%$ of baseline annotation information content, and outperforms naive violation deletion in micro $F_{\max}$ across all four multicellular animals and vascular plants (by $+0.0006$ to $+0.0157$, while improving endogenous 6-species CAFA $F_{\max}$ from $0.7096$ to $0.7117$; Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}).
  - *Meaning & Bridge:* Enforcing anatomical consistency in multicellular organisms requires cross-ontology reasoning over Uberon, PO, and CL, and semantic ancestor relaxation preserves the true conserved tissue- or process-level function that naive deletion discards. Finally, we examined the $<2.5\%$ of proteins where deterministic whole-genome priors still leave borderline ambiguity.

### Subsection 2.6: Selective neuro-symbolic agentic disambiguation under an ontology guardrail (Suite 8)
- **Paragraph 1 (Question: When deterministic whole-genome inference leaves borderline gaps or conflicts, what does selective neuro-symbolic agentic reasoning add beyond threshold rules and supervised tabular classifiers?):**
  - *How:* Evaluated the Stage 3 neuro-symbolic agent (`Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8` with five read-only genomic/ontology tools and a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail) across 120 de-identified post-Stage-2 anomalies and 50 ambiguous multi-candidate LRO metabolic gaps (all gene symbols masked to `"uncharacterized"`), comparing against deterministic Stage 1+2, a lowered posterior threshold ($\ge 0.35$), and 5-fold cross-validated supervised tabular classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, GBDT, and MLP; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}, Table~\ref{tab:agent}, and Supplementary Section~S5 / Supplementary Tables~S7–S8).
  - *Finding:* Triggered on a mean of only $1.84\%$ of proteins per proteome, zero-shot Stage 3 with the symbolic guardrail achieves $F_1 = 0.9115 \pm 0.0008$ and $92.8\%$ resolution accuracy across 3 seeds on the 120 anomalies with $0.0\%$ accepted ontology violations, outperforming pre-tool 3-scalar supervised classifiers ($F_1 = 0.7500$–$0.8283$) and approaching the in-distribution 8-feature supervised upper bound ($F_1 = 0.9216$–$0.9720$) without requiring labeled anomaly training data (Table~\ref{tab:agent}). Unlike binary tabular classifiers, the guarded agent also performs multi-class semantic ancestor relaxation on cross-clade conflicts ($13.7$ valid relaxations across 3 seeds vs.\ $0.83\%$–$2.50\%$ accepted violations when the guardrail is ablated) and elevates Hit@1 on the 50 ambiguous LRO metabolic gaps from $34.0\%$ (RLGC alone) to $82.7\%$ ($p = 1.19 \times 10^{-7}$; Figure~\ref{fig:agent}c and Table~\ref{tab:agent}), while resolving candidate osmoprotection and oxidative-stress pathway gaps across five desert extremophile isolates from the Empty Quarter (*Rub' al Khali*; Supplementary Table~S8).
  - *Meaning:* Selective neuro-symbolic reasoning provides zero-shot disambiguation of dispersed complexes, tied metabolic paralogs, and cross-clade conflicts on the small fraction of loci that remain uncertain after deterministic whole-genome refinement, while the symbolic guardrail guarantees zero ontology violations.

## 6. Section 3: Discussion & Section 3.1: Limitations (6 Connected Paragraphs)
- **Paragraph 1 (Synthesis of the Three Drivers):**
  - Return to the central thesis: while protein function prediction has long been treated as an isolated sequence-classification task \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,kulmanov2024deepgose}, the routine availability of complete genomes from long-read sequencing and modern assembly \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,kolmogorov2020metaflye,logsdon2020longread} makes the whole genome the natural unit of functional inference. Synthesize how GSPA's gains arise from the interplay of Driver 1 (multi-predictor calibration and pooling, extending the protein-level ensemble paradigm of GOLabeler \citep{you2018golabeler} and NetGO \citep{you2019netgo,wang2023netgo3}), Driver 2 (whole-genome operon, complex, and cross-ontology anatomical constraints), and Driver 3 (systems-level stoichiometric flux modeling and selective neuro-symbolic reasoning).
- **Paragraph 2 (Driver 3 vs. METEOR — Stoichiometric systems biology without zero-flux padding):**
  - Discuss how GSPA advances beyond reaction-level MILP gap-filling and our prior METEOR formulation \citep{niu2026meteor}: explain conceptually why negative addition costs in MILP cause $83.7\%$ of added reactions to carry zero flux and why uniform reaction-to-gene boosting inflates paralog false positives on catabolic and heteromeric enzymes, whereas role-aware DNF aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic pFBA, cognate-substrate probing, and competitive locus responsibility against an unassigned-gene hypothesis ensure that stoichiometric updates remain both flux-certified and locus-specific.
- **Paragraph 3 (Driver 2 + Driver 1 — Interplay of PLM representations and genomic architecture):**
  - Discuss how LRO and LSO benchmarks clarify the division of labor between sequence embeddings and genomic context: PLMs capture conserved sequence-to-function relationships when close reference centroids exist, whereas local operon synteny, complex stoichiometry, and Stage 3 reasoning drive recovery in the twilight zone, when embeddings are masked, or when multiple paralogs share similar domain architectures.
- **Paragraph 4 (Driver 2 — Cross-ontology anatomical reasoning in multicellular organisms):**
  - Discuss why enforcing ontology consistency in multicellular eukaryotes requires bridging GO with Uberon, PO, and CL \citep{mungall2012uberon,cooper2013plant,diehl2016cell}, and why semantic ancestor relaxation outperforms naive violation deletion when developmental regulators are transferred across clades.
- **Paragraph 5 (Driver 3 — When selective neuro-symbolic agents vs. supervised classifiers are warranted):**
  - Discuss the complementary regimes established by comparing the zero-shot Stage 3 agent against 3-scalar and 8-feature supervised classifiers: fast tabular classifiers suffice when in-distribution labeled anomaly splits and pre-extracted tool features are available for binary promotion, whereas zero-shot tool-calling under a deterministic symbolic guardrail excels on unseen clades, dispersed non-operonic complexes, and multi-class ancestral GO relaxation.
- **Subsection 3.1: Limitations (1 cohesive paragraph):**
  - Consolidate the five methodological boundaries positively (conserved homology dominance on reference genomes and isolated-sequence benchmarks where linking target accessions to genome assemblies will unlock operon/FBA priors; Stage 0 candidate pool recall and pretrained PLM exposure motivating time-split PLMs and remote fold search; steady-state stoichiometric assumptions motivating transcriptomic and transporter integration; current coverage of explicit GO–Uberon/PO/CL cross-products and experimental verification of MAG/extremophile hypotheses; and runtime trade-offs between zero-shot LLM tool use and supervised tabular classifiers).
