2. **The Three Core Drivers of Improvement (Central Narrative Arc):**
   - The paper's framing in the Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion must be built around the **three reasons why GSPA improves functional annotation**:
     - **Driver 1 — Combining complementary per-protein predictors (Stage 0):** Ensembling sequence homology, domain profiles, protein language models, and structural similarity establishes a strong calibrated per-protein baseline, building on multi-source protein-level ensemble paradigms such as GOLabeler (`you2018golabeler`) and NetGO (`you2019netgo`, `wang2023netgo3`).      - **Driver 2 — Exploiting whole-genome information and genomic architecture (Stages 1 and 2A):** Moving the unit of inference from an isolated protein sequence to the whole genome (chromosomal operons, gene neighborhoods, core cellular essentiality, obligate heteromeric complex stoichiometry, and organism-wide taxonomic and Uberon/PO/CL anatomical consistency). Explain why this shift is **timely**: historically, short-read sequencing yielded fragmented contigs from which individual proteins could be predicted without complete chromosomal architecture; with long-read sequencing (`amarasinghe2020longread`, `logsdon2020longread`) and modern genome and metagenome assemblers (`kolmogorov2020metaflye`), complete or near-complete genomes are now routinely recovered, making whole-genome context broadly accessible.
     - **Driver 3 — Going beyond homology through systems biology, stoichiometric flux modeling, and neuro-symbolic reasoning (Stages 1, 2B, and 3):** Our initial effort to move beyond homology, METEOR (`niu2026meteor`, accepted at the PSB 2027 "Biological Molecular Function: Beyond Homology" session), coupled metabolic network gap-filling with enzyme prediction via negative-cost reaction-indicator MILP, which introduced zero-flux reaction padding and uniform paralog boosting without genomic context. GSPA goes substantially beyond METEOR by exploiting systems biology together with genomic architecture: (i) reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) and co-directional operon coupling in Stage 1; (ii) role-aware DNF complex/isozyme aggregation, positive-cost lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA), and competitive gene-locus responsibility against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis in Stage 2B; and (iii) selective tool-using language-model reasoning under a deterministic symbolic ontology guardrail on the `< 2.5%` of loci that remain ambiguous after deterministic inference in Stage 3.
