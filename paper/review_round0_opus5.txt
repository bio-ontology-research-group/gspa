The manuscript titled "Whole-Genome Context, Flux-Certified Stoichiometric Refinement, and Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning Solve Proteome-Wide Function Annotation" presents GSPA v2.1.0, a four-stage system for assigning GO and EC terms to every protein in a genome jointly rather than one protein at a time. Stage 0 calibrates up to seven per-protein evidence channels (DIAMOND against experimental Swiss-Prot GOA, HMMER/Pfam2GO, InterProScan 5, ESM-2 and ProtT5 kNN transfer, DeepGOWeb/DeepGO-SE/ProteInfer, and FoldSeek/DeepFRI) by isotonic regression or Platt scaling, weights them by GO evidence code, and pools them with a Noisy-OR over the GO DAG (Eq. 1). Stage 1 runs up to 20 anchored log-odds updates over the whole proteome using six priors: essentiality, pathway and complex coherence, SAT4J-based taxon consistency with MUS extraction, a GO-Uberon/PO/CL anatomical prior, a gap-filling prior with a reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) score combining homology, an operon Bayes factor, Pfam EC-class compatibility and an ESM-2 reaction centroid, and an operon neighbourhood prior keyed to GFF3 coordinates (Eq. 2). Stage 2A deterministically removes or semantically relaxes taxonomically and anatomically forbidden terms to their nearest valid GO ancestor (Eq. 4) using 1,076 direct GO taxon constraints and 2,714 extracted GO-Uberon/PO/CL cross-product axioms over 8,330 anatomical taxon constraints. Stage 2B compiles GPRs into DNF mechanisms, aggregates isozyme disjunctions by Top-3 Noisy-OR and subunit conjunctions by geometric mean (Eq. 3), solves a positive-cost two-step lexicographic pFBA repair in HiGHS, and attributes certified flux back to loci against an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis (Eq. 5). Stage 3 routes the fewer than 2.5% of proteins carrying residual anomalies to a tool-using local language model behind a symbolic guardrail. The evaluation is unusually broad: a nine-genome prior ablation (81 runs), a 900-claim cross-clade injection experiment, 13- and 21-genome annotator panels, 1,000 RefSeq bacterial genomes, 16,125 CAFA temporal-holdout proteins, 769 CAFA6 targets, 125 post-cutoff Swiss-Prot enzymes, 95 homology-masked genomes (69,666 proteins), 88 four-kingdom proteomes, 256/414/144-case leave-reaction-out benchmarks, 175 leave-subunit-out cases, 108 metabolic reconstructions plus six BiGG GEMs, the Price-149 RB-TnSeq benchmark across eight enzyme predictors, BacDive panels up to 99,672 assays across 6,313 strains, 97 MAGs, and a desert extremophile case study. No supplementary material, per-genome tables, or appendix was provided to me, so several claims could be checked only against what the main text and the five figures and five tables assert.

The central idea is a good one and I am sympathetic to it: GO taxon constraints and GO-Uberon/PO/CL cross-products are genuinely underused in function prediction, the observation that direct GO taxon constraints catch only a few percent of cross-clade developmental false positives while the anatomy bridge catches the rest is a real and useful result, semantic ancestor relaxation is the right thing to do instead of deletion, and the audit of negative-cost MILP gap-filling (97.6% of added reactions carry zero flux) is a sharp and valuable piece of diagnostic work. The Price-149 table is also the one place where the paper does the right experimental bookkeeping: baseline, plus MILP, plus SLR, plus full GSPA, per predictor. Against that, I cannot recommend publication in its present form, for three reasons that I think are decisive. First, the headline genome-scale quality metric (GAEF) is arithmetically inconsistent with its own definition, and every one of its components is the direct optimisation target of a corresponding GSPA prior, so the reported GAEF gains are circular by construction and currently unfalsifiable. Second, the paper's own ablation shows that Stages 1 to 3 move protein-centric accuracy by between -0.001 and +0.002 F_max, while the title says these components "Solve" proteome-wide function annotation and the comparative text attributes Stage 0 pooling wins to "GSPA"; the Stage 0 versus Stages 1-3 separation that the authors clearly intend is stated correctly in two sentences and then lost everywhere else. Third, roughly a third of the named 2024-2026 baselines (DiASPora, ECrecer, DCGM/NeSy-Adapter, BacDive-AI, Agentic AFP, ProteInfer, DeepGraphGO, the ESM2+ProstT5 MLP) carry no citation at all, gLM2 is cited to the gLM paper, and BacDive-AI is cited to the BacDive database paper. I set out below what I think would make the work publishable; most of it is reporting and framing rather than new experiments, but comments 1, 2, 3, 6 and 11 do require work.

Comments:

1. Equation (7) cannot reproduce the GAEF values in Table 1. The weights sum to one, so GAEF is bounded above by 0.30 C_ess + 0.25 C_path + 0.20 C_cplx + 0.25. For A1 the table gives C_ess = 0.9400, C_path = 0.4975, C_cplx = 0.8636, whose weighted sum is 0.5791; even with C_cons = 1 and normalized IC = 1 the maximum attainable GAEF is 0.8291, yet the table reports 0.8459. The same holds for every row: A6 has a ceiling of 0.8291 against a reported 0.8542, and A7 to A9 have a ceiling of 0.8401 against a reported 0.8643. Either Eq. (7) is not the formula that was computed, or the component columns in Table 1 are not the quantities that enter it, or the weights differ. Since GAEF is the paper's primary genome-scale metric and Figure 2a,b plot it, please state the exact definition actually used, give the two missing columns (C_cons and the normalized IC term), define the normalization of IC and the constant lambda in C_cons = exp(-lambda V), and recompute the table.

2. GAEF is not independent of the method it evaluates. C_ess is the quantity that EssentialityPrior boosts, C_path is the quantity that CoherencePrior boosts, C_cplx is the quantity that CoherencePrior and the Stage 2A singleton pruner change, and C_cons is the quantity that ConsistencyPrior, AnatomicalPrior and the Stage 2A enforcer are defined to drive to zero. Reporting that "the GAEF Composite Score improves across every genome from 0.8459 to 0.8643" therefore establishes that the code implements its own specification; it is not evidence that the annotations became more correct. The same objection applies to the strongest numbers in the Abstract: "lifting 1,000-genome MetaCyc pathway coherence from 36.7% to 66.8%, complex coherence from 66.0% to 100.0%, and eliminating 100.0% of taxonomic violations". The 100.0% figures in particular are theorems about Stage 2A, correctly stated as guarantees in the Methods ("guarantees 100% compliance"), and then re-presented in the Abstract and Discussion as empirical findings. The scientifically interesting question, which the manuscript does not answer, is whether the roughly 30 percentage points of MetaCyc coherence bought by Stage 1 consists of correct annotations or of plausible-looking false positives. Macro F_max over the same 1,000 genomes moves from 0.6929 to 0.6938, which is compatible with either. I would encourage the authors to validate a random sample of the newly promoted pathway-completing and complex-completing annotations against held-out experimental evidence (the BacDive enzyme panel and the Price-149 fitness data are already in hand and could be used for exactly this), and to report precision of the promotions separately from the coherence counters.

3. The contribution of Stages 1 to 3 to protein-centric accuracy is close to zero in every experiment where it can be isolated, and the title and Abstract do not reflect this. In Table 1, Stage 0 alone (A1) gives CAFA F_max 0.8407 and micro F_max 0.8705; all six Stage 1 priors plus Stage 2 (A7 to A9) give 0.8398 and 0.8705, that is, a 0.0009 decrease in CAFA F_max and no change in micro F_max. On 1,000 RefSeq genomes, Stage 0 pooling gives 0.6929 and Stage 1+2 gives 0.6938. On the 88 four-kingdom proteomes the gains are +0.0022 (Bacteria), +0.0004 (Archaea), +0.0021 (Eukarya) and +0.0030 (Viruses). The authors state the honest version of this in one sentence ("Stage 0 multi-channel pooling establishes the bulk protein-centric F_max baseline ... while Stages 1 and 2 selectively resolve twilight-zone candidates"), and I give them credit for it, but the title asserts that whole-genome context and neuro-symbolic reasoning "Solve" proteome-wide function annotation, and the comparison paragraph attributes to "3-source GSPA" and "multi-evidence GSPA" wins over PGAP, InterProScan and metagenomic-deepFRI that are entirely attributable to Stage 0. Please change the title (a claim of the form "solve" is not supportable by any of these data), and add a Stage 0 / +Stage 1 / +Stage 2 / +Stage 3 decomposition column to every results table, as is already done correctly in Table 3. Without that decomposition a reader cannot tell which component produced any reported number.

4. Several of the "baselines" in the 21-genome panel are GSPA's own Stage 0 input channels. The text reports that multi-evidence GSPA reaches 0.869 micro F_max "surpassing genomic language model gLM/gLM2 (0.7280), ProteInfer (0.6600), ESM2-DeepGOPlus (0.3250), FoldSeek + ProstT5 3Di (0.2490), ESM-2 kNN (0.858), ProtT5 kNN (0.853), and DIAMOND (0.840)". ESM-2 kNN, ProtT5 kNN, DIAMOND, ProteInfer and FoldSeek are listed in Section "Stage 0" as channels (1), (4), (5), (6) and (7) of GSPA itself. An ensemble beating its own members by 0.011 to 0.016 is expected and is not a state-of-the-art comparison. Please separate the table into external systems and internal channels, and state explicitly that the ensemble-over-members comparison is an ablation.

5. The 2024-2026 baselines are largely uncited, and two are cited to the wrong paper. DiASPora (appears eight times across Table 4 and the Results text), ECrecer, DCGM / NeSy-Adapter, "Agentic AFP", "ESM2 + ProstT5 3Di MLP (2025)", ProteInfer, DeepGraphGO, DeepGOPlus, DeepGOCNN and "InterPro MLP" have no entry in references.bib and appear only as names with a bare parenthetical year. gLM2 is cited as "\citep{hwang2024genomic}", which is Hwang et al., Nat Commun 15:2888 (2024), the gLM paper; the gLM2 reference (Cornman et al., the OMG corpus) is already present in the bibliography as cornman2024omg and goes unused. "BacDive-AI RF \citep{schober2025bac}" cites Schober et al., NAR 53:D748 (2025), which is the BacDive database description and does not present a random-forest phenotype predictor. Please add the correct primary references for every named system, and for each state whether the number reported is taken from the original publication or produced by the authors re-running the tool, with version, weights and command. For the systems the authors re-implemented (DiASPora GBDT and BacDive-AI RF appear to be re-implementations, since they are evaluated under the authors' own genus-blocked folds), say so plainly and give the hyperparameters.

6. The reported ROC-AUC comparisons in Table 4 are not comparable across methods. For CarveMe, ModelSEED and gapseq, the ROC-AUC column is numerically identical to the balanced accuracy column in every single blind row (0.6233/0.6233, 0.7214/0.7214, 0.8082/0.8082, 0.5774/0.5774, 0.6310/0.6310, 0.7461/0.7461), and the same identity holds for DeepProZyme and the MILP in the 10-diagnostic-EC row. That is what happens when an AUC is computed from a binary prediction: it degenerates to balanced accuracy. GSPA, which emits a continuous posterior, is then credited with "ROC-AUC = 0.8820, +7.38% over gapseq". That comparison is an artefact of one method producing scores and the other producing labels. Please either remove the ROC-AUC column for the binary reconstruction tools or derive a genuine continuous score for them (for example, flux magnitude or gap-fill cost) and state how. Relatedly, the F1 claim on the 17,496-assay diagnostic enzyme panel does not survive inspection: GSPA Track 1 scores 0.7754 against gapseq 0.7753, with lower precision (0.8376 against 0.8384) and lower balanced accuracy (0.7454 against 0.7461), yet GSPA's recall and F1 are bolded and the text states that GSPA "improves" on this panel. A 0.0001 F1 difference on a panel where two of three other metrics favour the comparator should be described as a tie.

7. The headline LRO numbers are conditioned on an outcome of the method itself, and the conditional comparison is made over different subsets for different methods. The Abstract reports "90.1%-93.5% Top-1 LRO enzyme recovery given the candidate pool", but the candidate pool is produced by GSPA's own candidate generation, and the unconditional numbers in the same rows of Table 2 are 63.67% and 50.00%. Worse, within the E. coli strict block the sequence-only and MILP rows have n_cov = 175 while the RLGC and GSPA Full M4 rows have n_cov = 181, so the "Given Pool" columns of the compared methods are computed over different denominators and are not directly comparable. Please lead with the unconditional Hit@1 everywhere, report the conditional number only alongside the pool coverage (70.7% for n=256, 54.6% for n=414), and recompute all conditional comparisons on the intersection of the pools.

8. The geometric mean does not implement a conjunction, which undercuts the paper's central claim about heteromeric complexes. Equation (3) defines the obligate-subunit AND as S_mech(m) = (prod_u Q(u))^(1/|R(m)|). For three subunits with Q = 0.9, 0.9, 0.01 the geometric mean gives 0.20 whereas the conjunction of independent events gives 0.0081, so the geometric mean is strongly permissive precisely in the singleton-evidence case the method is supposed to suppress. The Results then attribute the urease F1 improvement (0.5748 and 0.5610 up to 0.6716) to "GSPA's subunit-AND conjunction and Stage 2 singleton pruning". Given Eq. (3), I suspect the improvement comes from the Stage 2A singleton pruner (a hard threshold at 35% subunit support) rather than from the aggregation, in which case the aggregation is doing the opposite of what is claimed. Please justify the geometric mean, give the ablation separating Eq. (3) from the singleton pruner, and reconcile the choice with the stated goal.

9. The stated behaviour of the unassigned-gene hypothesis does not follow from Equation (5) with the stated constants. The text asserts that "when all candidate genes fall below tau_orphan, h_empty absorbs the majority of the responsibility mass (r_j(h_empty) > 0.5)". With pi_empty = 0.15, tau_orphan = 0.20 and beta = 2.0, the h_empty term in the denominator is 0.15 * 0.04 = 0.006. Three candidates at q just below 0.20 with priors summing to one contribute about 0.04 to the denominator, giving r_j(h_empty) of roughly 0.13, not above 0.5. Please either correct the constants, correct the claim, or show the derivation. In the same equation pi_j(p, u) is never defined and kappa_+ is never given a value.

10. The convergence claim in Stage 1 is asserted, not established. "Anchoring each iteration to L^(0) ... guarantees convergence to a stationary fixed point when max |P^(k) - P^(k-1)| < 10^-3" conflates a stopping criterion with a convergence proof. The priors B_m depend on P^(k-1) through maxima over the proteome (P_max in EssentialityPrior), indicator-valued coverage functions in CoherencePrior, and a SAT call in ConsistencyPrior, none of which is continuous, so there is no obvious contraction. As it stands, "guarantees" is speculation. Either prove the map is a contraction under stated conditions, or weaken the claim to the empirical observation (3 to 6 iterations in practice) and report what fraction of runs failed to meet the criterion within K_max = 20 and what is done in that case.

11. How were the genome-scale priors applied to the CAFA suites, and what is the Stage 0 number on CAFA6? Suite 1 is 16,125 individual CAFA temporal-holdout proteins and the CAFA6 suite is 769 targets drawn from 223 species. GSPA's entire premise requires a complete proteome with GFF3 coordinates, a draft metabolic network, operon structure and an organism taxon. CAFA target sets supply none of these. Please state explicitly, for each of these two suites, whether full proteomes and GFF3 files were retrieved for all 223 species, how the draft networks were built, and what happens to the six priors when the proteome context is absent. Without this the Suite 1 and CAFA6 results cannot be interpreted. The CAFA6 result is additionally attributed to "GSPA Stage 3 neuro-symbolic integration" (F_max 0.5433 against 0.4957 for the ESM2+ProstT5 MLP), but Stage 3 is specified to touch fewer than 2.5% of proteins, which on 769 targets is about 19 proteins; a change of +0.048 F_max cannot come from 19 proteins. Report the Stage 0, Stage 1+2 and Stage 3 numbers on CAFA6 separately. Please also state the CAFA6 evaluation date and the provenance of the ground-truth annotations, since the challenge evaluation is prospective.

12. "Protocol H" does not deliver the zero leakage it claims for the PLM channels. The forbidden donor set restricts the DIAMOND database, the ESM-2 and ProtT5 kNN indices, the reaction centroids, the FoldSeek and DeepFRI templates and the calibrators, but ESM-2 and ProtT5 weights were pretrained on UniRef, which contains the test proteins and their close homologs, and no retraining is described. Describing Track B as "Strict Zero-Leakage" therefore overstates what the protocol achieves. Please rename it, state precisely which forms of leakage are and are not controlled, and say whether the comparator predictors (CLEAN, DeepProZyme, EnzBERT, GraphEC, MAPred) were subjected to any equivalent masking; if they were not, the comparison on the 95 homology-masked genomes handicaps GSPA's channels while leaving the baselines' training data untouched, or the reverse, and either way the direction of the bias needs to be stated.

13. Methods define a substantial amount of machinery that the Results never report. "Protocol D" (hyperparameters frozen on E. coli K-12 and evaluated zero-shot on 24 non-Escherichia proteomes) is specified and no Protocol D result appears anywhere. "Track B: Strict Zero-Leakage" is defined and never reported. Of the five formal recoverability ceilings, only C_task is used; C_struct, C_unique, C_operon and C_complex are defined and never instantiated with a number, although the Discussion claims the ceilings "clarify the complementary domains of stoichiometry and genomic synteny". Stratum B is split into B1 and B2 in the Methods and reported only as an undifferentiated "Stratum B". LRO-1, LRO-2 and LRO-3 holdout types are defined and never distinguished in the Results. The Methods state "all 15-genome LRO benchmarks" but the Results report LRO on two genomes (E. coli and B. subtilis). Please either report these or remove them; at present the Methods describe a different study from the one evaluated.

14. Two of the six Stage 1 priors contribute nothing in the ablation, and one of them is the mechanism the metabolic pillar depends on. In Table 1, A4 (GapFillingPrior) and A5 (GenomicContextPrior) are identical to A1 in all seven metric columns to four decimal places, including the GAEF composite. GapFillingPrior is the prior whose RLGC scorer carries the entire Pillar 3 result. Please explain why it has no measurable effect on the nine-genome panel, and whether the panel (nine well-annotated reference genomes with few genuine gaps) is simply the wrong test bed for it; if so, say so in the text rather than leaving two null rows unremarked. Relatedly, A7, A8 and A9 are identical in every column, so this ablation supplies no evidence whatever for the paper's claim that relax beats remove; the evidence for that is in Table 5, on eukaryotes only. Please state that explicitly.

15. The injection experiment reports suppression against two different denominators, and the overall elimination percentage in the eukaryote section does not match its own table. For the injection experiment, 900 claims are injected (100 per genome) but A1 is said to produce "a mean of 13.9 formal constraint violations per genome"; the relationship between 100 injected claims and 13.9 counted violations per genome is never explained, and the "99.7% suppressed" figure is computed on the 900 basis while "100.0% elimination of all direct taxon violations" is on a third basis. Separately, Table 5 gives E1 minus E2 eliminations of 33, 18, 3, 3, 23 and 12, which sum to 92 out of 2,377, that is 3.9%, whereas the text states "105/2,377 = 4.4% overall" and then derives "95.6%" from it. Please reconcile.

16. Three different values are quoted for the A6 configuration. Table 1 gives A6 micro F_max 0.8708 and CAFA F_max 0.8405. The first Results paragraph says A6 increases micro F_max "to 0.8708". The second paragraph says A6, A8 and A9 restore "micro F_max (0.8707) and CAFA F_max (0.8406)". 0.8707 is the A3 value in the table. Please make the text and the table agree, and say which configuration each quoted number belongs to.

17. The same macro F_max value, 0.6938, is reported as the result of two unrelated benchmarks: the 1,000-genome RefSeq panel ("applying GSPA Stage 1+2 raises macro F_max to 0.6938") and the Suite 1 CAFA temporal holdout ("GSPA (Iter 1 to Iter 3) reaches macro F_max = 0.6938"). An exact four-decimal coincidence across a 1,000-genome bacterial panel and a 16,125-protein CAFA holdout is not credible; please check for a transcription error.

18. Three cells in Table 3 are identical across three different input predictors. CLEAN, DCGM/NeSy-Adapter and ECrecer all end at "71 (0.4797)" Top-1 and "0.6317" F_max after full GSPA, despite baselines of 0.5374, 0.2962 and 0.2250 respectively. If the refined output is literally the same annotation set regardless of the input predictor, then for those rows GSPA is not refining the predictor at all but replacing it, and the reported deltas of +0.3355 and +0.4067 measure the strength of GSPA's own channels, not an improvement to DCGM or ECrecer. Please state what happened. Note also that the DCGM and ECrecer rows give n = 149 while 71/0.4797 implies a denominator of 148.

19. No confidence intervals or significance tests appear anywhere, despite the Methods stating that F_max and S_min are "computed across theta in {0.01, ..., 0.99} with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals". Not one interval is reported in any of the five tables or quoted in the text. Differences of +0.0009 (1,000 genomes), +0.0004 (15 archaea), +0.0003 (archaeal sub-30% identity subset) and +0.0001 (17,496 BacDive enzyme assays) are presented as improvements and in several cases bolded. Please report the intervals you say you computed, use a paired test across genomes where the comparison is paired, and stop bolding differences that fall inside them. The 1,000 RefSeq genomes and the 6,313 BacDive strains are also phylogenetically non-independent; some clade-level blocking or a per-genus random effect is needed before macro averages over them can be read as 1,000 or 6,313 independent observations.

20. The runtime comparison is not like-for-like. "6.1 s" for GSPA against "420.0 s" for gapseq, and "0.42 s/genome" against "313.8 s/genome" for the MILP, exclude Stage 0 entirely; the Abstract is careful to scope its timing claim to "Stage 1+2", but Table 4 places GSPA's 6.1 s in the same column as gapseq's 420 s, and gapseq's 420 s includes annotating the genome from scratch. Please report end-to-end wall-clock including DIAMOND, HMMER, InterProScan, ESM-2, ProtT5 and FoldSeek, with the hardware and thread count, alongside the stage-restricted figure.

21. The Stage 3 guardrail experiment is close to tautological as designed. The 120 anomalies include "40 borderline conflict and adversarial cross-clade hallucination traps", and the traps are constructed from the same taxon and anatomical constraint tables that SymbolicGuardrail checks. Config 3 blocking 8/8 traps that Config 4 accepts is therefore a property of the construction, not a finding. The internally consistent part of Table 6 (precision 54/61 = 0.885, accuracy 113/120 = 0.942, Config 1 abstention accuracy 66/120 = 0.550) is fine arithmetically, but I would encourage the authors to build the trap set from a held-out constraint source the guardrail does not consult, or to drop the guardrail ablation and simply state the guarantee. In addition, the agent is stochastic and the whole Stage 3 evaluation rests on a single run of 120 cases; please report repeated runs with temperature, seed, the prompt templates, and variance across runs, and give confidence intervals on precision.

22. The six curated BiGG GEMs are used to report CarveMe's and the MILP's reaction F1 but not GSPA's. The text gives CarveMe a mean reaction F1 of 0.6955 (EC F1 0.8536) and negative-cost padding a reaction F1 of 0.1877 to 0.2208 on iML1515, STM_v1_0, iYL1228, iJN1463, iYS854 and iYO844, and then reports for GSPA only that it adds 7.75 reactions per genome of which 100% are flux-certified. Flux certification is a precision-like property that is trivially satisfied by adding almost nothing: GSPA adds 7.75 reactions where the MILP adds 313.35. Without GSPA's reaction precision, recall and F1 on the same six curated GEMs, the comparison is one-sided. Please add the GSPA row to that comparison.

23. "Erroneous predictions of auxotrophies by CarveMe" (price2023carveme) is cited twice for claims it does not make. In the Introduction it supports the statement that MILP gap-fillers "apply uniform reaction-to-protein probability boosts that cannot disambiguate which specific gene locus encodes a missing enzymatic step", and in the Discussion it supports "why naive FBA post-processing has struggled to improve gene-level function annotation". That Comment reports specific auxotrophy prediction errors; it says nothing about probability boosting or locus attribution. Please cite it for what it shows, or find the right source.

24. The main MILP comparator is the authors' own unpublished preprint. METEOR (niu2026meteor, bioRxiv 2026, Niu, Kulmanov and Hoehndorf) is the "negative-cost MILP gap-filling" that is criticised in the Abstract, Introduction, two Results sections and the Discussion, and it shares two authors with this manuscript. That is legitimate, and auditing one's own prior method is admirable, but it should be disclosed in the text rather than left for the reader to infer from the citation, and the version, commit and parameter settings of METEOR used here must be stated, since the entire "97.6% zero-flux padding" result depends on the choice of c_j^add.

25. The paper's internal nomenclature is a serious obstacle to reading it. A reader must simultaneously track Pillars 1-6, Suites 1, 2, 2A, 2C, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, Tracks 1-2, Protocol H and Protocol D, Track A and Track B, Strata A, B1, B2 and C, LRO-1/2/3, configurations A1-A9, E1-E5, M1-M4, and Config 1-4, with no table mapping any of these to one another. The Abstract says "eight benchmark suites" while the labels enumerate at least ten, and Pillars and Suites are used interchangeably in places ("Suites 3, 4, and 5" versus "Stratum A/B/C"). Please add one mapping table and collapse the schemes that are redundant.

26. The Noisy-OR in Equation (1) takes a product over all descendants t of f and over all channels, under an independence assumption that does not hold for channels (4) DIAMOND, (4) ESM-2 kNN and (5) ProtT5 kNN, which transfer from overlapping donor sets. For a shallow term with thousands of descendants the product drives P_0 to 1 for nearly every protein, and the clamp at 1 - 10^-4 hides this. This matters because the paper's own framing argument ("roughly 85% of annotated proteins in reference model organisms carry high-confidence homology, P_0 >= 0.90") rests on this quantity, and because per-channel isotonic calibration is destroyed by the pooling step. Please report a reliability diagram for P_0 before and after pooling, and state whether any dependence correction is applied.

27. The semantic relaxation gain may be an artefact of Stage 1's own penalty. Under true-path propagation, Eq. (1) already gives P_0(p, a) >= P_0(p, t) for any ancestor a of t, so the transfer max(P^(K)(p,a), 0.95 P_0(p,t)) in Eq. (4) can only increase the ancestor's score when Stage 1 has previously depressed it, which is exactly what the soft ConsistencyPrior and AnatomicalPrior penalties do by propagating upward. The reported E4-to-E5 gains of +0.0089 to +0.0285 CAFA F_max may therefore measure the recovery of mass that GSPA itself removed, rather than an advantage over a plain post-filter that never penalised the ancestor in the first place. Please add that third arm: no Stage 1 ontology penalty, Stage 2A removal only. Separately, relaxation moves annotations to shallower, lower-information terms (cell migration, circulatory system development), which unweighted F_max rewards; please report the information-accretion-weighted WF_max and S_min for E4 and E5, as you do for CAFA6, since that is where the cost of relaxation would show.

28. Please define every symbol. The following are used without definition or without a value: pi_j(p,u) and kappa_+ in Eq. (5); lambda_BF, lambda_dom, lambda_PLM and the function sigma_cat in Eq. (2a); lambda_add, lambda_del, tau_del, tau_add and the weights w_j in Stage 2B; the tilde on S_rxn; lambda in C_cons = exp(-lambda V); the normalization of the IC term; and theta_eval, which appears once in Stage 2A. The symbol theta is overloaded between the decision threshold and the Protocol H sequence-identity threshold in adjacent paragraphs, and G denotes both the set of genomic coordinates (Overview) and a gene set (C_unique in the recoverability ceilings). Counting the alphas, betas, thetas, taus, gammas and the omega grading set, the method has on the order of 25 free parameters with no sensitivity analysis and no statement of how any of them was chosen; Protocol D was presumably designed to address this and is not reported.
