## Section 3: Discussion

### D1: How the three drivers fit together

```latex
\section{Discussion}

Protein function prediction has been formulated as a per-protein classification problem in which each sequence or structure is scored on its own \citep{radivojac2013large,gligorijevic2021structure,kulmanov2024deepgose}. GSPA formulates annotation as inference over the complete proteome of an organism, and our evaluation separates three contributions to this inference. Calibrated pooling of complementary per-protein predictors provides the initial probability of each protein--term pair (Driver 1). Whole-genome priors and deterministic ontology enforcement then update these probabilities with constraints that relate proteins to each other and to the organism: operon membership, the essential function profile of the domain, the subunit composition of obligate heteromeric complexes, and the taxonomic and anatomical admissibility of each term (Driver 2). Stoichiometric flux modelling, competitive locus attribution, and selective guarded reasoning finally connect the requirements of the metabolic network to individual gene loci (Driver 3). The three drivers act on different sets of proteins. Per-protein pooling determines most of the protein-centric accuracy on conserved proteins, and the genome-scale stages change the predictions for the smaller set of twilight-zone, pathway-gap, complex-subunit, and taxon-conflicted loci, where they restore pathway connectivity, complex completeness, and ontological consistency across the proteome.
```

### D2: Driver 1 compared with GOLabeler and NetGO

```latex
Stage 0 belongs to the lineage of multi-source protein ensembles. GOLabeler combines homology, domain, motif, and sequence-composition features through a learning-to-rank model \citep{you2018golabeler}; NetGO adds protein interaction networks \citep{you2019netgo}, and NetGO 3.0 adds protein language model embeddings \citep{wang2023netgo3}. These methods showed that different representations of a protein make partly independent errors, so that their combination ranks terms more accurately than any single representation. Our channel comparisons reproduce this observation on the reference panels and on the temporal holdout (Figure~\ref{fig:pillar12}d). GSPA uses the ensemble as the prior of a genome-scale inference. Calibrating each channel to a probability, taking the maximum within each group of correlated channels, and pooling across groups with Noisy-OR yields a log-odds value for every protein--term pair; the later stages add genome-scale shifts to this value, and the result retains its interpretation as a probability. Sequence homology, domain profiles, language models, and structural similarity therefore remain the foundation of the method. The whole-genome priors change the probabilities of candidate pairs that Stage 0 proposes, and the recall of the Stage 0 candidate set determines which functions the later stages can recover.
```

### D3: Driver 2 and the move to complete genomes (two paragraphs: prokaryotic context, then eukaryotic anatomy)

```latex
The whole-genome priors use information that becomes available when an assembly recovers gene order and the complete gene complement of an organism. Long-read sequencing and repeat-graph assemblers now produce complete or near-complete chromosomes for cultivated isolates and for many metagenome-assembled genomes \citep{amarasinghe2020longread,logsdon2020longread,kolmogorov2020metaflye}. Operon structure, the full set of complex subunits, and the presence or absence of each essential function can therefore be read directly from the assembly. Our experiments separate two roles of this information. Global organismal constraints identify which invariant a proteome annotation violates: the complex coherence prior detects that an obligate heteromeric complex lacks a subunit, the essentiality prior detects a missing core function, and the cross-ontology axioms detect a term that is inadmissible in the clade of the organism. Local chromosomal context identifies which gene supplies the missing function. In the leave-subunit-out trials, the complex prior raised the recall of the missing subunit while ranking the true subunit at the level of the Stage 0 baseline, and operon synteny raised top-ranked recovery from $56.0\%$ to $93.7\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:complex_lso}). Reaction-local genomic context localizes missing pathway enzymes in the same way, by scoring candidate genes according to their distance from genes annotated with neighbouring reactions.

In multicellular eukaryotes, the relevant global constraint is the body plan of the clade. Many developmental and physiological GO processes refer to an anatomical structure through a logical definition, and the taxon restriction is stated on that Uberon, PO, or CL class \citep{mungall2012uberon,cooper2013plant,diehl2016cell}. In our eukaryotic stress test, $44\%$ of the violations carried their taxon restriction on the linked anatomical class, and checking them required the cross-product axioms that connect each GO process to its anatomical structure (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). Semantic ancestor relaxation replaces a forbidden term with its most specific admissible ancestors, which converts each violation into a less specific valid annotation. In multicellular animals and vascular plants, where reference annotations include these tissue- and process-level ancestors, relaxation retained more correct annotations than deletion of the violating term.
```

### D4: Driver 3 and how GSPA goes beyond METEOR

```latex
METEOR was our first method to use the requirements of the metabolic network as evidence for enzyme function beyond sequence similarity \citep{niu2026meteor}. METEOR selects reactions with a mixed-integer linear program in which candidate reactions above an addition threshold receive a negative cost, and it transfers the support of each selected reaction uniformly to the genes that can catalyse it. The 108-genome audit identifies the consequence of the negative cost: the objective rewards each added reaction independently of its flux, and most reactions added in this way ($83.7\%$) carry zero steady-state flux (Figure~\ref{fig:fba_bacdive}a). Uniform transfer then raises the scores of all paralogs associated with a selected reaction. GSPA replaces both steps. A positive addition cost and a two-step lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis \citep{lewis2010omic} select a minimal set of repair reactions that carry flux in a mass-balanced network, and role-aware aggregation over isozymes and subunits determines how gene-level evidence supports each reaction. Competitive locus responsibility then distributes each flux-carrying repair among the candidate genes and an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis, weighted by sequence evidence, operon context, and synteny, so that a repair raises the score of a gene in proportion to the evidence that this gene supplies the reaction relative to its competitors. Reaction-local genomic context adds the chromosomal neighbourhood of pathway-adjacent genes to this evidence and allows a gap to be localized when homology and embedding donors are masked. Two experimental benchmarks test whether these locus-specific updates reflect biology. They improve enzyme identification on bacterial enzymes validated by mutant fitness \citep{price2018mutant}, and they reproduce carbon-source growth and diagnostic enzyme phenotypes in BacDive, including heteromeric enzymes such as urease, for which uniform transfer to single subunits reduces accuracy (Tables~\ref{tab:price149} and~\ref{tab:bacdive}).
```

### D5: Selective neuro-symbolic reasoning compared with supervised classifiers

```latex
Stage 3 applies a tool-using language model to the proteins that remain ambiguous after the deterministic stages. The anomaly triggers select fewer than $2.5\%$ of proteins in every domain of life, which makes multi-turn reasoning for each selected protein feasible on complete proteomes. The language model and the symbolic guardrail have separate roles. The language model reads operon neighbourhoods, pathway and complex gaps, protein evidence, and constraint reports through read-only tools and proposes a resolution, including an ancestral GO term when a specific term conflicts with the taxon of the organism. The guardrail checks every proposal against the Gene Ontology, NCBI Taxonomy, and the cross-ontology constraints and admits only proposals that satisfy them. Ablating the guardrail admitted cross-clade proposals and lowered $F_1$ (Table~\ref{tab:agent}); formal verification therefore contributes to accuracy as well as to admissibility. This design couples generation with external verification, as do GeneAgent, which checks language-model claims against curated databases \citep{wang2025_geneagent}, and SPIRES, which grounds extracted entities in ontology identifiers \citep{caufield2024_spires}; in GSPA the verification is a deterministic logical check against taxon and anatomical constraints. The comparison with supervised tabular classifiers determines when each approach applies. Classifiers trained on the eight post-tool features and combined with the same guardrail reached the highest binary promotion accuracy under cross-validation, and they are the appropriate choice when labelled anomalies from the same distribution are available and the decision is binary. The zero-shot agent outperforms classifiers restricted to pre-tool features and proposes structured ancestral relaxations, which makes it applicable to clades that lack curated anomaly labels and to conflicts that require traversal of the ontology.
```

## Subsection 3.1: Limitations

### L1: Stage 0 candidate recall, pretrained representations, and isolated-sequence benchmarks

```latex
\subsection{Limitations}

Our evaluation measures what genome-scale inference adds to a per-protein candidate set, and three properties of this design determine the next experiments. On well-annotated reference genomes, about $85\%$ of proteins have high-confidence Swiss-Prot homologs ($P_0 \ge 0.90$); Stage 0 therefore determines most of the protein-centric $F_{\max}$, and Stages 1--3 act on the remaining twilight-zone, pathway-gap, complex-subunit, and taxon-conflicted loci. In leave-reaction-out gap-filling, unconditional Hit@1 is the product of the Stage 0 candidate-pool recall ($175$--$181$ of $256$ reactions in \textit{E.~coli}; $76$ of $144$ in \textit{B.~subtilis}) and the conditional ranking accuracy, which we report separately (Table~\ref{tab:metabolism}). Track B uses ESM-2 weights pretrained on UniRef50, and Track A, which masks embedding donors and excludes reaction centroids, is therefore the stricter test of recovery from genomic context. The leave-subunit-out decoys are drawn from unrelated genomic loci, which measures the ability of operon context to separate a true subunit from domain-matched proteins elsewhere in the genome. The prospective benchmarks \texttt{CAFA6} and \texttt{Holdout-125} supply target sequences separated from their genome assemblies, and these evaluations measure the combined effect of Stage 0, Stage 2A, and Stage 3. Four experiments extend these results: adding remote structural fold search to Stage 0 candidate generation, evaluating protein language models trained on time-split sequence sets, sampling leave-subunit-out decoys from paralogs in the same operon, and linking prospective targets to their source assemblies so that operon and stoichiometric context apply to them.
```

### L2: Scope of steady-state stoichiometry and cross-ontology axioms

```latex
Stage 2B models steady-state, mass-balanced flux through reactions mapped in the sanitized universal network under a specified medium, and the five recoverability indicators (Section~\ref{sec:methods_slr}) identify the held-out reactions that this model can recover. Growth and diagnostic enzyme phenotypes in \texttt{Price-149} and BacDive additionally depend on transcriptional regulation, transporter specificity, and cofactor availability. Transcriptomic expression priors and automated transporter substrate models will extend Stage 2B to condition-specific and secondary pathways, and we will continue to pair the internal structural audit score with held-out experimental validation. The cross-ontology bridge covers the $2{,}715$ GO terms that carry logical definitions referring to Uberon, PO, or CL classes in the current GO release. Semantic ancestor relaxation improves $F_{\max}$ over deletion in multicellular animals and vascular plants, whose reference annotations include tissue-level ancestors, and improves recall and information content in yeast and moss. Additional logical definitions for GO processes and taxon restrictions on anatomy classes will extend this coverage to further clades and processes.
```

### L3: Deploying Stage 3 and the experimental status of candidate repairs

```latex
Stage 3 was evaluated with one open-weight language model across three random seeds; evaluating further open-weight models on the same de-identified benchmarks will establish how the accuracy of the agent depends on the underlying model. When labelled anomalies from the target distribution are available, supervised classifiers on the eight post-tool features combined with the symbolic guardrail resolve binary promotions more than $100$-fold faster than the agent, and GSPA supports both configurations. ## Section 4: Methods
```

### 4.1 Problem formulation and four-stage architecture

```latex
\section{Methods}

\subsection{Problem formulation and four-stage architecture}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_N\}$ denote the protein-coding genes of a target genome, and let $\mathcal{K} = \{(c_i, s_i, e_i, \zeta_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ denote their genomic coordinates, where $c_i$ is the contig, $s_i$ and $e_i$ are the start and end positions, and $\zeta_i \in \{+, -\}$ is the strand of gene $p_i$. The organism has the NCBI Taxonomy identifier $\tau_{\text{tax}}$. Let $\mathcal{T}$ denote the directed acyclic graph of the Gene Ontology (GO), in which the true-path relation $t \sqsubseteq a$ states that term $t$ is a subclass or part of its ancestor $a$, and let $\text{Anc}(t)$ denote the set of ancestors of $t$. The metabolic network $\mathcal{M}$ consists of a universal set of reactions with stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and associations between reactions, Enzyme Commission (EC) numbers, and GO molecular function terms. The axiom set $\mathcal{O}$ contains GO taxon constraints and the cross-ontology axioms that link GO terms to anatomical classes and their taxon constraints (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}). Proteome annotation assigns to each gene a set of terms; GSPA represents this assignment as the binary matrix $\mathbf{Y} \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times |\mathcal{T}|}$ and estimates the probability $P(p, f)$ that $Y_{p,f} = 1$ for every protein $p$ and term $f$, conditioned on $\mathcal{K}$, $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, $\mathcal{M}$, and $\mathcal{O}$. In the enzyme benchmarks, the same stages operate on EC numbers, which $\mathcal{M}$ links to reactions.

GSPA estimates these probabilities in four sequential stages (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Stage 0 calibrates and pools the claims of per-protein predictors into an initial probability for each protein--term pair. Stage 1 updates these probabilities iteratively with six genome-scale priors. Stage 2A enforces taxonomic and anatomical constraints deterministically and relaxes forbidden terms to admissible ancestors, and Stage 2B repairs the metabolic network with flux balance analysis and attributes each repair to gene loci. Stage 3 resolves the remaining anomalies with a tool-using language model whose proposals are verified against the ontology constraints. The benchmark suites, their cohorts, Stage 0 channel configurations, and the stages active in each suite are listed in Supplementary Table~S1.
```

### 4.2 Stage 0

```latex
\subsection{Stage 0: multi-channel evidence calibration and correlation-grouped Noisy-OR pooling}

Stage 0 converts the outputs of per-protein predictors into one probability for each protein--term pair. GSPA accepts either a single standalone predictor, such as CLEAN \citep{yu2023enzyme}, MAPred \citep{rong2025mapred}, DeepProZyme \citep{kim2023dpz}, EnzBERT \citep{buton2023enzbert}, GraphEC \citep{song2024accurately}, or TopEC \citep{vanderweg2025topec}, or claims from up to seven evidence channels $e \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{ch}}$: (1) sequence alignment with DIAMOND \citep{buchfink2021sensitive} against Swiss-Prot entries with GO annotations; (2) profile HMM search with HMMER 3 \citep{eddy2011accelerated} against Pfam-A with Pfam2GO mappings; (3) domain signatures from InterProScan v5 \citep{jones2014interproscan}; (4) $k$-nearest-neighbour transfer in the ESM-2 (650M) embedding space \citep{lin2023evolutionary}; (5) $k$-nearest-neighbour transfer in the ProtT5-XL-U50 embedding space \citep{elnaggar2021prottrans}; (6) sequence-based neural classifiers (ProteInfer \citep{sanderson2023proteinfer}, DeepGOWeb \citep{kulmanov2021deepgoweb}, and DeepGO-SE \citep{kulmanov2024deepgose}); and (7) structural similarity transfer with FoldSeek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and ProstT5 3Di sequences \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}.

A claim $c$ states that a protein $p$ has a term $f$ and carries the raw confidence $s(c) \in [0, 1]$ reported by its source tool $e(c)$. Raw confidences of different tools have different scales, and we therefore map each to a calibrated probability by Platt logistic calibration, $\tilde{q}(c) = \text{clip}\bigl(\sigma_{\text{log}}(a_{e(c)}\, s(c) + b_{e(c)}),\, \varepsilon_{\text{cal}},\, 1 - \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\bigr)$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}} = 10^{-4}$, where $\sigma_{\text{log}}$ is the logistic function and $(a_e, b_e)$ are tool-specific parameters (Supplementary Section~S1). Calibration places all tools on a common probability scale; to account for the differing reliability of evidence types, we multiply each calibrated probability by a reliability weight $r_{e(c)} \in (0, 1]$ assigned to the evidence type of the tool, ranging from $0.45$ for genomic context to $0.85$ for antimicrobial-resistance domain predictors (Supplementary Section~S1). This gives the weighted claim probability $q(c) = r_{e(c)}\, \tilde{q}(c)$.

Channels that rely on the same underlying evidence, such as alignment and domain matching to the same homologs, make correlated errors, and multiplying their probabilities as if they were independent would count the same evidence several times. We therefore partition the claims for each pair $(p, f)$ into correlation groups $\mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)$ by evidence type: homology (sequence similarity, sequence domains, and orthology), protein-sequence machine learning (neural classifiers and language-model embeddings), structure (structural similarity and structure-based deep learning), genomic and metabolic context, genomic language models, inferred context (reaction-local context and cross-genome transfer), sequence motifs, subcellular localization, and domain-specific predictors (complete assignment in Supplementary Section~S1). Within each group we take the maximum weighted probability, and across groups we pool with Noisy-OR, which treats the groups as independent sources of evidence:
\begin{equation}
  q_g(p, f) = \text{clip}\Bigl(\max_{c \in g} \bigl(r_{e(c)} \tilde{q}(c)\bigr) + \beta_{\text{so}} \, q_{g,(2)}(p, f), \, 10^{-3}, \, 0.999\Bigr), \qquad P_0(p, f) = \text{clip}\!\left(1 - \prod_{g \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{corr}}(p, f)} \bigl(1 - q_g(p, f)\bigr), \, 10^{-6}, \, 1 - 10^{-6}\right),
  \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
where $q_{g,(2)}(p, f)$ is the second-highest weighted probability in group $g$ and $\beta_{\text{so}}$ is a second-opinion weight (default $\beta_{\text{so}} = 0$). We convert $P_0(p, f)$ to log-odds and clip the result to $[L_{\min}, L_{\max}] = [-12, 12]$, which gives the Stage 0 anchor $L^{(0)}(p, f) = \text{clip}\bigl(\log \frac{P_0(p, f)}{1 - P_0(p, f)}, -12, 12\bigr)$. GO annotations are propagated to all ancestors along \textit{is\_a} and \textit{part\_of} relations according to the true-path rule.
```

### 4.3 Stage 1

```latex
\subsection{Stage 1: whole-proteome iterative Bayesian log-odds refinement}

Stage 1 updates the Stage 0 log-odds with six priors, each of which encodes one genome-wide invariant. A prior $B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr)$ is a log-odds shift for the pair $(p, f)$ computed from the current probabilities $\mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}$ of all proteins in the genome. The evidence for one protein therefore depends on the annotations of the rest of the proteome, and because these annotations change as the priors are applied, we compute the update iteratively. In iteration $k$, we add the weighted prior shifts to the Stage 0 anchor and move the current log-odds part of the way towards the result (Jacobi under-relaxation):
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f) &= \text{clip}\!\left(L^{(0)}(p, f) + \sum_{m=1}^{6} w_m \, B_m\bigl(p, f \mid \mathbf{P}^{(k-1)}, \mathcal{K}, \tau_{\text{tax}}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{O}\bigr), \, [-12, 12]\right), \\
    L^{(k)}(p, f) &= (1 - \gamma_{\text{iter}}) L^{(k-1)}(p, f) + \gamma_{\text{iter}} \tilde{L}^{(k)}(p, f), \qquad P^{(k)}(p, f) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(k)}(p, f)\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:stage1}
\end{equation}
with prior weights $w_m = 1$ and damping factor $\gamma_{\text{iter}} = 0.5$. Because every prior is added to the fixed anchor $L^{(0)}$, the Stage 0 evidence enters each iteration with its full weight. When a lower bound $L_{\text{pin}}(p, f)$ is specified for a pair, $L^{(k)}(p, f)$ is raised to $\min(12, L_{\text{pin}}(p, f))$ after damping. Iteration stops after at most $K_{\max} = 6$ iterations or when the mean absolute probability change over the changed pairs $\Delta_{\text{chg}} = \{(p, f) : |P^{(k)}(p, f) - P^{(k-1)}(p, f)| > 10^{-9}\}$ falls below $\epsilon = 5 \times 10^{-3}$; if the mean change increases in two consecutive iterations, GSPA returns the last stable iterate. A pair is active at iteration $k$ when $P^{(k)}(p, f) > 0.50$. The six priors are defined as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The essentiality prior $B_1$ ($\alpha_{\text{ess}} = 1.5$) raises candidates for core functions that the genome lacks. Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the essential GO profile of the domain of the organism (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukaryota, or Viruses), let $\mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \subseteq \mathcal{F}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the essential categories $e$ with $P^{(0)}(p, e) \le 0.50$ for all $p \in \mathcal{P}$ after ancestor propagation, and let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) = \bigcup_{e \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{ess}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})} \{t \in \mathcal{T} : t \sqsubseteq e\}$ be the set of all subclasses of the uncovered categories. Then
  \begin{equation*}
    B_1(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{ess}} \, \mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{uncov}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})\bigr].
  \end{equation*}

  \item The coherence prior $B_2$ ($\alpha_{\text{coh}} = 1.0$) raises missing parts of processes, pathways, and complexes. For a pair of GO processes $(X, Y)$ linked by \textit{has\_part} in which $X$ is active and $Y$ is missing, every subclass $f \sqsubseteq Y$ receives the process weight $w_{\text{proc}}(f) = \max(0, 1 - c_{\text{proc}})$, where $c_{\text{proc}}$ is the fraction of satisfied \textit{has\_part} pairs in the genome. For a reference pathway $W$ with at least one active step and step coverage $c_W \in (0, 1)$, every GO term $f \in \text{Req}(W)$ that maps to a missing step receives the pathway weight $w_{\text{path}}(f) = \max_{W \ni f} \max(0.25, 1 - c_W)$. For an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$, let $N_{\text{strong}}(f) = |\{p' : P^{(0)}(p', f) > 0.50\}|$ count the proteins with strong Stage 0 support and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) = |\{p' : \max(P^{(0)}(p', f), P^{(k-1)}(p', f)) \ge 0.08\}|$ count the candidate proteins. A candidate partner receives $w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f) = w_{\text{part}} = 0.85$ when $N_{\text{strong}}(f) = 1$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) \ge 2$, and $0.65\, w_{\text{part}}$ when $N_{\text{strong}}(f) \ge 2$; an isolated strong singleton ($N_{\text{strong}}(f) = 1$ and $N_{\text{cand}}(f) \le 1$) receives $w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f) = -w_{\text{sing}} = -0.75$. Then
  \begin{equation*}
    B_2(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{coh}} \Bigl(\max\bigl(w_{\text{proc}}(f), \, w_{\text{path}}(f)\bigr) + w_{\text{cplx}}(p, f)\Bigr).
  \end{equation*}

  \item The taxonomic consistency prior $B_3$ ($\alpha_{\text{cons}} = 3.0$) penalizes terms that conflict with the taxon of the organism. Once per iteration, we form the set of active terms $\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)} = \{t \in \mathcal{T} : \max_p P^{(k-1)}(p, t) > 0.50\}$ and encode the taxon constraints of these terms, together with multi-parent \textit{is\_a} relations, explicit disjointness axioms, and the asserted organism taxon, as a propositional satisfiability problem solved with SAT4J. The conflict set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)} = \mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{TC}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \cup \text{Core}_{\text{SAT}}(\mathcal{A}^{(k-1)})$ combines the terms directly forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ with the terms in the unsatisfiable core. A pair $(p, f)$ receives $B_3(p, f) = -\alpha_{\text{cons}}$ when $f$ or an ancestor of $f$ belongs to $\mathcal{V}_{\text{conf}}^{(k-1)}$, and $B_3(p, f) = 0$ otherwise.

  \item The anatomical prior $B_4$ ($\alpha_{\text{anat}} = 3.5$, $\alpha_{\text{boost}} = 0.65$) applies the body plan of the clade. Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the GO terms that are anatomically forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ through the cross-ontology bridge (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}), together with their descendants, and let $\mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}})$ be the non-root anatomical structures that are valid in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ and supported by at least $N_{\text{sup}} = 2$ distinct proteins with $P^{(0)} \ge 0.50$ for a term referring to the structure. With $\text{Anat}(f)$ the anatomical structures referenced by $f$,
  \begin{equation*}
    B_4(p, f) = \begin{cases}
      -\alpha_{\text{anat}}, & \text{if } (\{f\} \cup \text{Anc}(f)) \cap \mathcal{T}_{\text{forb}}^{\text{anat}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \ne \emptyset, \\
      +\alpha_{\text{boost}}, & \text{else if } \text{Anat}(f) \cap \mathcal{U}_{\text{act}}(\tau_{\text{tax}}) \ne \emptyset, \\
      0, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}

  \item The metabolic gap-filling prior $B_5$ ($\alpha_{\text{gap}} = 1.5$) raises candidates for open gaps in the metabolic network. Each open gap $r \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}$ is mapped to EC numbers and GO terms through explicit EC-to-GO mappings, with prefix expansion for partial EC numbers. A pair whose term matches a gap receives $B_5(p, f) = \alpha_{\text{gap}}$, reduced to $0.7\, \alpha_{\text{gap}}$ when the gap reaction was proposed by gapseq from low-homology evidence. After Stage 1 converges, reaction-local genomic context (RLGC) ranks candidate genes for each gap reaction $R$ by their chromosomal proximity to genes annotated with neighbouring reactions. A breadth-first search of depth $K_{\text{hop}} = 2$ over the reaction adjacency graph collects the GO terms $g \in \mathcal{N}_1(R) \cup \mathcal{N}_2(R)$ of reactions at hop distance $d(g) \in \{1, 2\}$, each weighted by $\alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)}$ with $\alpha_{\text{rxn}} = 0.5$. A gene $p'$ on the same contig is an anchor when $\max_g P(p', g) \ge \tau_{\text{anc}} = 0.30$, with anchor weight $w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) = \max_{g : P(p', g) \ge \tau_{\text{anc}}} \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} P(p', g)$. Anchor support is accumulated with a Gaussian kernel over base-pair distance, $K_{\text{bp}}(\Delta\text{bp}) = \exp\bigl(-\Delta\text{bp}^2 / (2 h_{\text{bp}}^2)\bigr)$ with $h_{\text{bp}} = 5{,}000$~bp, and combined with further genomic features into the RLGC score
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \sum_{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 4 h_{\text{bp}}} w_{\text{anc}}(p', R) \, K_{\text{bp}}\bigl(\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')\bigr), \\
      S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) &= \log D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) + \beta_{\text{div}} \text{Div}(p) - \gamma_{\text{com}} \text{Commit}(p) - \delta_{\text{self}} P(p, f_R) + \varepsilon_{\text{str}} \text{Strand}(p) - \zeta_{\text{gap}} \log_{10}\!\bigl(\max(1, \Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p))\bigr).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rlgc}
  \end{equation}
  Here $\text{Div}(p) = \sum_{g} \alpha_{\text{rxn}}^{d(g)} \bigl(1 - \prod_{p' \in W_8(p) \setminus \{p\}} (1 - P(p', g))\bigr)$ measures the diversity of neighbouring reactions represented in the window $W_8(p) = \{p' : |\Delta\text{bp}(p, p')| \le 8 h_{\text{bp}}\}$ ($\beta_{\text{div}} = 1.0$); $\text{Commit}(p) = -\log \max\bigl(10^{-4}, 1 - \max_{g \ne f_R} P(p, g)\bigr)$ penalizes genes already committed to a neighbouring reaction ($\gamma_{\text{com}} = 1.0$); $P(p, f_R)$ is the existing annotation of $p$ with the target term $f_R$ ($\delta_{\text{self}} = 2.0$); $\text{Strand}(p) \in [0, 1]$ is the fraction of anchors on the strand of $p$ ($\varepsilon_{\text{str}} = 0.2$); and $\Delta\text{bp}_{\min}(p)$ is the distance to the nearest anchor ($\zeta_{\text{gap}} = 0.1$). We score the top $Q = 50$ candidates with $D_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R) \ge 0.01$, normalize the top $20$ by a softmax $P_{\text{RLGC}}(p \mid R) = \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R)) / \sum_{p''} \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p'', R))$, and report the Bayes factor $\text{BF}_{\text{RLGC}} = \exp(S_{\text{RLGC}}(p, R))$. In the leave-reaction-out rankers, $S_{\text{RLGC}}$ is combined with cross-genome orthogroup co-occurrence (weight $\lambda_{\text{cg}} = 0.5$), Pfam domain compatibility, and, in Track B, the cosine similarity between the ESM-2 embedding of the candidate and the reaction centroid computed from Swiss-Prot enzymes.

  \item The operon prior $B_6$ ($\alpha_{\text{ctx}} = 1.0$, $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}} = 1.5$, $w_{\text{op\_cplx}} = 0.95$) transfers pathway and complex membership within co-directional operons. For an operon $O$ with $|O| \ge 2$ and a reference pathway $W$, let $k_O(W)$ be the number of members of $O$ with an active term in $\text{Req}(W)$, and let $c_O(W) = k_O(W) / |O|$ be the pathway consensus of the operon. A pair $(p, f)$ with $p \in O$ and $f \in \text{Req}(W)$ receives a shift proportional to $c_O(W)$, multiplied by $\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}}$ when $f$ also fills an open metabolic gap, and a pair for an obligate heteromeric complex receives an additional shift when another member of the operon is active for the same complex:
  \begin{equation*}
    B_6(p, f) = \max_{W \ni f : k_O(W) \ge 1} \Bigl(\alpha_{\text{ctx}} \, c_O(W) \, \bigl(\alpha_{\text{gap\_ctx}}\bigr)^{\mathbb{I}[f \in \mathcal{G}_{\text{gap}}]}\Bigr) + w_{\text{op\_cplx}} \, \mathbb{I}\bigl[f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}} \wedge \exists p' \in O \setminus \{p\} : P^{(k-1)}(p', f) > 0.50\bigr].
  \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}

We calibrated the $13$ Stage 1 hyperparameters (the prior strengths, the damping factor, $K_{\max}$, and $\epsilon$) and the $5$ Stage 2B hyperparameters ($\tau_{\text{core}}$, $\alpha_{\text{bio}}$, $\eta_{\text{null}}$, $\gamma_{\text{FBA}}$, and $\gamma_{\text{damp}}$; Sections~\ref{sec:methods_fba} and~\ref{sec:methods_slr}) on the \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 proteome and kept them fixed for all other genomes and benchmarks (Supplementary Table~S2).
```

### 4.4 Cross-ontology constraint bridge

```latex
\subsection{Cross-ontology taxonomic and anatomical constraint bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}

Many GO terms for developmental and physiological processes are defined by reference to an anatomical structure, and the taxonomic restriction of such a term follows from the restriction of the structure. To make these restrictions available to Stages 1 and 2A, we extracted five resources from the releases of GO, Uberon \citep{mungall2012uberon}, the Plant Ontology (PO) \citep{cooper2013plant}, the Cell Ontology (CL) \citep{diehl2016cell}, and NCBI Taxonomy (release versions and resource statistics in Supplementary Table~S3). The first resource contains $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon constraints ($13{,}819$ \textit{only in taxon} and $13{,}092$ \textit{never in taxon}) on $18{,}159$ GO terms \citep{deegan2010formalization}. The second contains $2{,}715$ equivalence and subclass axioms from GO logical definitions, which link $2{,}674$ GO terms to $1{,}294$ Uberon, PO, and CL classes through $30$ object properties, including \textit{results in development of} (RO:0002296), \textit{results in morphogenesis of} (RO:0002298), \textit{results in formation of} (RO:0002297), \textit{results in maturation of} (RO:0002299), \textit{occurs in} (BFO:0000066), and transport and organization relations. The third contains $8{,}331$ taxon constraints ($4{,}767$ \textit{only in taxon} and $3{,}564$ \textit{never in taxon}) on $1{,}247$ Uberon, PO, and CL classes. The fourth and fifth are the anatomical hierarchy ($7{,}722$ edges over $2{,}855$ Uberon, PO, and CL classes) and the taxonomic hierarchy ($1{,}818$ edges over $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades). A GO term $t$ is anatomically forbidden in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ when a logical definition links $t$ to an anatomical class $u$, and a taxon constraint on $u$ or on an ancestor of $u$ in the anatomical hierarchy excludes $\tau_{\text{tax}}$ or one of its ancestors in the taxonomic hierarchy.
```

### 4.5 Stage 2A

```latex
\subsection{Stage 2A: deterministic symbolic enforcement and semantic ancestor relaxation}

The Stage 1 penalties $B_3$ and $B_4$ lower the probability of a forbidden term, but a term with strong Stage 0 support can remain above the decision threshold. Stage 2A therefore enforces the constraints deterministically after Stage 1 and before Stage 2B. First, Stage 2A identifies the forbidden terms among the annotations of the proteome: terms that violate a direct GO taxon constraint or an anatomical constraint entailed through the bridge in $\tau_{\text{tax}}$, including the closure over GO ancestors. When no organism taxon is asserted, Stage 2A computes the minimum-cost set of annotations to remove as a weighted MaxSAT problem over the same propositional encoding.

Deleting a forbidden term also discards the evidence that the protein participates in a more general process that is valid in the organism. For each protein $p$ and forbidden term $t$, Stage 2A therefore traverses $\text{Anc}(t)$, excluding the three GO roots (GO:0008150, GO:0003674, and GO:0005575), and selects the most specific valid ancestors $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$, that is, the valid ancestors $a$ for which no valid ancestor $b \in \text{Anc}(t)$ satisfies $b \sqsubset a$. The evidence for $t$ before the constraint penalties is $P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(L^{(0)}(p, t) + \sum_{m \in \{1, 2, 5, 6\}} w_m B_m(p, t) + \sum_{m \in \{3, 4\}} w_m \max(0, B_m(p, t))\bigr)$, and semantic ancestor relaxation transfers this evidence to each valid ancestor $a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$:
\begin{align}
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, a) &= \max\Bigl(P^{(K)}(p, a), \, \gamma_{\text{relax}} \, P_{\text{unpen}}(p, t)\Bigr), \nonumber \\
  P_{\text{Stage2A}}(p, t) &= 0,
  \label{eq:relax}
\end{align}
with relaxation factor $\gamma_{\text{relax}} = 1.0$ and $P^{(K)}$ the converged Stage 1 probability. When $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(t, \tau_{\text{tax}})$ is empty, $t$ is removed. In the deletion configuration used for comparison, every forbidden term is removed. Second, Stage 2A attenuates isolated subunit calls. For an obligate heteromeric complex $f \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{het}}$ with exactly one protein at $P \ge 0.50$ and no candidate partner at $P \ge 0.08$ anywhere in the proteome, the probability of the singleton is set to $0.45$. Every relaxation, removal, and attenuation is recorded with its provenance.
```

### 4.6 Stage 2B: reaction support and pFBA

```latex
\subsection{Stage 2B: role-aware reaction support and positive-cost lexicographic pFBA}
\label{sec:methods_fba}

Stage 2B tests whether the annotated enzymes allow the organism to produce biomass and, where they do not, selects the smallest flux-carrying set of reactions that repairs the network. It operates on a sanitized universal metabolic network $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$ constructed from the ModelSEED biochemistry database \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, which contains $47{,}880$ reactions over $33{,}978$ metabolites. Of these reactions, $14{,}243$ carry EC mappings, and $11{,}826$ are mass- and charge-balanced enzymatic reactions; we flag the remaining $2{,}417$ pool or imbalanced reactions with an imbalance penalty $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100$ and add basal exchange bounds for inorganic compounds and cofactors.

The support of a reaction depends on how its catalysts combine. A reaction $j$ can be catalysed by any of several mechanisms $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j) = \{m_1, \dots, m_{K_j}\}$ (isozymes, a disjunction), and a mechanism $m$ requires all of its roles or subunits $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m) = \{u_1, \dots, u_{D_m}\}$ (a conjunction); this gives the gene--reaction rule in disjunctive normal form. Single-EC mappings in the universal network have $D_m = 1$, and multi-subunit rules have $D_m \ge 2$. Let $s_{p, u} \in (0, 1)$ be the current probability that gene $p$ performs role $u$, and let $\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)$ be the candidate genes for $u$. We aggregate support over the top $M_{\text{iso}} = 3$ candidates per role and gate the subunit conjunction at $\theta_{\text{sub}} = 0.25$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q(u) &= 1 - \prod\limits_{p \in \text{Top-}M_{\text{iso}}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u))} \bigl(1 - s_{p, u}\bigr), \\
    S_{\text{mech}}(m) &= \begin{cases}
      \Bigl(\prod\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u)\Bigr)^{1 / D_m}, & \!\min\limits_{u} Q(u) \ge \theta_{\text{sub}}, \\
      \min\limits_{u \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}(m)} Q(u), & \!\text{otherwise,}
    \end{cases} \\
    S_{\text{rxn}}(j) &= \max_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{\text{dnf}}(j)} S_{\text{mech}}(m).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:gpr_agg}
\end{equation}
The geometric mean rewards complete complexes, and the bottleneck gate assigns a mechanism with a weakly supported subunit the support of that subunit.

Reactions with $S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge \tau_{\text{core}} = 0.50$, together with open medium exchanges and spontaneous reactions, form the draft core $\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}$. Candidate repair reactions are the reactions outside the core in the solver domain $\mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}}$, which contains reactions with $S_{\text{rxn}}(j) \ge 10^{-3}$ and the universal flux-support skeleton derived from the 108-genome audit. Each candidate receives a strictly positive addition cost $c_j^{\text{add}} = \lambda_{\text{add}} (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j)) + w_{\text{imb}}(j) + \epsilon_{\text{par}}$ with $\lambda_{\text{add}} = 1.0$, $\epsilon_{\text{par}} = 10^{-3}$, and $w_{\text{imb}}(j) = 100$ for imbalanced reactions and $0$ otherwise. A positive cost makes every added reaction increase the objective, and the optimum therefore contains only reactions that the biomass constraint requires. We split reversible fluxes into non-negative parts, $v_j = v_j^+ - v_j^-$, and set the biomass target for medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$ to $v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \max\bigl(\alpha_{\text{bio}} \, v_{\text{bio}}^{\max}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}), 0.1\bigr)$ with $\alpha_{\text{bio}} = 0.10$. Stage 2B then solves a two-step lexicographic parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA) \citep{lewis2010omic} with the HiGHS linear programming solver (feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$). Step 1 minimizes the repair cost $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ and $v_{\text{bio}} \ge v_{\text{bio}}^{\text{target}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$, which yields the minimum cost $C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$. Step 1 is first solved with all imbalanced candidates fixed at zero flux, so that a balanced route is used whenever one exists, and the penalized imbalanced reactions are admitted only if this problem is infeasible. Step 2 minimizes the total non-biomass flux $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{dom}} \setminus \{\text{bio}\}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-)$ subject to the same constraints and $\sum_{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}}} c_j^{\text{add}} (v_j^+ + v_j^-) \le (1 + \delta_{\text{opt}}) C^*(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) + 10^{-9}$ with $\delta_{\text{opt}} = 10^{-6}$. The repair set $\mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}) = \{j \notin \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} : |v_j^{\text{pFBA}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})| > \epsilon_{\text{flux}}\}$ with $\epsilon_{\text{flux}} = 10^{-6}$ contains, by construction, only reactions that carry flux.

We assess each repair with three certificates and combine them into the repair confidence $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 0.5\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}] + 0.3\,\mathbb{I}[j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}] + 0.2\,\omega_{\text{ens}}(j) \in [0, 1]$. A repair is flux-variability essential ($j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{FVA}}$) when its minimum flux exceeds $\epsilon_{\text{flux}}$ among all solutions with repair cost at most $1.01\,C^*$. It is knockout essential ($j \in \mathcal{R}_{\text{KO}}$) when fixing $v_j = 0$ makes growth infeasible or raises the minimum repair cost above $1.10\,C^*$. The stability score $\omega_{\text{ens}}(j)$ equals $1.0$ when $j$ is active in at least $80\%$ of $N_{\text{ens}} = 5$ repair problems solved with $\pm 5\%$ uniform multiplicative noise on the costs, $0.65$ when it is active in at least one, and $0$ otherwise. When certificate auditing is disabled, $\pi_j^{\text{FBA}} = 1$ for every repair.
```

### 4.7 Stage 2B: locus responsibility and substrate probing

```latex
\subsection{Stage 2B: competitive gene-locus responsibility and substrate probing}
\label{sec:methods_slr}

A repair reaction states that some gene in the genome performs role $u$; Stage 2B then determines which gene. For each role $u$ of a repaired reaction $j$, the candidate genes compete with an explicit unassigned-gene hypothesis $h_\varnothing$, which represents the possibility that the catalysing gene is absent from the candidate set or unannotated. With the sequence odds $o_{p, u} = s_{p, u} / (1 - s_{p, u})$, the responsibility of gene $p$ is
\begin{align}
  w_{j, u}(p) &= \bigl(o_{p, u}\bigr)^{\beta_1} \, \text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u)^{\beta_2} \, \exp\bigl(\beta_3 C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) + \beta_4 S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)\bigr), \nonumber \\
  r_{j, u}(p) &= \frac{w_{j, u}(p)}{\eta_{\text{null}} + \sum_{p' \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cand}}(u)} w_{j, u}(p')},
  \label{eq:responsibility}
\end{align}
with $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = 1.0$, $\beta_3 = \beta_4 = 1.5$, and prior mass $\eta_{\text{null}} = 0.05$ for $h_\varnothing$. The operon Bayes factor $\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p, u) = \exp(B_5(p, u) / 1.5)$ is derived from the Stage 1 gap-filling shift, the synteny score $C_{\text{syn}}(p, u) = \text{clip}(B_6(p, u) + B_2(p, u), 0, 1)$ combines the operon and coherence shifts, and $S_{\text{ESM2}}(p, u)$ is the highest raw score among the protein language model and CLEAN claims for $(p, u)$. Because the responsibilities of competing genes share one denominator, a repair raises the score of a gene in proportion to its share of the evidence. The resulting log-odds shift is $\Delta L_{\text{FBA}}(p, u) = \gamma_{\text{FBA}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(u)} \pi_j^{\text{FBA}}\, r_{j, u}(p)\, (1 - S_{\text{rxn}}(j))$ with $\gamma_{\text{FBA}} = 2.2$, where $\mathcal{J}_{\text{rep}}(u)$ are the repairs that require role $u$. GSPA applies this shift iteratively with step weight $\lambda_t = \lambda_0 \gamma_{\text{damp}}^{t-1}$ ($\lambda_0 = 0.70$, $\gamma_{\text{damp}} = 0.60$) and recomputes Equations~\eqref{eq:gpr_agg}--\eqref{eq:responsibility} after each step.

A repair on minimal glucose medium captures enzymes of core biosynthesis, and enzymes of alternative catabolic pathways carry flux only on their own substrates. For the \texttt{Price-149} benchmark of enzymes with mutant fitness phenotypes \citep{price2018mutant}, we therefore evaluate three variants on each of the $22$ host genomes. The minimal-glucose variant solves the glucose repair problem and raises candidate EC numbers in repaired or active reactions. The cognate-substrate variant additionally probes the stoichiometric neighbourhood of each candidate reaction, giving support to reactions that share non-currency metabolites with the products of neighbouring genes at one or two reaction steps. The full variant combines the Stage 1 operon and coherence shift, computed from EC numbers of chromosomal neighbours within $\pm 10$ genes weighted by $1 / (1 + 0.25\,|\Delta g|)$, with the stoichiometric probe shift in a damped outer loop of three iterations with step weights $0.70$, $0.42$, and $0.252$. Multi-predictor refinement blends the single-predictor probability with the Noisy-OR probability of a five-predictor ensemble with weights $0.55$ and $0.45$. The complete probe and outer-loop equations and their coefficients are given in Supplementary Section~S4.

To characterize which held-out metabolic genes a stoichiometric model can recover, we define five binary recoverability indicators for each held-out reaction $j$ and true gene $p^*$. The structural indicator $\mathcal{C}_{\text{struct}}(j)$ equals $1$ when $j$ is present and mass-balanced in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{clean}}$. The task indicator $\mathcal{C}_{\text{task}}(j, \mathcal{E}_{\text{med}})$ equals $1$ when blocking $j$ requires repair flux on medium $\mathcal{E}_{\text{med}}$; it equals $1$ on minimal glucose for core biosynthetic reactions (Stratum A), $1$ on the cognate substrate for alternative catabolic reactions (Stratum B), and $0$ for secondary metabolism (Stratum C). The uniqueness indicator $\mathcal{C}_{\text{unique}}(j)$ equals $1$ when no unmasked isozyme can carry the full flux of $j$. The operon indicator is $\mathcal{C}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) = \mathbb{I}[\text{BF}_{\text{operon}}(p^*, j) > 1]$, and the complex indicator $\mathcal{C}_{\text{complex}}(p^*, j)$ equals $1$ when a multi-subunit complex with partner subunits present in the genome catalyses $j$.
```

### 4.8 Stage 3

```latex
\subsection{Stage 3: selective neuro-symbolic annotation completion}

Stage 3 addresses the loci that remain ambiguous after the deterministic stages. It is triggered by four categories of post-Stage-2 anomaly. A pathway gap is a pathway with completeness of at least $0.50$ that lacks a step $f$ for which candidates have probabilities in $[0.05, 0.50)$. An incomplete complex is an obligate heteromeric complex with exactly one protein at $P \ge 0.50$ and at least one candidate in $[0.05, 0.50)$. A borderline essential anomaly is an unfulfilled core essential category with sub-threshold candidates. A borderline conflict is a claim with probability in $[0.35, 0.62]$ that is subject to a taxonomic or anatomical conflict, where inspection of the domain architecture can identify an admissible ancestral term. Stage 3 evaluates up to $8$ candidates per anomaly and $150$ anomalies per genome.

For each anomaly, Stage 3 removes all evaluation metadata and runs a bounded multi-turn tool-calling loop with at most $6$ turns (at most $2$ turns in the fast configuration). The language model is the FP8-quantized $27$-billion-parameter open-weight instruction model Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 from the Qwen model family \citep{yang2024qwen25}, served locally with vLLM with a context window of $32{,}768$ tokens, temperature $0$, and at most $450$ output tokens per turn. The model has five deterministic read-only tools: inspection of the operon neighbourhood of a protein, inspection of the pathway or complex gap, retrieval of the evidence claims for a protein, a check of the symbolic constraints for a proposed term, and submission of a resolution. A resolution promotes a candidate, demotes it, relaxes a conflicting term to an ancestor, or abstains. The symbolic guardrail verifies every proposed resolution before acceptance against the $48{,}101$ GO term stanzas in the GO basic release ($40{,}122$ active and $7{,}979$ obsolete), the $1{,}305$ NCBI Taxonomy clades, the $26{,}911$ direct GO taxon constraints, and the $2{,}715$ GO--Uberon/PO/CL axioms, and it rejects every proposal that fails verification. Tool schemas and prompts are given in Supplementary Section~S7.

We evaluated Stage 3 on a de-identified benchmark of $120$ anomalies, $30$ per category, from seven genomes (\textit{E.~coli} K-12, \textit{P.~aeruginosa}, \textit{B.~subtilis}, \textit{M.~tuberculosis}, \textit{S.~cerevisiae}, \textit{C.~elegans}, and \textit{A.~thaliana}). For this benchmark, pathway gaps were sampled from pathways with completeness of at least $0.65$ with the top $5$ candidates in $[0.15, 0.49]$, and incomplete complexes from complexes with at least $50\%$ partner subunit coverage. Gene symbols of all candidates are replaced by ``uncharacterized'', candidates appear in a deterministic hash order, and true and decoy candidates expose the same domain and neighbourhood features. The $90$ completeness anomalies comprise $54$ promotable gaps and $36$ decoys with matched posteriors ($0.24$--$0.36$) or RLGC Bayes factors ($1.5$--$4.2$); the $30$ borderline conflicts comprise $15$ real cross-clade conflicts and $15$ adversarial traps, each of which must be demoted or relaxed to a valid ancestor. We additionally evaluated re-ranking of the top five RLGC candidates on $50$ ambiguous leave-reaction-out gaps in \textit{E.~coli} K-12 with distinct true proteins under the same de-identification. Each configuration was run with seeds $42$, $43$, and $44$ (per-seed results in Supplementary Table~S8). As baselines, we used the deterministic Stage 1 and Stage 2 output, a posterior threshold of $0.35$ combined with the guardrail, and Logistic Regression, Random Forest, gradient-boosted decision tree, and multi-layer perceptron classifiers trained by $5$-fold cross-validation. The classifiers used either three pre-tool features (current posterior, maximum claim score, and RLGC suggestion score) or eight post-tool features (additionally the likelihood log-odds, the final log-odds, the largest prior contribution, the RLGC Bayes factor, and the mean claim score), and the post-tool classifiers were combined with the guardrail.
```

### 4.9 BacDive phenotype protocols

```latex
\subsection{Experimental phenotype prediction in BacDive}
\label{sec:methods_bacdive}

We evaluated organism-level predictions against experimental phenotypes in BacDive \citep{schober2025bac}: carbon-source growth ($2{,}332$ strain--substrate assays in $529$ bacterial genomes matched to ProTraits), diagnostic enzyme activity ($17{,}496$ strain--EC assays in $3{,}017$ type-strain assemblies), and an extended enzyme panel ($50{,}075$ assays for $10$ diagnostic EC numbers in $6{,}313$ strains); cohort construction is described in Supplementary Section~S3. For each strain $s$ and assay $y$, with $M = 37$ carbon substrates or $M = 10$ diagnostic enzymes, gapseq v1.2 \citep{zimmermann2021gapseq}, ModelSEED v2.6.1 \citep{seaver2021modelseed}, and CarveMe v1.5.2 \citep{machado2018fast} provide binary calls $g(s, y)$, $m(s, y)$, and $c(s, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ for the pathway or reaction that the assay tests.

Assays that depend on shared pathways are correlated across strains, and GSPA uses this correlation without phenotype labels. We blend the three reconstructions into $B(s, y) = 0.55\,g(s, y) + 0.30\,m(s, y) + 0.15\,c(s, y)$ for carbon and $B(s, y) = 0.55\,g(s, y) + 0.28\,m(s, y) + 0.17\,c(s, y)$ for enzymes, centre each assay column, $\tilde{B}_{s, y} = B(s, y) - \bar{B}_y$, and compute the non-negative cosine similarity between assays, $\Psi_0(y, y') = \max\bigl(0, \tilde{B}_{:,y}^\top \tilde{B}_{:,y'} / (\|\tilde{B}_{:,y}\|_2 \|\tilde{B}_{:,y'}\|_2 + 10^{-9})\bigr)$ with $\Psi_0(y, y) = 0$. After row normalization, $\Psi(y, y') = \Psi_0(y, y') / (\sum_{y''} \Psi_0(y, y'') + 10^{-9})$, the cross-assay coupling score of strain $s$ for assay $y$ is $\psi(s, y) = \sum_{y'} \Psi(y, y') \bigl(B(s, y') - 0.5\bigr)$. In the blind Track 1 protocol, each binary call is mapped to a calibrated probability ($p_{\text{gap}} \in \{0.12, 0.88\}$, $p_{\text{ms}} \in \{0.22, 0.80\}$, and $p_{\text{cm}} \in \{0.30, 0.72\}$ for carbon; $\{0.14, 0.86\}$, $\{0.24, 0.78\}$, and $\{0.28, 0.74\}$ for enzymes), and the calls are pooled in log-odds space:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y) = \sigma_{\text{log}}\!\Bigl(w_{\text{gap}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{gap}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{ms}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{ms}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_{\text{cm}}\operatorname{logit}\bigl(p_{\text{cm}}(s, y)\bigr) + w_\psi\,\psi(s, y)\Bigr),
  \label{eq:bacdive_t1}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\text{gap}}, w_{\text{ms}}, w_{\text{cm}}, w_\psi) = (0.56, 0.28, 0.16, 0.65)$ for carbon and $(0.55, 0.27, 0.18, 0.55)$ for enzymes, and a decision threshold of $0.50$.

The supervised baselines are Traitar \citep{weimann2016traitar} (an L1-regularized linear support vector machine with probability calibration, $C = 0.3$ for carbon and $C = 0.2$ for enzymes), a BacDive Random Forest baseline \citep{koblitz2025diaspora,schober2025bac} ($200$ trees for carbon and $150$ for enzymes, maximum depth $8$), and DiASPora \citep{koblitz2025diaspora} (gradient boosting with $150$ trees, depth $4$, and learning rate $0.05$ for carbon; histogram gradient boosting with $100$ iterations, depth $4$, and learning rate $0.08$ for enzymes). All supervised models were trained and evaluated by $5$-fold cross-validation blocked by genus, with genera assigned to folds by a deterministic hash, so that every test strain belongs to a genus absent from training. The baselines use the three reconstruction calls and a one-hot assay indicator. The GSPA hybrid model forms a fold-local pooled score $p_{\text{pool}}(s, y)$ from training-fold calibrated gapseq and ModelSEED posteriors, the training-fold assay prevalence, $P_{\text{Track1}}(s, y)$, and $\psi(s, y)$, appends these quantities, the mean blended call of the strain, and the binary call vector of the strain across all assays to the baseline features, and trains a gradient-boosting classifier whose out-of-fold probability is blended with the pooled score as $P_{\text{Hyb1}} = 0.88\,P_{\text{ML}} + 0.12\,p_{\text{pool}}$. Pooling weights, feature definitions, and classifier settings are listed in Supplementary Section~S5.

The Track 2 protocol tests whether partial phenotypic observations of a strain refine predictions for its remaining assays. A deterministic hash reveals $30\%$ of the assays, and evaluation uses the held-out $70\%$. For carbon, the Track 2 anchor combines the pooled score with the revealed substrate prevalence $s_{b,\text{emp}}(y)$ and the coupling shift computed from revealed assays $\psi_{\text{rev}}(s, y)$, $P_{\text{Track2}} = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(0.72\operatorname{logit}(p_{\text{pool}}) + 0.28\operatorname{logit}(s_{b,\text{emp}}) + 0.85\,\psi_{\text{rev}}\bigr)$; for enzymes, it combines the pooled score with the revealed genus-level EC posterior $g_{e,\text{emp}}(s, y)$, $P_{\text{Track2}} = \sigma_{\text{log}}\bigl(0.70\operatorname{logit}(p_{\text{pool}}) + 0.30\operatorname{logit}(g_{e,\text{emp}})\bigr)$. Decision thresholds are tuned on the revealed assays only. The Track 2 hybrid blends a classifier trained on the revealed assays with the anchor as $P_{\text{Hyb2}} = 0.65\,P_{\text{ML2}} + 0.35\,P_{\text{Track2}}$ at threshold $0.50$.
```

### 4.10 Benchmarks, masking, metrics, and structural audit score

```latex
\subsection{Benchmarks, donor-homology masking, metrics, and structural audit score}

The eight benchmark suites, their cohorts, and their comparators are listed in Supplementary Table~S1, and the construction of each cohort is described in Supplementary Section~S3. All hyperparameters were calibrated on \textit{E.~coli} K-12 MG1655 and fixed for all other genomes (Supplementary Table~S2).

Homology to reference proteins can make a benchmark measure retrieval of known annotations. We therefore mask donor homology in two ways. Identity masking removes every reference protein $r$ with pairwise sequence identity $\text{pident}(g, r) > \eta_{\text{id}}$ to any test protein $g$, at $\eta_{\text{id}} \in \{100\%, 70\%, 50\%, 30\%\}$; taxonomic masking removes every reference protein from the genus or family of the test organism. Masked proteins are removed from the DIAMOND database, the ESM-2 and ProtT5 nearest-neighbour indices, the FoldSeek templates, and the data used to fit calibrators; for pre-merged claims without donor identity, the homology confidence is capped at $\eta_{\text{id}} / 100$. For the neural enzyme predictors CLEAN, DeepProZyme, and EnzBERT, reference lookup databases and training-split homologs above $\eta_{\text{id}}$ are filtered according to the homology-split protocol of each tool. The pretrained ESM-2 and ProtT5 weights are used as released and may have been exposed to UniRef50 sequences during pretraining. In the leave-reaction-out benchmarks, the gene--reaction links of the held-out reaction are removed. Track A masks sequence-alignment donors and embedding donors with cosine similarity above $0.50$ to the held-out gene and uses no reaction centroids; Track B masks sequence-alignment donors and retains ESM-2 reaction centroids computed from Swiss-Prot enzymes. Track A therefore provides the stricter test of recovery from genomic context.

We report two variants of $F_{\max}$ \citep{radivojac2013large,jiang2016expanded}. Protein-centric (CAFA) $F_{\max}$ computes precision and recall for each protein at threshold $\theta$, averages precision over proteins with at least one prediction and recall over all proteins, and takes the maximum over $\theta$ of the harmonic mean of the two averages. Pair-level micro $F_{\max}$ sums true positives, false positives, and false negatives over all protein--term pairs of a genome, computes one $F_1$ per threshold, and takes the maximum over $\theta$. We additionally report information-accretion-weighted $WF_{\max}$ and the minimum semantic distance $S_{\min}$ \citep{clark2013information}. Thresholds range over $\theta \in \{0.01, 0.02, \dots, 0.99\}$, or $\theta \in \{0.10, 0.20, \dots, 0.90\}$ in the eukaryotic anatomical perturbation suite. Ranking benchmarks report Hit@$k$, the fraction of trials in which the true gene ranks among the top $k$ candidates, and the mean reciprocal rank. We report $95\%$ bootstrap confidence intervals or exact Clopper--Pearson intervals for proportions, and compare paired configurations with two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across genomes or exact binomial McNemar tests across trials. Runtimes and peak memory were measured on dual-socket AMD EPYC 7763 nodes with $8$ CPU threads per genome; the language model and protein language model embeddings ran on an NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU.

The structural audit score of the Genome-Scale Annotation Evaluation Framework (GAEF) summarizes the genome-wide invariants that Stages 1 and 2 target, evaluated at $\theta_{\text{eval}} = 0.50$. It combines essential function completeness $C_{\text{ess}}$ (weight $w_{\text{ess}} = 0.30$); functional coherence $C_{\text{func}}$ ($w_{\text{func}} = 0.40$), the mean of the available pathway, complex, and process coherence scores; taxonomic and anatomical consistency $C_{\text{cons}} = \max(0, 1 - 0.01\,V(\mathcal{P}))$ ($w_{\text{cons}} = 0.30$), where $V(\mathcal{P})$ is the number of constraint violations; and functional resolution $C_{\text{res}} = \min(1, \text{meanIC} / 10)$ ($w_{\text{res}} = 0$). The weights are renormalized over the dimensions that can be evaluated:
\begin{equation}
  \text{GAEF}(\mathcal{P}) = \frac{\sum_{d \in \{\text{ess}, \text{func}, \text{cons}, \text{res}\} : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d \, C_d}{\sum_{d : C_d \ne \text{null}} w_d},
  \label{eq:gaef}
\end{equation}
which equals $0.30\, C_{\text{ess}} + 0.40\, C_{\text{func}} + 0.30\, C_{\text{cons}}$ when all three structural dimensions are evaluated. Because these dimensions measure the invariants that Stages 1 and 2 optimize, we use GAEF as an internal structural diagnostic and report it alongside evaluation against held-out experimental and curated annotations.
```
