**Nature Methods, Round 1 referee report**
Manuscript: Whole-Genome Context, Flux-Certified Stoichiometric Refinement, and Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning for Proteome-Wide Function Annotation (Al-Saeed, Kulmanov, Hoehndorf). Line numbers refer to `paper/main.tex`.

The manuscript titled "Whole-Genome Context, Flux-Certified Stoichiometric Refinement, and Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning for Proteome-Wide Function Annotation" presents GSPA v2.1.0, a four-stage system that assigns GO and EC terms to every protein of a genome jointly. Stage 0 calibrates up to seven per-protein evidence channels and pools them with a Noisy-OR over the GO DAG (Eq. 1). Stage 1 iterates an anchored log-odds update (Eq. 2) with six genome-scale priors: essentiality, pathway and complex coherence, SAT4J taxon consistency with MUS extraction, a GO-Uberon/PO/CL anatomical prior, a gap-filling prior with a reaction-local genomic context score (Eq. 3), and an operon prior. Stage 2A relaxes forbidden terms to the nearest constraint-satisfying ancestor (Eq. 5) using 1,076 direct GO taxon constraints and 2,714 extracted cross-product axioms; Stage 2B compiles reactions into DNF mechanisms (Eq. 4), runs a positive-cost two-step pFBA repair in HiGHS, and attributes certified flux to loci against an unassigned-gene hypothesis (Eq. 6). Stage 3 routes residual anomalies to a 27B open-weight language model behind a symbolic guardrail. The evaluation spans a nine-genome ablation, a 900-claim injection experiment, 13- and 21-genome panels, 1,000 RefSeq genomes, 16,125 CAFA holdout proteins, 769 CAFA6 targets, 125 post-cutoff enzymes, 95 masked genomes, 88 four-kingdom proteomes, LRO benchmarks in E. coli (n=256/414) and B. subtilis (n=144), 175 LSO trials, 108 reconstructions plus six BiGG GEMs, Price-149 across eight EC predictors, BacDive panels up to 99,672 assays, 97 MAGs and an extremophile vignette. The work is in scope for Nature Methods, and the two ideas I regard as genuinely new (the GO-Uberon/PO/CL bridge with ancestor relaxation rather than deletion, and the flux-certification audit of negative-cost MILP gap-filling) are good ones.

My assessment is that the manuscript cannot be accepted in its present form and needs a major revision before it can be properly assessed. The decisive problem is bookkeeping: the tables, the figures and the deposited result files disagree on most headline numbers, and in several places the deposited files reverse the direction of an effect claimed in the text (Comment 1). A second decisive problem is that the deposited files contain control rows omitted from the paper that change the attribution of the main results: on the LRO benchmark a per-protein ESM-2 reaction-centroid channel alone already reaches 62.1% Hit@1 against 63.7% for the full genome-context model; on the 125-enzyme holdout the Noisy-OR ensemble already matches GSPA's F_max exactly; and a plain positive-cost LP already achieves 0% zero-flux padding in the 108-genome audit (Comment 2). Third, the Methods contain enough undefined quantities, symbol collisions and unspecified update rules that the system could not be re-implemented from the text (Comment 8), and two headline configurations (the GSPA + DiASPora hybrid and the Track 2 Bayesian anchor) are never described (Comment 6). Fourth, hyperparameters are tuned on E. coli K-12, which is a test genome in five evaluations (Comment 3), and the Stage 3 agent is evaluated on the most heavily memorised genomes in any LLM pretraining corpus with no control for that (Comment 5). Most of what follows is reporting, consistency and framing; Comments 2, 3, 5, 6 and 9 need additional analysis of data the authors already have. I credit the authors for the honest statements that survive (Stages 1-2 leave bulk F_max "statistically unchanged", line 233; the CarveMe result at line 318; the n/a cells in Table 3); the revision should be built outward from those.
